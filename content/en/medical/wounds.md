> [!CAUTION]
> **Do not close a dirty wound, a bite or a deep puncture.** Closing traps germs inside, which can cause a deadly infection. Leave these wounds open and keep them clean.
>
> **After a bite from a dog, bat, monkey or other mammal, wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes, then get rabies vaccine as soon as possible.** Once rabies symptoms start, it is almost always fatal.
>
> **Stop severe bleeding first.** Cleaning comes later. See [Stopping Severe Bleeding](../medical/severe-bleeding.md).

## Quick steps

1. **Stop the bleeding** with firm pressure.
2. **Wash your hands** with soap. Wear gloves if you have them.
3. **Flush the wound** with lots of drinkable water, poured or squirted with force, for several minutes.
4. **Remove dirt** you can see, with clean tweezers or by scrubbing gently with clean cloth.
5. **Wash the skin around** the wound with soap and water.
6. **Decide:** close a small, clean, fresh cut with tape strips. Leave dirty wounds, bites and punctures open.
7. **Cover** with a clean dressing. Change it every day, or when it gets wet or dirty.
8. **Watch for infection** every day: spreading redness, swelling, pus, red streaks, fever.

## Washing out the wound

Washing out dirt and germs is the most important thing you can do to prevent infection.

- **Use drinkable water.** Clean tap water or treated water is as good as sterile salt water (saline) for cleaning wounds. Boiled and cooled water is fine. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).
- **Use a lot.** At least 1 liter (1 quart) for a small dirty wound, and more for large or very dirty wounds.
- **Use force.** A gentle trickle does not remove much dirt. Fill a clean plastic bag with water, make a pinhole in one corner and squeeze hard. A plastic bottle with a small hole in the cap, or a syringe without a needle, also works. Hold the wound open while you flush.
- **If you have no safe water,** boil some if you can. If that is impossible, still wash out heavy dirt with the cleanest water you have, then watch closely for infection. Dirt left inside is very dangerous.

Pick out remaining bits of dirt, glass or gravel with tweezers. Clean the tweezers first by boiling them or wiping them with alcohol. For a scrape with ground-in dirt, scrub gently with a clean wet cloth. This hurts, but dirt left in the skin causes infection and permanent dark marks.

### Soap and antiseptics

- **Soap and water:** use on the skin around the wound. Do not scrub soap deep into a wound.
- **Do not pour alcohol, hydrogen peroxide or full-strength iodine into a wound.** They damage the flesh and slow healing. Plain water is better.
- Povidone-iodine diluted with clean water to the color of weak tea can be used to wash very dirty wounds. It is not needed for clean ones.
- A thin layer of petroleum jelly, or antibiotic ointment if you have it, keeps the wound moist and stops the dressing from sticking.

## Close it or leave it open?

**Leave the wound open** if it:

- is a bite from any animal or person
- is a puncture (nail, thorn, knife tip, tooth)
- was dirty and could not be cleaned completely
- is more than about 6-12 hours old
- is crushed, ragged or has dead tissue
- already shows signs of infection
- is a gunshot wound or very deep

An open wound heals on its own from the bottom up. It takes longer and leaves a bigger scar, but it is much safer. Cover it with moist, clean gauze and change it every day. If it stays clean with no sign of infection for 3-5 days, the edges can then be pulled closer with tape strips.

**You can close a wound** if it is small, clean, straight, less than 6 hours old, and not a bite or puncture.

1. Clean and dry the skin around the cut completely.
2. Cut strips of tape about 5 mm (1/4 in) wide. Medical adhesive strips are best.
3. Stick one end of a strip on one side of the cut. Push the edges together and stick the other end across.
4. Start in the middle of the cut. Add strips about 3 mm (1/8 in) apart.
5. Leave small gaps so fluid can drain.
6. Leave the strips on until they fall off, usually within 7-10 days.

Medical skin glue works well for small, clean, straight cuts. Put it only on top of the closed edges, never inside the wound.

> [!WARNING]
> Gaping wounds over joints, on the hands, the face or the genitals, and wounds where you can see tendons, bone or fat, need a trained person to close them. Do not try to stitch a wound yourself unless you have been trained.

## Dressings and daily care

1. Cover the wound with a non-stick layer (gauze with petroleum jelly, or a non-stick pad), then an absorbent layer, then tape or a bandage.
2. Change the dressing **every day,** and whenever it is wet or dirty.
3. Wash your hands before and after.
4. If the dressing sticks, soak it off with clean water.
5. Look at the wound and smell it each time. Flush it again if it is dirty.

**No dressings?** Boil clean cotton cloth for 5 minutes and let it dry in a clean place, or iron it hot. Keep it in a clean plastic bag until you need it.

## Signs of infection

Some redness, swelling and pain in the first 2 days is normal. These signs are not:

- redness that **spreads** outwards. Mark the edge with a pen and check again in a few hours.
- **red streaks** running from the wound towards the body
- pus, a bad smell, or increasing swelling and pain after day 2
- swollen, painful lumps in the armpit, groin or neck
- **fever** or chills
- dark or black skin, blisters, or a crackling feeling under the skin

For an infected wound, open it if it was closed, flush it daily, keep it uncovered enough to drain, and raise the limb. Red streaks, fever, fast spreading or crackling skin need antibiotics and urgent care. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).

## Tetanus

Tetanus germs live in soil, dust and animal dung. They enter through deep, dirty wounds, punctures, burns and bites. Tetanus causes a stiff jaw ("lockjaw") and painful muscle spasms, usually 3 to 21 days after the injury. It is often fatal without intensive care.

- **Vaccination is the only reliable protection.** Adults need a booster every 10 years.
- After a **deep or dirty wound,** get a booster if the last one was more than 5 years ago, or if you are not sure.
- People who were never vaccinated need the vaccine and tetanus immune globulin after a dirty wound.
- When no vaccine exists, thorough cleaning and leaving dirty wounds open are the best you can do.

## Animal bites and rabies

Rabies is spread by the saliva of infected mammals, most often dogs. Bats, monkeys, foxes, cats and other mammals can also carry it.

1. **Wash the bite or scratch at once** with soap and lots of running water for **15 minutes.**
2. Then apply povidone-iodine or alcohol to the wound if you have it.
3. **Get rabies vaccine as soon as possible.** Bites to the head, neck and hands are the most urgent. Deep bites also need rabies immune globulin.
4. It is never too late to start the vaccine, even weeks after a bite, as long as symptoms have not started.
5. Do not wait to see whether the animal gets sick before you get the vaccine.
6. Do not close the wound. Bites become infected often. Cat bites and human bites are the worst.

Any contact with a bat counts as a possible exposure, even if you cannot see a bite. For venomous bites and stings, see [Bites, Stings and Venom](../medical/bites-stings.md).

## Honey on wounds

**Medical-grade honey,** which is sterilized, has good evidence for helping some burns and wounds heal. **Raw honey from the shop or the hive** is less studied and can contain germs. If you have nothing else, a thin layer on a clean, shallow wound under a dressing may help, but the evidence is limited. **Never give honey by mouth to a baby under 1 year.** It can cause infant botulism.

## Blisters

- **Small blisters:** leave them closed. Cover with a padded dressing.
- **Large, painful blisters on the feet:** clean the skin, pierce the edge with a needle sterilized in a flame, press the fluid out, and leave the roof of skin on. Cover it.
- Do not pop blisters from burns. See [Burns](../medical/burns.md).
- Stop blisters forming: cover red, sore spots (hot spots) with tape as soon as you feel them.

## Splinters

1. Wash the area.
2. Grip the end with clean tweezers and pull out at the same angle it went in.
3. If the end is under the skin, lift it gently with a sterilized needle first.
4. Wash again and cover.

Leave deep splinters of glass or metal, or anything under a fingernail you cannot reach, for a trained person. Watch for infection.
