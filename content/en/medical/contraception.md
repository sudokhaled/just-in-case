> [!CAUTION]
> **Emergency contraception works best the sooner it is used.** Pills work up to **5 days (120 hours)** after unprotected sex. Levonorgestrel pills work best within **3 days (72 hours)**. Do not wait to see if a period comes.
>
> **After rape or sexual assault, medicines to prevent HIV must start within 72 hours.** Go to a health clinic as soon as possible.
>
> **Breastfeeding prevents pregnancy only when all 3 LAM conditions below are met.** Many women become pregnant while breastfeeding.

## Quick steps

1. **Choose a method** you can keep getting. Implants and IUDs last for years.
2. **Use a condom every time** if either partner may have HIV or another sexually transmitted infection. Only condoms protect against both infection and pregnancy.
3. **After unprotected sex or a broken condom,** get emergency contraception at once.
4. **Missed a pill?** Take it as soon as you remember. If you missed 3 or more combined pills in a row (2 or more low-dose pills), also use condoms or avoid sex for 7 days.
5. **Running low?** Ask at a health post or mobile clinic before you run out. Use condoms until you get more.

## Why it matters in a crisis

When clinics, transport and blood supplies break down, pregnancy and birth become more dangerous. Waiting at least 2 years between births protects women and babies. Each person decides for themselves, and no one may be forced to use or stop a method.

## Methods compared

WHO figures for typical use: how many women in 100 become pregnant in the first year, as the method is commonly used. Lower is better. With no method, about **85 in 100** become pregnant.

| Method | Pregnancies per 100 per year | Lasts | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant (small rods under the skin of the arm) | 0.1 | 3 to 5 years, by type | Trained provider to insert and remove |
| Vasectomy (male sterilization) | 0.15 | Permanent | Minor operation. Use condoms for the first 3 months |
| Female sterilization | 0.5 | Permanent | Operation |
| Hormonal IUD (small device in the womb) | 0.7 | 3 to 8 years, by type | Trained provider |
| Copper IUD | 0.8 | Up to 12 years | Trained provider |
| Lactational amenorrhea method (LAM) | 2 | Up to 6 months after birth | All 3 conditions met |
| Injectable (DMPA) | 4 | 3 months per injection | Health worker, or self-injection after training |
| Pills | 7 | 1 pill every day | A new pack each month |
| Male condom | 13 | 1 use | A new condom every time |
| Fertility awareness methods | About 12 to 23, by method | Ongoing | Training and regular cycles |
| Withdrawal (pulling out) | 20 | Each time | Self-control |
| Female condom | 21 | 1 use | A new condom every time |

### Notes on each method

- **Implants and IUDs** need nothing to remember and nothing to buy. Write down the type and the date it was put in. Fertility returns soon after removal. A copper IUD has no hormones but can make periods heavier.
- **Hormonal methods** often cause spotting, irregular periods or no periods. This is not harmful.
- **Injectable (DMPA):** WHO says the next injection can be up to **4 weeks late** and still protect. After that, use condoms. After stopping, pregnancy may take a few months longer than with other methods.
- **Pills:** if you breastfeed a baby under 6 months, use progestin-only pills, not combined pills (with estrogen), which can reduce milk.
- **Condoms:** use a new one from start to finish, every time. **Never use oil, petroleum jelly, butter or lotion** with latex condoms: they make them break. Use a water-based or silicone lubricant. Never use 2 condoms at once, or a male and a female condom together: they rub and tear.
- **Fertility awareness:** avoid sex or use condoms on fertile days. For example, a woman whose cycles are always 26 to 32 days long avoids unprotected sex on days 8 to 19, counting the first day of bleeding as day 1. They fail when cycles are irregular: after birth, while breastfeeding, and during stress and hunger.
- **Sterilization** is permanent. It must be a free, informed choice.

### Lactational amenorrhea method (LAM)

Breastfeeding protects against pregnancy only when **all 3** are true:

1. The baby is **under 6 months** old.
2. **Monthly bleeding has not returned.** Bleeding in the first 8 weeks after birth does not count.
3. The baby is **fully or nearly fully breastfed,** often, day and night.

As soon as any one stops being true, start another method. Implants, IUDs, injectables, progestin-only pills and condoms can all be used while breastfeeding. A health worker will advise when to start each one after the birth. See [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).

### Storing supplies

- Keep pills, injectables and condoms **cool, dry and out of sunlight,** in their packets. Heat ruins condoms. Do not carry one in a pocket for weeks.
- Check expiry dates. Do not use a condom with a torn wrapper, or one that is sticky or stiff. Expired pills may work less well: use condoms as well until you get new ones.

## Emergency contraception

Use it after sex without protection, a broken condom, missed pills, a late injection, or sexual assault.

| Method | Use within | How well it works |
|---|---|---|
| Copper IUD | 5 days (120 hours) | Most effective: fewer than 1 in 100 become pregnant. It can stay in for years. |
| Ulipristal acetate pill, 30 mg | 5 days (120 hours) | Works better than levonorgestrel on days 4 and 5 |
| Levonorgestrel pill, 1.5 mg | Best within 3 days (72 hours), up to 5 days | Works less well each day you wait |

- Emergency pills **do not cause an abortion** and do not harm an existing pregnancy. They delay the release of an egg.
- If you vomit within 2 hours of a levonorgestrel pill, or within 3 hours of ulipristal, take another dose.
- Pills may work less well in women with a high body weight. Take one anyway if it is all you have.
- They do not protect against sex later in the same cycle. Use condoms. After ulipristal, wait 5 days before starting hormonal pills.
- If your next period is more than 7 days late, take a pregnancy test.
- Some ordinary birth control pills can be taken in a larger dose instead. This works less well. Ask a pharmacist or health worker how many of your brand to take.

## Missed pills

Read the leaflet in your pack first: rules differ between types. These are the general WHO rules for **combined pills**:

1. **Missed 1 or 2 pills** (or started a new pack 1 or 2 days late): take a pill as soon as you remember. Take the next one at the usual time, even if that means 2 in 1 day. You are still protected.
2. **Missed 3 or more in a row** (or started 3 or more days late): take a pill at once and keep going. **Use condoms or avoid sex for 7 days.** If you had sex in the last 5 days, consider emergency contraception.
3. **If those 3 or more were in the third week** of hormone pills, also skip the hormone-free pills (or the pill-free week) and start the next pack straight away.

For low-dose pills (20 micrograms of ethinyl estradiol or less), follow rule 2 if you missed 2 or more. If you vomit within 2 hours of a pill, take another from the pack.

**Progestin-only pills:** take the missed pill as soon as you remember. If it was more than 3 hours late, use condoms or avoid sex for the next 2 days.

## Running out of supplies

- If pills or injections run out, switch to condoms, and ask about an implant or IUD.
- In emergencies, health agencies follow a plan called the **Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP)**. Under it, health posts should offer **free condoms**, other contraceptives, emergency contraception and care after sexual violence. Ask at government and mobile clinics, pharmacies, women's centers, and clinics run by UN agencies, the Red Cross or Red Crescent, or other aid groups.

## After sexual violence

1. **Go to a health clinic as soon as possible.** In most places, you do not have to report to the police to get care.
2. Ask for:
   - **emergency contraception,** within 5 days (120 hours)
   - **HIV prevention medicines (PEP),** started within **72 hours** and taken for **28 days**
   - medicines for other sexually transmitted infections, and vaccines against hepatitis B and tetanus
   - care for injuries, and emotional support
3. Care for injuries, infections and emotional health still helps after 72 hours. Men and boys can get the same care.

See [Staying Safe from Other People](../community/personal-safety.md).

## Myths

- **"Breastfeeding always prevents pregnancy."** Only when all 3 LAM conditions are met.
- **"Washing or passing urine after sex prevents pregnancy."** It does not.
- **"You cannot get pregnant the first time, or during your period."** You can.
- **"Contraception makes you unable to have children."** Fertility returns after stopping.
- **"An IUD can travel to the heart or brain."** It cannot.

## When to get help

- **Severe pain low in the belly, or fainting,** with a late period or while using an IUD: this may be a pregnancy outside the womb. Go now.
- **On combined pills:** severe chest pain, pain and swelling in one leg, a sudden severe headache, or problems seeing or speaking. Stop the pills and get help now.
- **With an IUD:** fever, bad-smelling discharge, or strings that feel different or missing. Use condoms until it is checked.
- A missed period on any method. See [Pregnancy Care Without a Clinic](../medical/pregnancy-care.md).
