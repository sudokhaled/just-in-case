> [!CAUTION]
> **Breathing comes before the neck.** If a person with a possible neck injury is not breathing, open the airway and start CPR. If they are vomiting, roll them onto their side. A spine injury is serious; a blocked airway kills in minutes.
>
> **A person can seem fine after a head injury and then get worse hours later.** Worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion or drowsiness mean bleeding inside the skull. Evacuate urgently.
>
> **Do not move someone with a possible spine injury unless they are in danger.** Keep the head, neck and body in a straight line.

## Quick steps

1. **Make the scene safe.** Do not move the person unless they are in danger where they are.
2. **Tell them to keep still.** Do not let them shake or nod their head to answer you.
3. **Hold the head still** in the position you found it, with your hands on both sides. Or pack rolled clothes or blankets on both sides of the head.
4. **Check breathing.** Not breathing normally: start CPR (see [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md)). Vomiting: roll onto the side, keeping the head and body in line.
5. **Stop bleeding** from scalp wounds with firm pressure.
6. **Call for help.** Watch breathing and how awake they are every 5-15 minutes. See [First Aid Basics: Check, Call, Care](../medical/first-aid-basics.md).

## When to suspect a spine injury

Assume the neck or back may be injured after:

- a fall from more than standing height, or down stairs
- a car, motorcycle or bicycle crash
- diving into shallow water
- a heavy blow to the head, neck or back, or being crushed
- a blast, or being thrown

And especially if the person has:

- pain or tenderness in the neck or back
- numbness, tingling or weakness in the arms or legs
- been knocked out, or is confused, drunk or drugged
- another painful injury that could distract them from neck pain

## Keeping the spine still

Current first aid guidelines (IFRC 2020) no longer recommend that first aiders put a rigid neck collar on an injured person. Collars can cause harm and are hard to fit correctly.

- **If the person is awake,** ask them to stay still. Many will hold themselves in the least painful position.
- **To hold the head:** kneel behind the head, put your hands over the ears, and keep the head in line with the body. Do not pull.
- **Do not force the head straight** if it is turned and moving it causes pain or resistance. Support it as it is.
- Keep them warm. Put insulation under them only if you can do it without twisting the spine.

## Moving someone with a possible spine injury

Move the person only if they are in danger (fire, traffic, water, a collapsing building) or you must turn them to keep the airway clear.

**With helpers (log roll):**

1. One person holds the head and gives all the commands.
2. Others kneel on the same side, at the shoulders, hips and legs.
3. On the count of 3, roll the person onto their side as one unit, like a log. The head, shoulders and hips turn together.
4. Roll them back the same way, or onto a board, door or blanket to carry them.

**Alone:** if you must move them, grab the clothes at both shoulders and drag them headfirst, in a straight line along the length of the body. Never drag them sideways.

## When help will never come

If no help can come and you must decide whether the person can move, a serious spine injury is **unlikely** if **all** of these are true:

- They are fully alert, not drunk or drugged.
- They have no neck or back pain.
- Pressing gently down the middle of the back of the neck and spine causes no pain.
- They have no numbness, tingling or weakness in any limb.
- They have no other painful injury distracting them.

If all are true, let them move carefully on their own. If any are not, keep them as still as possible and plan evacuation.

A person who cannot move or feel their legs or arms needs evacuation at any cost. Until then, turn them gently every 2 hours to prevent pressure sores, keep them warm (they may not feel cold), and watch their breathing.

## Concussion

A concussion is a brain injury from a blow or jolt to the head. The person may or may not have been knocked out.

**Common signs:** headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, memory loss about the injury, blurred vision, feeling slow or "foggy", sensitivity to light and noise, mood changes.

### Danger signs: evacuate urgently

- headache that gets worse
- vomiting more than once
- one pupil (black center of the eye) larger than the other
- confusion, strange behavior, or getting harder to wake
- a seizure (fit)
- clear fluid or blood coming from the nose or ears
- slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side
- knocked out for more than a moment
- bruising behind the ears or around both eyes
- in a baby or small child: will not stop crying, will not feed, or a swollen soft spot on the head

People who take blood-thinning medicine, people over 65, and people who were drunk are at higher risk of bleeding in the skull.

### Watching someone after a head injury

1. **Someone stays with them** for the first 24-48 hours.
2. **Sleeping is fine.** Keeping a person awake is a myth. During the first night, check every 2-4 hours that they breathe normally, wake up easily, and know who and where they are.
3. If you cannot wake them normally, or any danger sign appears, treat it as an emergency.
4. Give paracetamol (acetaminophen) for headache, as the package says. Avoid aspirin, which can increase bleeding.
5. No alcohol or sleeping pills for at least 48 hours.

### Recovery

- **Rest for 1-2 days.** Limit screens, reading and noise if they make symptoms worse.
- Then slowly return to normal activity. Stop and rest if symptoms come back.
- No contact sports, climbing or heavy work until all symptoms have gone.
- Most people feel normal within 2 weeks. Some take longer.

## Scalp wounds and skull fractures

- **Scalp wounds bleed heavily.** Press firmly with a cloth for at least 10 minutes. See [Stopping Severe Bleeding](../medical/severe-bleeding.md).
- **Suspect a skull fracture** if there is a dent or soft area, bone visible in a wound, clear fluid from the nose or ears, or bruising behind the ears or around the eyes.
- **Do not press on a dent.** Press around the edges instead. A ring of rolled cloth around the wound lets you press without pushing on the bone.
- **Do not plug the ears or nose** if fluid is coming out. Cover loosely with a clean pad and let it drain.
- **Do not remove objects stuck in the head.** Pad around them.
- **Leave a motorcycle helmet on** unless it blocks breathing or you need to do CPR.

## Evacuate if

- any danger sign of brain bleeding appears
- they were knocked out at all
- a skull fracture is possible
- they have numbness, tingling, weakness or paralysis
- they cannot move or feel a limb
- the neck or back pain is severe

When evacuating, carry the person flat with the head, neck and body in line. See [Signaling for Rescue](../navigation/signal-rescue.md). See [Moving and Carrying the Injured](../medical/moving-injured.md).
