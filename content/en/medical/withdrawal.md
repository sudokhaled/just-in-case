> [!CAUTION]
> **Stopping alcohol suddenly after weeks of heavy daily drinking can cause seizures, delirium and death.** So can suddenly stopping benzodiazepine sleeping or anxiety pills taken every day.
>
> **After even a few days without opioids, the body loses its tolerance.** Taking the old amount can kill by overdose.
>
> **Never stop insulin, epilepsy medicine or long-term steroid tablets suddenly.**

## Quick steps

1. **Count your medicines today.** Get more before they run out.
2. **Do not suddenly stop** epilepsy medicines, steroid tablets, sleeping or anxiety pills (benzodiazepines), antidepressants, heart and blood pressure medicines, or opioids. If one will run out, see a health worker early.
3. **A heavy daily drinker cut off from alcohol:** watch closely for 4 days. Seizure, confusion or seeing things: evacuate.
4. **Give fluids, food and thiamine (vitamin B1)** to anyone in alcohol withdrawal.
5. **After any break from opioids,** use far less, never alone, with naloxone nearby.
6. **Never take someone else's prescription** unless a life is in danger and there is no other choice.

## Why running out is dangerous

The body adapts to some medicines and drugs taken daily. Stopping suddenly causes a rebound: shaking, a racing heart, seizures, or the illness returning worse. In disasters, supplies stop without warning.

## Medicines never to stop suddenly

| Medicine | Examples | What sudden stopping can do |
|---|---|---|
| Benzodiazepines and "Z-drug" sleeping pills | Diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam, clonazepam, zolpidem, zopiclone | Anxiety, sleeplessness, shaking, confusion, seizures. Can kill after weeks of daily use |
| Epilepsy medicines | Phenobarbital, carbamazepine, valproate, phenytoin, levetiracetam | Seizures, including seizures that do not stop |
| Beta-blockers | Names ending in "-olol": propranolol, atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol | Fast heartbeat, chest pain, heart attack in people with heart disease |
| Clonidine | Clonidine | Very high blood pressure within 1-2 days: headache, agitation, shaking |
| Steroid tablets taken for more than a few weeks | Prednisolone, prednisone, hydrocortisone, dexamethasone | Adrenal crisis: severe weakness, vomiting, collapse. Can kill |
| Antidepressants | Sertraline, paroxetine, venlafaxine, amitriptyline | Dizziness, electric-shock feelings, flu-like illness, anxiety, low mood. Rarely dangerous, but can be very unpleasant |
| Antipsychotics | Haloperidol, olanzapine, risperidone, clozapine | Psychosis returning, sleeplessness, nausea, sweating |
| Daily opioid painkillers | Morphine, oxycodone, codeine, tramadol, methadone, buprenorphine | Withdrawal (see below) |

Also: insulin in type 1 diabetes, levodopa for Parkinson's disease, baclofen, gabapentin, pregabalin and lithium. See also [Diabetes, Asthma, Heart Disease and Epilepsy Without Supplies](../medical/chronic-conditions.md).

> [!WARNING]
> **Clozapine:** if doses have been missed for more than 2 days, a health worker must restart it at a low dose. Restarting the old dose can be dangerous.

### If one of these will run out

- Go to a health worker, pharmacy or aid clinic **before** you run out. Bring the box or your medicine list.
- A health worker can switch you to a medicine they have, or plan a slow reduction. **A planned slow reduction is far safer than a sudden stop.** Do not invent your own plan if anyone can advise you.
- Do not take extra doses to "catch up" when you get more.

## Alcohol withdrawal

**At risk:** people who have drunk heavily every day for weeks or longer, or drink in the morning to stop shaking. Risk is highest after past withdrawal seizures or delirium. Occasional drinkers are not at risk.

| Time after last drink | What can happen |
|---|---|
| 6-24 hours | Shaking hands, sweating, anxiety, nausea, vomiting, fast heartbeat, poor sleep |
| Within 48 hours | Seizures (fits). Some people see or hear things that are not there |
| 48-96 hours (2-4 days) | Delirium tremens: severe confusion, terror, seeing things, fever, heavy sweating, very fast heartbeat. **It can kill** |

Mild withdrawal usually settles within about a week.

### When help exists

**Medically supervised withdrawal ("detox") is much safer.** Health workers use medicines that prevent seizures and delirium, and give thiamine. A heavy daily drinker who wants to stop should see a health worker first.

### When there is no medical help

- **Do not stop all at once** if a heavy daily drinker still has alcohol. UK NHS alcohol services advise people who are physically dependent to reduce slowly over days, ideally with professional support, because stopping suddenly can cause fatal seizures. Without medical support, seizures can still happen, so keep watching for danger signs.
- **Never mix alcohol with sleeping pills or opioids.** Together they can stop breathing.
- **If alcohol runs out suddenly,** stay with the person for 4 days, watching for danger signs.
- **Give fluids often:** water, or oral rehydration solution if they sweat or vomit. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
- **Give food and thiamine.** If you have thiamine (vitamin B1) tablets, give them as the package says. Thiamine-rich foods: whole grains, beans, peas, peanuts, pork and seeds.
- **Keep them calm and safe:** a quiet, lit room, one familiar person, nothing sharp nearby. Do not argue about things they see.
- **Seizure:** protect the head, put nothing in the mouth, and use the recovery position afterwards.

### Danger signs: evacuate at any cost

- A seizure.
- Confusion about where they are or what day it is.
- Seeing, hearing or feeling things that are not there.
- Fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or more, heavy sweating, or a resting pulse above 100 per minute that keeps rising.
- Unsteady walking, strange eye movements and confusion: Wernicke's disease, from lack of thiamine. It needs thiamine injections urgently.
- Vomiting blood, yellow eyes, or vomiting so much they cannot drink.

## Opioid withdrawal

**Signs:** yawning, runny nose, tears, sweating, goose bumps, large pupils, aches, cramps, diarrhea, vomiting, anxiety, poor sleep and strong craving. They start 6-12 hours after heroin or morphine, and later after methadone. The worst usually passes within a week. Craving can last months.

It is very unpleasant but **rarely deadly by itself** in a healthy adult. The real dangers:

- **Dehydration** from vomiting and diarrhea. Give oral rehydration solution.
- **Pregnancy.** Sudden withdrawal can harm the baby. A pregnant woman using opioids should see a health worker and not stop suddenly.
- **Overdose afterwards.** Tolerance falls within days. The old dose can stop breathing. Many deaths happen soon after leaving prison, hospital or detox.

To lower the risk:

- After any break, use much less than before. Never use alone. Do not mix with alcohol or sleeping pills.
- Keep naloxone nearby and teach others to use it. Signs of overdose: very sleepy, tiny pupils, slow or no breathing. Give naloxone as the package says, give rescue breaths, and keep watching for hours: naloxone wears off. See [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md) and [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
- On methadone or buprenorphine? Contact your program before supplies end. Many can arrange emergency doses.

For aches, give paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen as the package says, with fluids, small meals and rest.

## Nicotine and caffeine

These are unpleasant but not dangerous.

- **Nicotine:** irritability, anxiety, poor concentration, hunger, poor sleep and craving. Worst in the first week, mostly gone in 2-4 weeks. Nicotine patches or gum help, as the package says.
- **Caffeine:** headache, tiredness and low mood, starting 12-24 hours after the last coffee or tea and lasting 2-9 days. Drink water. Paracetamol helps the headache.

## Stretching supplies safely

- **Ask a pharmacist.** Some countries let pharmacists give an emergency supply of a regular medicine without a new prescription, often for a few days to a month, but usually not opioids or benzodiazepines.
- **Go to aid clinics early** with the box or your list. They can often supply or substitute common medicines.
- **Ask which of your medicines are life-saving and which can wait.** For example, cholesterol tablets (statins) can usually be paused for some weeks with little immediate risk. Ask before pausing anything.
- **Do not skip or halve doses** of medicines in the table above without advice.
- **Never take someone else's prescription,** except to save a life with no alternative, such as a reliever inhaler in a severe asthma attack or naloxone for overdose.
- **Avoid street sellers.** Fake medicines are common in shortages. See [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).

**Plan ahead:** keep 1-3 months of essential medicines and a written list. Ask your doctor now which ones are dangerous to stop. Refill as soon as allowed, not when you run out.

## Evacuate if

- Seizures, confusion, hallucinations or fever after stopping alcohol or a medicine in the table.
- Signs of adrenal crisis.
- Chest pain, a racing heart or severe headache after stopping a beta-blocker or clonidine.
- Opioid overdose, even if naloxone worked.
- Thoughts of suicide, or psychosis returning, after stopping mental health medicines.
- Vomiting or diarrhea so bad the person cannot keep fluids down.
