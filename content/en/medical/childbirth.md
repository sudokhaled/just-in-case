> [!CAUTION]
> **Never pull on the baby or on the cord.** Let the mother's pushing do the work. Pulling can tear the womb or cause deadly bleeding.
>
> **Heavy bleeding after birth kills mothers within hours.** If blood soaks through a cloth or pad within minutes, rub the womb firmly through the belly, put the baby to the breast, and get help at any cost.
>
> **A wet newborn gets cold fast, and cold babies die.** Dry the baby at once and put it skin-to-skin on the mother's chest.

## Quick steps

1. **Call for help** (a midwife, health worker, or emergency services). Most births go well on their own.
2. **Wash your hands** with soap. See [Handwashing and Personal Hygiene](../hygiene/handwashing.md). Lay out a clean surface, clean cloths and warm covers.
3. **Let the mother choose her position** and push when she feels the urge.
4. **Support the baby's head** as it comes out. Do not pull.
5. **Dry the baby at once** and place it skin-to-skin on the mother's chest. Cover both.
6. **Wait 1-3 minutes** before tying and cutting the cord.
7. **Let the placenta come by itself,** usually within 30 minutes.
8. **Feel the womb** through the belly. If it is soft, rub it until it is hard. Watch for heavy bleeding.

## Signs of labor

- **First stage:** regular, painful contractions (tightenings of the belly) that come closer together. Mucus with a little blood may come out, and the waters may break. For a first baby this often lasts 12 hours or more.
- **Second stage:** a strong urge to push, the vaginal opening bulging, the head appearing. **Birth is close: do not travel now.**
- **Third stage:** the placenta (afterbirth) comes out.

## Prepare

- Soap, water, and gloves if you have them.
- A plastic sheet covered with clean cloths, in a warm room without drafts.
- 3-4 clean, dry cloths for the baby, and a warm cover.
- **Two cord ties:** clean string or cloth strips, boiled for 20 minutes.
- **A clean blade:** a new razor blade still in its wrapper is best. Otherwise boil scissors or a blade for 20 minutes.
- A bowl for the placenta, and pads or cloths for bleeding. See [Pregnancy Care Without a Clinic](../medical/pregnancy-care.md).

## Delivering the baby

1. **Let her move and pick her position:** squatting, kneeling, on all fours, on her side, or half sitting. Avoid lying flat on her back for long.
2. **Do not tell her to push** before she feels a strong urge. Never press on the top of her belly.
3. **As the head appears,** ask her to stop pushing hard and to pant or give small pushes, so the head comes out slowly. Rest your palm lightly on the head. Do not hold it back.
4. **Once the head is out,** feel around the neck for the cord. If it is there and loose, slip it gently over the head. If it is too tight to loosen, leave it. Most babies are born safely with the cord around the neck.
5. **The head turns to one side** by itself. With the next contraction, the shoulders and body come. Support the baby: it is slippery.

> [!WARNING]
> **If the head is out but the body does not come with the next contraction or two (the shoulders are stuck):** help her lie on her back and pull both knees up towards her chest, as far as she can. A helper presses firmly downwards just above her pubic bone while she pushes. Or help her turn onto all fours. Never pull hard on the head.

## Caring for the newborn

1. **Dry the baby at once** with a warm cloth: head, body, arms and legs. Rubbing also helps the baby breathe.
2. **Remove the wet cloth.** Put the baby on the mother's bare chest, skin-to-skin. Cover both with a dry cloth. Cover the baby's head.
3. Most babies cry or breathe well within the first minute. Wipe the mouth and nose only if they are blocked with mucus. Do not suck them out routinely.
4. **Start breastfeeding within the first hour.** It helps the womb contract and reduces bleeding.
5. Do not bathe the baby for at least 24 hours.

### If the baby is not breathing

1. **Keep drying and rubbing** the back firmly for a few seconds.
2. **Clear the mouth and nose** with a clean cloth if they are blocked.
3. **Lay the baby on its back** on a flat, warm surface, head level, face pointing up. A rolled cloth under the shoulders can help.
4. **Give breaths:** cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth. Give small puffs from your cheeks, just enough to make the chest rise. Give about **40 breaths per minute** (breathe, 2, 3, breathe).
5. **Keep going** until the baby breathes or cries.
6. If you must move the baby to do this, tie and cut the cord quickly first.

If there is no heartbeat at all (feel the base of the cord, or put your ear to the left side of the chest), and no breathing or movement after **10-20 minutes** of good breaths, the baby has died. It is right to stop. This is not your fault.

## The cord

1. **Wait 1-3 minutes** after birth before tying, or until the cord stops pulsing. This gives the baby extra blood.
2. Tie the first tie tightly about **2 cm (1 in)** from the baby's belly. Tie the second about **5 cm (2 in)** from the belly.
3. Cut between the two ties with your clean blade or scissors.
4. Check the stump after 15 minutes. If it oozes blood, add another tie.
5. **Keep the stump clean and dry.** Put nothing on it: no ash, dung, oil or herbs. It falls off in 5-15 days.

Where many babies die in their first month and babies are born at home, WHO recommends putting **7.1% chlorhexidine digluconate** (gel or liquid) on the stump once a day for the first week, if you have it.

## The placenta

1. **Do not pull on the cord.** The placenta usually comes within **30 minutes,** often with a contraction and a gush of blood. Ask her to push with the contraction. Catch it in a bowl.
2. If it has not come after 30 minutes: put the baby to the breast, ask her to empty her bladder, and let her squat or kneel.
3. **If it has not come after 1 hour,** or she bleeds heavily, she needs a hospital urgently.

## Bleeding after birth

Some bleeding is normal. **More than about 500 mL (2 cups),** a pad soaked within minutes, a steady flow, dizziness, pale skin or a fast pulse are dangerous.

1. **Rub the womb:** put your hand on her belly just below the belly button. Feel for the womb. Rub firmly in circles until it becomes hard, like a large orange. Check it every 15 minutes for the first 2 hours.
2. **Put the baby to the breast,** or rub the nipples. This makes the womb contract.
3. Ask her to empty her bladder. A full bladder stops the womb contracting.
4. Lay her flat, raise her legs, and keep her warm. If she is awake and can swallow, give sips of fluid.
5. **Get her to a hospital at any cost.**

### Misoprostol

Misoprostol tablets help the womb contract and reduce bleeding. **Give them only after the last baby is born.** Check the belly for a second baby first. Giving it with a baby still inside can tear the womb.

- **To prevent heavy bleeding,** WHO recommends that community and lay health workers give **600 micrograms by mouth** (3 tablets of 200 micrograms) right after the birth, where no injected medicine (oxytocin) is available.
- **To treat heavy bleeding,** in a woman who has not already had misoprostol, the WHO treatment dose is **800 micrograms placed under the tongue** (4 tablets of 200 micrograms).
- Shivering and fever for a few hours afterwards are common side effects.

## Danger signs: get to a hospital

**For the mother:**

- heavy bleeding, before or after the birth
- fits (seizures), or severe headache with blurred vision
- fever, or a bad-smelling discharge from the vagina
- strong labor for more than about **12 hours** without the baby coming
- the waters broke more than 18 hours ago and the baby has not come
- the placenta has not come after 1 hour

**At the birth:**

- **the cord comes out first.** Help her onto her knees and elbows with her hips raised high. Do not push the cord back. Cover it with a warm, damp cloth.
- **an arm, a shoulder, or the bottom or feet come first.** An arm or shoulder first cannot be born normally. If the bottom or feet are already coming, do not pull: let the body come by itself and support it.
- green or brown water
- the baby is more than about 3 weeks early, or there are twins

**For the baby:** not breathing well, very small, cold, will not feed, fever, yellow skin in the first day, or a red or smelly cord stump. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md) and [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).
