> [!CAUTION]
> **Handle a cold, confused or unconscious person gently and keep them lying flat.** Rough movement can stop a very cold heart.
>
> **A very cold person can look dead but still be alive.** Breathing and pulse can be very slow and weak. Check for a full **1 minute** before starting CPR.
>
> **Never thaw frostbite if it might freeze again.** Thawing and refreezing destroys far more tissue than staying frozen a little longer.

## Quick steps

1. **Stop the cooling.** Get out of wind, rain and snow. Put insulation under the person: the ground steals heat fast.
2. **Handle gently.** Keep them lying down if they are confused, drowsy or not shivering.
3. **Remove wet clothes** once sheltered. Cut them off rather than pull, so the person moves less.
4. **Wrap them:** insulation below and all around, and a plastic or foil layer outside. Cover the head and neck.
5. **Add heat to the trunk:** wrapped warm water bottles or heat packs on the chest, armpits and back. Not on arms and legs.
6. **Give warm sweet drinks only if fully alert** and able to swallow safely.
7. **If they are unconscious,** check breathing for up to 1 minute. Start CPR only if there is no breathing.

## How cold are they?

Judge mainly by how awake the person is. Shivering is not reliable: an exhausted, hungry or very cold person may stop shivering.

| Level | Signs | Core temperature (if measured) |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | Alert, shivering hard, pale, clumsy hands | 32-35°C (90-95°F) |
| Moderate | Confused, slurred speech, stumbling, sleepy, poor decisions; shivering may slow or stop | 28-32°C (82-90°F) |
| Severe | Barely responds or unconscious; shivering usually stopped; breathing and pulse slow and weak | Below 28°C (82°F) |

**The "umbles":** a person who **stumbles, mumbles, fumbles and grumbles** is getting hypothermic. Act now.

Some severely cold people start taking off their clothes. This is a sign of severe hypothermia, not of feeling warm.

Hypothermia does not need freezing weather. Wet clothes, wind, cold water and exhaustion can cause it well above freezing. Cold water cools the body much faster than cold air. Ordinary thermometers do not read low enough, so do not rely on them.

## Mild hypothermia (alert and shivering)

1. Get into shelter, or build a windbreak.
2. Replace wet clothes with dry ones. Add a hat, gloves and dry socks.
3. Give **warm, sweet drinks and food.** Sugar fuels shivering, which is the body's own heater.
4. Wrap in blankets or a sleeping bag.
5. Once dry, fed and warming, they can walk gently if they need to reach shelter. Watch them closely.

## Moderate and severe hypothermia

These people are in danger of their heart stopping. **Evacuate if any help exists,** but move them as little as possible.

1. **Keep them lying flat** and still. Do not let them walk or stand.
2. **Handle them very gently.** Do not rub or massage arms and legs.
3. **Make a hypothermia wrap:**
   - **Below:** a sleeping pad, foam, cardboard, branches or spare clothes on the ground.
   - **Around:** sleeping bags, blankets or dry clothing, wrapped close.
   - **Outside:** a plastic sheet, large bags, a tarp or a foil emergency blanket, to keep out wind and wet.
   - **If wet clothes cannot be removed,** put a plastic layer directly over them, then the insulation.
   - Cover the head and neck. Leave the face open to breathe.
4. **Add heat to the chest, armpits and back:** hot water bottles, bottles of warm water, heat packs or warm stones. **Always wrap them in cloth.** Cold skin burns easily.
5. **Give no food or drink** unless they are fully alert and can swallow safely.
6. **Check breathing often.** If they vomit, roll them gently onto their side.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not put a moderately or severely hypothermic person in a hot bath or shower. Do not give alcohol. Do not rub the skin. All of these can cause a sudden collapse.

### If they seem dead

1. Check for breathing and a pulse for **up to 1 minute.** In severe cold the pulse can be very slow and hard to feel.
2. If there is no breathing, start CPR. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
3. Keep insulating and warming them during CPR.

Rescuers say a cold person is "not dead until warm and dead". People have survived hours of CPR when it led to a hospital that could rewarm them. If you must carry the person, short pauses in CPR to move them are acceptable in severe hypothermia.

**When no hospital can be reached, survival after the heart stops from cold is rare.** If rescue is impossible, it is reasonable to stop after about 30 minutes of CPR with no sign of life. Do not start CPR if the chest is frozen solid, there are injuries no one could survive, or rescuers would be put in danger.

## Frostbite

Frostbite is frozen skin and flesh. It hits fingers, toes, nose, ears and cheeks first.

| Type | Signs | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Frostnip | Pale or red, numb or tingling, still soft | Full recovery if warmed now |
| Superficial frostbite | White or gray, waxy, numb, firm on top but soft beneath | Clear blisters after thawing |
| Deep frostbite | Hard and wooden, white, gray or blue, no feeling | Blood-filled blisters, then black dead tissue |

### Frostnip

Warm the area at once with body heat. Put fingers in the armpits or on the belly. Cover the face and ears with warm hands or cloth. Get out of the wind.

### Treating frostbite

1. **Treat hypothermia first.** The body's core comes before fingers and toes.
2. **Do not rub it,** with snow or anything else. Rubbing destroys frozen tissue.
3. **Do not thaw it if it could freeze again.** It is better to walk out on frozen feet than to thaw them and have them refreeze. Walking on thawed feet causes more damage.
4. **Remove rings, watches and tight boots** before swelling starts.
5. **Rewarm in water at 37-39°C (99-102°F)** for about **30 minutes,** until the skin is soft and turns red or purple. Without a thermometer, the water should feel warm but not hot to an uninjured hand. Add warm water as it cools, taking the injured part out first.
6. **Never use a fire, stove, heater or hot engine to thaw.** Numb skin burns without feeling it.
7. **Expect strong pain.** Ibuprofen at the dose on the package helps both pain and healing, if the person can take it.
8. **Air dry gently.** Do not rub with a towel.

If you have no warm water, thaw with body heat: armpits, belly or another person's skin. This is slower and less effective than warm water.

### After thawing

- **Leave blisters alone.** Do not break them. Cover loosely with clean, dry dressings.
- Put clean gauze or cloth **between fingers and toes.**
- **Raise the limb** above the heart to reduce swelling.
- Keep it warm and dry. **Protect it from refreezing.**
- Do not walk on thawed feet unless you must.
- **Do not cut off dark or black tissue.** It can take 1-3 months to see what will recover. Watch for infection: spreading redness, pus, bad smell or fever. See [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md).

All frostbite beyond frostnip needs a doctor. Hospital treatment in the first 24 hours can save fingers and toes.

## Trench foot (immersion foot)

Trench foot comes from feet that stay **wet and cold for many hours or days,** even above freezing. Feet become numb, swollen, pale or blotchy. After warming they turn red, hot and very painful.

- **Prevent it:** dry your feet and put on dry socks **at least once a day.** Do not sleep in wet boots. Loosen tight laces. Dry spare socks inside your clothes, against your body.
- **Treat it:** dry the feet, warm them slowly at room temperature, raise them and rest. **Do not rub them or put them in hot water.** Avoid walking on them.

## Preventing cold injury

- Stay dry. Wet clothing loses most of its warmth. Cotton is worst. Wool and synthetics are better.
- Wear layers, a hat and something over the face and neck.
- Eat and drink regularly. A hungry, dehydrated body cannot make heat.
- Check each other's faces for white patches.
- Avoid alcohol. It makes you feel warm while you lose heat faster.

See [Staying Warm in the Cold](../shelter/stay-warm.md) and [Building an Emergency Shelter](../shelter/emergency-shelter.md). If you heat a closed space with fire, read [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).

## Evacuate if

- The person is confused, drowsy, unconscious, or cold and not shivering.
- They needed CPR, even if they recovered.
- They were in cold water.
- There is frostbite beyond frostnip, especially blood-filled blisters or hard, wooden skin.
- Trench foot does not improve within a day, or the skin breaks down.
