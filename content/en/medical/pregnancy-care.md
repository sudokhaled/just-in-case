> [!CAUTION]
> **Bleeding from the vagina, fits, severe headache with blurred vision, or severe belly pain can kill a pregnant woman or her baby within hours.** Go to a hospital at once, day or night.
>
> **A baby that moves less than usual may be in danger.** Get checked the same day. Do not wait until tomorrow.
>
> **Iron tablets can kill a small child.** Store them high up and out of reach.

## Quick steps

1. **Eat a little more** every day. Use iodized salt. Take **iron and folic acid** daily if you can get them.
2. **Drink only safe water.** No alcohol, no tobacco.
3. **Get the tetanus vaccine.** In malaria areas, **sleep under a treated bed net every night.**
4. **Learn the danger signs** and teach them to your family: bleeding, fits, severe headache with blurred vision, swollen face or hands, fever, severe belly pain, the baby moving less, waters breaking early, or fast breathing. Any of these needs a hospital now.
5. **See any health worker or mobile clinic you can reach.** WHO recommends at least **8 contacts** in pregnancy, the first before 12 weeks.
6. **By 7 months, prepare for the birth:** a clean birth kit, a helper and a transport plan.

## Signs and due date

Signs: a missed period, nausea, sore breasts, tiredness and passing urine more often. Most women first feel the baby move at **18 to 20 weeks**. A urine pregnancy test usually works from the first day of a missed period.

Pregnancy lasts about **40 weeks** from the **first day of the last period**. To find the due date, take that day, add **9 months**, then add **7 days**. Example: last period started 10 January, plus 9 months is 10 October, plus 7 days is **17 October**. Birth between 37 and 42 weeks is on time.

If the date is unknown, feel the top of the womb through the belly. It reaches the belly button at about 20 weeks, and just below the ribs at about 36 weeks.

## Food

- **Eat about 300 kcal more per day** in the second half of pregnancy, for example a large handful (50 g, 2 oz) of peanuts. See [Food Priorities and Rationing](../food/food-rationing.md).
- **Eat foods with iron:** meat, fish, eggs, beans, lentils and dark green leaves. Fruit or tomatoes at the same meal help the body take up iron. Tea and coffee block it, so drink them between meals.
- **Use iodized salt.** Too little iodine damages the baby's brain.
- **Iron and folic acid:** WHO recommends **30 to 60 mg of iron and 400 micrograms (0.4 mg) of folic acid every day** throughout pregnancy. Folic acid protects the baby's brain and spine, so start early. Iron can turn stools black; this is harmless.

**Avoid:**

- **Alcohol.** No amount is known to be safe.
- **Tobacco and smoke,** including indoor cooking smoke.
- **Raw or undercooked meat, fish and eggs, and unboiled milk.** They can carry germs that cause miscarriage. Wash hands after touching soil, raw meat or cat feces.
- **Medicines you do not need.** For pain or fever, paracetamol (acetaminophen) at the package dose is the usual choice. Avoid ibuprofen and aspirin, especially after 20 weeks, unless a health worker advises them.

## Water, rest and heat

- **Drink safe water,** enough that your urine is pale. Boil it or use chlorine, not iodine. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).
- **Rest** when tired. **Avoid heavy lifting** and carrying heavy loads of water or firewood.
- **Heat:** pregnant women overheat and dehydrate faster. Stay in shade in the hottest hours and drink more.
- **From about 28 weeks,** sleep on your side, not flat on your back, which can cause dizziness.

## Tetanus and malaria

- **Tetanus** kills newborns, often through a dirty cord. If you were never vaccinated, or are not sure, get **2 doses at least 4 weeks apart,** the second at least 2 weeks before the birth. The vaccine is safe in pregnancy.
- **Malaria** in pregnancy causes severe anemia, miscarriage and small babies. Sleep under an insecticide-treated bed net. Take the preventive malaria treatment offered at antenatal visits. **Get any fever tested the same day.** See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).

## Common discomforts

These are normal:

- **Nausea and vomiting,** mostly in the first 3 months: small, dry meals often. Eat before getting up. Try ginger.
- **Heartburn:** small meals. Do not lie down right after eating.
- **Constipation:** more water, fruit, vegetables and beans. Walk.
- **Swollen feet at the end of the day:** rest with the feet raised.

Vomiting so much that nothing stays down for a day, or burning when passing urine, needs a health worker the same day.

## Danger signs

Any of these needs a hospital **now**, day or night.

| Danger sign | Possible cause |
|---|---|
| Bleeding from the vagina | Miscarriage, pregnancy outside the womb, placenta problem |
| Fits (seizures) | Eclampsia: dangerous high blood pressure |
| Severe headache with blurred vision | Pre-eclampsia, which can lead to fits |
| Swelling of the face or hands | Pre-eclampsia |
| Fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher | Infection or malaria |
| Severe belly pain | Pregnancy outside the womb, placenta coming away, infection |
| The baby moves less, or stops moving | The baby is in distress |
| Waters break before labor starts | Infection, early labor |
| Labor pains more than 3 weeks early | Early labor |
| Fast or difficult breathing, or very pale and weak | Severe anemia, infection, heart problem |

On the way: if she has **fits**, lay her on her left side and put nothing in her mouth. If she is **bleeding or her waters broke**, put nothing into the vagina.

## Preparing for birth

**Clean birth kit:** soap, gloves if possible, a clean plastic sheet about 1 m (3 ft) square, a new razor blade still in its wrapper, 2 or 3 clean cord ties, 3 or 4 clean cloths for the baby, pads or cloths for the mother, and a torch (flashlight). Aid agencies often give free clean delivery kits: ask at health posts.

**Birth plan:**

1. **Decide where to give birth.** A health facility with a skilled midwife or doctor is safest.
2. **Choose a helper** to stay with the mother, and a second person to run for help.
3. **Plan transport** to the nearest hospital, by day and night, and the money or fuel for it.
4. **Find a relative** willing to give blood if needed.

**Give birth with a midwife or doctor,** moving near a hospital before labor if you can, after a previous caesarean operation, heavy bleeding or stillbirth, or with twins, a baby lying across or bottom-first, high blood pressure or severe anemia.

For the birth itself, see [Emergency Childbirth](../medical/childbirth.md).

## Miscarriage

Miscarriage is the loss of a pregnancy before the baby can survive, most often in the first 3 months. It is common. It is usually caused by a problem in the pregnancy itself, not by work, sex, stress or food.

Signs: bleeding, cramping pain, and passing clots or tissue. Usually the bleeding is like a heavy period and slowly lessens over 1 to 2 weeks. Rest, use pads (not tampons or cups), and take paracetamol at the package dose for pain. Get checked if you can: tissue left in the womb can cause bleeding and infection.

**Get help urgently if:**

- Bleeding soaks **2 large pads or thick cloths an hour, for 2 hours in a row.**
- She is dizzy, faints, or is pale with a fast pulse. Lay her flat, raise her legs and keep her warm.
- She has fever, a bad-smelling discharge, or worsening belly pain.
- She has **severe pain low on one side of the belly** in the first 3 months, even with little bleeding. This can be a pregnancy outside the womb (ectopic). It bleeds inside the belly and can kill within hours.
- Something was put into the vagina, or medicines or herbs were taken, to end the pregnancy, and there is pain, fever or bleeding. Tell the health worker exactly what was used.

**Grief:** losing a pregnancy is a real loss, often for both partners. It is not her fault. Let her grieve in her own way. See [Psychological First Aid](../community/psychological-first-aid.md).

She can become pregnant again within 2 weeks. WHO advises waiting **at least 6 months** before the next pregnancy.

## After the birth

For 6 weeks the mother needs rest, help with heavy work, and about 500 kcal more food per day while breastfeeding. Bleeding is heavy for a few days, then lighter and browner, stopping by about 6 weeks.

**Get help now if the mother has:**

- Bleeding that soaks a pad within 5 minutes, or gets heavier instead of lighter.
- Fever, or a bad-smelling discharge from the vagina.
- Severe belly pain, fits, or severe headache with blurred vision.
- Fast or difficult breathing.
- Pain, swelling and redness in one leg (a possible blood clot).
- **Severe sadness,** cannot care for herself or the baby, or **thoughts of harming herself or the baby.**

Feeling tearful for a few days after birth is common and passes within 2 weeks. Longer sadness needs help. If she speaks of harm, stay with her and get help now.

**Spacing:** WHO advises waiting **at least 2 years** after a birth before trying for another pregnancy. A woman who is not breastfeeding can become pregnant as early as **4 weeks** after birth, before her periods return. See [Family Planning and Contraception](../medical/contraception.md).

## When to get help

- Any danger sign above, during pregnancy or after the birth: go now.
- Fever in a malaria area: get tested the same day.
- When a health worker is near, get checked even if you feel well. Tests for anemia, HIV and syphilis find problems that can be treated.
