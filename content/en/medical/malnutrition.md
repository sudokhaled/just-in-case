> [!CAUTION]
> **Swelling of both feet in a child means severe malnutrition,** even if the child looks plump. So does an arm measurement (MUAC) below 115 mm (4.5 in). These children can die quickly. Take them to a health worker or feeding center.
>
> **Do not force big meals on a starving child or adult.** A sudden large meal can stop the heart.
>
> **Severely malnourished children get cold and get low blood sugar fast.** Keep them warm and feed them day and night.

## Quick steps

1. **Measure the arm** of every child aged 6 months to 5 years.
2. **Press the top of both feet** for 3 seconds. A dent that stays on both feet means severe malnutrition.
3. **Red (below 115 mm), swelling of both feet, or any danger sign:** feeding center or hospital today.
4. **Yellow (115-124 mm):** feed 5 or more times a day, add oil, keep breastfeeding, find a nutrition program.
5. **Measure again every week.**

## Measure the arm (MUAC)

MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) is the quickest reliable test for children aged 6 to 59 months. If you do not know the age, use it for children 65-110 cm (26-43 in) tall.

1. Use the bare left arm. Bend the elbow to a right angle.
2. Find the halfway point between the bony tip of the shoulder and the tip of the elbow. A string folded in half finds it. Mark it.
3. Let the arm hang loose and straight.
4. Wrap the tape around the arm at the mark, touching the skin all the way round without squeezing.
5. Read where the tape meets its start line. Measure twice.

| MUAC, child 6-59 months | Tape color | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below 115 mm (4.5 in) | Red | Severe acute malnutrition | Feeding center or hospital now |
| 115-124 mm | Yellow | Moderate acute malnutrition | Home care, measure weekly |
| 125 mm (4.9 in) or more | Green | Not acutely malnourished | Measure monthly |

### Make your own MUAC strip

1. Cut a strip 2 cm (3/4 in) wide and 30 cm (12 in) long from something that does not stretch: thin card, stiff paper, or plastic from a bottle.
2. Draw a start line across it, 1 cm from one end.
3. With a ruler, mark lines exactly **11.5 cm** and **12.5 cm** from the start line. Write "RED", "YELLOW" and "GREEN" in the zones.
4. Hold the start line on the arm at the mark. Wrap the long end once around. The zone lying over the start line is the result.

```
 start line
 |                  11.5 cm      12.5 cm
 |<------ RED ------->|<-YELLOW->|<---- GREEN ---
 +--------------------+----------+---------------
```

No strip? Wrap a string around the arm, pinch it where it meets, and measure it on a ruler. On an inch ruler, 11.5 cm is 4 1/2 in and 12.5 cm is just under 5 in.

## Check for swelling of both feet

1. Press your thumbs firmly on the top of both feet for **3 seconds.**
2. Lift your thumbs. If a dent stays on **both** feet, this is nutritional swelling (edema, also called kwashiorkor): **severe malnutrition, whatever the arm measurement.**
3. Check the legs, hands and face. Swelling that has spread is more dangerous.

Skin may peel like old paint, and hair may turn thin and pale. Swelling of one foot only usually has another cause.

**Wasting you can see** also counts: ribs and shoulder blades showing, loose skin folds on the buttocks, an old-looking face. If a child looks very thin but the tape says green, trust your eyes and get them checked.

## Therapeutic food (RUTF)

Ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) is a fortified peanut paste in packets, from aid agencies and health services. It needs no cooking. It cannot be made correctly at home.

In feeding programs, a health worker offers the child some RUTF and watches (the **appetite test**). A child who eats it and has no danger signs can usually be treated at home with weekly checks. A child who refuses needs hospital care.

If your child gets RUTF, breastfeed first, give safe water with it, and **do not share it** with anyone. The sick child needs every packet.

## When to get help

Go to a feeding center, health worker or hospital the same day for:

- Swelling of both feet, or MUAC below 115 mm.
- Will not eat, cannot drink or breastfeed, or vomits everything.
- Very sleepy, hard to wake, or fits (seizures).
- Feels cold, or temperature below 35°C (95°F).
- Fever, cough or fast breathing. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).
- Diarrhea with dehydration, very pale palms, cloudy eyes, or open skin sores.
- A baby under 6 months who is thin, is not gaining weight, or feeds poorly.

## If help is days away

For a severely malnourished child:

1. **Keep the child warm,** skin-to-skin with the mother under a blanket, head covered.
2. **Feed small amounts every 2-3 hours, day and night.** Breastfeed first, then give soft food with oil or milk. Do not force it.
3. **If the child becomes drowsy or weak and can swallow,** give sugar water: 1 rounded teaspoon of sugar in 50 mL (3 1/2 tablespoons) of clean water.
4. **For diarrhea, give ORS slowly, in small sips.** Normal amounts can overload the heart. Health workers use special fluids. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
5. **Do not give water pills (diuretics)** for swelling. They are dangerous for these children.
6. **Do not give iron** until the child is eating well and gaining weight.
7. **Keep moving toward help.** Health workers usually give antibiotics, because hidden infections are common.

## Home care for moderate malnutrition

- **Keep breastfeeding** until 2 years or longer. A thin mother still makes good milk.
- **Feed 5 or more times a day:** 3 meals and at least 2 snacks.
- **Give thick food, not watery soup.** Add 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of oil, butter or peanut paste to each bowl.
- **Add egg, milk, fish, meat, liver, beans or lentils** daily if you can, plus dark green leaves and orange fruit and vegetables.
- **Give the child their own bowl** and help them eat.
- **Ask health workers for vitamin A, deworming tablets and measles vaccine.** Never give more vitamin A than the package says: too much is poisonous.

For meal sizes by age, see [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).

## Vitamin and mineral diseases

| Missing | Signs | Foods that help |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin A | Cannot see in dim light (night blindness); dry eyes; later a cloudy eye and blindness | Liver, eggs, milk, red palm oil, dark green leaves, orange fruit and vegetables |
| Vitamin C (scurvy) | Swollen, bleeding gums; easy bruising; wounds that do not heal | Fresh fruit, vegetables, potatoes, green leaves, sprouted beans |
| Niacin (pellagra) | Dark, rough rash on skin exposed to sun; diarrhea; confusion | Peanuts, beans, meat, fish, eggs; maize (corn) cooked with lime |
| Thiamine, vitamin B1 (beriberi) | Numb or weak legs; swelling; breathlessness. Breastfed babies: hoarse or lost voice, vomiting, fast breathing | Whole grains, brown or parboiled rice, beans, peanuts, pork |
| Iodine | Swelling at the front of the neck (goiter); in pregnancy, brain damage to the baby | Iodized salt, seafood, seaweed, milk, eggs |
| Iron (anemia) | Pale inner eyelids, palms and nails; tiredness; breathlessness | Meat, liver, fish, beans, lentils, dark green leaves |

- **Eye changes are urgent.** A health worker can give high-dose vitamin A before the eye goes blind.
- **Beriberi in a baby can kill within hours.** Baby and mother both need thiamine from a health worker.
- Keep iron tablets away from children: an overdose can kill.

## Pregnant and breastfeeding women

- Many programs treat a MUAC below about **230 mm (9 in)** in a pregnant or breastfeeding woman as malnutrition. Some use a lower number.
- Take iron and folic acid tablets in pregnancy, as the health worker or package says. Use iodized salt.

See [Pregnancy Care Without a Clinic](../medical/pregnancy-care.md).

## Adults and older people

- **Warning signs:** loose clothes, weakness, feeling cold, slow healing, or losing more than 1 in 10 of body weight in 3 to 6 months without trying.
- **Body mass index (BMI):** weight in kg, divided by height in meters, divided by height in meters again. Below 18.5 is underweight. **Below 16 is severe.**
- **Arm:** many programs treat an adult MUAC below about **160 mm (6 1/4 in)** as severe.
- **Older people** may lose appetite, have bad teeth, or give their food away. Give soft food in small meals and check that they eat.

**After more than 5 days of little food, restart eating slowly.** See [Food Priorities and Rationing](../food/food-rationing.md).
