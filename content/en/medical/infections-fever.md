> [!CAUTION]
> **Sepsis kills within hours.** An ill person who becomes confused, breathes very fast, has cold blotchy skin, or passes almost no urine needs hospital care now. Evacuate at any cost.
>
> **Any fever in a baby under 3 months is an emergency.**
>
> **Never give aspirin to anyone under 16.** It can cause Reye syndrome, which damages the liver and brain.

## Quick steps

1. **Measure:** **38°C (100.4°F)** or higher is a fever.
2. **Look for danger signs:** fever in a baby under 3 months, stiff neck, a rash that does not fade under a pressed glass, confusion, trouble breathing, fits, or any fever in a malaria area. If there are any, get help now.
3. **Give fluids often:** water, ORS, soup, breast milk.
4. **Dress them lightly.** Do not wrap a feverish child in blankets.
5. **Give paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen** at the package dose if they are uncomfortable.
6. **In a malaria area, test and treat within 24 hours.**
7. **Check again every 4 hours.** Write down temperature, breathing rate, drinking and urine.

## Measuring fever

- Use a digital thermometer. Put it in the armpit for children under 5, and under the tongue for older children and adults.
- Leave it in place for the time in its instructions.
- Without a thermometer, feel the chest or back with the back of your hand. This is unreliable but better than nothing.
- How the person looks and behaves matters more than the number.

## Managing fever

Fever helps the body fight infection. On its own it rarely causes harm. Treat the person, not the number.

- **Give extra fluids.** Fever dries out the body. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
- **Keep them cool, not cold:** light clothing, a light sheet, moving air.
- **Do not sponge with cold water or alcohol.** Cold water causes shivering. Alcohol can poison a child through the skin.
- **Medicine:** paracetamol or ibuprofen helps comfort. Follow the package for age, weight and maximum doses. Avoid ibuprofen in someone who is dehydrated or vomiting, or who may have dengue.

### Fits with fever

Children aged 6 months to 5 years sometimes have a fit (seizure) with fever.

1. Lay the child on their side on the floor, away from hard objects.
2. Put nothing in the mouth.
3. Time the fit.

Most stop within 5 minutes. **A fit longer than 5 minutes, a second fit, or a child who does not wake up normally is an emergency.** A first fit always needs a health worker, because it can be meningitis.

## When to get help

Get help now for any of these danger signs:

- **Baby under 3 months** with 38°C (100.4°F) or more. Baby 3-6 months with 39°C (102°F) or more.
- **Stiff neck** (cannot touch chin to chest), bulging soft spot on a baby's head, or dislike of bright light.
- **Rash that does not fade under a glass.** Press the side of a clear glass firmly on the spots. If you can still see them through the glass, this may be meningitis or sepsis. On dark skin, check the palms, soles and inside the eyelids.
- **Confusion,** very drowsy, floppy or hard to wake.
- **Trouble breathing,** fast breathing, grunting or blue lips.
- **Fits.**
- Cannot drink, vomits everything, or passes very little urine.
- Fever that lasts **more than 5 days.**
- Any fever in a malaria area, or after travel to one.

## Sepsis

Sepsis is the body's extreme reaction to an infection, which starts damaging its own organs. It can start from any infection: chest, urine, skin, wound, belly or womb after childbirth.

**Signs in adults:**

- New confusion, slurred speech or drowsiness.
- Breathing fast: **22 or more breaths a minute** at rest.
- Signs of low blood pressure: a fast weak pulse, fainting or dizziness on sitting up, cold, clammy, pale or blotchy (mottled) skin.
- Little or no urine all day.
- Severe shivering or muscle pain, or saying "I feel like I am going to die".

**Signs in children:** very fast breathing, grunting, fits, blue, pale or mottled skin, a rash that does not fade, very sleepy or floppy, or feeling cold to touch. Babies and young children may stop feeding, vomit repeatedly, or not pass urine for 12 hours.

**What to do:** sepsis needs antibiotics into a vein and fluids, fast. Every hour of delay raises the risk of death. Evacuate. While waiting, give fluids if they can drink, keep them warm if cold, lay them on their side if drowsy, and clean any infected wound. If a health worker gave antibiotics for this situation, follow their instructions exactly.

## Pneumonia

Look for cough or difficult breathing **plus** fast breathing. Count breaths for a full **60 seconds** while the person is calm or asleep. Watch the chest or belly rise.

| Age | Fast breathing |
|---|---|
| Under 2 months | 60 or more per minute |
| 2-12 months | 50 or more per minute |
| 1-5 years | 40 or more per minute |
| Adults | 22 or more is a warning; 30 or more is severe |

**Chest indrawing** is when the lower chest wall sucks in as the child breathes in. It means pneumonia that needs a health worker. Children with pneumonia usually need antibiotics. Babies with pneumonia may have no fever.

Get help urgently for any child who cannot drink, vomits everything, has a fit, is very sleepy, has blue lips, or makes a harsh noise breathing in while calm.

At home: keep breastfeeding, give fluids, sit the person up, and keep them away from smoke, including cooking smoke.

## Malaria

In areas with malaria, **any fever may be malaria.** Get a test and treatment **within 24 hours.** Untreated malaria can become severe and kill quickly, especially in children under 5 and pregnant women.

Signs of severe malaria: confusion, fits, inability to sit, stand or drink, repeated vomiting, trouble breathing, dark urine, yellow eyes, unusual bleeding or very pale skin. This is an emergency.

Malaria is treated with combination medicines from a health worker. Take the full course. Sleep under a treated bed net to prevent it. See [Bites, Stings and Venom](../medical/bites-stings.md). See [Living in a Tropical Rainforest](../land/jungle-living.md).

## Skin infections and abscesses

- **Cellulitis** is spreading redness, warmth, swelling and pain. Draw a pen line around the red edge. If the redness spreads past the line, red streaks run up the limb, or fever starts, the person needs antibiotics urgently.
- **An abscess (boil)** is a painful lump full of pus. Hold a clean cloth soaked in warm water on it for 20 minutes, 3-4 times a day. **Do not squeeze it,** especially on the face. When it opens, let it drain, wash gently with soap and water, and cover it. Wash your hands after.
- Large abscesses, or those on the face, hands, breast or groin, or with fever, need a health worker to drain them.

See [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md).

## Urinary infections

- **Bladder infection:** burning when passing urine, needing to go often, cloudy or smelly urine, low belly pain. Drink plenty of water. A health worker can give antibiotics.
- **Kidney infection:** fever, shivering, pain in the back or side below the ribs, vomiting. This needs antibiotics urgently: it can turn into sepsis.
- Always get help for urine infections in men, children and pregnant women, or with blood in the urine.

## Antibiotics: the honest truth

- Antibiotics kill bacteria. **They do nothing against viruses:** colds, flu, most coughs, most sore throats and most diarrhea.
- Using them when not needed causes side effects and makes bacteria resistant, so antibiotics fail when they are really needed.
- Different infections need different antibiotics, doses and lengths of treatment. **If a clinic, health worker or pharmacist exists, use them.**
- Take antibiotics exactly as the health worker says. Do not save leftovers or share them.
- Antibiotics save lives in sepsis, pneumonia, meningitis, kidney infections, spreading skin infections and dysentery. Get the person to someone who can give them.

For storing medicines and spotting fakes, see [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).

## When no help can come for days

- Check and write down temperature, breathing rate, pulse, drinking, urine and alertness every 4 hours. Getting worse is a reason to evacuate.
- Keep up fluids and food. Help them wash. Turn a person who cannot move every 2 hours to prevent pressure sores.
- If the illness could spread, separate them and protect carers. See [Caring for the Sick and Stopping Spread](../medical/infectious-disease.md).
