> [!CAUTION]
> **Chemical in the eye: flush with water at once, for at least 20 minutes.** Do not stop to find eye drops. Seconds matter, most of all for lime, cement, lye and ammonia.
>
> **Never pull out an object stuck in the eye, and never press on an injured eye.** Pressure can push the inside of the eye out and blind it forever.
>
> **Put nothing in the eye except clean water or eye medicine.** Urine, milk, herbs, oil and traditional remedies cause blinding infections.

## Quick steps

1. **Chemical splash:** flush with clean water right away, for at least 20 minutes, holding the lids open. Then get medical help.
2. **Dust or grit:** do not rub. Blink, then rinse with clean water.
3. **Object stuck in the eye, or a cut eyeball:** do not touch or press it. Tape a paper cup over the eye. Get help urgently.
4. **Blow to the eye:** hold a cold cloth gently on the closed eye for 10-15 minutes. No pressure.
5. **Get urgent care** for sudden vision loss, severe pain, double vision, blood inside the eye, or any chemical, cut or puncture.

## Chemicals in the eye

1. **Start flushing now** with the cleanest water you can get within seconds: a tap, shower, bottle or jug. Do not wait for warm water.
2. Quickly brush dry powder, such as lime or cement, off the face first.
3. Turn the head so the injured eye is lowest. Pour water into the **inner corner, next to the nose,** so it runs out toward the ear, not into the other eye.
4. **Hold the eyelids wide open.** Ask them to roll the eye in every direction while you pour.
5. Flush for **at least 20 minutes** by the clock. Alkalis (lime, wet cement, plaster, lye, drain cleaner, ammonia) keep burning after the pain eases: flush them for **at least 30 minutes,** and keep going on the way to help.
6. Take out contact lenses after a few minutes if they come out easily. Wipe lumps of lime or cement off the white of the eye with a wet cloth corner, then flush again.

If both eyes are hit, flush both under a shower. **Every chemical eye injury needs a medical check.** A cloudy or white front of the eye, or blurred vision after flushing, means a severe burn. For skin, see [Burns](../medical/burns.md) and [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md).

## Dust, grit and small specks

1. Do not rub the eye. Blink, then rinse with clean water, or blink with the face in a bowl of clean water.
2. Pull the upper lid down over the lower lid by its lashes, then let go. The lower lashes can brush a speck away.
3. To find the speck, pull the lower lid down while they look up. Then lift the upper lid while they look down.
4. With clean hands, lift a speck off the **white of the eye** or inside a lid with the corner of a clean, moist cloth or cotton bud.
5. **Never touch the colored part or the black center.** If a speck is stuck there, keep rinsing. If it stays, cover the eye loosely and get help. Never use tweezers, needles or fingernails.

A scratch can still feel like a speck after the speck is gone. Most small scratches heal in 1-2 days. A tight eye patch does not speed healing.

**Get help if** it is not better after 24 hours, a white spot or pus appears, or the speck came from hammering or grinding: it may be inside the eye.

## Objects stuck in the eye, and cut eyeballs

Suspect a deep injury if you see an object in the eye, a cut on the eyeball, jelly or dark tissue coming out, or a pupil that is not round.

1. **Do not remove the object.** Do not wash the eye or put a patch on it.
2. **Tape a rigid shield over the eye:** the bottom of a paper or plastic cup, a cut-off bottle, or a cone of stiff card. Its edges rest on the bones of the brow and cheek, never on the eye.
3. Keep them still, with the head raised. They must not bend over, strain, rub or blow their nose: these raise the pressure in the eye.
4. Both eyes move together. Covering the good eye too, for a short time, reduces eye movement. Do it only if it calms them: many people panic when they cannot see.
5. Give nothing to eat or drink if surgery can be reached within hours. **Evacuate urgently.**

## Blows to the eye and black eyes

1. Hold a cold cloth, or a cold pack wrapped in cloth, gently on the closed eye for 10-15 minutes at a time, several times a day for 1-2 days. **Do not press on the eyeball.**
2. Sleep with the head raised on extra pillows.
3. For pain, use paracetamol (acetaminophen) as the package says. Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen, which can make bleeding in the eye worse.
4. Do not blow the nose hard: if the bone under the eye is broken, air can be pushed into the eye socket.

A bright red patch on the white of the eye, with normal vision and little pain, is usually harmless and fades in 2-3 weeks.

**Blood in front of the colored part** (a red haze, or a red layer at the bottom when sitting up) is serious. Keep the head raised, tape a shield over the eye, and evacuate.

## Cuts to the eyelid

Check the eyeball first: if it may be cut, use a shield and no pressure. If only the lid is cut, press gently with a clean cloth, then cover it. See [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md).

Small cuts that do not reach the edge of the lid usually heal well. Cuts through the edge, near the inner corner (where tears drain), or with yellow fat showing need repair by a trained person, ideally within a day.

## Flash burns and snow blindness

Ultraviolet light from welding, or from sun reflected off snow, ice, water or white sand, burns the surface of the eye.

**Signs** start **6-12 hours later,** often at night: severe pain, a gritty feeling, watering, red eyes, swollen lids and pain in bright light.

1. Take out contact lenses. Rest in a dark room with cool, damp cloths on the closed eyes.
2. Take pain relief as the package says. Do not rub.
3. Do not keep using numbing eye drops. They slow healing and can damage the eye.

It usually heals by itself in 1-3 days. Get help if vision is still poor after 3 days.

**Prevention:** on snow or water, wear sunglasses that block UV, with side protection, all day, even when it is cloudy. Sunglasses do not protect against a welding arc. **Improvised slit goggles:** cut 2 narrow slits, about 3 mm (1/8 in) high, in a strip of card, wood, bark or leather. Tie it across the eyes.

## Red, sticky eyes (conjunctivitis)

**Signs:** a red eye, a gritty feeling, watering or sticky discharge, lashes stuck together in the morning. Most cases clear by themselves in 1-2 weeks.

1. Clean the eye with cotton or cloth soaked in boiled, cooled water. Wipe once from the inner corner outward. Use a new piece for each wipe and each eye.
2. Wash hands with soap before and after touching the eyes. See [Handwashing and Personal Hygiene](../hygiene/handwashing.md).
3. Give the sick person their own towel and pillowcase. Do not share eye drops or makeup.
4. No contact lenses until the eye is normal again.

## Contact lenses in a disaster

- **Never rinse or store lenses in water** of any kind, or in saliva. Water carries a tiny organism that causes a blinding eye infection.
- Switch to glasses when solution or clean hands are hard to get, and in smoke, dust or ash. Keep spare glasses in your kit.
- Never sleep in lenses. Take them out at once if the eye becomes red, painful or watery, or vision blurs.

## When to get help

Get to a doctor or eye clinic the same day if you can for:

- any chemical in the eye, after flushing
- an object stuck in the eye, a cut eyeball, or a speck from hammering, grinding or an explosion
- sudden loss of vision, or blurring that does not clear with blinking
- severe pain, or a painful red eye with headache, vomiting or halos around lights
- double vision, or a numb cheek or upper lip after a blow (broken bone under the eye)
- blood in front of the colored part, or a pupil that is not round
- flashes of light, a shower of new floaters, or a dark curtain over the vision
- a newborn baby in its first 4 weeks with red, swollen eyes or pus
- a red, painful eye in a contact lens wearer
