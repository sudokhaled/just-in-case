> [!CAUTION]
> **Press hard, right on the wound, right now.** Severe bleeding kills in minutes. Do not wait for gloves, a kit, or someone else. Use your hands if you have nothing else.
>
> **Once a tourniquet has stopped the bleeding, do not loosen it.** Loosening it can restart bleeding that kills. Only a doctor or trained medic should remove it.
>
> **Never use thin cord, wire, string, shoelaces or cable ties as a tourniquet.** They cut into the limb and do not stop the bleeding.

## Quick steps

1. **Make sure you are safe.** Put on gloves or plastic bags only if they are in your hand.
2. **Shout for help.** Send someone to call emergency services and bring a first aid kit.
3. **Find the source.** Open or cut away clothing to see the wound.
4. **Press hard** on the wound with a cloth, or your bare hands. Lean your body weight on it. Keep pressing for **at least 10 minutes** without lifting to look.
5. **Arm or leg still bleeding?** Put on a tourniquet **5-7 cm (2-3 in) above the wound,** not on a joint. Tighten it until the bleeding stops.
6. **Deep wound in the neck, armpit or groin?** Stuff cloth tightly into the wound, then press hard on top.
7. **Lay the person down and keep them warm.** Watch for shock.

## Is it life-threatening?

Act at once if you see any of these:

- Blood **spurting** or pulsing out.
- Blood **flowing steadily** and not slowing down.
- Blood **pooling** on the ground.
- Clothing or bandages **soaked through.**
- Part of an arm or leg cut off.
- Bleeding with pale, cold, sweaty skin, or confusion. These are signs of shock.

Bleeding can hide under thick clothes, under the body, or in snow, sand or darkness. Run your hands over the body, including the back, then look at your hands.

## Direct pressure

1. Lay the person down.
2. Uncover the wound.
3. Put a pad of gauze or clean cloth directly on the point where the blood is coming out. If you have nothing, use your hand.
4. **Press hard** with both hands, arms straight, using your body weight. It should hurt the person.
5. **Keep pressing for at least 10 minutes.** Do not lift to check. Use a watch.
6. If blood soaks through, do not remove the pad. Put more cloth on top and press harder. Make sure you are pressing on the exact spot.
7. When the bleeding stops, wrap a bandage firmly over the pad. Check that the fingers or toes beyond it stay warm and pink.

Raising the limb and pressing on "pressure points" away from the wound do not work well. Do not waste time on them.

## Wound packing

Use packing for deep wounds where pressure on top cannot reach the bleeding, and for the **neck, armpit, groin and buttock,** where a tourniquet cannot go.

1. Find where the blood is coming from inside the wound.
2. Push gauze or clean cloth into the wound with your fingers, right onto the bleeding spot.
3. Keep feeding more cloth in, pressing tightly, until the wound is completely full.
4. Press hard on top: **at least 3 minutes** with blood-stopping (hemostatic) gauze, **at least 10 minutes** with plain gauze or cloth.
5. Leave the packing in. Wrap a firm bandage over it. If blood soaks through, add more on top and press again.

- **Neck:** pack and press, but never wrap a bandage tightly around the neck.
- **Chest or belly:** do not pack into the wound. Press on top with a cloth only to stop bleeding. Do not seal a chest wound with plastic or tape, on any number of sides: leave it open or cover it loosely. See [Chest and Belly Injuries](../medical/chest-injuries.md).
- **Organs coming out of the belly:** do not push them back. Cover them with a clean, damp cloth.

## Tourniquets

A tourniquet squeezes an arm or leg so hard that blood stops flowing past it. It is the fastest way to stop heavy bleeding from a limb. Use one when pressure does not stop the bleeding, or when you cannot keep pressing (many injured, you must move, danger).

### Commercial tourniquet (best)

A commercial tourniquet with a twisting rod (windlass) works far better than anything improvised. Keep one in every kit. See [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).

1. **Place it 5-7 cm (2-3 in) above the wound,** between the wound and the heart. Never on the elbow or knee. If the wound is just below a joint, put it above the joint.
2. **If you cannot see the wound clearly,** there are several wounds, it is dark, or you must move quickly: put it **"high and tight"**, as high on the arm or thigh as it will go.
3. Pull the strap as tight as you can and fasten it before you twist.
4. **Twist the rod until the bleeding stops.** This will hurt a lot. Pain does not mean you are doing it wrong.
5. Lock the rod in its clip.
6. **Write the time** on the tourniquet or on the person's forehead, for example "TQ 14:30".
7. If bleeding continues, twist more. If it still bleeds, put a **second tourniquet** just above the first.
8. Leave it where it can be seen. Tell every helper it is there.

Put it on bare skin if you can do so quickly. It can go over thin clothing. A thick thigh often needs 2 tourniquets.

### Improvised tourniquet

Improvised tourniquets often fail. They loosen, or do not squeeze hard enough to stop blood in an artery. Use one only when there is no commercial tourniquet, and keep pressing on the wound as well.

1. Use a strong band **at least 4 cm (1.5 in) wide:** a folded triangular bandage, a torn strip of strong cloth, or a scarf.
2. Wrap it twice around the limb at the place described above. Tie a half knot.
3. Lay a strong stick, metal rod or similar on the knot. Tie a full knot over it.
4. Twist the stick until the bleeding stops.
5. Tie the stick down so it cannot unwind.
6. Write the time. Check it often, because improvised tourniquets slip.

### How long can it stay on?

- **Up to about 2 hours:** lasting damage to the limb is uncommon.
- **Longer:** the risk of nerve damage, muscle death and losing the limb rises with time.
- **More than about 6 hours:** the limb is often lost. Removing the tourniquet now, outside a hospital, can release poisons from the damaged limb that stop the heart.

**A limb can be lost. A life cannot be replaced.** Do not loosen a tourniquet "to let blood in". That old advice is wrong. Leave it on and get the person to surgical care, at any cost.

> [!WARNING]
> Trained medics can sometimes replace a tourniquet with packing and a pressure bandage within the first 2 hours. This needs training and constant watching. If you are not trained, do not try it.

## Objects stuck in a wound

Do not pull out a knife, stick, glass or metal that is stuck in a wound. It may be plugging the bleeding. Press around it, not on it. Pad both sides with rolled cloth and bandage over the pads to hold it still.

## Body parts that are cut off

Care for the person first. Then wrap the part in a clean, damp cloth and put it in a plastic bag. Keep the bag cool, for example in cold water or on ice, but never put the part directly on ice. It is only useful if a surgeon can be reached within a few hours.

## Nosebleeds

1. Sit up and **lean forward.** Do not tilt the head back. Swallowed blood causes vomiting.
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose firmly, just below the hard bone.
3. Hold it for **10-15 minutes** without letting go. Breathe through the mouth. Spit out blood.
4. If it still bleeds, pinch again for another 10-15 minutes.
5. For the next 24 hours, do not blow or pick the nose, or lift heavy things.

Get medical help if it does not stop after 2 rounds of pinching, is very heavy, started after a head injury, or the person takes blood-thinning medicine.

## Internal bleeding

Bleeding inside the body cannot be seen or stopped by first aid. Suspect it after a crash, a fall, a crush, a blow or a blast, even if the person looks fine at first.

**Signs:**

- signs of shock with no visible bleeding
- bruising, swelling or pain in the belly, chest, back or side
- a hard, swollen belly
- vomiting blood, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds
- coughing up blood
- black, sticky stools, or blood in the stool or urine
- in a woman who might be pregnant: sudden belly pain, fainting or bleeding from the vagina (pregnancy outside the womb) See [Pregnancy Care Without a Clinic](../medical/pregnancy-care.md).

A broken thigh bone or pelvis can lose a lot of blood inside the body. See [Broken Bones, Sprains and Dislocations](../medical/fractures-sprains.md).

**What to do:** lay them flat, keep them warm, give nothing to eat or drink if help can come within hours, and evacuate urgently. A surgeon is the only cure.

## Shock

Losing a lot of blood causes shock: pale, cold, sweaty skin, a fast weak pulse, fast breathing, thirst and confusion. Stop the bleeding, lay them flat and keep them warm. **Cold makes bleeding worse,** because blood clots poorly when the body is cold. Put insulation under them, not only on top. See [First Aid Basics: Check, Call, Care](../medical/first-aid-basics.md).

## Evacuate if

Get the person to a hospital, by any means, if:

- a tourniquet was used
- a wound needed packing
- bleeding cannot be controlled
- there are signs of shock or internal bleeding
- part of a limb was cut off
- there is a deep wound in the neck, chest, belly, groin or buttock
- a nosebleed will not stop

For cleaning and caring for wounds after the bleeding stops, see [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md).
