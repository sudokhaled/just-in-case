> [!CAUTION]
> **Stool, vomit, blood and other body fluids spread disease.** Cover your hands with gloves or plastic bags when you touch them. Wash your hands with soap after every contact with a sick person.
>
> **Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, acids or other cleaners.** It releases poison gas.

## Quick steps

1. **Separate the sick person** in their own room, or a screened-off corner with fresh air.
2. **Choose one caregiver,** ideally a healthy adult who is not pregnant, old or chronically ill.
3. **Wash hands with soap** before and after every contact.
4. **Wear a well-fitting mask** near a coughing person. The sick person wears one too, if they can.
5. **Clean surfaces daily** with soap and water, then **0.1% bleach solution.** Use **0.5%** for spills of stool, vomit or blood.
6. **Handle laundry and waste with gloves.** Never shake soiled sheets.
7. **Keep a record** of who is sick and when it started. Tell health workers.

## Setting up a sick room

- Use a separate room or tent if you can. If not, screen off a corner and keep beds **at least 1 m (3 ft)** apart.
- **Keep air moving.** Open a window or door to the outside, unless the cold is dangerous. Fresh air greatly reduces the spread of coughing illnesses.
- Give the sick person their own cup, plate, spoon, towel and, if possible, toilet or bucket.
- Put a handwashing station and a bin with a lid at the door.
- Keep visitors out, especially children, old people and pregnant women.
- For diarrhea or vomiting, put a plastic sheet under the bed sheet and a bucket by the bed.

## Protecting the caregiver

- **Masks:** a respirator mask (N95, FFP2 or similar) protects best, then a medical mask, then a cloth mask of 2-3 layers. It must cover the nose and mouth with no gaps. Replace it when it is wet or dirty.
- **Gloves** for any body fluids. Plastic bags work if you have no gloves. Wash your hands after taking them off.
- **An apron or raincoat** protects clothes from splashes. Do not touch your face while caring.
- Eat, drink and sleep. Take turns with another carer if possible. Watch yourself for signs of the same illness.

## Making bleach solutions

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends **0.1% chlorine** for cleaning surfaces and **0.5% chlorine** for body-fluid spills. Use plain, unscented household bleach. Check the strength (sodium hypochlorite %) on the label.

Put the bleach in a container, then add water to make **1 liter (1 quart):**

| Bleach on label | 0.1% for surfaces | 0.5% for body fluids |
|---|---|---|
| 3% | 33 mL (about 2 tablespoons) | 167 mL (about 2/3 cup) |
| 5% | 20 mL (4 teaspoons) | 100 mL (about 7 tablespoons) |
| 6% | 17 mL (about 3.5 teaspoons) | 83 mL (about 5.5 tablespoons) |
| 8.25% | 12 mL (about 2.5 teaspoons) | 60 mL (4 tablespoons) |

A teaspoon is 5 mL. A tablespoon is 15 mL. For other strengths, divide the bleach % by the % you want and subtract 1: that is the parts of water for 1 part bleach. Example: 5 divided by 0.5 is 10, minus 1 is **9 parts water to 1 part 5% bleach** for 0.5%.

1. Use cool water and a plastic container. Label it clearly.
2. **Make it fresh every day.** It weakens quickly. Keep it covered, out of the sun, and away from children.
3. **Clean first.** Wash off dirt with soap and water. Dirt stops bleach from working.
4. Wipe the bleach solution on and leave it wet for **at least 1 minute,** then let it dry.
5. **For spills of stool, vomit or blood:** wear gloves, cover the spill with cloth or paper, pour 0.5% solution over it, wait about 10 minutes, then wipe up and put it in a latrine or bag. Clean the area again.

These solutions are for surfaces, buckets and floors. Do not use them on skin, and never drink them. For drinking water, see [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

## Laundry

1. **Wear gloves.** Carry soiled items in a bucket or bag, held away from your body. Never shake them.
2. Scrape solid stool into a toilet or latrine.
3. **Wash with soap and hot water,** 60-90°C (140-194°F) if you can. Soak in hot soapy water and stir with a stick to avoid splashing.
4. If you have no hot water, after washing, **soak for 30 minutes in 0.05% bleach solution** (0.1% solution mixed with an equal amount of water). Then rinse.
5. **Dry completely,** in direct sun if possible.
6. Wash at least 30 m (100 ft) from wells and streams. Pour used water into a latrine or soak pit.

## Waste

- Empty stool and vomit into a toilet or latrine, never into drains, fields or streams. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).
- Rinse buckets and potties with 0.5% solution after emptying. Pour the rinse into the latrine.
- Put used dressings, gloves and masks in a tied bag. Burn or bury them.
- Put needles and blades in a hard, closed container.

## Cholera and severe diarrhea

- **Oral rehydration solution is the main treatment.** See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
- Keep a bucket by the bed for stool and vomit. Empty it into a latrine and rinse it with 0.5% solution.
- Wash hands after every contact, and before touching food or water.
- A person with diarrhea should not cook for others until **48 hours** after it stops.
- **The body of someone who died of cholera is very infectious.** Washing the body and funeral meals have spread cholera. Follow health workers' advice.

## Coughing and flu-like illness

- Keep air moving. Wear masks. Cover coughs with an elbow or tissue.
- The sick person sleeps apart, and keeps away from others until they have had **no fever for at least 24 hours** without fever medicine and feel better.
- Watch for danger signs such as fast breathing. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).

## Measles

Measles is one of the most contagious diseases. It spreads through the air, and the virus can stay in a room for up to 2 hours after the sick person leaves. It kills many children in crowded camps.

- **Signs:** high fever, cough, runny nose and red eyes, then 3-5 days later a rash that starts on the face and spreads down the body.
- **It spreads from 4 days before the rash until 4 days after.** Isolate the person for that time.
- **Dangers:** pneumonia, diarrhea, blindness, brain swelling and weight loss.
- **Care:** fluids, food and breastfeeding, fever care, and wiping the eyes with a clean wet cloth. Health workers give vitamin A, which reduces deaths and blindness. Ask for it.
- **Vaccinate.** Two doses of measles vaccine protect about 97% of people. In camps, measles vaccination of children from 6 months to 15 years is a top priority.

## When an outbreak starts in a camp or community

Report to health authorities **at once:**

- Even 1 suspected case of cholera, measles or meningitis, any child with sudden weakness or paralysis of a limb, or fever with unusual bleeding.
- Several people with the same illness in the same place or week.
- Deaths that nobody can explain.

Then act together:

1. Set up a separate treatment area away from living areas.
2. Chlorinate drinking water. Stop people dipping cups into shared water.
3. Build or repair toilets, with handwashing stations and soap.
4. Reduce crowding where people sleep. Share clear information and correct rumors quickly.

See [Pandemics and Disease Outbreaks](../disasters/pandemic.md).

## Keeping records

Keep a notebook. For each sick person, write the date, name, age, sex, where they sleep, main signs, the day the illness started, treatment given, and the outcome (better, sent to clinic, or died). This shows health workers where an outbreak is and whether it is growing.

## Dignity

- Talk to the sick person. Explain what you are doing and why. Give privacy for washing and the toilet.
- Help them keep in touch with family, from a distance if needed.
- **Do not blame or shame.** Fear of stigma makes people hide illness, and hidden illness spreads faster.
- Respect religious and cultural customs. Ask health workers for safe ways to carry them out.

## When to get help

- The sick person shows danger signs of dehydration or serious infection.
- Anyone has signs of cholera, measles, meningitis or bleeding with fever.
- The caregiver becomes ill.
