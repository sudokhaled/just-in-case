> [!CAUTION]
> **Do not make the person vomit.** No fingers down the throat, no salt water, no syrup of ipecac. Vomiting causes more harm, and salt water can kill.
>
> **A person can feel fine and still be dying.** Paracetamol (acetaminophen) overdose and death cap mushrooms can cause few symptoms for many hours, then destroy the liver. Get help even if they seem well.
>
> **Protect yourself first.** Do not enter a room, tank, well or pit full of fumes. Wear gloves or plastic bags on your hands if a chemical is on the person.

## Quick steps

1. **Separate the person from the poison.** Move them to fresh air. Remove soaked clothing. Take away remaining pills or liquid.
2. **Check breathing.** If unconscious and breathing, lay them on their side. If not breathing, start CPR. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
3. **Find out what, how much and when.** Keep the container, pills, plant or mushroom.
4. **Call a poison center or emergency number** if phones work, and follow their advice.
5. **Give nothing to eat or drink** unless a poison center or health worker says so.
6. **Rinse skin or eyes** with running water for **at least 20 minutes.**
7. **Get medical help** if there are any symptoms, or if they swallowed medicine, fuel, a chemical, a button battery, a wild mushroom or something unknown.

## Swallowed poisons

Look for burns around the mouth, a smell of fuel or chemicals, vomiting, drowsiness, strange behavior and empty containers nearby. Do not give salt water, milk, oil, vinegar or other "antidotes" unless a poison center advises it.

### Corrosives

Corrosives include drain cleaner, oven cleaner, strong acids, lye, strong bleach and dishwasher products.

1. Rinse the mouth with water and spit it out.
2. If the person is fully alert and can swallow, give **a few small sips of water.** Stop if it hurts or makes them vomit.
3. Do not make them vomit: the corrosive burns again on the way up.
4. **Get help urgently** if they drool, cannot swallow, have a changed voice or have trouble breathing. These mean the throat is burned and may swell shut.

### Fuel and oils

Kerosene (paraffin), petrol (gasoline), lamp oil, lighter fluid and turpentine are among the most common poisons in children. The main danger is the lungs: a tiny amount breathed in causes severe pneumonia.

- Do not make them vomit. Keep them sitting up or on their side.
- Coughing, choking or gasping after swallowing means fuel reached the lungs.
- Watch them for at least **6 hours.** Fast breathing, wheezing, blue lips or fever need a hospital.

### Button batteries and magnets

A swallowed button battery can burn through the food pipe in **2 hours.** Go to a hospital immediately, even with no symptoms. Swallowing 2 or more magnets can also tear the gut.

If a child **over 1 year** swallowed a button battery in the last 12 hours and can swallow, US poison centers advise giving **2 teaspoons (10 mL) of honey every 10 minutes, up to 6 times,** on the way to hospital. **Never give honey to a baby under 1 year.**

### Medicines

- **Paracetamol (acetaminophen):** the person often feels fine at first. Liver damage appears after 1-3 days. The antidote works best within **8 hours.** Go now.
- **Iron tablets,** including pregnancy vitamins, can kill a small child.
- **Opioids** (morphine, codeine, tramadol, heroin, fentanyl): very sleepy, tiny pupils, slow or stopped breathing. Give naloxone if you have it, as the package says. Give rescue breaths. Naloxone wears off before many opioids do, so watch for hours. See [Withdrawal and Running Out of Medicines](../medical/withdrawal.md).
- **Any child who swallowed an adult's medicine** needs a health worker.

If someone took an overdose on purpose, stay with them, remove other medicines and dangerous items, and get help. See [Psychological First Aid](../community/psychological-first-aid.md).

### Bad alcohol (methanol)

Home-made or illegally sold alcohol may contain methanol. This becomes more common during shortages. Signs often start **12-24 hours** after drinking: blurred vision, blindness, severe belly pain, vomiting, fast breathing and confusion. It is deadly. Go to a hospital. Others who drank the same alcohol need help too.

### Pesticides and rat poison

- **Insecticides** (organophosphates) cause sweating, drooling, tiny pupils, vomiting, diarrhea, muscle twitching and breathing trouble. Wearing gloves, remove clothing and wash the skin with soap and water. Get to hospital fast: it has an antidote.
- **Rat poison** of the common type causes bleeding days later from the gums, nose or urine. A hospital can treat it.
- **Never spray food plants with fuel, tobacco or unknown chemicals.** Safer ways to protect crops are in [Protecting Crops From Pests, Disease and Animals](../farming/crop-pests.md).

## Breathed-in poisons

- **Carbon monoxide** from generators, stoves, charcoal or engines in closed spaces causes headache, dizziness, nausea and confusion, often in several people at once. Get everyone out. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
- **Gas leaks:** do not switch anything electrical on or off. Open doors and windows, leave, and turn off the gas at the main if safe.
- **Mixed cleaning products:** bleach with ammonia or acids makes poison gas. Leave and air out the room.
- **Wells, pits, tanks, sewers, silos and manure pits** can fill with gas that kills in seconds. Rescuers often die too. Do not go in. Ventilate it and use a rope from outside.

## Poison on skin or in eyes

### Skin

1. Protect your hands with gloves or plastic bags.
2. **Brush off dry powders** (such as lime) first.
3. Remove contaminated clothing and jewelry. Cut clothes off rather than pull them over the head.
4. **Rinse with lots of running water for at least 20 minutes.** Use soap for oily chemicals and pesticides.

### Eyes

1. **Rinse at once** with clean running water for **at least 20 minutes**, and at least 30 minutes for alkalis such as lime, cement, lye or ammonia. Hold the eyelids open. See [Eye Injuries](../medical/eye-injuries.md).
2. Tilt the head so the water runs away from the other eye.
3. Remove contact lenses if they come out easily. Do not rub the eye.
4. Strong alkalis (lime, cement, drain cleaner) cause the worst damage. Keep rinsing longer and get help.

## Plants and mushrooms

**Do not eat wild mushrooms.** No home test shows that a mushroom is safe. Tests with silver spoons, peeling, cooking, or watching animals eat them are myths that kill. The deadliest mushrooms, such as the death cap, look like edible ones.

**Death cap symptoms start 6-24 hours after eating:** severe vomiting and watery diarrhea, then a few hours or a day when the person seems better, then liver failure. **If mushroom symptoms start 6 hours or more after eating, assume a deadly mushroom.** Go to a hospital at once, even if the person improves.

Keep any leftover mushrooms or plant parts in a paper bag for identification.

Many plants and seeds can kill, including oleander, foxglove, water hemlock, castor beans and datura. Teach children never to eat berries, seeds or leaves they find. See [Foraging Wild Plants Safely](../food/foraging.md).

## Activated charcoal

Activated charcoal binds some poisons in the gut. Current first aid guidance: **give it only if a poison center or health worker tells you to.** It works best within 1 hour of swallowing. It does not work for corrosives, fuel, alcohol, methanol, iron or lithium. A drowsy or vomiting person can breathe it into the lungs.

Charcoal from a fire or burnt toast is **not** activated charcoal and does not work.

## Preventing poisoning in a disaster

Disasters bring more fuel, lamp oil, bleach, pesticides and rat poison into homes.

- **Never store fuel, kerosene, bleach or chemicals in drink bottles or food containers.** This is one of the most common ways children are poisoned.
- Label every container. Store chemicals and medicines high up, locked if possible, away from food and water.
- Never call medicine "sweets" or "candy".
- Never mix cleaning products. Run generators and charcoal burners outside only.

## When to get help

- Unconsciousness, deep drowsiness, fits, trouble breathing, drooling or burns in the mouth.
- Any swallowed medicine in a child, and any adult overdose.
- Swallowed corrosives, fuel, button batteries, magnets, pesticides, rat poison, bad alcohol, wild mushrooms or unknown substances.
- Eye pain or blurred vision after rinsing.

## When no help can come for days

- Lay drowsy people on their side and check breathing often. Give water only if they are alert and swallow normally.
- Write down what was taken, when, and every symptom.
- Watch for delayed signs: yellow skin or eyes (liver damage), bleeding, fast breathing or fever (fuel in the lungs).
- Many serious poisonings need antidotes that only hospitals have. If any danger sign appears, evacuate at any cost.
