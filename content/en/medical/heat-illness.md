> [!CAUTION]
> **Heat stroke kills within hours. Cool first, transport second.** If a hot person is confused, behaving strangely, fitting or collapsed, start cooling them **immediately,** where they are.
>
> **Never leave a baby, child, elderly person or animal in a parked car,** not even for a few minutes, not even with the windows slightly open.

## Quick steps

1. **Move them** to shade or the coolest place nearby. Remove extra clothing.
2. **Check their mind.** Ask their name, where they are and what happened. Any confusion, strange behavior, fits or collapse means **heat stroke.**
3. **Heat stroke: cool them now.** Put them in cold water up to the neck. If you cannot, soak them with water and fan them hard, without stopping.
4. **Keep cooling** until they start to shiver or their mind clears.
5. **Then get help.** Watch them closely: heat stroke can come back.
6. **Heat exhaustion (mind clear):** lie them down in the cool, raise the legs, wet and fan the skin, and give sips of cool water or ORS. They should improve within 30 minutes. If not, treat it as heat stroke.

## Which is it?

| Sign | Heat cramps | Heat exhaustion | Heat stroke |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mind | Clear | Clear, may feel faint | **Confused, strange behavior, slurred speech, fits, collapse** |
| Skin | Sweaty | Sweaty, often pale and clammy | **Hot; may be dry or still sweating** |
| Other | Painful muscle spasms | Weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, vomiting, fast pulse | Fast pulse, fast breathing, vomiting |
| Danger | Low | Can turn into heat stroke | **Life-threatening** |

**The key is the mind.** A hot person who is not thinking normally has heat stroke until proven otherwise. A sweating person can still have heat stroke, especially after hard work or exercise.

Heat stroke usually means a core temperature above **40°C (104°F).** Do not wait to measure before cooling. Armpit, mouth and forehead readings are not accurate in heat stroke.

## Heat stroke: cool first

### Best: cold water immersion

1. **Fill a bath, tank, large bin, plastic pool or a lined pit** with the coldest water you have. Add ice if you have it. A cool stream or lake also works, with people holding the person.
2. **Put the person in, up to the neck.** Keep their head above water. One person holds them under the armpits, or uses a sheet passed under the arms.
3. **Stir the water** around them.
4. **Keep them in** until they start to shiver, their mind clears, or a rectal thermometer reads about **39°C (102°F).**
5. **Take them out** so they do not get too cold. Dry them. Lay them on their side if drowsy.

**Tarp method:** if there is no tub, lay the person on a tarp or plastic sheet. Several people lift the edges to make a basin. Pour in water and ice, and rock the tarp gently to move the water over the body.

### If you cannot immerse them

- **Soak and fan:** pour water over the whole body again and again, and fan hard. Wet sheets or towels work if you rewet them every few minutes and keep fanning.
- **Ice or cold packs** to the neck, armpits and groin help, but only together with wetting the whole body. Cold packs alone are too slow.
- **Use any moving air:** a breeze, a fan, a vehicle with air conditioning, a cool room.

Cooling within **30 minutes** of collapse saves almost everyone. Do not stop cooling to transport the person unless cooling can continue on the way.

> [!WARNING]
> Paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen and aspirin **do not lower** the temperature in heat stroke, and may harm the liver or kidneys. Do not give them for heat stroke.

### After cooling

- **Give nothing by mouth** if they are confused, drowsy or vomiting. They can choke.
- If they are fully alert, give sips of cool water or oral rehydration solution (ORS). See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
- If unconscious but breathing, lay them on their side. If they stop breathing, start CPR. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
- Watch them for several hours. Their temperature can rise again. If confusion returns, cool them again.

## Heat exhaustion

1. Move to shade or a cool room. Lie down. Raise the legs if dizzy.
2. Loosen or remove extra clothing.
3. Cool the skin: wet it and fan it, or use cool, wet cloths.
4. Give **cool water or ORS** in sips. If they vomit, wait 10 minutes and try again slowly.
5. Rest for the rest of the day, out of the sun and away from hard work.

They should feel better within **30 minutes.** If they do not, or if they become confused, treat it as heat stroke.

## Heat cramps

1. Stop work. Rest in the shade.
2. Gently stretch the cramping muscle.
3. Drink ORS, or water with salty food. Cramps come from losing salt and water in sweat.

## Who is at highest risk

- **Older people,** especially over 65 and living alone.
- **Babies and young children.**
- **Outdoor workers,** soldiers, athletes and anyone doing hard work in heat.
- **Pregnant women.**
- People with **heart, lung or kidney disease, diabetes, obesity or mental illness.**
- People who are already ill with fever or diarrhea, and people who drink alcohol.
- People on **certain medicines:** water pills (diuretics), some heart and blood pressure medicines, some medicines for mental illness, Parkinson's disease, allergies or bladder problems, and stimulants. Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own, but take extra care.

Check on these people at least twice a day during a heatwave. See [Elderly, Disabled and Chronically Ill People](../community/vulnerable-people.md).

### Babies

- Babies cannot tell you they are hot. Watch for hot, red skin, fewer wet diapers, unusual sleepiness or crying, and poor feeding.
- **Breastfeed more often.** Babies under 6 months who are only breastfed do not need extra water, even in hot weather.
- Dress them lightly. Never cover a stroller or pram with a blanket: it traps heat.

### Cars and closed spaces

**A closed car in the sun can heat up by about 10°C (19°F) in the first 10 minutes,** even on a mild day. Most of the rise happens in the first 30 minutes. Opening the windows a little does not help much. Children die in cars every year, often because an adult forgot they were there. Always check the back seat when you leave a vehicle.

Closed tents, metal sheds, shipping containers and rooms under metal roofs can become just as deadly.

## Drinking in the heat

- Drink regularly, before you feel very thirsty. Pale urine means you are drinking enough.
- When sweating for hours, also eat salty food or drink ORS.
- **Do not drink huge amounts of plain water without food.** More than about **1.4 liters (1.5 quarts) an hour** for several hours can dilute the salt in the blood. This causes confusion, fits and death, and looks like heat illness.

For keeping cool and working safely in heat, see [Surviving Extreme Heat](../shelter/stay-cool.md).

## When to get help

**Heat stroke always needs medical care, after cooling has started.** Also get help or evacuate if:

- Heat exhaustion does not improve within 30 minutes of rest and cooling.
- The person cannot drink, keeps vomiting, or passes very little dark urine.
- They had a fit, collapsed or were confused at any time.
- Their urine is dark brown or cola-colored, or their muscles are very painful and weak. This can mean muscle breakdown, which damages the kidneys.
- A baby or elderly person is hot, drowsy, or not feeding or drinking.

## When no help can come for days

- After heat stroke, keep the person resting in the coolest place you have for several days. Do not let them work in the heat.
- Keep giving water and ORS while they are alert.
- Check their temperature (by touch if needed), alertness and urine every few hours.
- Heat stroke can damage the kidneys, liver and brain. Little or no urine, yellow skin or eyes, unusual bleeding, or confusion that does not clear means serious organ damage. Evacuate at any cost.
