> [!CAUTION]
> **After a snakebite, never cut the wound, suck it, tie a tourniquet, put ice on it, give electric shocks or give alcohol.** None of these remove venom, and all can cause serious harm. Keep the person still and get them to antivenom.
>
> **Rabies kills almost everyone once symptoms start.** After any bite or scratch from a dog, cat, bat, monkey or other mammal, wash the wound with soap and running water for **15 minutes** and get the vaccine as soon as possible.

## Quick steps

These steps are for snakebites. For bee and wasp stings, scrape the stinger out and watch for swelling of the face or trouble breathing.

1. **Move away** from the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it. A photo from a safe distance helps.
2. **Keep the person calm and still.** Lay them down. Movement spreads venom.
3. **Remove rings, watches, bracelets and tight clothing** from the bitten limb before it swells.
4. **Splint the limb** so it cannot bend, as for a broken bone.
5. **Carry them to the nearest hospital or clinic with antivenom.** Use a vehicle, boat, bicycle or stretcher. Do not let them walk if you can avoid it. See [Moving and Carrying the Injured](../medical/moving-injured.md).
6. **For pain, give paracetamol** (acetaminophen). Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen: they increase bleeding.

## Snakebite

Many bites inject little or no venom, but you cannot tell at first. **Treat every bite as dangerous and watch the person for at least 24 hours.**

### Signs that venom is working

- Swelling that spreads up the limb, blistering or darkening skin.
- Bleeding from the gums, nose or wound, or blood in urine or vomit.
- Drooping eyelids, double vision, trouble swallowing, weakness, trouble breathing.
- Vomiting, collapse, dark urine or little urine.

Any of these means the person needs **antivenom.** Nothing else treats venom.

### Pressure bandage: only for some snakes

A firm bandage over the whole limb slows the spread of venom. Use it only for snakes whose venom mainly paralyzes and causes **little swelling** at the bite: all dangerous snakes in **Australia and New Guinea,** and kraits, mambas, sea snakes and some cobras elsewhere.

**Do not use it for vipers** (adders, rattlesnakes, puff adders, saw-scaled vipers, Russell's vipers). It can increase tissue damage. **If the bite swells quickly, do not use it.**

1. Use a broad elastic or crepe bandage, 10-15 cm (4-6 in) wide, or strips of cloth.
2. Wrap firmly over the bite first.
3. Then wrap the whole limb, from the fingers or toes up as far as you can, as tight as for a sprained ankle. Fingers and toes must stay warm and not change color.
4. Splint the limb. Leave the bandage on until hospital.

### Venom in the eyes

Spitting cobras spray venom into the eyes. **Rinse the eyes with lots of water** or any other bland liquid for at least 15 minutes. Then get help.

### When antivenom cannot be reached

- Keep the person lying down, with the limb splinted. Give water and food if they can swallow.
- Watch breathing closely. Some venoms paralyze the breathing muscles, but **the paralysis wears off over hours to days.** People have survived when others gave them rescue breaths for many hours. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
- Keep the wound clean. Do not cut away tissue.
- Keep trying to reach antivenom. It can still help days after the bite, while signs of venom continue.

### Preventing snakebite

- Wear boots and long trousers in grass. Carry a light at night.
- **Sleep off the floor, under a tucked-in mosquito net.** Kraits bite sleeping people.
- Look before putting hands into holes, under rocks or into wood piles.
- Do not handle snakes, even dead ones. The head can still bite.

## Scorpions

Most stings are very painful but not dangerous. Some species in North Africa, the Middle East, India and the Americas can kill, mostly children.

1. Wash the sting. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for pain.
2. Give paracetamol at the package dose.
3. **Get help urgently** for sweating, drooling, vomiting, jerking movements, roving eyes, a racing heart or trouble breathing.

Shake out shoes, clothes and bedding before use.

## Spiders

- **Widow spiders** (worldwide): spreading muscle pain, cramps, a stiff belly and sweating. Very painful, rarely deadly. Use cold packs and pain medicine. Get help for severe pain or weakness.
- **Recluse spiders** (mainly the Americas): painless at first, then over days a sore may form and break down. Clean it, cool it, raise the limb and see a health worker.
- **Funnel-web spiders** (eastern Australia): can kill quickly. Use the pressure bandage above and go to hospital.

Many "spider bites" are really skin infections. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).

## Bees, wasps and hornets

1. **Get the stinger out fast.** Scrape it or pull it: speed matters more than method.
2. Wash the area and apply a cold pack.
3. For itching or swelling, take an antihistamine at the package dose.
4. **Watch for anaphylaxis** for at least 1 hour: trouble breathing, swelling of the face, lips or throat, hives all over, dizziness or collapse. Use an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector if there is one. See [Severe Allergic Reactions (Anaphylaxis)](../medical/allergic-reactions.md).

**Dozens of stings** can poison a person even without allergy. If a swarm attacks, cover your face and run to a building or vehicle.

## Jellyfish and sea creatures

1. Get out of the water. Rescuers should avoid touching tentacles.
2. **Box jellyfish** (tropical Indo-Pacific and northern Australia): **pour vinegar** on the sting for at least 30 seconds. This stops stinging cells that have not fired yet. Then remove the tentacles. Be ready to do CPR.
3. **Other jellyfish:** rinse with seawater, not fresh water. Pick off tentacles with gloves, tweezers or a card.
4. **For pain, put the area in hot water at about 45°C (113°F)** for 20 minutes, or as hot as an uninjured hand can stand without scalding. This works for many jellyfish stings.
5. **Stingrays, stonefish, lionfish and sea urchins:** hot water also eases the pain. These wounds may hold spines and often get infected.

**Do not rub with sand, urinate on a sting, or pour alcohol on it.**

## Ticks

1. **Use fine-pointed tweezers.** Grasp the tick as close to the skin as you can.
2. **Pull straight out** with steady, even pressure. Do not twist or squeeze the body.
3. Wash the skin and your hands with soap. Do not burn the tick or cover it with oil or nail polish.

Check bodies and clothes every day in tick areas. Lyme disease usually needs the tick attached for 36-48 hours, so early removal helps. **Watch for fever, a spreading rash, or a red ring around the bite over the next 30 days.** These need a health worker. See [Living in a Forest](../land/forest-living.md).

## Mosquitoes

Mosquitoes spread malaria, dengue, Zika, chikungunya, yellow fever and other diseases.

- **Sleep under an insecticide-treated bed net,** tucked in. This is the best protection against malaria.
- Use repellent with DEET, picaridin (icaridin), IR3535 or oil of lemon eucalyptus. Follow the label, especially for children.
- Wear long sleeves and trousers.
- **Empty standing water every week:** buckets, tires, pots. Dengue mosquitoes breed in small containers and bite during the day. See [Living in a Tropical Rainforest](../land/jungle-living.md).
- **Fever in a malaria area needs a test and treatment within 24 hours.** With possible dengue, use paracetamol, not aspirin or ibuprofen.

## Dog, cat, monkey, bat and other animal bites

1. **Wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes.** This is the most important step against rabies.
2. Apply an antiseptic such as povidone-iodine or alcohol, if you have it.
3. **Get rabies vaccine as soon as possible** after any bite, scratch, or lick on broken skin from a mammal that could carry rabies, and after any contact with a bat. It works only if given before symptoms start.
4. Do not close the wound tightly. Bites get infected easily. See [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md).
5. If possible, confine a dog or cat that bit someone and watch it for 10 days. Do not wait for this to start the vaccine.

Human bites also get infected easily.

## When to get help

- Any snakebite with signs that venom is working.
- Any sign of anaphylaxis, or dozens of stings.
- Any mammal bite or scratch where rabies exists, and any bat contact.
- Scorpion stings in small children, and box jellyfish stings.
- Fever or rash after a tick bite, or any fever in a malaria area.
