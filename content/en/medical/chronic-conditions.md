> [!CAUTION]
> **Never stop insulin in type 1 diabetes,** even if the person cannot eat. Without it they can die within days.
>
> **Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes may be a heart attack.** Stop all activity and get help.
>
> **A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes is an emergency.** Never put anything in the mouth during a seizure.
>
> **Do not suddenly stop epilepsy, steroid, heart or mental health medicines.** See [Withdrawal and Running Out of Medicines](../medical/withdrawal.md).

## Quick steps

1. **Keep taking every regular medicine** as prescribed. Get more before it runs out.
2. **Keep insulin below 30°C (86°F).** Never let it freeze.
3. **Low blood sugar** (shaky, sweaty, confused): give 15-20 g of fast sugar, wait 15 minutes, repeat if needed, then food.
4. **Asthma attack:** sit upright, use the reliever with a spacer.
5. **Seizure:** time it, protect the head, nothing in the mouth, then the recovery position.

## Diabetes

### Keeping insulin without a fridge

- Below 30°C (86°F), most insulin lasts about **28 days** out of the fridge, opened or not. Some types differ: check the leaflet. Write the date on it.
- **Never freeze it.** Keep it shaded, never in sun or a closed car.
- **Clay pot cooler:** stand an unglazed clay pot inside a bigger one, fill the gap with wet sand, and cover with a wet cloth. Put the insulin, in a sealed bag, in the small pot. Keep it in shade with moving air. Re-wet the sand twice daily. It works best in dry air.
- Warm insulin often still works: in a 2021 study, common insulins kept their strength for 4 weeks at 25-37°C (77-99°F). Use it, and check sugar more often.

### Type 1: insulin every day

Without insulin, the body makes acids (diabetic ketoacidosis, DKA). Signs, over hours to 2 days: great thirst, lots of urine, vomiting, belly pain, **deep fast breathing, fruity-smelling breath,** drowsiness, confusion. **This kills. Evacuate at any cost,** giving insulin and sips of water on the way.

### Sick-day rules

1. **Keep taking insulin,** even without food. Never stop it.
2. **Check sugar every 2-4 hours,** and ketones if you have strips.
3. **Drink small amounts often,** about 3 liters (3 quarts) a day.
4. **If you cannot eat,** sip sugar in fluids: milk, juice or sweet drinks.
5. **Ask a health worker now** which tablets to pause during vomiting or dehydration. Metformin, "-gliflozin" tablets and some blood pressure tablets often are.

### Low blood sugar

Insulin and some tablets (gliclazide, glibenclamide (glyburide), glimepiride) cause it, especially with missed meals. If they can swallow, give **15-20 g of fast sugar:** 4 teaspoons of sugar in water, or 150-200 mL (5-7 fl oz) of juice. Wait 15 minutes and repeat if needed, then give starchy food. If they cannot swallow, put nothing in the mouth. Details: [Elderly, Disabled and Chronically Ill People](../community/vulnerable-people.md).

### Food and feet

- Never skip meals after insulin or those tablets. If food is short, ask a health worker to adjust doses. Carry sugar. Avoid alcohol.
- **Very high sugar for days** (great thirst, weakness, confusion) is an emergency.
- **Check feet daily.** Always wear shoes. Keep numb feet away from fires. Get help for a wound with spreading redness, pus or black skin.

## Asthma and COPD

### Inhaler and spacer

1. Remove the cap, shake the inhaler and fit it into the spacer.
2. Breathe out, then seal lips around the mouthpiece.
3. Press once. Breathe in and out slowly **5 times.** Repeat for each puff.

**Bottle spacer:** cut a hole in the bottom of a 500 mL (17 fl oz) plastic bottle to fit the inhaler mouthpiece. Seal gaps with tape. Breathe through the bottle neck. In a 1999 trial in children with asthma attacks, it worked as well as a factory spacer.

**Triggers:** smoke from fires, stoves, cigarettes and wildfires; cold air (cover nose and mouth); dust and mold. Keep using the daily preventer inhaler.

### Severe attack

Signs: reliever not helping, cannot speak full sentences, neck and rib muscles pulling, blue or grey lips, drowsiness.

1. **Sit them upright,** leaning slightly forward. Never lay them down.
2. Give the reliever (often blue) through a spacer, as their asthma plan or package says. In a life-threatening attack, sharing one is acceptable.
3. Get help. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).

**COPD:** breathe out slowly through pursed lips, sitting forward.

## Heart disease and blood pressure

### Heart attack

Signs: chest pain, pressure or tightness lasting more than a few minutes, often spreading to the arm, jaw or back; sweating, breathlessness, nausea. Women, older people and people with diabetes may only feel breathless, sick or tired.

1. **Stop all activity.** Sit them half-upright. Do not let them walk.
2. **Get help or evacuate.**
3. First aid guidelines advise an adult to **chew 1 regular aspirin tablet** (about 300 mg), unless allergic to aspirin, told never to take it, or showing stroke signs. Never give it to children.
4. Help them take their own nitroglycerin (glyceryl trinitrate) as prescribed.
5. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR.

With no help for days, rest for days, then slowly do more.

### Heart failure

Warning signs: more breathless, needing more pillows, swollen ankles, weight gain over 2 kg (4 1/2 lb) in 3 days. Sleep propped up, cut salt, keep taking water pills. **Breathless at rest or coughing pink froth is an emergency.**

### Blood pressure and stroke

- High blood pressure has no signs. Keep taking the tablets.
- Eat less than **5 g of salt a day** (1 level teaspoon). Canned food, instant noodles and stock cubes are very salty.
- **Stroke signs:** face drooping, a weak arm, slurred speech. Note the time and evacuate. Give no aspirin, and no food or drink unless they swallow normally.

## Epilepsy

### Seizure first aid

1. **Time it.**
2. Move them away from fire, water and hard edges. Cushion the head.
3. **Do not hold them down. Put nothing in their mouth.** They cannot swallow their tongue.
4. When the jerking stops, use the recovery position and check breathing. See [First Aid Basics: Check, Call, Care](../medical/first-aid-basics.md).
5. Stay until they are fully awake.

**Emergency:** longer than **5 minutes,** another seizure before waking, breathing problems, injury, a first seizure, pregnancy, or diabetes (check sugar). Give a prescribed rescue medicine only as their plan says.

### Missed medicine

In people with epilepsy, missed doses are the commonest cause of seizures. Stopping suddenly can cause seizures that do not stop. Get more early: aid health posts often carry phenobarbital (phenobarbitone) or carbamazepine. Switching medicines needs a health worker. Sleep enough, avoid alcohol, never swim alone, and cook away from open fire.

## Kidney disease and dialysis

Missed dialysis lets water, salt, potassium and waste build up. **High potassium can stop the heart without warning.** Tell rescuers early that you need dialysis.

Until dialysis, follow a strict emergency diet, like the US Kidney Community Emergency Response (KCER) diet:

- **Fluids:** only the emergency amount your kidney team gave you, much less than usual. Soup and fruit count.
- **Salt:** none added. Avoid canned, salted and pickled food.
- **Potassium:** avoid bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, dried fruit, beans, nuts, coconut water and salt substitutes.
- **Protein:** smaller portions of meat, fish and eggs.

## Other long-term medicines

- **Thyroid tablets (levothyroxine):** missing a few days is not dangerous.
- **Steroid tablets taken for more than a few weeks** (prednisolone, prednisone, hydrocortisone, dexamethasone): never stop suddenly. Illness raises the need: ask now for a sick-day plan. **Adrenal crisis** (severe weakness, fainting, vomiting, confusion) needs an injection: evacuate.
- **Mental health medicines:** do not stop suddenly. **Lithium** becomes poisonous with dehydration: worse shaking, vomiting, unsteadiness, confusion. Get help urgently.

## Make a medicine plan now

1. **List** every medicine: generic name, strength, dose, times and purpose.
2. **Keep 1-3 months** of supply if you can.
3. **Ask your doctor** which medicines are dangerous to stop, your sick-day rules, and for a written action plan.
4. **Keep spares:** inhaler, spacer, test strips, sugar.

See [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).

## Evacuate if

- Diabetes: DKA signs, very high sugar with confusion, or low sugar not improving after 2 treatments.
- Chest pain lasting more than a few minutes, or stroke signs.
- Severe asthma not improving, blue lips or drowsiness.
- A seizure over 5 minutes, repeated seizures, or not waking up.
- Missed dialysis with weakness, breathlessness or an irregular pulse.
- Heart failure with breathlessness at rest, or adrenal crisis signs.
