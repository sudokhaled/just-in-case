> [!CAUTION]
> **If you smell gas or hear hissing, get everyone out now.** Do not light a flame, do not switch lights or appliances on or off, and do not use a phone until you are outside and away from the building.
>
> **Never run a generator, grill, charcoal or camp stove indoors**, in a garage or near an open window. Carbon monoxide kills whole families in their sleep after disasters.
>
> **Do not walk or drive into floodwater.** 15 cm (6 in) of moving water can knock you down. 30 cm (1 ft) can float a car.

## Quick steps

1. **Protect yourself** from the danger happening now.
2. **Check for injuries**, yourself first, then others. Stop heavy bleeding first.
3. **Check for fire, gas, damaged wiring and building damage.** Leave if the building is unsafe.
4. **Decide: stay or go.** Follow official orders if you have them.
5. **Send one short text** to family and to your out-of-area contact.
6. **Save water** while you still can: fill containers, close the main water valve if pipes are broken.
7. **Get information** from a battery, crank or car radio.
8. **Save phone battery** for important messages.

## First minutes: stay alive

- **Earthquake:** drop to the ground, cover your head and neck under a strong table, and hold on until the shaking stops. Then leave if the building is damaged.
- **Near the coast after a strong or long earthquake:** go to high ground or inland at once. Do not wait for a warning. A tsunami can arrive within minutes.
- **Fire:** get out, stay low under smoke, and do not go back in.
- **Flood:** go to higher ground, or to an upper floor or roof if you are trapped.
- **Trapped under rubble:** cover your mouth with cloth. Tap on a pipe or wall, or use a whistle, so rescuers can hear you. Shout only when you hear people close by, because shouting makes you breathe in dust.

Put on sturdy shoes and gloves before you move around. Broken glass causes many injuries.

### Injuries

1. Check yourself. People in shock often do not feel their injuries.
2. Check others. Treat the worst first: not breathing, heavy bleeding, unconscious.
3. Press hard on any heavy bleeding. See [Stopping Severe Bleeding](../medical/severe-bleeding.md).
4. Do not move someone who may have a neck or back injury, unless fire, water or collapse threatens them.

## First hour: make it safe

### Utilities

| Utility | Turn it off when | How |
|---|---|---|
| Gas | You smell gas, hear hissing, or see a broken gas pipe | At the meter: turn the valve a quarter turn with a wrench, so it lies across the pipe |
| Electricity | Wires spark or are broken, you smell hot insulation, or water has reached sockets or the fuse box | Switch off each circuit, then the main switch. **Never** stand in water to do it |
| Water | Pipes are broken, or the town supply may be dirty (flood, earthquake) | Close the main valve where the pipe enters the building |

- Do not turn off the gas "just in case". Once off, a trained person must turn it back on and relight every pilot flame. After a big disaster, this can take weeks.
- Closing the main water valve also keeps clean water trapped in your pipes and water heater. You can drink it later. See [Finding Water](../water/find-water.md).

### Stay or go

**Leave if:**

- the building is badly cracked, leaning, or has fallen in part;
- there is fire, or a gas leak you cannot stop;
- water is rising, or the ground is sliding;
- officials order you to evacuate;
- you are in a tsunami zone after a strong earthquake.

**Stay inside (shelter in place) if:**

- officials tell you to;
- there is a chemical cloud outside: close windows and doors, turn off fans and air conditioning, and go to an inside room above ground level;
- there is a radiation emergency: go to the middle of a solid building or a basement and stay at least 24 hours unless told otherwise;
- a storm or blizzard is still raging, roads are blocked, or there is violence outside.

**If you leave:** take your emergency bag, medicines, documents, water, phone and charger. Leave a note on the door with names, the date and time, and where you are going. Go by a route you know is safe.

## First day

### Water

- **Do not ration drinking water.** Drink what you need today, at least 2 to 3 liters (2 to 3 quarts), and look for more tomorrow.
- If taps still run, fill every clean container now. Treat it before drinking if the supply may be dirty.
- Find hidden water: water heater tank, pipes, the toilet tank (cistern) but never the bowl, and ice cubes.
- Treat all doubtful water. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

### Food

- A closed fridge keeps food safe for about **4 hours**. A full freezer keeps food frozen for about **48 hours** (24 hours if half full), if you keep the door closed.
- Eat food from the fridge first, then the freezer, then cans and dry food.
- Throw away any food that touched floodwater. Only undamaged metal cans can be kept: remove the labels, then wash and disinfect the cans before opening.

### Toilets

If water or sewer pipes are broken, do not flush. Line the toilet or a bucket with a strong plastic bag and use that. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).

### Family and neighbors

- Go to the meeting places in your family plan. See [Family Emergency Plan](../reference/family-plan.md).
- Contact one relative in a different region. Everyone in the family reports to that person.
- Write your name and a phone number on a card in each child's pocket, or on their arm with a pen.
- Check on neighbors, especially old people, disabled people, people living alone and families with babies.
- Do not search damaged buildings alone. See [Finding and Freeing Trapped People](../navigation/search-rescue.md).

### Information

- Listen to official radio broadcasts at set times. Write down important details: shelters, water points, medical help, curfews.
- Do not trust or pass on rumors. Ask: who said this, and how do they know?

### Phone battery

- **Text, do not call.** Texts often get through when networks are too busy for calls.
- Send one message with all the facts: "All 4 of us OK. House damaged. Going to the school shelter at 3 pm."
- Turn on battery-saver mode. Turn the screen down. Turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and location when you do not need them.
- With no signal, the phone wastes battery searching. Use airplane mode, and turn it on only at set times to check messages.
- In cold weather, keep the phone in an inside pocket. Cold drains batteries.
- Charge from a car only with the car outside in the open air, never in a garage.

### Tonight

- Use flashlights, not candles. If you must use a candle, put it on a plate, away from curtains, and never leave it burning alone.
- Keep any generator outside, at least 6 m (20 ft) from doors and windows. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
- Take turns staying awake if you need to watch for fire, rising water or aftershocks. Everyone else sleeps.

## Days 2 and 3

- **Make a routine.** Fixed times for meals, water collection, cleaning and sleep help everyone cope.
- **Wash hands** with soap and water after the toilet and before handling food.
- **Clean and cover every wound.** Watch for redness that spreads, pus, swelling or fever.
- **Clean up safely.** Wear boots, gloves and long sleeves. Do not use chainsaws or climb ladders when you are exhausted. Stay out of damaged buildings.
- **Count medicines.** Check how many days of medicine everyone has left. Ask for help early, not on the last day.
- **Decide again each day: stay or go.** Moving to an official shelter or to relatives may be safer.
- **Make yourself visible** if you are cut off. Put a large "HELP" sign or bright cloth on the roof or in an open space.
- **Work with neighbors.** Share tools and water, cook together, watch children in turns.
- **Expect strong feelings.** Fear, crying, anger and trouble sleeping are normal. See [Survival Mindset: Controlling Fear and Stress](../start/survival-mindset.md).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Help may take longer than 72 hours, especially after big disasters or in remote places. Plan as if you must manage alone for 2 weeks.
