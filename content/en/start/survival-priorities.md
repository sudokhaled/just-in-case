> [!CAUTION]
> **Deal with the fastest killer first.** Blocked breathing, severe bleeding, fire, smoke and gas kill in minutes. Lack of water kills in days. Lack of food kills in weeks. Do not go looking for food while someone is bleeding or while you are cold and wet.
>
> **Many deaths come after the disaster itself:** carbon monoxide from generators and grills used indoors, diarrhea from dirty water, driving into floodwater, electric shock from fallen wires, and cold or heat.

## Quick steps

1. **Get out of immediate danger.** Move away from fire, smoke, gas smell, rising water, damaged buildings, fallen power lines and traffic. Protect yourself first. An injured rescuer saves nobody.
2. **Check breathing and bleeding.** If someone is not breathing normally, start CPR. If blood is spurting or pooling, press hard on the wound and keep pressing.
3. **Protect from cold, heat and wet.** Get out of wind and rain, or into shade. Change into dry clothes. Get off cold ground.
4. **Get safe water.** Drink enough. Treat all water that is not from a sealed bottle.
5. **Let people know where you are.** Signal, text, call or leave a note.
6. **Food comes last.** Most healthy adults can live for weeks without food.
7. **Stop and plan.** Rest, think, and plan the next 24 hours.

## The rule of 3s

| Without | You can die in about | So first |
|---|---|---|
| Air, or with severe bleeding | 3 minutes | Open the airway, stop the bleeding |
| Shelter in harsh weather (cold, wet, wind, strong heat) | 3 hours | Get warm and dry, or cool and shaded |
| Water | 3 days | Find water and make it safe |
| Food | 3 weeks | Ration food, find more later |

These numbers are a memory aid, not a promise. Their value is that they tell you the **order** to act in.

### Where the rule is wrong

- **Bleeding can be faster.** A person can bleed to death from a large wound in less than 5 minutes.
- **Cold water kills in minutes, not hours.** Sudden cold water makes you gasp and breathe fast for about 1 minute. Float on your back and calm your breathing first. Within about 10 minutes your hands and arms stop working well, so get out or onto something that floats as fast as you can. See [Water and Ice Rescue](../navigation/water-rescue.md).
- **Wet and wind are worse than cold.** Hypothermia can happen above 4°C (40°F) if you are wet from rain, sweat or water.
- **Heat cuts the water time.** In desert heat with no water, a person may live only 1 to 2 days, and less if they walk in the sun.
- **Some people have much less time.** Babies, small children, old people, and sick or injured people suffer from heat, cold, thirst and hunger sooner.
- **Medicines can matter within days.** People who depend on insulin, dialysis, oxygen, or medicines for epilepsy or the heart are in danger quickly if supply stops. See [Diabetes, Asthma, Heart Disease, Epilepsy Without Supplies](../medical/chronic-conditions.md).
- **Food can last longer.** Many healthy adults survive more than 3 weeks without food if they have water and stay warm.

## What to do, in order

### 1. Immediate danger (seconds)

- **Fire or smoke:** get out, stay low under the smoke, close doors behind you. Do not go back in.
- **Gas smell or hissing:** leave. Do not light flames or switch lights on or off.
- **Rising water:** go to higher ground. Never walk or drive through moving water. 15 cm (6 in) of moving water can knock you down. 30 cm (1 ft) can carry away a car.
- **Fallen power lines:** stay at least 10 m (33 ft) away. Water or metal touching a wire can also carry current.
- **Damaged buildings:** after shaking stops, get out and stay out. Expect aftershocks.

### 2. Breathing and bleeding (minutes)

- Not breathing normally: start CPR. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
- Heavy bleeding: press hard on the wound with cloth or your hand. Do not lift it to look. See [Stopping Severe Bleeding](../medical/severe-bleeding.md).
- Unconscious but breathing: roll them onto their side so vomit can drain.

### 3. Body temperature (hours)

- **Cold:** get out of wind and wet. Put a layer between your body and the ground, because the ground pulls heat away fast. Cover your head and neck. Share body heat. See [Staying Warm in the Cold](../shelter/stay-warm.md).
- **Heat:** stay in shade. Rest during the hottest hours. Wet your skin and fan it. Someone who is hot and confused may have heat stroke: cool them at once with water.

### 4. Water (days)

- Adults need at least 2.5 to 3 liters (about 3 quarts) a day for drinking and food, and more in heat.
- Do not ration drinking water while you are thirsty in the heat. Drink what you need today and look for more.
- Treat all doubtful water. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

### 5. Getting found and staying together

- Rescue often comes faster than you can survive alone. Stay where people will look for you, unless that place is unsafe.
- Keep the group together. Agree on a meeting place if anyone must leave.

### 6. Food (weeks)

- Eat food that spoils first. Share fairly.
- Do not eat wild mushrooms or plants you do not know.

## Deadly threats people forget

- **Carbon monoxide.** Generators, charcoal, grills, camp stoves, gas heaters and running cars in closed spaces kill whole families while they sleep. You cannot see or smell it. Keep generators at least 6 m (20 ft) from doors, windows and vents. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
- **Dirty water.** After floods and earthquakes, diarrhea and cholera spread fast and kill young children most. Treat all water. Start oral rehydration at the first watery stool. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
- **Injuries during cleanup.** Falls from roofs and ladders, chainsaws, lifting, nails and broken glass injure many people in the days after a disaster. Wear boots and gloves. Clean every cut, because small wounds can become infected.
- **Exposure.** Wet clothes, wind and cold floors cause hypothermia, even indoors in an unheated house. Old people living alone are at most risk from both cold and heat. Check on them.
- **Floodwater.** It hides deep holes, carries sewage and can be electrified. More than half of flood drownings happen in vehicles driven into water.
- **Fire.** Candles, cooking fires and heaters cause house fires when people are tired. Use flashlights if you can. Never leave a flame alone.
- **Panic and exhaustion.** Tired, frightened people make deadly mistakes. Stop, breathe and think before big decisions.

## Common mistakes

- Looking for food before shelter and water.
- Leaving a safe shelter at night or in a storm.
- Splitting up the group without a plan to meet again.
- Drinking alcohol "to keep warm". It makes your body lose heat faster.
- Saving water while already thirsty in the heat. Save sweat instead: rest in shade and move in the cool hours.
