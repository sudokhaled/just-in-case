> [!CAUTION]
> **Moldy grain and nuts can kill.** Molds on maize (corn), groundnuts (peanuts), sorghum and other crops make aflatoxin. It is invisible, has no taste, and cooking does not destroy it. Large amounts cause acute liver poisoning; small amounts over years cause liver cancer. In Kenya in 2004, moldy home-grown maize killed 125 people.
>
> **Dry first, then store.** Damp grain, beans and nuts mold within days, even in sealed bags.
>
> **Never mix crop pesticides, fuel or mothballs into food grain,** and never store food in containers that held them.

## Quick steps

1. **Harvest when ripe** (see the table), in dry weather.
2. **Dry grain, beans and nuts fast,** within days: on tarps, mats or racks, never on bare soil. Cover them at night and in rain.
3. **Thresh, winnow and sort.** Throw out every moldy, shriveled, discolored, broken or insect-holed grain.
4. **Test dryness** with the bite and salt tests.
5. **Cure** potatoes, sweet potatoes, squash, onions and garlic before storing.
6. **Store airtight,** off the ground, away from walls, safe from rats.
7. **Check stores every month.**

## When to harvest

| Crop | Ready when | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Maize (dry grain) | Husks brown and dry; kernels hard; a black spot forms where the kernel joins the cob | In wet weather, pick the ears, husk them and finish drying under a roof |
| Sorghum, millet | Grain too hard to dent with a fingernail | Birds strike fast: harvest promptly |
| Wheat, barley, rice | Stems and heads golden; grain hard | Cut in dry weather, after the dew has dried |
| Dry beans, cowpeas, peas | Most pods dry and papery; seeds rattle | Pull plants when two-thirds of pods are dry |
| Groundnuts | Leaves yellowing; inside of most shells dark; kernels fill the shell | Dig 2 or 3 plants to check first |
| Potatoes | 10 days to 3 weeks after the tops die; skin does not rub off with a thumb | Dig on a dry day; keep tubers out of the sun |
| Sweet potatoes | 3 to 5 months; before soil cools below 13°C (55°F) | Dig gently: cuts and bruises rot |
| Winter squash, pumpkins | Rind too hard to dent with a fingernail; stem dry and corky | Cut with 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) of stem; harvest before frost |
| Onions | Half or more of the tops have fallen over | Lift them about 1 to 2 weeks later, in dry weather |
| Garlic | Lower third to half of the leaves brown | Do not wait until all leaves die: bulbs split |
| Cassava | 8 to 24 months | Roots spoil 2 to 3 days after digging: dig only what you can eat or process |

## Grain: dry, thresh and winnow

1. **Dry the crop.** Stand cut bundles upright, heads up, or spread heads and cobs on tarps, mats, a swept floor or raised racks. Turn them several times a day. Store maize on the cob in a narrow, roofed, airy crib on legs.
2. **Thresh.** Beat heads in a sack or on a tarp with a stick, walk on them, or beat bundles of rice against a board. Shell maize by twisting 2 cobs against each other.
3. **Winnow.** Pour the grain slowly from shoulder height in a steady light breeze onto a tarp. The light chaff blows away. Repeat 2 or 3 times.
4. **Sort.** Spread the grain thinly and pick out moldy, discolored, shriveled, broken and insect-damaged grains. This removes much of the aflatoxin.
5. **Dry again** until the grain passes the tests below.

### Is it dry enough?

Grain and beans must be dry before storage. These are the moisture levels for long storage in warm climates:

| Crop | Safe moisture |
|---|---|
| Maize, wheat, sorghum, millet, milled rice | 13% or less |
| Paddy (rice in the husk) | 14% or less |
| Beans, cowpeas | 15% or less; below 13% if you will plant them |
| Groundnuts (shelled) | 7% or less |

You cannot measure this without a meter. Use these tests. They are approximate.

- **Bite test:** dry maize, wheat and beans crack sharply between the teeth. Damp grain dents or feels chewy.
- **Sound test:** dry grain pours freely with a hard, rattling sound. Damp grain sticks and sounds dull.
- **Salt test:**
   1. Dry 2 to 3 tablespoons of salt in a hot pan, and let it cool.
   2. Fill a dry glass jar or clear bottle one-third full of grain. Add the salt and close it.
   3. Shake for 1 to 2 minutes. Wait at least 15 minutes, better a few hours, then shake again.
   4. If salt sticks to the glass or forms lumps, the grain is above about 15% moisture: **too wet.**

The salt test only catches grain that is clearly too wet. Grain that passes may still be above 13%. As a rule of thumb, dry it 1 or 2 more sunny days before long storage.

## Beans, cowpeas and groundnuts

- Pull bean plants when most pods are dry and finish drying them under a roof. Thresh by beating pods in a sack.
- Cowpea weevils (bruchids) lay eggs in the field. Store cowpeas airtight as soon as they are dry, or heat them in the sun first (see below).
- **Groundnuts mold fast.** Dry them in the pod within days: stack plants with the pods up and outward, or pick the pods and spread them on tarps. Never leave them in big piles on the ground. They are dry when the kernels rattle in the shell. Store them in the shell, and sort out shriveled, discolored or moldy nuts before eating.

## Curing roots, squash and bulbs

Curing heals skin wounds and thickens the skin, so the crop keeps for months. Do not wash crops before curing. Brush off soil.

| Crop | Cure at | For | Then store at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | 10 to 20°C (50 to 68°F), dark, damp air | 1 to 2 weeks; up to 3 when cool | 4 to 7°C (40 to 45°F), dark, damp |
| Sweet potatoes | 27 to 32°C (80 to 90°F), damp air | 4 to 7 days; up to 10 if badly skinned | 13 to 16°C (55 to 60°F); **never below 10°C (50°F)** |
| Winter squash, pumpkins | 27 to 29°C (80 to 85°F), airy | About 10 days | 10 to 13°C (50 to 55°F), dry |
| Onions | Warm, dry, airy shade | 2 to 3 weeks, until necks are dry and tight and skins rustle | 0 to 4°C (32 to 40°F), dry; or airy shade |
| Garlic | Warm, dry, airy shade | 2 weeks or more | Cool, dry, airy; never sealed in plastic |
| Yams | 32 to 40°C (90 to 104°F), damp air | 1 to 4 days | Shaded, airy yam barn |

- **No curing room:** in the tropics, outdoor heat is often right for sweet potatoes and yams. Pile them in shade and cover with dry grass and sacks to hold in damp air: about 4 days for yams, 4 to 7 for sweet potatoes. Cure potatoes in a dark shed under sacks.
- **Acorn squash** keeps worse if cured: store it straight away.
- Store only sound, cured crops. Use cut or bruised ones first.

For root cellars and clamps, see [Preserving Food Without Power](../food/food-preservation.md).

## Stopping insects

Weevils, bruchids and grain moths can destroy a third or more of a grain store. Start with a clean store: sweep out old grain and dust, and never mix new grain with old, infested grain.

### Airtight (hermetic) storage

Insects in a sealed container use up the oxygen, then stop feeding and die. No chemicals are needed. This works for grain, beans, groundnuts and seed.

- **Use:** triple-layer bags such as PICS bags (2 thick plastic liners inside a woven sack), sealed metal or food-grade plastic drums, or airtight jars.
- **Grain must be dry.** Wet grain molds in a sealed container.
- **Bags:** check the liners for holes. Fill the inner liner while shaking gently, so no air pockets remain and no grain falls between the liners. Twist the top of the inner liner, fold it over and tie it. Then tie the second liner, then the outer sack: **each one separately.**
- **Improvised:** 2 or 3 strong, unholed plastic bags, one inside the other, each tied separately.
- Fill containers full. Keep them on a platform, away from walls and out of the sun. Check them monthly.
- If you open one, take what you need and reseal it at once. PICS bags can keep grain 1 to 2 years and be reused for about 3 seasons.

### Other methods

- **Sun heating (beans, cowpeas):** lay black plastic or dark cloth on the ground. Spread the beans 1 layer deep. Cover with clear plastic and weigh down the edges with stones. Leave in full midday sun for **at least 2 hours.** The beans get too hot to hold, about 60°C (140°F) or more, which kills eggs and grubs inside. Germination and cooking are not harmed. Let them cool, then seal them airtight at once.
- **Ash or sand (traditional):** mix grain or beans with fine, dry, sieved wood ash or fine sand until it fills the spaces between grains, and cover the top with a layer about 2 cm (1 in) thick. This makes it hard for insects to move and lay eggs. It works best in small, closed pots. A thin dusting does little, and protection fails once the layer is disturbed. Wash the grain before cooking.
- **Cold:** most storage insects stop breeding in cold grain, below about 15°C (60°F). In cold climates, keep grain in an unheated store. With a working freezer, freeze it at -18°C (0°F) for 3 days.
- **A few weevils:** sieve them out and spread the grain in the sun. They are not dangerous to eat.

## Stopping rats and mice

Rats eat stored food and foul it with urine that spreads disease (leptospirosis).

- Store food in metal, glass or thick containers with tight lids. Rats chew through sacks and thin plastic.
- Raise stores on legs about 1 m (3 ft) high. Fit a smooth metal collar or an upside-down tin on each leg.
- Keep stores 1 m (3 ft) from walls, trees and anything rats can jump from.
- Clear spilled grain, rubbish and long grass around the store. Use traps and cats.

## Preventing mold and aflatoxin

Aflatoxin molds grow on maize, groundnuts, sorghum, rice, chilies and tree nuts. They start in drought-stressed or insect-damaged crops in the field, and grow fast in grain that dries slowly or gets damp in storage.

1. Harvest as soon as the crop is mature. Do not leave it in the field in rain.
2. Dry it **within a few days,** never on bare soil.
3. Avoid damaging grain. Cracked and insect-holed grains mold first.
4. **Sort:** remove every moldy, discolored, shriveled or damaged grain or nut.
5. Store it dry, cool and free of insects. Do not keep it longer than you must.
6. Smell stores monthly. A musty smell means damp: dry and sort the grain again at once.

- **Do not eat moldy grain or nuts.** Cooking, roasting and brewing do not make them safe.
- **Do not feed moldy grain to animals.** They get sick, and the poison passes into their milk.
- **Signs of aflatoxin poisoning:** yellow skin or eyes, vomiting, belly pain and swelling after eating moldy grain. Stop eating that food and get medical help.

## Common mistakes

- Drying grain or groundnuts on bare soil.
- Storing grain warm and damp straight from the field.
- Sealing grain that is not fully dry.
- Leaving bags in the sun, where damp gathers inside.
- Mixing new grain with old infested grain.
