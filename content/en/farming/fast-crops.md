> [!CAUTION]
> **Raw sprouts can carry Salmonella and E. coli.** Cook sprouts until steaming hot for children, pregnant women, older people and anyone who is sick or has a weak immune system. Use only seed sold for eating or sprouting. **Never sprout planting seed with a colored coating:** it is poisoned. Do not eat raw kidney bean sprouts.
>
> **Greens alone will not keep you alive.** They give vitamins but almost no calories. Plant calorie crops at the same time as your fast crops.
>
> **Never eat potato, tomato or rhubarb leaves, potato berries, or potatoes that have turned green.**

## Quick steps

1. **Today:** start a jar of sprouts. You can eat them in 2 to 6 days.
2. **This week:** sow microgreens in a tray on a bright windowsill. Harvest in 7 to 21 days.
3. **Sow fast greens and radishes outdoors.** In cool weather: radish, mustard, pak choi, lettuce, spinach, turnips. In hot weather: amaranth, cowpeas for leaves, sweet potato vines.
4. **In the warm season, sow bush beans, zucchini, cucumbers and okra.** First pick in 6 to 9 weeks.
5. **Where it is cool, plant early potatoes.** Dig the first small ones after 2 to 3 months.
6. **Plant calorie crops now, too.** See [Calorie Crops That Feed a Family for a Year](../farming/staple-crops.md).
7. **Sow again every 2 to 3 weeks** so the supply never stops.

## The fast food ladder

Days are counted from sowing (or planting) to the first harvest. Cool weather slows everything; warm weather speeds it up. Varieties differ.

| Crop | Days to first harvest | Climate or season | What you eat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sprouts (mung bean, lentil, chickpea, alfalfa, radish, wheat) | 2-6 (most 3-5) | Any, indoors | Whole sprout | No soil, no sun. See the steps below |
| Microgreens (radish, mustard, cabbage, pea, sunflower, amaranth) | 7-21 | Any, on a bright windowsill | Seedling leaves and stems | Cut when the first true leaves appear |
| Pea shoots | 10-20 | Cool, or indoors | Tips 5-10 cm (2-4 in) long | Leave some leaves; regrows 2 or 3 times |
| Amaranth greens (callaloo, bayam, chaulai) | 20-45 (most 3-5 weeks) | Warm to hot, tropics | Leaves and tender tips, cooked | Cut weekly; up to 10 cuts |
| Cowpea leaves | 21-28 | Hot, survives drought | Young leaves, cooked | Picking many leaves cuts the bean harvest |
| Radish (small types) | 25-35 | Cool | Root; leaves cooked | Turns woody and hot in heat |
| Mustard greens | 25-45 | Cool, survives light frost | Leaves, raw or cooked | Baby leaves first, then pick outer leaves |
| Turnip | 30-40 for greens, 40-60 for roots | Cool | Leaves and roots | Two foods from one sowing |
| Pak choi (bok choy) | 30 for baby plants, 45-60 full size | Cool | Whole plant | Flowers fast in heat and long days |
| Loose-leaf lettuce | 30-45 (baby leaves from 25) | Cool | Leaves | Pick outer leaves; flowers in heat |
| Spinach | 40-50 | Cool | Leaves | Flowers in heat and long days |
| Sweet potato leaves | 42-56 (6-8 weeks) | Hot, no frost | Leafy tips, cooked | Recut every 2 weeks; heavy picking means smaller roots |
| Zucchini (courgette), summer squash | 45-55 | Warm | Young fruits, flowers; young leaves cooked | Pick at 15-20 cm (6-8 in) |
| Bush beans (green beans) | 50-60 | Warm | Young pods | Pick every 2-3 days |
| Okra (ladies fingers) | 50-65 | Hot | Pods 5-10 cm (2-4 in); young leaves cooked | Pick every 2 days |
| Cucumber | 50-70 | Warm | Fruits | Mostly water; little food value |
| Green onions (scallions, spring onions) | About 60 from seed; 2-3 weeks to regrow from cut bases | Mild | Leaves and small bulbs | Replant the root ends of onions you eat |
| New potatoes (early varieties) | 60-90 | Cool to mild, no frost | Small tubers | Calories, not just vitamins |

Warm-season crops (beans, squash, cucumbers, okra) need soil of at least 16°C (60°F). See [When and How to Plant](../farming/planting-guide.md).

## What fast crops give you, and what they do not

Per 100 g (3.5 oz) of fresh food. Values vary a lot with variety, soil and the source of the data.

| Food | Energy (kcal) | Protein (g) | Iron (mg) | Vitamin C (mg) | Vitamin A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amaranth leaves | 45 | 4.6 | 8.9 | 50 | Very high |
| Sweet potato leaves | 49 | 4.6 | 6.2 | 70 | Very high |
| Cowpea leaves | 45 | 4.7 | 5.7 | 56 | High |
| Pumpkin leaves | 19-25 | 3-4 | 0.8-2.2 | 11-80 | High |
| Lentil sprouts | 106 | 9.0 | 3.2 | 16.5 | Low |
| Soybean sprouts (cook them) | 122 | 13.1 | 2.1 | 15.3 | Low |
| Potatoes | 77 | 2.1 | 0.8 | 19.7 | None |
| Lettuce, cucumber | Very low | Low | Low | Low | Lettuce some, cucumber little |

- **Dark green leaves are the most valuable fast crop.** A 100 g (3.5 oz) handful of amaranth, sweet potato or cowpea leaves gives 50 to 70 mg of vitamin C, about an adult's daily need (sources set it at 45 to 90 mg), plus vitamin A, iron, calcium and some protein.
- **Pale crops give much less.** Lettuce and cucumber are mostly water.
- **Sprouting adds vitamin C to stored beans and grain.** Dry lentils have little; lentil sprouts have useful amounts. Sprouts protect against scurvy on a diet of stored food.
- **Cook leaves briefly in a little water** and eat the water too, as soup. Long boiling destroys vitamin C.
- **Eat greens with a little oil or fat.** The body needs fat to absorb vitamin A.
- **Iron from leaves is poorly absorbed.** Eating them with vitamin C foods (tomatoes, citrus, raw sprouts where safe) helps.

### Why you also need calorie crops

An adult needs about 2,100 kcal a day (see [Food Priorities and Rationing](../food/food-rationing.md)). To get that from amaranth leaves you would have to eat about 5 kg (11 lb) every day. Nobody can.

So plant two kinds of crop at the same time:

- **Fast crops** for vitamins and minerals now.
- **Calorie crops** for energy later: potatoes, sweet potatoes, cassava, maize (corn), beans, grains. New potatoes are the fastest calorie crop, at 2 to 3 months. See [Calorie Crops That Feed a Family for a Year](../farming/staple-crops.md).

## Sprouts in a jar

You need: a clean jar of about 1 liter (1 quart), a piece of clean cloth or netting, a string or rubber band, and safe water (see [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md)).

1. **Choose seed sold for eating or sprouting:** mung beans, lentils, chickpeas, alfalfa, radish, wheat. Pick out stones and broken seeds.
2. **Measure:** about 2 tablespoons of small seeds (alfalfa, radish), or about 1/3 cup (60 to 70 g) of beans or lentils. They swell to many times their size.
3. **Rinse** the seeds well in safe water.
4. **Soak** them in the jar with 3 times their depth of safe water: 6 to 8 hours for small seeds, 8 to 12 hours (overnight) for beans.
5. **Drain.** Tie the cloth over the mouth and pour the water out through it.
6. **Lay the jar tilted mouth-down** in a bowl, so water keeps draining. Keep it out of direct sun, at room temperature.
7. **Rinse and drain 2 to 3 times a day,** more often in hot weather. Never leave the seeds standing in water.
8. **Eat** when the shoots are 1 to 3 cm (0.5 to 1 in) long: mung beans and lentils in 2 to 4 days, alfalfa in 4 to 6 days, when small leaves form.
9. **Store:** rinse once more and eat the same day if you have no fridge. In a fridge they keep about 5 days.

> [!WARNING]
> Throw away sprouts that are slimy, smell sour or bad, or show black spots, brown patches or fluffy mold. Fine white root hairs are normal. Wash the jar in hot, soapy water or boil it between batches. Cook sprouts until steaming hot all the way through for anyone at risk (see the box at the top).

## Microgreens

Microgreens are seedlings grown in soil and cut young. They are safer than sprouts because the leaves grow in air and light, but wash them before eating.

1. Use any tray or box 3 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) deep, with holes in the bottom. Fill it with moist soil or compost.
2. Scatter seed thickly, almost touching: radish, mustard, cabbage, kale, amaranth, beet. Soak peas and sunflower seeds overnight first.
3. Press them down, cover with a thin layer of soil, and keep moist.
4. Put the tray in the brightest window you have. In the northern hemisphere a south-facing window is best; in the southern hemisphere, a north-facing one.
5. Cut with scissors just above the soil when the first true leaves appear, after 7 to 21 days. Radish and mustard are among the fastest.
6. Most microgreens do not regrow. Pea shoots do, 2 or 3 times, if you leave some leaves.

## Cut-and-come-again greens

Many greens give several harvests from one sowing.

- **Pick outer leaves** and leave the center to keep growing: lettuce, mustard, pak choi, spinach, chard, kale. Never take more than about half of a plant's leaves at once.
- **Or cut the whole plant** about 2 to 3 cm (1 in) above the soil. Lettuce and mustard often regrow 2 or 3 times.
- **Amaranth:** first cut about 20 days after sowing, then weekly. Cut off the top to make the plant branch.
- **Sweet potato leaves:** pick the soft tips of the vines. They regrow in about 2 weeks. Pick lightly if you also want roots.
- **Cowpeas:** pick young leaves from about 3 weeks. Pick lightly if you also want the dry beans.
- **When greens send up a flower stalk** ("bolting"), the leaves turn bitter. Pull the plant, and sow again. In hot weather, switch from lettuce and spinach to amaranth, cowpea and sweet potato leaves.

## Fast warm-season crops

- **Sow after the last frost,** when the soil is warm. These crops die in frost.
- **Pick often.** A plant that is allowed to ripen seeds stops making new pods and fruits.
- **Bush beans:** pick when the pods are about as thick as a pencil, before the seeds bulge. Leave a few plants to dry for seed and dry beans. See [Saving Seeds](../farming/save-seeds.md).
- **Zucchini and summer squash:** pick at 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in). Missed fruits become huge, tough marrows. The flowers are edible. If fruits shrivel while small, pollinate by hand (see [Growing Food Fast](../farming/grow-food.md)).
- **Okra:** pick pods at 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in), a few days after the flower opens. Older pods turn woody. Wear gloves if the plant's hairs irritate your skin.
- **Cucumbers:** give them a fence or sticks to climb. Pick before the skin yellows.

## New potatoes

1. Plant early ("first early") varieties 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) deep, 30 cm (12 in) apart.
2. Pile soil around the stems as they grow, so no potato sees light.
3. When the plants flower, about 2 to 3 months after planting, feel into the side of the ridge and take a few egg-sized potatoes. Leave the plant to keep growing.
4. Or dig whole plants for a bigger crop of small potatoes.

New potatoes have thin skins and do not store. Eat them within days. Cut away any green parts; throw away potatoes that are green all through or taste bitter.

## Common mistakes

- **Planting only greens.** You will have vitamins but still go hungry.
- **Sowing too much at once.** You get a glut, then nothing. Sow small amounts often.
- **Growing lettuce and spinach in hot weather.** They flower and turn bitter. Grow amaranth and sweet potato leaves instead.
- **Leaving beans, zucchini and okra unpicked.** The plant stops producing.
- **Eating raw sprouts when you are pregnant, very young, old or ill.** Cook them.
