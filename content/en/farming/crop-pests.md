> [!CAUTION]
> **Never put fuel, kerosene (paraffin), motor oil, battery acid, bleach, household insect spray, rat poison or any unknown chemical on food plants.** They poison the crop and the soil, and they do not wash off.
>
> **Do not make tobacco or cigarette sprays.** Nicotine is a strong nerve poison that passes through the skin. Small amounts can make a child very ill or kill. It also kills bees and the insects that eat pests, and tobacco can carry a virus to tomatoes and peppers.
>
> **Treat every spray as a poison,** even homemade ones: wear gloves, keep it away from eyes and children, and wash all produce in safe water before eating.

## Quick steps

1. **Walk the garden every 1 or 2 days,** early morning or evening. Look under leaves.
2. **Pick off pests and eggs by hand,** and remove sick leaves at once.
3. **Block what you cannot pick:** collars on seedlings, netting over crops, fences against animals.
4. **Keep plants strong:** good soil, enough space, water on the soil not the leaves, and a new place for each crop family each year.
5. **Spray only if a pest is spreading fast:** a jet of water first, then soap spray, then neem.
6. **Pull out badly diseased plants,** and burn or bury them. Never compost them.
7. **Protect the insects, birds, frogs and lizards** that eat pests.

## Prevention first

- **Healthy soil:** strong plants resist pests. Too much nitrogen, from fresh manure or strong urine, makes soft growth that aphids love. See [Soil, Compost and Natural Fertilizer](../farming/soil-compost.md).
- **Rotation:** move each plant family to a new bed every year, and back only after 3 to 4 years. This matters most for the tomato family (blights, wilts) and the cabbage family.
- **Local and resistant varieties:** they have survived local pests for generations. Save seed only from healthy plants (see [Saving Seeds](../farming/save-seeds.md)).
- **Spacing:** air must move between plants so leaves dry. Crowded plants get mildew and rot.
- **Timing:** sow so plants are strong before the worst pest season. Sow a few extra plants to allow for losses.
- **Clean up:** remove weeds that shelter pests, and destroy crop remains after harvest.
- **Plant seed potatoes and cuttings only from healthy plants.**

## The daily walk

| Sign | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Holes and chewed edges | Caterpillars, beetles, grasshoppers, slugs |
| Seedlings cut off at soil level overnight | Cutworms |
| Silvery slime trails | Slugs, snails |
| Curled leaves, sticky leaves, ants climbing | Aphids or whiteflies (ants guard them) |
| Fine pale speckles, tiny webs under leaves | Mites |
| Wilting at midday that does not recover by evening, in moist soil | Wilt disease or root damage |
| Spots, powder or fuzz on leaves | Fungal disease |

- **Hand-pick** caterpillars, beetles and snails into a can of soapy water. Crush egg clusters under leaves.
- **Search at night** with a light for cutworms, slugs and snails. Night picking is very effective.
- **Shake beetles** onto a cloth in the early morning, when they are slow.
- **Knock aphids off** sturdy plants with a strong jet of water, early in the day. Most cannot climb back.
- **Ducks and chickens** eat slugs and snails, but keep them away from seedlings.

## Barriers and traps

- **Netting or cloth over hoops** keeps out moths, beetles and flies. Use fine netting, old mosquito net or thin cloth, and bury the edges. Lift it when squash, melons and cucumbers flower, so insects can pollinate them.
- **Cutworm collars:** a ring of cardboard, paper or a tin can with both ends removed, about 7 to 10 cm (3 to 4 in) tall, pushed 2 to 3 cm (1 in) into the soil around each seedling.
- **Board trap for slugs and snails:** a board about 30 x 40 cm (12 x 15 in), raised 2.5 cm (1 in) on small sticks. Every morning, lift it and kill those hiding underneath.
- **Beer or yeast trap:** sink a deep, straight-sided container to its rim. Fill it with beer, or sugar water with a pinch of yeast. Check daily and refill every few days.
- **Slugs:** remove boards, stones and weeds where they hide, and water in the morning. Eggshells and coffee grounds have not been shown to stop them. Dry ash stops them only until it gets wet.
- **Ants on fruit trees:** a band of sticky material around the trunk stops them guarding aphids.
- **Fruit bags:** tie paper or cloth bags over young fruit to keep fruit flies and birds off.
- **Birds:** cover ripening grain and fruit with netting, or guard the field at dawn and dusk.

## Fences against animals

| Animal | Fence |
|---|---|
| Rabbits, hares | Wire mesh no wider than 2.5 cm (1 in), 60 cm (2 ft) high for small rabbits, 90 cm (3 ft) for hares. Bury the bottom 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in), or bend it outward along the ground. |
| Deer | 2.1 to 2.4 m (7 to 8 ft) high; 3 m (10 ft) on slopes. A fence slanted outward can be lower. |
| Goats | Strong and tight, at least 1.2 to 1.5 m (4 to 5 ft) high, no gaps or footholds: goats climb. |
| Cattle, donkeys, pigs | Strong posts; pigs dig, so bury or peg the bottom. |
| Monkeys, baboons, wild pigs, elephants | Fences rarely stop them. Guard the field, keep dogs, plant dense thorny hedges. |

- **A living fence** of local thorny shrubs, planted close, takes 2 to 4 years to grow thick. Protect it with a dead-branch fence meanwhile.
- **Scare devices** (noise, lights, scarecrows, shiny strips, ultrasonic boxes) usually work for only a few days, because animals get used to them. Move and change them often, and combine them with fences or dogs.

## Homemade sprays: what works

**Rules for every spray:**

1. **Test first:** spray a few leaves, wait 24 hours, and look for burnt spots.
2. **Spray in the early morning or evening.** Never in full sun or above 32°C (90°F). Never on plants that are wilting from drought.
3. **Wet the undersides of leaves.** Soap and neem work only where they touch.
4. **Do not spray flowers while bees are visiting.**
5. **Use separate containers,** never cooking pots or water jars. Label them.

| Spray | Recipe for 1 liter (1 quart) | Works on | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soap | 10 to 20 mL (2 to 4 teaspoons) of liquid soap, or 10 to 20 g of grated bar soap dissolved in warm water: a 1 to 2% mix | Aphids, whiteflies, mealybugs, mites, thrips, young scale insects | Good, on contact only. Not caterpillars or beetles |
| Neem seed | 50 g ground neem seed, soaked 24 hours, strained, plus 4 g soap | Caterpillars, aphids, whiteflies, leaf miners, grasshoppers, hoppers | Good; slow: insects stop feeding and fail to grow |
| Neem leaf | 200 g fresh leaves, soaked overnight, pounded, squeezed, strained, plus 4 g soap | As neem seed | Weaker than seed |
| Chili | 1 cup dry or 2 cups fresh chilies, pounded, soaked at least 1 hour, strained, plus 1 teaspoon soap | Ants, aphids, caterpillars | Weak; mainly drives pests off |
| Garlic | 2 cloves grated into a little cooking oil overnight, then water, strained, plus 2 teaspoons soap | Caterpillars, mites, thrips | Weak |
| Milk | 1 part milk to 9 parts water | Powdery mildew on squash and cucumbers | Some, if sprayed weekly from the first spots |
| Wood ash | Fine, cool ash dusted thinly on wet leaves | Some beetles, grasshoppers | Weak; too much harms plants and soil |

### Soap spray

- **Use real soap:** plain bar soap, soap flakes or simple liquid soap. **Do not use laundry detergent, dishwasher powder, degreasers or anything with bleach.** They burn plants.
- **Never make it stronger than 2%.** Stronger soap burns leaves.
- **Hard water** makes soap useless. Mix it in a jar and let it stand 15 minutes. If scum forms, use rainwater.
- Repeat every 4 to 7 days while pests remain, but not more than needed: repeated sprays damage leaves.
- Some extension services advise only commercial insecticidal soap, because homemade soap injures plants more often. Test first.

### Neem extract

- Use seeds from ripe, dried neem fruit. Remove the pulp and husk, and grind the kernels. A typical batch is 250 g of ground seed in 5 liters of water.
- Use it fresh. Seed extract does not keep; leaf extract keeps up to about 1 week in a cool, dark place.
- Spray in the evening, because sunlight breaks it down. Repeat at least every 2 weeks, or after heavy rain.
- **Wait at least 3 days after spraying before harvest,** and wash the produce.
- Neem has low toxicity to people, but do not swallow it, breathe the powder, or get it in your eyes.
- Neem (*Azadirachta indica*) grows in hot, dry tropics. **Do not confuse it with chinaberry** (Persian lilac, *Melia azedarach*), which has leaves divided twice and poisonous yellow berries.

### Chili and garlic

- Wear gloves or a plastic bag over your hand. Keep away from your face and from children. Do not spray in wind.

## Friends of the garden

- **Insects:** ladybirds (ladybugs) and their larvae, which look like small black-and-orange crocodiles; lacewings; hoverflies; ground beetles; tiny wasps that turn aphids into swollen brown shells; spiders; praying mantises.
- **Animals:** birds, frogs, toads, lizards and bats eat huge numbers of insects.
- **Keep them:** spray as little as possible, even soap and neem. Grow flowering plants such as coriander, dill, fennel and sunflowers nearby. Leave stones, logs and hedges as shelter, and a shallow dish of water with stones in it, emptied every 3 days.

## Common diseases

| Disease | Signs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Damping-off | Seedlings fall over, stem pinched at soil level | Sow in well-drained soil, water less, thin crowded seedlings |
| Blight (tomatoes, potatoes) | Brown or black patches on leaves and stems; late blight spreads fast in cool, wet weather, with white fuzz under leaves | Remove spotted leaves at once; pull whole plants if it spreads; do not keep potatoes from blighted plants |
| Powdery mildew | White powder on leaves (squash, cucumbers, peas) | Remove worst leaves, give space, milk spray |
| Downy mildew | Yellow patches on top, gray or purple fuzz below | Remove leaves, water the soil only, rotate |
| Wilt | Plant wilts and does not recover by evening, though the soil is moist; stem brown inside | Pull and burn; no tomato-family crops there for several years |
| Rot | Soft, smelly stems, roots or fruit | Improve drainage, raise beds, water less, harvest on time |
| Virus (mosaic, leaf curl) | Leaves mottled yellow and green, curled, plant stunted | Pull and destroy; control aphids and whiteflies, which spread it |

**For every disease:**

- Burn sick plants, or bury them at least 30 cm (1 ft) deep away from the garden. Never compost them.
- Wash hands and tools after handling sick plants. Do not work among plants while they are wet.
- Water the soil in the morning, not the leaves. See [Watering Crops With Little Water](../farming/watering-crops.md).
- Wash your hands after smoking or handling tobacco before touching tomatoes or peppers.

## After harvest

- Wash all produce in safe water, peel roots, and cook when unsure.
- For weevils, rats and moulds in stored food, see [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md).
- If someone swallows a spray or chemical, see [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md).
