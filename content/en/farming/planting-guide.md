> [!CAUTION]
> **Never sow all your seed at once.** A false start of the rains, a late frost or a storm can kill every seedling. Sow half or less, and keep the rest until the first sowing is up and growing.
>
> **Do not sow beans, maize (corn), squash or cucumbers into cold soil.** The seeds rot. Wait until the soil is at least 10°C (50°F) for maize, and 16°C (60°F) for beans, squash and cucumbers.
>
> **Never eat leftover planting seed with a bright pink, blue, green or red coating.** It is treated with poison. Wash your hands after sowing it.

## Quick steps

1. **Find your season.** In the tropics, sow when the rains have truly started. Elsewhere, sow cool-season crops around the last frost and warm-season crops after it, in warm soil.
2. **Prepare a bed** 1 to 1.2 m (3 to 4 ft) wide, loosened one spade deep, with compost. Raise it in wet places; sink it in dry places.
3. **Test your seed:** sprout 10 seeds in a damp cloth.
4. **Sow at 2 to 3 times the seed's width,** firm the soil, water gently, and keep the surface moist.
5. **Thin** the seedlings to the right spacing.
6. **Raise tomatoes, peppers, cabbages and onions as seedlings.** Harden them off for 7 to 10 days, then transplant in the evening.
7. **Sow a little more every 2 to 3 weeks.**

## Reading your seasons without a calendar

### Frost (temperate and cold climates)

- Ask older neighbors when the last spring frost and first autumn frost usually come. Each year, write down both dates and what you sowed. After 2 or 3 years you have your own calendar.
- Frost forms on **clear, still nights.** Cold air flows downhill, so hollows get the most frost. Plant tender crops on slopes or higher ground.
- Plant frost-tender crops (beans, maize, squash, tomatoes) after **2 weeks with no frost,** once nights stay above about 10°C (50°F).
- For autumn crops, count back from the first frost: the crop's days to harvest, plus 2 weeks.
- **Plant signals:** farmers time sowing by when local wild trees leaf out or flower ("plant corn when oak leaves are the size of a squirrel's ear" in North America). Note which plant was flowering when your sowings did well.

### Rainy and dry seasons (tropics)

- Sow the main crop when the rains have truly started. A rule used by weather services: at least **20 to 25 mm (about 1 in) of rain within 1 to 3 days.** Measure it in a straight-sided can: 25 mm is 2.5 cm (1 in) of water in the bottom.
- Early rains are often followed by a dry spell of 1 to 2 weeks that kills seedlings. This is why you keep seed back.
- Use the cooler dry season for vegetables you can water: tomatoes, onions, cabbages, greens. Many have fewer diseases in dry weather.
- Local seed is safest: some onion, sorghum, rice and soybean types grow well only in the day length they were bred for.

### Soil temperature without a thermometer

Your hand cannot judge soil temperature reliably. Instead:

- **Thermometer:** push one 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) into the soil early in the morning, when the soil is coldest.
- **Test sowing:** sow 5 bean seeds. Up in about 1 week: the soil is warm, about 25°C (77°F). Up in 2 weeks or more: cool, about 15°C (59°F). Rotted: too cold for warm-season crops.
- **Weeds:** when local summer weeds start sprouting, the soil is usually warm enough.
- Pulling back mulch and using raised beds on sunny ground warms soil faster in spring.

## Cool-season and warm-season crops

**Cool-season crops** grow best at 15 to 20°C (59 to 68°F) and many survive light frost. **Warm-season crops** die in frost and need warm soil to germinate.

| Crop | Lowest soil temperature | Days to come up at 10°C (50°F) | At 25°C (77°F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lettuce | 2°C (35°F) | 7 | 2 |
| Spinach | 2°C (35°F) | 12 | 5 |
| Peas | 4°C (40°F) | 13 | 6 |
| Radish | 4°C (40°F) | 11 | 3 |
| Cabbage, kale | 4°C (40°F) | 15 | 4 |
| Carrot | 4°C (40°F) | 17 | 6 |
| Tomato | 10°C (50°F) | 43 | 6 |
| Maize (corn) | 10°C (50°F) | 22 | 4 |
| Beans | 16°C (60°F) | none | 8 |
| Cucumber, pepper | 16°C (60°F) | none | 4-8 |
| Squash, pumpkin, melons, okra | 16°C (60°F) | none | 4-5 for melons |

- Lettuce and spinach germinate poorly above about 30°C (86°F). In heat, sow them in shade, in the evening, into cool moist soil.
- Warm-season crops grow far better once the soil is above 20°C (68°F).
- Plant potato tubers in cool soil around the last frost. Cover young shoots with soil if frost threatens.

## Preparing a bed

1. **Choose a place** with at least 6 hours of direct sun, near water, away from tree roots and standing water.
2. **Squeeze a handful of soil.** If it forms a sticky ball that smears, it is too wet: wait, because digging wet soil packs it hard for the season. If the ball breaks when poked, dig.
3. **Mark the bed** 1 to 1.2 m (3 to 4 ft) wide, with paths 30 to 45 cm (12 to 18 in) wide, so you reach the middle from the path. **Never step on the bed.**
4. **Dig one spade deep,** about 25 to 30 cm (10 to 12 in). Remove roots of weeds that regrow. Break up clods.
5. **Mix compost into the top layer** and rake it fine. See [Soil, Compost and Natural Fertilizer](../farming/soil-compost.md).

**Double digging** helps root crops on hard ground: dig a trench one spade deep, loosen its bottom with a fork another spade deep (about 60 cm or 24 in in total), fill it with topsoil from the next trench, and repeat. It is needed only once if nobody walks on the bed.

### Raised beds for wet places, sunken beds for dry places

```
Wet climate or heavy clay: raised bed

      +----------------+
      |    plants      |  15-30 cm above path
 _____|                |_____
 path     water drains     path

Dry climate: sunken bed

 _____                  _____
 path |                | path
      |    plants      |  10-15 cm below path
      +----------------+
        water collects
```

- **Heavy rain, clay or a high water table:** heap the bed 15 to 30 cm (6 to 12 in) above the paths so roots do not drown. Sweet potatoes and cassava grow well on ridges or mounds.
- **Deserts and dry areas:** dig the bed 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) below the paths, inside a low earth wall, so water collects at the roots. Zuni "waffle gardens" in the American Southwest divide such beds into small squares. Mulch them. Do not use sunken beds where heavy rain falls: they flood.

## Sowing depth and spacing

**The rule:** sow a seed **2 to 3 times as deep as its width.** Tiny seeds (lettuce, carrots) get only a dusting of fine soil. When in doubt, go shallower. Seed packet instructions come first.

1. Make a groove or hole at the right depth. Sow 2 or 3 seeds per spot if the seed is old.
2. Cover, then **press the soil down firmly** so the seed touches moist soil.
3. Water with a fine sprinkle. Keep the surface moist until shoots appear. In hot sun, cover slow seeds (carrots, onions) with a sack, and **remove it the moment shoots show.**
4. At 2 to 4 true leaves, pull out extras to reach the final spacing. Eat the thinnings.

| Crop | Depth | Spacing in the row | Between rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lettuce | 0.5 cm (1/4 in) | 20-30 cm (8-12 in) | 30 cm (12 in) |
| Carrot | 0.5-1 cm (1/4-1/2 in) | 5-8 cm (2-3 in) | 20-30 cm (8-12 in) |
| Radish | 1 cm (1/2 in) | 3-5 cm (1-2 in) | 15 cm (6 in) |
| Cabbage (transplant) | 1 cm (1/2 in) | 40-60 cm (16-24 in) | 60 cm (24 in) |
| Tomato (transplant) | 0.5 cm (1/4 in) | 45-60 cm (18-24 in) | 90 cm (36 in) |
| Peas | 2.5-5 cm (1-2 in) | 5 cm (2 in) | 45-60 cm (18-24 in) |
| Bush beans | 2.5-4 cm (1-1.5 in) | 10-15 cm (4-6 in) | 45-60 cm (18-24 in) |
| Maize (corn) | 3-5 cm (1-2 in) | 20-30 cm (8-12 in) | 75 cm (30 in) |
| Squash, pumpkin | 2.5 cm (1 in) | 90-120 cm (3-4 ft) | 120-180 cm (4-6 ft) |
| Potatoes (tubers) | 10-15 cm (4-6 in) | 30-37 cm (12-15 in) | 60-75 cm (24-30 in) |

**In beds,** plant on a grid instead of rows, using the in-row spacing in every direction, so grown leaves just touch. This shades out weeds and keeps the soil moist.

## Direct sowing or seedlings

- **Sow directly:** root crops (carrot, radish, beet, turnip), whose roots bend if moved; beans, peas and maize; squash, cucumber and melons, whose roots dislike disturbance.
- **Raise as seedlings:** tomato, pepper, eggplant (aubergine), cabbage, kale, broccoli, onions, leeks. Lettuce and amaranth work either way.

### Raising seedlings

1. Use pots, cut-down bottles, leaf cups or boxes with drainage holes, or a small nursery bed.
2. Fill with fine soil mixed with sifted compost and a little sand. Sow 2 or 3 seeds per pot; thin to 1.
3. Keep them warm and moist, with **full light** as soon as they appear; in dim light they grow thin and weak.
4. In the tropics, raise the nursery off the ground, with light shade and shelter from heavy rain.
5. Plant out at 4 to 6 true leaves, usually 4 to 8 weeks after sowing.

### Hardening off (7 to 10 days)

1. Day 1: put seedlings outside for about 1 hour, in shade, out of the wind.
2. Each day add a few hours and a little more sun. Water slightly less often, but never let them wilt. Do not feed them.
3. Once they manage 10 to 12 hours outside, leave them out day and night for 2 days. Then transplant.

### Transplanting

1. Water the seedlings 1 to 2 hours before. Transplant in the **evening or on a cloudy day.**
2. Hold each seedling by a leaf, never by the stem: a torn leaf regrows, a crushed stem does not.
3. Set it at the depth it grew. Tomatoes and cabbages can go deeper, up to their lowest leaves.
4. Firm the soil and water well. In hot sun, shade them for 2 to 3 days with a leafy branch.

## Testing seed: the 10-seed test

1. Spread **10 seeds** on a damp (not dripping) cloth or paper towel, and fold it over.
2. Put it in a container or bag that is **not sealed:** seeds need air or they rot.
3. Keep it warm, about 20 to 30°C (68 to 86°F), and damp. Check daily.
4. After 5 to 10 days (up to 14 for carrot, parsley and pepper), count the seeds with a root. Each one is 10%.

- **8 to 10 sprouted:** sow normally.
- **5 to 7:** sow about twice as thickly.
- **3 to 4:** sow 3 times as thickly, and look for better seed.
- **0 to 2:** use only if you have nothing else.

## Succession planting for a steady supply

One big sowing gives a glut, then nothing.

- Sow a short row of lettuce or other greens every 2 to 3 weeks, radishes every 1 to 2 weeks, bush beans or maize every 2 to 3 weeks. Divide a bed into 3 or 4 parts and sow one part each time.
- When a crop finishes, replant the bed the same week.
- Stop when a crop's days to harvest no longer fit before the first frost or the end of the rains.
- In hot weather, switch to heat-loving greens. See [Fast Crops: Food in 3 Days to 3 Months](../farming/fast-crops.md).

## A simple planting calendar

"Spring" is about March to May in the northern hemisphere and September to November in the southern. In the tropics, follow the rains, not the months.

| Climate | Main sowing time | Sow then | Second chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical, wet and dry seasons | True start of the rains | Maize, sorghum, millet, beans, cowpeas, groundnuts, squash, sweet potatoes, cassava | Late rains: beans, cowpeas. Cool dry season, with water: tomatoes, onions, cabbages, greens |
| Humid tropics | Any time except the heaviest rains | Cassava, sweet potatoes, taro, plantain, amaranth, cowpeas | Lettuce, cabbage and carrots only in highlands |
| Hot desert or dry climate | Cool months, autumn to early spring | Wheat, barley, chickpeas, lentils, peas, onions, carrots, greens, potatoes | Summer, with water and shade: okra, cowpeas, sorghum, millet, melons, sweet potatoes |
| Temperate, 4 to 6 months without frost | Early spring, once the soil is workable | Peas, spinach, lettuce, radish, onions, cabbage; potatoes around the last frost | After the last frost: beans, maize, squash, tomatoes. Midsummer: turnips, carrots, kale. Autumn: garlic, wheat |
| Short cool summer, under 3 months without frost | Seedlings indoors 4 to 8 weeks before the last frost | Potatoes, peas, barley, oats, turnips, radish, spinach, kale, cabbage | Earliest varieties, raised beds, clear covers. Avoid sweet potatoes, cassava, most maize |
