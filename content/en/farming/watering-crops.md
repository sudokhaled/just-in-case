> [!CAUTION]
> **Never water food crops with water that holds toilet waste.** Keep greywater off every part you eat.
>
> **Do not let water stand for more than 3 days** in pits, furrows, swales, pots or barrels. Mosquitoes that spread malaria and dengue breed in it within about a week. Cover every container.
>
> **Do not dig swales or large catchments on steep or unstable slopes, or just above homes, wells or latrines.** Soaked ground can slide, and soaking water can carry latrine waste into wells.

## Quick steps

1. **Check before you water:** push a finger 5 cm (2 in) into the soil. Water only if it is dry there.
2. **Water early in the morning,** at the base of the plants, until the soil is wet 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) deep. Then wait until the top dries.
3. **Mulch** all bare soil 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) deep.
4. **Put water only where roots are:** buried clay pots, bottles, basins and planting pits. Do not wet paths.
5. **In dry lands, stop rain running away:** dig zai pits, half-moons or swales across the slope.
6. **Grow drought-hardy crops,** and save scarce water for seedlings and for flowering.

## How much water crops need

**1 mm of water on 1 square meter is 1 liter.** A fully grown crop uses about this much per day (FAO):

| Climate | Cool, below 15°C (59°F) | Warm, 15-25°C (59-77°F) | Hot, above 25°C (77°F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Humid | 1-2 liters/m² | 3-4 liters/m² | 5-6 liters/m² |
| Sub-humid | 3-4 | 5-6 | 7-8 |
| Semi-arid | 4-5 | 6-7 | 8-9 |
| Desert | 4-6 | 7-8 | 9-10 |

- Seedlings and young plants use about **half** this.
- Maize, sorghum, millet, beans, tomatoes and potatoes use about 10% more at their peak.
- The temperate rule, 2.5 cm (1 in) a week, is 25 liters per square meter (5.5 gallons per square yard) weekly.

Water for a whole season (FAO), less any rain:

| Crop | Liters per m² per season |
|---|---|
| Beans | 300-500 |
| Cabbage, onions | 350-550 |
| Sorghum, millet | 450-650 |
| Tomatoes | 400-800 |
| Potatoes, groundnuts | 500-700 |
| Maize (corn) | 500-800 |

So a 10 m² (110 sq ft) bed of beans needs 3,000 to 5,000 liters (800 to 1,300 gallons) a season. Plant only what your water can supply.

## Check soil moisture by hand

Take soil from root depth, 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) down, and squeeze it:

| Feel | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dry, powdery, falls apart | Water now |
| Crumbly, barely holds together | Water soon |
| Makes a ball, damp, no drops | Enough |
| Drips water, sticky mud | Too wet: wait |

Midday drooping that recovers by evening is normal. Early-morning drooping means water now. An hour after watering, dig to see how deep it went.

## When and how to water

- **Early morning is best:** less water is lost and leaves dry fast. Evening is next best, at the base only. Never at midday.
- **Deep and less often:** soak the root zone, then wait until the top 5 cm (2 in) is dry. Daily sprinkles keep roots shallow.
- **Sand** needs less, more often. **Clay** needs slow watering, so it soaks in.
- **Make a basin:** a low ring of soil around each plant or bed holds the water.
- **Water the soil, not the leaves:** wet leaves spread disease (see [Protecting Crops From Pests, Disease and Animals](../farming/crop-pests.md)).
- **When water is short,** never let seedlings, transplants, or plants in flower or forming fruit and grain dry out. Maize suffers most at tassel and silk time. Grain crops can dry off once the grain is hard. Leafy crops need water to the end.

## Mulch

- Spread **5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in)** of straw, dry grass, leaves or chopped stalks on moist soil. Use wood chips around trees, stones in deserts.
- Keep it a few centimeters from stems, so they do not rot.
- It cuts evaporation, cools the soil, stops crusting and feeds the soil (see [Soil, Compost and Natural Fertilizer](../farming/soil-compost.md)). Termites eat it in the tropics: add more.

## Put water at the roots

### Buried clay pot (olla)

1. Use an **unglazed** clay pot of 5 to 10 liters. Filled with water, its outside should turn damp within an hour or two. Glazed, painted or plastic pots do not work.
2. Bury it to the neck, with the rim just above the soil.
3. Plant 2 to 4 plants around it, about 30 cm (12 in) away.
4. Fill it and cover the mouth with a lid or stone against evaporation and mosquitoes.
5. Refill it before it is empty: every 2 to 7 days, depending on heat, soil and plants.

Water seeps out as the soil dries, so little is lost. Before freezing weather, empty and lift the pot, or it cracks.

### Bottle drip

1. Make 1 to 3 small holes with a hot needle in the cap of a 1.5 to 2 liter plastic bottle. Cut off its bottom.
2. Push it neck down into the soil, 10 cm (4 in) from the plant.
3. Fill it and cover it. It should empty over many hours; add holes if slower.

## Catch rain in dry lands

### Zai (tassa) planting pits

For crusted, hard soils with 300 to 800 mm (12 to 31 in) of rain a year. Wetter places waterlog.

1. **In the dry season,** dig pits 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) wide and 10 to 20 cm (4 to 8 in) deep, 60 to 100 cm (2 to 3 ft) apart, in staggered rows across the slope.
2. **Throw the soil downhill** of each pit as a small curved wall.
3. **At the start of the rains,** put 2 handfuls (about 300 g) of manure or compost in each pit and mix it with 5 cm (2 in) of soil.
4. **Sow into the pits:** in Burkina Faso, 8 to 12 millet or sorghum seeds per pit. Reuse the pits next season.

### Half-moons (demi-lunes)

For gentle slopes, up to about 5%.

1. Mark a contour line (see the A-frame below).
2. Put a peg on the line. With a rope 2 m (6.5 ft) long, mark a half-circle on the downhill side. Both tips touch the contour line.
3. Dig inside the half-circle 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in) deep. Pile the soil along the curved edge as a bank about 25 cm (10 in) high. Put stones at the tips.
4. Make the next half-moon 2 to 4 m (6.5 to 13 ft) along the line. Start the next row 4 to 8 m (13 to 26 ft) downhill, staggered, so it catches water running between the ones above.
5. Add manure inside, and plant millet, sorghum, or 1 or 2 trees.

```
     uphill: rain runs down the slope
   ----o-----------o---o-----------o----  contour
        \  catch  /     \  catch  /
         \_______/       \_______/   earth bank
   --------------o-----------o---------  next row,
                  \  catch  /           staggered
                   \_______/
     downhill
```

### Swales

A swale is a level ditch dug along a contour, with the soil piled on its downhill side as a bank. Runoff stops and soaks in, watering trees and crops on and below the bank.

1. Mark the contour with an A-frame.
2. Dig the ditch with a level bottom, so water spreads along its whole length. For a garden, 30 to 50 cm (1 to 1.5 ft) deep and wide is enough.
3. Make a **spillway** at one end: a low, stone-lined point where storm water overflows safely.
4. Plant and mulch the bank at once.
5. After rain, water should soak away within about 2 days. If not, the soil is too tight: use mulch and pits instead.

### Make an A-frame level

1. Tie two straight poles, about 2 m (6.5 ft) long, together at the top. Spread the feet about 2 m (6.5 ft) apart.
2. Tie a crossbar halfway up.
3. Hang a string with a stone from the top, past the crossbar.
4. **Find level:** stand the frame up and mark both foot spots with pegs. Mark where the string crosses the crossbar. Turn the frame around, each foot on the other peg, and mark again. Halfway between the two marks is level. Check it each day.
5. **Mark a contour:** put one foot at the start. Move the other up or down the slope until the string hangs on the level mark. Peg it, swing forward and repeat. Smooth sharp bends by eye.

```
              /\
             /  \
            /  | \     string with
           /   |  \    a stone
          /----o---\   crossbar: level
         /     *    \  mark at center
        /            \
       /              \
     foot            foot
      |<---- 2 m ---->|
```

A clear tube about 14 m (45 ft) long, full of muddy water and tied to two sticks, also works: the water in both ends always stands at the same height.

## Keyhole garden

A round raised bed with a compost basket in the middle, reached by a keyhole-shaped path. It holds water well.

1. Mark a circle 2 to 3 m (6.5 to 10 ft) across. Build a wall 60 cm to 1 m (2 to 3 ft) high of stones, bricks or mud bricks. Leave a notch path to the center.
2. In the center, make a basket of sticks woven with twigs, 50 to 80 cm (20 to 30 in) wide and taller than the wall.
3. Fill in layers: branches and stalks, dead plants, soil, manure, a little wood ash, then topsoil mixed with compost. Slope the top down from the basket.
4. Pour water and kitchen scraps into the basket; they feed the bed around it. No fresh manure. In the dry season, allow about 40 to 60 liters (10 to 16 gallons) a day.

## Shade and wind

- **Shade** seedlings and greens in the hottest hours: palm fronds, reeds or cloth on a frame 1 to 1.5 m (3 to 5 ft) high, or shade cloth blocking 30 to 50% of the sun.
- **Wind** dries soil and plants. A hedge or fence protects best for about **10 times its height** downwind, on the side the dry wind comes from. A barrier that lets some wind through works better than a solid wall, which makes gusts.

## Greywater and rainwater

- **Greywater** (from washing, never toilets): plain soap only, on the soil, never on leaves or crops eaten raw, within 24 hours, never from diapers (nappies) or anyone with diarrhea. Change where you pour it, so soap and salt do not build up. Full rules: [Growing Food Fast](../farming/grow-food.md).
- **Rainwater:** 20 mm (0.8 in) of rain on a 40 m² (430 sq ft) roof gives about 640 liters (170 gallons). Store it covered. Lead path runoff into beds. See [Collecting Rainwater](../water/rainwater.md).

## Crops for dry places

| Crop | Why it suits dry land |
|---|---|
| Pearl millet | The hardiest grain for hot, sandy, dry land |
| Sorghum | Survives drought, regrows when rain returns |
| Cowpea | Heat and drought hardy; leaves and seeds eaten |
| Tepary bean | Desert bean, very heat and drought hardy |
| Cassava | Survives months of drought; bitter types are poisonous unless processed |
| Sweet potato | Survives dry spells once rooted; leaves are food |
| Okra, amaranth | Love heat |
| Pigeon pea | Deep-rooted shrub, bears for years |

To grow and prepare these safely, see [Calorie Crops That Feed a Family for a Year](../farming/staple-crops.md) and [Living in the Desert](../land/desert-living.md).

## Stop mosquitoes

- Cover every container and screen openings. Scrub open containers weekly.
- Level and drain furrows, fill puddles, and empty tires and broken pots.
- Pits, half-moons and swales must soak dry within 2 to 3 days after rain.
- Buried pots and bottles keep water underground, safe from mosquitoes.
