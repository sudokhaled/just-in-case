> [!CAUTION]
> **Never eat or sprout seeds sold for planting that are coated in a bright powder** (pink, blue, green or red). They are treated with poison.
>
> **Never eat potato leaves, stems or berries, or potatoes that have turned green.** Never eat raw or undercooked red kidney beans: soak them, then boil hard for at least 10 minutes.
>
> **Never put water containing toilet waste on food crops,** and keep all greywater off the parts you eat.

## Quick steps

1. **Start sprouts indoors today.** You can eat them in 3 to 5 days.
2. **Sow radishes, greens and microgreens** in any container or bed. First harvest in 1 to 6 weeks.
3. **Plant calorie crops** (potatoes, sweet potatoes, beans, corn, squash) in the sunniest ground at the start of the warm or rainy season.
4. **Feed the soil** with compost, and cover it with mulch to save water.
5. **Water deeply at the base of plants,** early morning or evening.
6. **Save seed** from your best open-pollinated plants.

## Fastest food first

| Crop | Days to first harvest | Season |

| Sprouts (mung bean, lentil, alfalfa) | 3-5 | Any, indoors |
| Microgreens (pea, radish, mustard, sunflower) | 7-21 | Any, on a bright windowsill |
| Radish (small types) | 25-35 | Cool |
| Loose-leaf lettuce, mustard greens, pak choi | 30-45 | Cool |
| Spinach | 40-50 | Cool |
| Zucchini (courgette) | 45-55 | Warm |
| Bush beans (green beans) | 50-60 | Warm |
| Potatoes (small "new" potatoes) | 60-90 | Cool to warm, no frost |
| Corn (maize): fresh cobs / dry grain | 60-100 / 90-150 | Warm |
| Dry beans | 85-110 | Warm |
| Winter squash, pumpkin | 80-120 | Warm |
| Sweet potatoes | 90-150 | Hot, no frost |

**Cool-season crops** grow best in spring and autumn in temperate regions, or in the cooler dry season in the tropics. **Warm-season crops** need soil above about 16°C (60°F) and no frost. In the tropics, plant at the start of the rainy season.

> [!NOTE]
> Greens and radishes give vitamins, not calories. Growing all the food for one adult takes several hundred square meters and a full season. Expect a small garden to add to your stored food, not replace it.

See [Fast Crops: Food in 3 Days to 3 Months](../farming/fast-crops.md) for more fast crops and what they give you, and [When and How to Plant](../farming/planting-guide.md) for seasons and soil temperatures.

## Calorie crops

- **Potatoes:** among the most calories per area. Cut seed potatoes into pieces with at least 2 "eyes" (buds), let them dry for 1 to 2 days, and plant 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) deep and 30 cm (12 in) apart. Pile soil up around the stems so no potato sees light.
- **Sweet potatoes:** need heat and 3 to 5 months. Stand a sweet potato half in water; when shoots are 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) long, pull them off, root them in water and plant them. Cooked leaves are edible too.
- **Corn (maize):** plant in a block of at least 4 short rows, not one long row, so the wind pollinates it. Needs rich soil and plenty of water. If corn becomes a main food, cook it with wood ash or lime (calcium hydroxide) and eat beans with it; otherwise people can develop pellagra, a serious vitamin disease.
- **Beans:** dry beans give protein, store for years, and add nitrogen to the soil. Climbing beans can grow up corn stalks.
- **Squash and pumpkins:** winter squash stores for months. If fruits fall off small, pollinate by hand: pick a male flower (thin stem, no tiny fruit behind it) and touch its center to the center of a female flower (tiny fruit behind it), in the morning.

For yields, land per person, cassava and other crops, and how to make them safe to eat, see [Calorie Crops That Feed a Family for a Year](../farming/staple-crops.md). For storing the harvest, see [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md).

## Containers

- Use any container with **holes in the bottom:** buckets, sacks, boxes, tubs. Never use containers that held fuel, pesticides or chemicals.
- **Depth:** 15 cm (6 in) for greens and radishes; 30 cm (12 in) and about 20 liters (5 gallons) for potatoes, zucchini or tomatoes.
- **Sun:** most vegetables need **6 hours of direct sun** a day. Leafy greens manage with 3 to 4. Indoors, use the sunniest window: one that faces the equator (south in the northern hemisphere, north in the southern).

## Soil basics

- Good soil is dark and crumbly. A damp handful that squeezes into a sticky ball is clay; one that falls apart is sand. Compost improves both.
- **Fast bed on grass:** lay cardboard over the grass, soak it, and cover it with 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) of compost or soil. Plant into the top layer.
- **Contaminated soil:** soil near old painted buildings, busy roads, factories or floods may hold lead or chemicals. Use containers of clean soil, wash vegetables and peel roots.

See [Soil, Compost and Natural Fertilizer](../farming/soil-compost.md).

## Composting

1. Mix **"greens"** (food scraps, fresh grass, manure from plant-eating animals) with **"browns"** (dry leaves, straw, shredded cardboard). Use about 1 part greens to 2 or 3 parts browns.
2. Keep it as damp as a squeezed sponge.
3. Turn it every 1 to 2 weeks. A well-managed pile is ready in 2 to 6 months; a neglected one takes a year.
4. **Do not add** meat, fat, dairy, dog or cat feces, or diseased plants.

Apply raw manure at least **4 months (120 days) before harvest** for crops that touch the soil, and at least 3 months (90 days) for others. Composted manure is safer. For toilet waste, see [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).

## Watering efficiently

- Most vegetables need about **2.5 cm (1 in) of water a week**: about 25 liters per square meter (5.5 gallons per square yard).
- Push a finger 5 cm (2 in) into the soil. Water only if it is dry at that depth.
- Water **at the base**, deeply and less often, in the early morning or evening.
- **Mulch:** cover the soil with 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) of straw, dry leaves or grass clippings to cut evaporation and weeds.
- **Drip bottle:** bury a plastic bottle with small holes next to a plant, and fill it.
- Collect rain from roofs: see [Collecting Rainwater](../water/rainwater.md).
- See [Watering Crops With Little Water](../farming/watering-crops.md).

### Greywater cautions

Greywater is used water from washing, bathing and laundry, never from toilets.

- Use plain soaps. Avoid water with bleach, borax or strong detergents.
- Pour it on the soil, never on leaves or on crops eaten raw.
- Use it within 24 hours; stored greywater turns foul and breeds germs.
- Never use water from washing diapers (nappies) or from anyone with diarrhea.

## Starting seeds

1. Sow seeds at a depth of **2 to 3 times their width.** Tiny seeds go on the surface, lightly covered.
2. Keep the soil moist but not soggy until shoots appear, usually in 3 to 14 days.
3. Pull out crowded seedlings so each plant has room. Eat the extras.

**Test old seeds:** roll 10 seeds in a damp cloth or paper, keep them warm, and count how many sprout in 5 to 10 days. If only 5 sprout, sow twice as thickly.

See [When and How to Plant: Seasons, Spacing and Seedlings](../farming/planting-guide.md).

## Saving seeds

- **Open-pollinated** seeds (often called heirloom) grow true: their seeds give the same plant next year. Packets marked **"F1" or "hybrid"** do not grow true; their seeds give mixed, often weaker plants.
- **Easiest to save:** beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes, which mostly pollinate themselves.
- Squash, corn and cabbage-family plants cross with their relatives, so their seed may not grow true.
- Let seeds ripen fully on the plant: bean pods dry and rattle. Dry seeds indoors for 1 to 2 weeks, then store them cool, dry and dark in a sealed, labeled jar.
- Keep healthy small potatoes and sweet potatoes as next year's planting stock.
- See [Saving Seeds](../farming/save-seeds.md).

## Sprouting seeds indoors

Sprouts are the fastest fresh food and give some vitamin C.

1. Use seeds sold for eating or sprouting: mung beans, lentils, chickpeas, alfalfa, wheat. **Never use treated planting seed. Do not eat raw kidney bean sprouts.**
2. Rinse 2 tablespoons of small seeds, or a handful of beans, in safe water.
3. Soak in safe water for 8 to 12 hours.
4. Drain. Keep in a jar covered with clean cloth, tilted to drain, out of direct sun.
5. **Rinse and drain 2 to 3 times a day** with safe water.
6. Eat in 2 to 5 days, when the shoots are 1 to 3 cm (0.5 to 1 in) long.

> [!WARNING]
> The warm, damp conditions that grow sprouts also grow Salmonella and E. coli. Throw away sprouts that are slimy, smell bad or show mold (fine white root hairs are normal). Cook sprouts for young children, pregnant women, old people and anyone who is sick.
