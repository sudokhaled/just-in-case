> [!CAUTION]
> **Set aside next year's seed first, and never eat it,** even when you are hungry. Without it there is no harvest next year. Keep enough for 2 plantings, split between 2 places, so one loss to rats, damp or fire does not finish you.
>
> **Never eat seed coated in a colored powder** (pink, blue, green or red). It is treated with poison. Never eat seed potatoes that have turned green in the light.
>
> **Only seal seed that is completely dry.** Damp seed in a closed jar rots or dies within weeks.

## Quick steps

1. **Use open-pollinated seed,** not packets marked "F1" or "hybrid". Start with beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes: they are the easiest.
2. **Choose the best plants:** healthy, true to type, and many of them, not one.
3. **Keep varieties that cross apart** (see the table), or grow only one variety of each kind.
4. **Let seed ripen fully:** pods dry and rattle, fruit fully ripe or past eating.
5. **Clean it:** wash wet seeds (ferment tomato seeds for 1 to 3 days); beat and winnow dry seeds.
6. **Dry it in airy shade** until big seeds snap instead of bending. Keep seed below 35°C (95°F).
7. **Store it sealed, labeled, cool, dry and dark.**
8. **Test it** before you plant: sprout 20 seeds in a damp cloth.

## Which seed grows true

- **Open-pollinated (OP), heirloom or heritage** seed grows plants like the parent, as long as it did not cross with another variety.
- **Hybrid (F1)** seed comes from crossing 2 different parents. Its own seed gives mixed, often weaker plants. If hybrids are all you have, save their seed anyway and keep the best plants each year. After several years you will have your own local variety.
- **Patented or GMO** seed is often sold under contracts that forbid saving it. In a true emergency, feeding people comes first.
- **Market food can be seed.** Dried beans, peas, maize and pumpkin seeds often grow; test them first. White rice and split lentils will not.

## Easy and hard crops

**Self-pollinating** crops (beans, peas, lettuce, tomatoes) rarely cross, and seed from even 1 plant grows true.

**Cross-pollinating** crops get pollen from other plants by insects or wind. They cross with any variety of the **same species** nearby, including a neighbor's. They also need many plants: seed from only a few gives weaker plants each year (inbreeding). Maize suffers most.

Only the same species cross:

- Zucchini (courgette), acorn squash and most orange field pumpkins cross with each other. Butternut is another species, and Hubbard and buttercup squash a third. Different species rarely cross.
- Cabbage, kale, broccoli, cauliflower and collards cross with each other. So do turnip, pak choi, Chinese cabbage and many mustards.
- Hot chilies and sweet peppers cross. This year's fruit does not change, only its seed.

| Crop | Pollinated by | Distance between varieties | Plants to save from | Seed is ready when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common beans, peas | Self | 3 m (10 ft); 15 m (50 ft) for purer seed | 10 to 20 | Pods dry, brown and rattling |
| Lettuce | Self | 3 m (10 ft) | 10 to 20 | Two-thirds of the heads are fluffy |
| Tomatoes | Self, some insects | 3 m (10 ft); 12 to 15 m (40 to 50 ft) for potato-leaf types | 10 to 20 | Fruit fully ripe |
| Cowpeas, lima beans | Mostly self | 12 to 50 m (40 to 160 ft) | 40 | Pods dry |
| Sweet peppers / hot chilies | Self and insects | 50 m (160 ft) / 250 m (800 ft) | 10 to 40 | Fruit fully colored, never green |
| Okra, fava (broad) beans, runner beans | Insects and self | 250 m (800 ft) | 40 | Pods dry and hard |
| Squash, pumpkins, cucumbers, melons, watermelons | Insects | 500 to 800 m (1,600 ft to half a mile) | 10 to 20 | Fruit fully ripe; cucumbers yellow and soft |
| Maize (corn) | Wind | 500 m to 1.6 km (1,600 ft to 1 mile) | 100 to 200 or more | Husks dry, kernels hard |
| Cabbage family, radish, turnip, mustard | Insects | 500 to 800 m (1,600 ft to half a mile) | Up to 80 | Pods tan, before they split |
| Onions, carrots (seed in year 2) | Insects | 500 m (1,600 ft) or more | Up to 200 | Heads dry; black seed shows in onions |
| Beets, chard, spinach | Wind | 1 km (3,200 ft) or more | Up to 80 | Seed clusters brown |

These home-garden distances stop most crossing, not all. Sources disagree, so use the larger distance when you can. The plant numbers keep a variety strong for many years; fewer plants still give seed for next year.

**No room for distance?** Grow only 1 variety of each species, plant varieties so they flower at different times, or hand-pollinate. **Squash by hand:** in the evening, tape shut a male flower (thin stem) and a female flower (tiny fruit behind it) that will open next morning. In the morning, open both, rub the male's pollen onto the center of the female, and close the female again. Tie a string on its stem to mark the fruit for seed.

## Choosing the best plants

- Mark the healthiest, most productive plants. Pull out sick and odd-looking plants **before** they flower.
- For lettuce, spinach, cabbage and other leaf crops, save seed from the plants that flower (bolt) **last**, never the first. Seed from early bolters gives plants that bolt early.
- Take seed from many plants, not only the biggest fruit of 1 plant.
- Seed plants need time: lettuce eaten at 40 to 60 days may need 120 to 180 days to make seed.

## Collecting and cleaning

**Dry seeds** (beans, grains, lettuce, cabbage family, onions, okra):

1. Leave pods and seed heads on the plant until dry and brown. If rain threatens, pull plants when two-thirds of the pods are dry and hang them under a roof.
2. **Thresh:** put pods in a sack and beat or walk on it, or rub heads between your hands.
3. **Winnow:** pour the seed slowly from shoulder height in a light breeze onto a cloth. The light chaff blows away. See [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md).

**Wet seeds:**

- **Tomatoes:** squeeze seeds and juice into a jar and cover with a cloth. Leave at 21 to 29°C (70 to 85°F) for **1 to 3 days**, stirring daily, until a white mold film forms and seeds sink. In hot weather check daily: 3 days or more can damage seed. Add water, stir, pour off the floating pulp, repeat until clean, and strain.
- **Cucumbers:** leave fruit on the vine about **6 weeks** past eating stage, until large and yellow. Scoop out the seeds and wash them (ferment 1 day at most).
- **Melons, winter squash, pumpkins:** scoop the seed from fully ripe fruit and wash it.
- **Summer squash, zucchini:** leave fruit on the plant **6 to 8 weeks** past eating stage, until the skin is hard. Store it about 3 more weeks, then take the seed.
- **Peppers and chilies:** scrape the seeds from fully colored fruit. Wear gloves for hot chilies. Skip fruit with a moldy seed cavity.

Good seed usually sinks in water; empty seed floats.

## Drying

1. Spread seed thinly on cloth, a plate or a screen.
2. Dry in airy shade for **1 to 2 weeks**, stirring daily. Hot sun can cook seed.
3. **Test it:**
   - **Snap test:** a large seed (squash, bean) snaps cleanly. If it bends, keep drying.
   - **Hammer test:** a dry bean or maize kernel shatters. A damp one squashes.
   - **Bite test:** dry seed takes no tooth mark.

**In humid climates,** open air never dries seed enough. Finish it for 1 to 2 weeks in a sealed jar with a drying agent wrapped in cloth or paper:

- **Silica gel:** equal weight to the seed. Remove it after 1 to 2 weeks. To reuse it, heat it at about 120°C (250°F) until the color changes back.
- **Dried rice:** plain white rice, dried in a pan over low heat without browning, cooled in a closed jar. Use about 5 times the weight of the seed. Replace it each season.
- **Powdered milk:** 1 to 2 tablespoons.

## Storing

- Put each variety in a paper envelope. **Label it:** crop, variety, year, place.
- Put the envelopes in an **airtight** glass jar or tin with a tight lid. Plastic bags let damp in. Fill containers full: less air means fewer weevils.
- Keep it **cool, dry, dark and steady,** away from roofs and stoves. As a rough rule, every 5°C (9°F) cooler, or every 1% less water in the seed, doubles its life.
- A fridge or freezer helps **only** for fully dry seed in an airtight jar. Let the jar warm up before opening it, or water condenses on the seed.
- Check monthly for damp, mold, weevils and mice.

| Crop | Years alive, stored dry and cool |
|---|---|
| Onion, leek, parsnip, parsley | 1 to 2 |
| Maize (sweet corn), okra, peppers | 2 |
| Spinach | 1 to 3 |
| Beans, peas, carrots | 2 to 3 |
| Lettuce | 1 to 5 (sources disagree: test it) |
| Tomatoes, eggplant, cabbage family, squash, pumpkins, watermelon, beets, chard, radish | 3 to 4 |
| Cucumbers, melons | 5 |

Hot, humid storage shortens these times a lot.

## Testing seed before you plant

1. Count out **20 seeds** (10 if you have few).
2. Roll them in a damp cloth and put it in a jar or bag so it stays moist.
3. Keep it warm, about 20 to 25°C (68 to 77°F), and damp, not wet.
4. Count the sprouts after **7 to 14 days** (some crops: up to 3 weeks). 16 of 20 is 80%.

At 80% or more, sow normally. At 50 to 80%, sow more thickly. Under 50%, sow 2 to 3 times as thickly and grow a fresh seed crop this year. Do not count weak, slow sprouts.

## Potatoes, sweet potatoes, garlic and cassava

These carry diseases, so take them only from healthy plants.

- **Potatoes:** mark plants with clean green leaves. Skip plants with curled, yellow-mottled leaves (virus). Keep healthy tubers the size of a hen's egg. Store them cool and dark, or in thin layers on shelves in indirect daylight in a cool, airy room. In light they grow short, strong sprouts that plant well, but they turn green and poisonous: **label them and never eat them.** Potato berries are poisonous, and their seed does not grow true.
- **Sweet potatoes:** keep medium, unbroken roots. Cure them (see [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md)) and store at 13 to 16°C (55 to 60°F), **never below 10°C (50°F).** In warm climates, keep a patch growing all year and plant tip cuttings of its vines. In cold climates, sprout stored roots in warm, damp sand or water before planting time and break off the shoots (slips).
- **Garlic:** each clove grows 1 bulb. Keep your largest, firmest bulbs, stored dry and airy. Separate cloves only at planting time.
- **Cassava (manioc):** take stems from healthy plants at least 8 months old. Skip plants with yellow-mottled, curled leaves (mosaic virus). Use the woody middle of the stem, not the green top. Cut pieces **20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) long with 5 to 7 buds.** Plant within 1 week if you can. Until then, stand stems upright in shade, bases on moist soil; in sun they dry out and die.
