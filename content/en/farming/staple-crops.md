> [!CAUTION]
> **Never eat cassava (manioc) raw.** It contains cyanide. Bitter cassava must be peeled, then soaked, fermented, dried or wetted as flour before cooking: soak peeled pieces 3 to 5 days, changing the water daily, or wet the flour and leave it 5 hours in shade. Badly processed cassava can kill, or cause konzo, a permanent paralysis of the legs. If you do not know the variety, treat it as bitter.
>
> **If maize (corn) is most of what you eat,** cook it with lime or wood ash, and eat beans or groundnuts with it. Otherwise people develop pellagra, a deadly disease of the skin, gut and brain.
>
> **Never eat raw or undercooked kidney beans.** Soak them, pour off the water, and boil hard for at least **10 minutes.** Never eat potato leaves, stems, berries or green potatoes.

## Quick steps

1. **Work out the need:** about 2,100 kcal per person per day, or about **770,000 kcal per person per year.**
2. **Pick 2 or 3 main calorie crops** that suit your climate: potatoes where it is cool, sweet potatoes and cassava where it is hot and wet, sorghum, millet and cowpeas where it is hot and dry. Roots give the most calories per area; grains and dry beans store best.
3. **Plan roughly 400 to 800 m² (4,300 to 8,600 ft²) per person** for a full year. Start with what you can dig, water and guard.
4. **Always grow a legume** (beans, cowpeas, groundnuts, lentils) beside the starchy crop, for protein.
5. **Plant at the start of the main season.** See [When and How to Plant](../farming/planting-guide.md).
6. **Process risky crops safely:** cassava, maize, kidney beans, potatoes.
7. **Set aside next year's seed,** then dry and store the rest.

## Which crops give the most calories

Yields are for 10 m² (108 ft²), a bed 1 m by 10 m. Low: poor soil, little water or a first year. High: deep, compost-rich soil, enough water, good care. Grain and bean weights are dry, after threshing.

| Cassava (fresh roots) | Hot tropics, survives drought | 8-12 months (6-24) | 10-30 kg (22-66 lb) | 1,600 | 16,000-48,000 |
| Maize, dry grain | Warm, needs rain | 90-150 | 2-6 kg (4-13 lb) | 3,650 | 7,300-22,000 |
| Rice (milled) | Hot, a lot of water | 90-150 | 2-4 kg (4-9 lb) | 3,650 | 7,300-15,000 |

These are rough guides, not promises. Expect to lose 10 to 20% to pests and spoilage even in a good year.

## How much land for one person

These two plans use middle yields from the table and one harvest a year. They are **approximate.**

**Cool or temperate climate:**

| Crop | Share of calories | Harvest | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | 55% | 550 kg (1,200 lb) | 275 m² (2,960 ft²) |
| Wheat, barley or oats | 20% | 45 kg (100 lb) | 150 m² (1,600 ft²) |
| Dry beans or peas | 15% | 35 kg (77 lb) | 230 m² (2,500 ft²) |
| Winter squash, pumpkin | 5% | 95 kg (210 lb) | 50 m² (540 ft²) |
| Greens, other vegetables | 5% | Mixed | 50 m² (540 ft²) |
| **Total** | 100% | | **about 750 m² (8,100 ft²)** |

**Warm tropics with a rainy season:**

| Crop | Share of calories | Harvest | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cassava | 40% | 190 kg (420 lb) fresh roots | 160 m² (1,700 ft²) |
| Maize (corn) | 25% | 53 kg (117 lb) grain | 175 m² (1,900 ft²) |
| Sweet potatoes | 15% | 135 kg (300 lb) | 110 m² (1,200 ft²) |
| Beans, cowpeas or groundnuts | 15% | 35 kg (77 lb) beans or 20 kg (44 lb) groundnut kernels | 230 m² (2,500 ft²) |
| Pumpkins, greens, plantain | 5% | Mixed | 50 m² (540 ft²) |
| **Total** | 100% | | **about 730 m² (7,900 ft²)** |

- **Good soil, compost and water can halve the area.** Ecology Action reports a complete diet for 1 person on about 370 m² (4,000 ft²) of deep beds, including compost crops, against about 650 m² (7,000 ft²) with ordinary methods.
- **Two seasons a year,** common in the tropics, can halve the area for maize, beans and sweet potatoes.
- **Dry areas** growing sorghum, millet and cowpeas without watering may need 2 to 3 times as much land.
- **Keep seed back before you eat.** Potatoes need about 2 to 3 kg (4 to 7 lb) of healthy egg-sized tubers for every 10 m² you plant. See [Saving Seeds](../farming/save-seeds.md).
- For needs by age, see [Food Priorities and Rationing](../food/food-rationing.md).

## The crops in brief

- **Potatoes:** cool weather; in the tropics, highlands or the cool season. Plant egg-sized tubers 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) deep, 30 to 37 cm (12 to 15 in) apart, rows 60 to 75 cm (24 to 30 in) apart. Pile soil around the stems as they grow, until the ridge is 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) high. When leaves yellow, cut the tops, wait 10 days, then dig.
- **Sweet potatoes:** heat, no frost, well-drained soil. Plant vine cuttings 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) long with at least 2 leaf joints buried, on ridges 90 to 120 cm (3 to 4 ft) apart, 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) apart along the ridge. Dig carefully when leaves yellow; bruised roots rot. Orange-fleshed types are rich in vitamin A; pale ones have very little.
- **Cassava (manioc, yuca):** hot tropics, best at 20 to 29°C (68 to 84°F); frost kills it. It survives drought and poor soil. Plant pieces of mature stem 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) long, buds pointing up, at a slant, about 1 m (3 ft) apart each way. Dig only what you need: roots spoil **2 to 3 days** after digging, but keep for months in the ground.
- **Maize (corn):** warm weather, rich soil, steady water when tassels and silks appear. Sow 3 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) deep, 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) apart, rows 75 cm (30 in) apart, in a block of at least 4 rows for pollination. For grain, leave cobs on the plant until the kernels are hard.
- **Dry beans:** warm, no frost; harvest when pods rattle. **Cowpeas** suit heat and drought best. **Lentils and chickpeas** grow in the cool season. **Groundnuts** need loose, sandy soil, because the flower stalks push into the soil to form pods; pull whole plants and dry them upside down for 3 to 4 days. **Soybeans** have the most protein but must always be cooked. Legumes feed the soil with nitrogen and are 20 to 36% protein.
- **Squash and pumpkins:** few calories per kilogram, but heavy yields; winter squash keeps for months. The seeds are rich in fat and protein.
- **Grains:** wheat, barley, oats and rye for cool seasons; rice where water is plentiful; sorghum and millet for heat and drought. Sow in rows 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in) apart or scatter evenly. Harvest when stalks are golden and grain is too hard to dent with a fingernail: cut, bundle, dry, beat out the grain and winnow it. Grains give fewer calories per area than roots and take more work, but **dry grain keeps for years.** Guard sorghum and millet from birds.
- **Plantain and cooking bananas:** humid, frost-free places. Plant suckers 2 to 3 m (7 to 10 ft) apart. The first bunch takes 9 to 18 months; new suckers keep producing for years. See [Fruit Trees and Perennial Food Plants](../farming/perennial-food.md).

## Making staple crops safe to eat

### Cassava: remove the cyanide

**Sweet cassava:** peel thickly, removing the peel and the layer under it, boil until soft, and **pour away the water.**

**Bitter cassava, or any you are unsure of,** needs one of these before cooking:

1. **Soak and ferment:** peel, cut or grate, and soak in water for **3 to 5 days, changing the water daily,** until soft and sour-smelling. Then sun-dry and pound into flour, or cook.
2. **Grate and ferment** (as for gari): leave the grated mash in a cloth bag for 2 to 3 days, squeeze out the juice, then roast it dry in a pan.
3. **Sun-dry** peeled slices for 1 to 3 days. For bitter cassava, drying alone may not be enough; soak or ferment first.

**Wetting method for cassava flour** (makes any flour much safer):

1. Put the flour in a bowl and mark its level on the inside.
2. Add water while mixing. The level drops, then rises. Stop when it reaches the mark.
3. Spread the wet flour on a mat or tray **no thicker than about 1 cm** (half an inch, a fingernail's width).
4. Leave it **5 hours in the shade or 2 hours in the sun,** so the cyanide escapes as gas.
5. Cook it the same day, as stiff porridge.

**Cassava leaves:** pound for 10 to 15 minutes, then boil for at least 10 minutes.

> [!WARNING]
> Cassava grown in drought, and cassava eaten in famine with little else, is the most dangerous. Process it even more thoroughly, and eat beans, fish or other protein with it when you can. Never drink the soaking water. Cyanide poisoning causes vomiting, dizziness, headache, weakness and fast breathing within hours. See [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md).

### Maize: preventing pellagra

Pellagra comes from a lack of niacin (vitamin B3). The niacin in maize stays locked up unless maize is cooked with an alkali. Signs: a dark, rough rash on skin exposed to sun, diarrhea and confusion. It can kill. See [Malnutrition in Children and Adults](../medical/malnutrition.md).

**Eat beans, groundnuts, fish, meat or eggs with maize.** Groundnuts are especially rich in niacin.

**Nixtamalization (lime-cooking):**

1. For 1 kg (2.2 lb) of dry maize kernels, use 2 liters (2 quarts) of water and **10 to 20 g of slaked lime** (calcium hydroxide, "cal"), about 1 level tablespoon per liter of water.
2. Simmer for 20 to 60 minutes, until the skins loosen.
3. Leave to soak overnight, 8 to 16 hours.
4. Rinse in clean water 2 or 3 times, rubbing off the skins.
5. Grind wet into dough, or cook the whole kernels as hominy.

**No lime?** Traditional cooks use sifted ash from hardwood fires instead. Its strength varies, so cook until the skins slip off easily, then rinse very well. Keep lime and ash water out of eyes and away from children: both burn.

### Kidney beans and other dry beans

1. Soak for at least 5 hours, or overnight, and pour away the water.
2. Boil hard in fresh water for at least **10 minutes.**
3. Then cook until soft, in any way you like.

Beans heated but not boiled (slow cooker, haybox, solar cooker) can be **more** poisonous than raw beans. A few raw beans cause severe vomiting within 1 to 3 hours. See [Cooking Without Power](../fire/cook-without-power.md).

### Potatoes

The poison (solanine) collects in green skin, sprouts and eyes; cooking does not destroy it. Store potatoes in the dark, cut away green parts and sprouts deeply, and throw away potatoes that are green all through or taste bitter.

### Moldy grain and nuts

- **Aflatoxin:** mold on maize and groundnuts can make a liver poison. Throw away moldy, discolored or shriveled kernels and nuts, and dry crops quickly after harvest.
- **Ergot:** dark purple or black, horn-shaped grains in rye, wheat or barley are a poisonous fungus. Pick them out and burn them.
- **Grass pea** (*Lathyrus sativus*, khesari) survives drought, but eaten as a main food for weeks it causes permanent leg paralysis. Never make it the main food.

For drying and storage, see [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md).
