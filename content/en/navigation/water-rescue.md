> [!CAUTION]
> **Do not jump in to save someone.** Many rescuers drown, even strong swimmers. A drowning person climbs onto you and pushes you under. Reach or throw from land.
>
> **Drowning is silent.** The person cannot shout or wave, and may sink in less than a minute.
>
> **Cold water makes you gasp.** If you fall in, keep your face out of the water and float until your breathing calms, about 1 minute.

## Quick steps

1. **Shout for help.** Call 112, 911 or your local emergency number. Keep watching the person.
2. **Talk:** tell them to kick, float on their back and grab anything that floats.
3. **Reach:** lie down on the bank and hold out a pole, branch or clothing.
4. **Throw** anything that floats, or a rope tied to something that floats.
5. **Row** out in a boat only if you can do it safely.
6. **Go** into the water only if you are trained, with something that floats.
7. **Not breathing normally once out?** Start CPR with **5 rescue breaths**.
8. **Get everyone who nearly drowned checked.** Breathing problems can start hours later.

## What drowning looks like

Most people slip under quietly, often unnoticed by people nearby. Look for a person **upright** in the water, **head tilted back,** mouth at the surface, gasping, with **arms pressing down** at the sides instead of waving. They cannot breathe and shout at the same time.

Ask: "Are you all right?" No answer means act now. A child who suddenly goes quiet in the water is in danger.

## Talk, reach, throw, row, go

Use the first method that works. Each one lower down is more dangerous.

- **Talk.** Shout: "Kick! Lie on your back! Grab the rope!" If they go under, remember the exact spot.
- **Reach.** Lie flat on the bank, so you cannot be pulled in, and hold a post or have someone hold your ankles. Hold out a pole, branch, towel or belt.
- **Throw** a ring buoy (lifebuoy), life jacket, closed empty bottle, cooler, ball or plank. Tie a rope to a float, hold the other end, and throw it across them.
- **Row.** Wear a life jacket. Hold out an oar, then let them hold the back of the boat: pulling them over the side can tip it. Stop the engine near them.
- **Go (trained only).** Push a float to them so it stays between you. If they grab you, take a breath and sink: they let go to stay at the surface.

**Never tie a rope around yourself** in a water rescue. If the person or the current pulls hard, you go in too.

## Moving water: rivers and floods

- **Never wade in above your knees in moving water.** Fast water is much stronger than it looks. See [Living by a River or Lake](../land/river-living.md).
- **Do not stand up** in fast water deeper than your knees. A foot can jam between rocks and the current pushes you under.
- **If swept away:** float on your back, feet first downstream. To reach the bank, roll over and swim hard toward it at an angle.
- **Avoid fallen trees and fences in the water.** Water flows through; a person is pinned.
- **Stay away from weirs and low dams.** The water below them circles back and holds people under. Rescue only with a rope from the bank.

See [Floods](../disasters/flood.md).

## Rip currents

A rip current is a narrow, fast flow from the beach out to sea. It pulls you **out, not under,** at up to 2.4 m (8 ft) per second, faster than the best swimmers. Look for a gap where waves are not breaking, a channel of darker or choppy water, or foam moving out to sea.

1. **Do not swim against it.** Strong swimmers drown this way.
2. **Float on your back** to rest. Wave one arm and shout. Many rips circle back toward the beach.
3. **Swim parallel to the beach** until you are out of the pull, then at an angle to shore.

## Cold water

Water below about 15°C (60°F) is dangerous. The times in the **1-10-1** rule are approximate: they depend on the water, your body and your clothes.

| Time | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| About 1 minute | Cold shock: gasping, fast breathing | Face out of the water. Float, calm your breathing. |
| About 10 minutes | Hands and arms work, then weaken | Get out or onto something that floats, now. |
| About 1 hour | In icy water, unconscious from hypothermia | Keep still, head up, wait for rescue. |

- **Keep your clothes on.** Climb as far out of the water as you can, even onto an upturned boat.
- **Keep still** if you cannot get out. Swimming and treading water cool you faster.
- **HELP position** in a life jacket: knees to chest, arms crossed over chest. **Huddle** with others in life jackets: chests together, children in the middle.

Lift a rescued person lying flat if you can: lifting upright can make them collapse. See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](../medical/hypothermia-frostbite.md).

## Ice

### How thick is safe?

Rules of thumb for new, **clear, hard** ice. They are approximate and differ between countries.

| Clear ice | Use |
|---|---|
| Less than 10 cm (4 in) | Stay off |
| 10-15 cm (4-6 in) | One person on foot. 15 cm (6 in) is safer. |
| 20 cm (8 in) or more | A group, spread out |
| 20-30 cm (8-12 in) or more | A small car. Avoid driving on ice. |

- **White or cloudy ice** is about half as strong: double the thickness. **Grey ice** is melting: stay off.
- **Ice is thinner** on rivers, near inflows, outflows, reeds and rocks, and under snow.
- **Check as you go.** Measure at a hole near shore. Strike the ice ahead with a heavy pole every few steps. If it breaks easily, go back.
- **Never go alone.** Walk several meters apart. Carry a rope and 2 ice picks (large nails through short wooden handles).

### If you fall through

1. **Control your breathing** for the first minute. Keep your head up.
2. **Turn back toward the way you came.** That ice held you.
3. **Put your arms flat on the ice,** with ice picks if you have them.
4. **Kick hard** to lie flat, then kick and pull yourself onto the ice, like a seal.
5. **Do not stand up.** Roll away from the hole, then crawl back.
6. **Get warm at once:** shelter, dry clothes, wrap up.

Still in after about 10 minutes? Stop struggling and keep your arms on the ice. Your sleeves may freeze to it and hold you up.

### If someone falls through

1. **Do not run to the hole.** The ice will break under you too.
2. **Shout:** "Turn around! Arms on the ice! Kick!"
3. **Reach from shore** with a pole, rope or ladder. If you must go onto the ice, **lie flat** and push it ahead of you. A rope with a loop can go under their arms (see [Essential Knots](../skills/knots.md)).
4. **Pull while they kick.** Tell them to roll away from the hole.
5. **A human chain** of people lying flat, each holding the ankles of the one in front, is a last resort.

## After any rescue

1. **Not breathing normally:** give **5 rescue breaths,** then 30 compressions and 2 breaths. See [CPR: When Someone Is Not Breathing](../medical/cpr.md).
2. **Do not try to push water out.** It wastes time.
3. **If they vomit,** roll them onto their side, clear the mouth, and continue.
4. **Breathing but not responding:** recovery position. See [First Aid Basics: Check, Call, Care](../medical/first-aid-basics.md).
5. **Keep them warm,** even on a warm day.

**Everyone who nearly drowned needs a medical check,** even if they seem fine. With no doctor, watch them closely for at least 8 hours. Get help urgently for a cough that does not stop, fast or noisy breathing, blue lips, confusion or unusual sleepiness. Fever days later can be a lung infection.

## Preventing drowning

- **A baby can drown in a few centimeters (an inch or two) of water** without a sound. Never leave a baby alone in a bath, even for a moment.
- **Keep children under 5,** and any child who cannot swim, within arm's reach near water. The watching adult does nothing else.
- **Cover** wells, tanks and barrels. Empty buckets after use. **Fence pools** on all 4 sides, at least 1.2 m (4 ft) high, with a self-closing, self-latching gate.
- **Wear a life jacket** on boats and ice. Most people who drown in boating accidents were not wearing one. Do not board an overloaded boat.
- **No alcohol** when swimming, boating or watching children.

See [Keeping Children Safe and Calm](../community/children-safety.md).
