> [!CAUTION]
> **Stop walking as soon as you think you are lost.** Hurrying on in panic leads to falls, exhaustion and walking farther from the people searching for you.
>
> **Following a stream downhill can lead to waterfalls, cliffs and steep gorges.** Never climb down a waterfall or steep wet rock. In dry areas, dry streambeds can flood suddenly.
>
> **Prepare for the night before dark.** Cold, wind and rain can kill overnight, even in summer. See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](../medical/hypothermia-frostbite.md).

## Quick steps: STOP

1. **S - Stop.** Sit down. Drink some water and eat a little. Breathe slowly until you feel calmer.
2. **T - Think.** When and where did you last know where you were? Who knows your plan, and when will they raise the alarm?
3. **O - Observe.** Look and listen: landmarks, trails, roads, water, traffic or voices. Check the weather, how much daylight is left, and what you are carrying.
4. **P - Plan.** Decide to stay or to move. **If you are unsure, stay.**
5. **Make yourself easy to find:** whistle, bright colors, phone, signals.
6. **Make shelter and, if safe, a fire** at least 1 hour before dark.

Fear is normal. It passes faster when you have a task. See [Survival Mindset: Controlling Fear and Stress](../start/survival-mindset.md).

## Stay or move?

Searchers start from where you were last seen and from your planned route. Every hour you walk makes the search area much larger.

### Stay put when

- Someone knows your route and when you should be back.
- You are hurt, very tired, or with children or slow walkers.
- It is dark, foggy, or the weather is bad.
- You are with a vehicle, boat or aircraft.
- You have no idea which way to go.

### Move when

- Nobody knows you are there and nobody will search soon.
- Where you are is dangerous: rising water, fire, falling rocks, avalanche risk, or no shelter from cold or heat.
- You are sure of the way: you can retrace your steps to a known point, or you can see or hear a road or village.
- You have no water and know where to find it.
- Days have passed with no sign of a search, and you have a clear plan.

### If you move

1. **Leave a note** where you were, with the date, time, your direction and your plan. Mark it with an arrow on the ground.
2. **Travel only in daylight.** Stop at least 1 hour before dark.
3. **Mark your trail** as you go (see below), so you can return and others can follow.
4. **Head for a long feature** you cannot miss, such as a road, river, power line or coast. See [Navigating Without GPS](../navigation/navigate-no-gps.md).
5. **Go uphill for a view** only if it is safe. Do not climb cliffs or loose rock.
6. **Stop and rest** before you are exhausted. Tired people fall and make bad decisions.

## Make yourself findable

- **Stay in or near an open area** where you can be seen from the air, close to the trail if possible.
- **Spread out bright clothing** or gear in the open.
- **Blow 3 whistle blasts,** pause, repeat. A whistle carries farther than shouting and costs less energy.
- **Use your phone:** call the emergency number, or text if calls fail. Save the battery with airplane mode between tries.
- **Make ground signals and get a smoky fire ready.** See [Signaling for Rescue](../navigation/signal-rescue.md).
- **Answer searchers.** If you hear your name called, shout back and blow your whistle. Some lost people hide from searchers out of fear or embarrassment. Do not.

## Following water downstream

Water often leads to valleys, roads and villages, but not always, and the route can be dangerous.

- **Walk beside the stream,** on the bank or the slope above it, not in the water.
- **Turn back or go around** when you reach a waterfall, gorge or cliff. Never climb down it.
- **Avoid dense brush and swamps** along the water: they exhaust you quickly.
- **In deserts and dry hills,** do not camp in dry streambeds. Rain far away can send a flash flood without warning.
- In large wild areas, rivers may run for days without passing a road. Following water is a reason to move only when staying is not a choice. See [Living in a Tropical Rainforest](../land/jungle-living.md).

## Lost children

Teach children the **"hug a tree"** rule before every trip:

- **Stay in one place.** Pick a tree and stay next to it. Moving makes you harder to find.
- **Blow your whistle** 3 times, again and again. Shout if you have no whistle.
- **Do not hide.** People calling your name are there to help. **You will not be in trouble.**
- **Make yourself big and bright:** sit in the open, wear your bright jacket.
- **Stay warm:** sit on something dry, hug your knees.

Give every child a whistle on a cord, a bright jacket, a large plastic bag with a hole cut for the face, a snack and water. The bag works as a raincoat and windbreak.

> [!WARNING]
> Always cut a face hole in a plastic bag before giving it to a child. **Never let a bag cover a child's face or head:** it can suffocate them.

**If your child is missing,** call the emergency number at once. Do not wait to see if they come back. Check nearby water, wells, holes and parked cars first: these are where missing children are in the most danger. See [Keeping Children Safe and Calm](../community/children-safety.md).

## Trail markers

When you move, leave signs so you can find your way back and others can follow:

- **Arrows** of sticks or stones on the ground, pointing the way you went.
- **Broken branches** at eye level, left hanging and pointing your way.
- **Small stone piles** with a stone beside them pointing your direction.
- **Strips of bright cloth or tape** tied to branches, each one in sight of the last.
- **Scratched arrows** in dirt, sand or snow, and a note at every junction.

## Conserving energy

- **Rest more than you think you need.** Sit on your pack or branches, not on the cold ground.
- **In heat,** rest in the shade during the hottest hours, move only in the cool morning and evening, and keep covered. Drink the water you have when you are thirsty. Saving water while your body dries out does not help; sweat less instead.
- **In cold,** stay dry. Wet clothing loses heat very fast. Put on all your layers before you get cold.
- **Food can wait.** You can live for weeks without food, but only about 3 days without water. Shelter and water come first.

## The night

1. **Stop at least 1 hour before dark.** Walking at night causes falls, especially near cliffs and streams.
2. **Find or build shelter** out of the wind and away from dead trees, dry streambeds and steep slopes. See [Building an Emergency Shelter](../shelter/emergency-shelter.md).
3. **Insulate yourself from the ground** with a thick layer of dry branches, leaves, pine needles or your pack. The ground steals body heat faster than the air.
4. **Put on every layer** of clothing you have. Cover your head.
5. **Light a fire** if you can do it safely. It gives warmth, light, comfort and a signal.
6. **Keep your whistle and light** within reach. Searchers often keep working at night with lights and heat cameras. Flash your light toward any lights or engine sounds.

Night sounds are normal. Most wild animals avoid people. Talk or sing quietly to stay calm.

## Before you go

The best protection is to never be lost without anyone knowing:

- **Tell someone** where you are going, your route, and when you will be back. Tell them when to raise the alarm.
- **Carry** a whistle, a light, water, a warm layer, a charged phone, a map and a compass.
- **Agree a family plan:** "If we get separated, stay where you are, and we will come to you."
