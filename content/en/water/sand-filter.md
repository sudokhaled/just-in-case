> [!CAUTION]
> **A home-made filter does not make water safe to drink.** Even a well-built biosand filter lets some germs through, especially viruses. Always boil, chlorinate or use sunlight after filtering. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).
>
> **Filters do not remove salt, fuel, pesticides or heavy metals.** Charcoal reduces some smells and chemicals, but not enough to make poisoned water safe.

## Quick steps

These steps make a simple bucket or bottle filter.

1. **Make the container:** drill or punch small holes in the bottom of a bucket, or cut the bottom off a plastic bottle and turn it upside down.
2. **Cover the holes** (or the bottle neck) with a clean cloth.
3. **Add washed layers, from the bottom up:** 5 cm (2 in) small gravel, 5 cm (2 in) coarse sand, 5-10 cm (2-4 in) crushed charcoal, then at least 20 cm (8 in) fine sand.
4. **Put a cloth or a plate with holes on top,** so pouring does not dig into the sand.
5. **Run water through** until it comes out clear. Throw this first water away.
6. **Pour slowly** and collect the water in a clean container.
7. **Disinfect the filtered water.**

## What each layer does

- **Fine sand** traps mud and many germs. The finer the sand and the slower the flow, the more it traps.
- **Charcoal** holds on to some colors, smells, tastes and chemicals.
- **Coarse sand and gravel** stop the fine sand and charcoal from washing out.

Filtering cloudy water first makes chlorine and sunlight work much better, because dirt protects germs.

## Materials

### Sand and gravel

- **Fine sand:** grains about the size of table sugar or smaller. River sand or crushed rock both work. Beach sand works if you wash it until the rinse water no longer tastes salty.
- **Coarse sand:** grains about 1 to 6 mm.
- **Gravel:** small stones about 6 to 12 mm (1/4 to 1/2 in).
- **Wash everything:** put the sand in a bucket, cover it with water, stir hard and pour off the muddy water. Repeat until the water stays fairly clear.

### Charcoal

- Use charcoal from a wood fire: pieces that are black all the way through, with no ash.
- Crush it into pieces the size of rice grains or peas.
- Rinse it until the water runs clear, or black dust will come out of the filter.
- **Never use** charcoal briquettes or barbecue charcoal (they contain coal, glue and lighter fluid), or charcoal from painted wood, treated wood, plastic or rubbish.

### Charcoal or activated carbon?

| Question | Fire charcoal | Activated carbon |
|---|---|---|
| How it is made | Wood burned with little air | Charcoal heated with steam or chemicals to open millions of tiny pores |
| Removes taste, smell, color | A little | Well, until it is full |
| Removes chemicals | Very little | Some, such as chlorine and some pesticides and fuels. Not all |
| Kills germs | No | No |

Activated carbon comes from water filter cartridges, aquarium shops or pharmacies. Home "activation" with salt or lemon juice is unreliable. Treat home-made charcoal as ordinary charcoal.

## The biosand filter: the best home-made filter

A biosand filter is a tall, slow sand filter. A thin living layer of tiny organisms grows in the top 1 to 2 cm (1/2 to 1 in) of sand. This "biolayer" eats many germs. The water always stays 5 cm (2 in) above the sand, so the biolayer stays wet but still gets air.

```
  +======================+
  |  diffuser: plate     |
  |  with small holes    |
  |  - - - - - - - - - - |
  |                      |
  |~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~| A         +==> out
  |......................|           |
  |.... fine sand .......| 50 cm     |
  |......................| (20 in)   |
  |......................|           |
  |:::: small gravel ::::| 5 cm      |
  |oooo large gravel ====|===========+
  +======================+ 5 cm

  A = water rests here, 5 cm (2 in) above
      the sand, level with the outlet
```

### Build it

1. **Container:** a food-grade plastic barrel or bin, or a concrete box, about 90 cm (3 ft) tall, with a lid.
2. **Outlet pipe:** fix a pipe or tube that starts at the bottom, inside the large gravel, and rises to an outlet. The outlet must be exactly **5 cm (2 in) higher than the top of the sand.** This keeps the resting water at the right level.
3. **Large gravel:** 5 cm (2 in), covering the pipe opening.
4. **Small gravel or coarse sand:** 5 cm (2 in).
5. **Fine sand:** about 50 cm (20 in). Use at least 40 cm (16 in). Pour it in with water already in the container, so no air gets trapped.
6. **Diffuser:** a plate or lid with many small holes, resting a few centimeters above the water. It stops pouring from digging into the sand.
7. **Lid** on top, to keep out dust, insects and animals.

Once you have filled it, do not move the filter. Moving it mixes the layers.

### Use it

1. Pour in 1 bucket of water. It flows out of the outlet and then stops by itself when the water falls to the resting level.
2. **Wait at least 1 hour** after the flow stops before you pour the next bucket. The biolayer needs this time to work.
3. **Use it every day.** Do not leave it more than 2 days without water, and never let it dry out.
4. **Check the flow** when the filter is full: a 1 liter (1 quart) bottle should take at least 2.5 minutes to fill, which is 0.4 liters per minute or slower. If it fills faster, the sand is too coarse.
5. Collect the water in a clean container. Do not touch the outlet.

> [!NOTE]
> The biolayer takes **2 to 3 weeks** of daily use to grow, and up to 30 days. During this time the filter removes fewer germs. Even when mature, it removes most bacteria and parasites but fewer viruses. **Disinfect the water every time,** for as long as you use the filter.

### Clean it when it gets too slow

Use the "swirl and dump" method:

1. Pour in water so there is some above the sand.
2. Lift out the diffuser.
3. Swirl the top 1 to 2 cm (1/2 to 1 in) of sand gently with your hand.
4. Scoop out the dirty water and throw it away from drinking water.
5. Repeat until the flow is fast enough.
6. Put the diffuser back. Wash your hands with soap.

The biolayer is disturbed and needs a few days to recover.

## Looking after a simple bucket filter

- When the flow slows, scrape off the top 1 to 2 cm (1/2 to 1 in) of sand. Wash it or replace it.
- Take the filter apart and wash all layers every few weeks.
- Replace the charcoal about once a month, or sooner if the water starts to taste or smell bad again. Old charcoal can grow germs.
- Wash your hands after handling dirty sand.

## What filters can and cannot do

| Problem | Sand and charcoal filter | Biosand filter (mature) |
|---|---|---|
| Mud, cloudiness | Removes most | Removes most |
| Bad taste and smell | Reduces | Reduces |
| Bacteria | Some | Most, not all |
| Viruses | Few | Some |
| Parasites (giardia, crypto) and worm eggs | Some | Most |
| Salt | **No** | **No** |
| Fuel, pesticides, metals | **No** | **No** |
