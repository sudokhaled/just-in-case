> [!CAUTION]
> **Never drink seawater or urine,** not even a little, not even mixed with fresh water. The salt makes you lose more water than you drink. Only distillation (boiling and collecting the steam) makes them safe.
>
> **Never drink water from car radiators or home heating systems.** It contains antifreeze and other chemicals that kill. Antifreeze tastes sweet.
>
> **Treat all water you find** unless it is from a sealed bottle. Diarrhea from dirty water can kill, especially children. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

## Quick steps

1. **Look inside buildings first:** water heater tank, pipes, the toilet tank (not the bowl), roof tanks, ice cubes, bottles, liquid in canned food.
2. **Catch rain** with any clean sheet, roof or container.
3. **Find surface water:** springs, streams, rivers, lakes. Take it from moving water, upstream of people and animals.
4. **Melt snow or ice** in a pot. Do not eat it frozen.
5. **Dig** at the outside of a bend in a dry riverbed, or where green plants grow.
6. **Treat everything** by boiling, chlorine or sunlight before you drink it.
7. **Lose less water:** stay in shade, rest in the hottest hours, cover your skin and head.

## How much water you need

| Need | Per person per day |
|---|---|
| Survival: drinking and water in food | 2.5-3 liters (about 3 quarts) |
| Basic hygiene | 2-6 liters (0.5-1.5 gallons) |
| Basic cooking | 3-6 liters (0.75-1.5 gallons) |
| **Total basic needs (humanitarian minimum)** | **15 liters (4 gallons)** |

- In heat or with hard work, you can sweat more than 1 liter (1 quart) an hour. Drink more.
- Children, pregnant and breastfeeding women, and sick people need more.
- Pale yellow urine means you are drinking enough. Dark urine, little urine, headache and dizziness mean you need more. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Do not ration the water you have while you are thirsty.** Drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow. Save sweat instead: stay in shade and move only in the cool hours. When water is very short, eat little salty food or meat, because digesting them uses water.

## Best sources, in order

| Rank | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sealed bottled water, your stored water | Safest |
| 2 | Water hidden in buildings | Often clean. Treat if unsure |
| 3 | Rain, snow, ice | Usually clean. Treat anyway |
| 4 | Springs and deep wells | Often clean. Treat, especially after floods |
| 5 | Streams, rivers, lakes | Germs from people and animals. Always treat |
| 6 | Ponds, ditches, floodwater | Sewage and chemicals. Last resort |
| 7 | Dew, plants, digging | Small amounts |
| 8 | Solar still, distilled seawater or urine | Very small amounts. Emergency only |

Let muddy water settle and filter it before you treat it. See [Building a Sand and Charcoal Filter](../water/sand-filter.md). Keep the water you find in clean, covered containers. See [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

## Water hidden in a building

If the town water may be dirty (floods, earthquakes, broken mains), close the main water valve first. This stops dirty water from getting into your pipes and tank.

### Water heater tank

Hot water tanks and cylinders often hold 100 to 200 liters (25 to 50 gallons).

1. Turn off the electricity or gas to the heater. Do not turn it back on until the tank is full again, or it can be damaged or start a fire.
2. Close the cold water inlet valve, usually on the pipe at the top.
3. Open any hot water tap in the building to let air in.
4. Open the drain valve at the bottom and collect the water. Let hot water cool first.
5. Let rust and sediment settle, then treat the water.

### Pipes

1. Open the highest tap in the building to let air in.
2. Collect the water from the lowest tap.

### Other places

- **Toilet tank (cistern):** the box behind the seat holds 5 to 15 liters (1 to 4 gallons) of clean tap water. Treat it before drinking. Do not use it if it contains colored cleaner or tablets. **Never** use water from the toilet bowl.
- **Roof tanks and loft tanks:** common in many countries, and they can hold hundreds of liters. Treat this water: open tanks collect dust, insects and dead animals.
- **Ice cubes, and liquid in canned fruit and vegetables.**
- **Swimming pools, hot tubs and waterbeds:** use for washing and flushing toilets only. Their chemicals make them unsafe to drink.

## Rain

Rain is often the easiest clean source. Spread a tarp, plastic sheet or raincoat with one corner lower than the others, and put a container under that corner. Let the first rain wash the surface before you collect. See [Collecting Rainwater](../water/rainwater.md).

## Snow and ice

- **Melt it. Do not eat it.** Eating snow chills your body and gives very little water.
- Put a little water in the pot first, then add snow a handful at a time. Snow in a dry pot can scorch and burn through the pot.
- Use ice if you can. Fresh snow can be 10 times as bulky as the water it makes, so ice gives far more water for the same fuel.
- With no fuel, put snow in a dark container in the sun.
- Treat melted water. Do not use colored or dirty snow.

## Dew

Before sunrise, wipe dew from grass, plastic sheets or car roofs with a clean cloth and wring it into a container. Or tie clean cloths around your ankles and walk through tall wet grass. In very heavy dew this can give up to about 1 liter (1 quart) an hour, but most nights give much less. Avoid grass that may have been sprayed with chemicals.

## Plants

### Transpiration bag

1. Choose a leafy tree or bush in full sun. Use only a plant you know is not poisonous.
2. Put a clear plastic bag over a leafy branch, with a small stone inside so one corner hangs lowest.
3. Tie the bag tightly around the branch.
4. Wait several hours, or all day. Water gathers in the low corner.
5. Untie and pour the water out. Move the bag to a fresh branch each day.

Expect a small amount: often a cup (250 mL) or less per bag per day. Use several bags. If the water tastes bitter or strange, throw it away.

### Other plants

- **Green (young) coconuts** hold safe, plentiful liquid. The liquid of ripe brown coconuts can cause diarrhea if you drink a lot.
- Do not drink plant sap that is milky, sticky, colored or bitter.
- **Do not rely on cactus.** The juice of most cactus causes vomiting or diarrhea, which makes dehydration worse.

## Digging for water

**Dig** at the lowest point on the outside of a bend in a dry riverbed, where green plants grow in dry land, at the foot of cliffs, or where the ground is damp. See [Living in the Desert](../land/desert-living.md).

Dig in the early morning or evening, not in the midday heat. If you reach damp sand, dig a little deeper and wait. Water can take an hour or more to seep in. Let the mud settle, and treat the water.

**On a beach,** dig behind the first sand dune, well above the high-tide line. Fresh water can collect on top of the salty water underground. Taste a tiny sip. If it is salty, spit it out. See [Living by the Sea](../land/coast-living.md).

## Signs of water nearby

- Green plants in dry land: reeds, rushes, willows, palms, lines of trees along a valley.
- Animal trails that join together and lead downhill.
- Seed-eating birds such as pigeons and doves flying low and straight at dawn and dusk. Birds of prey are no guide.
- Bees, and swarms of mosquitoes or flies.
- Low ground, valley floors, and rock hollows that hold rain.
- Paths, wells and pumps made by people.

## Solar stills: very little water

Dig a bowl-shaped hole about 1 m (3 ft) across and 60 cm (2 ft) deep, in damp soil and full sun. Put a container in the middle. Cover the hole with clear plastic and seal the edges with soil. Put a small stone on the plastic right above the container. Water condenses under the plastic and drips into the container.

> [!NOTE]
> A solar still makes very little water: at best about 0.5 to 1 liter (2 to 4 cups) in 24 hours, and often much less. Digging it in the heat can cost more sweat than it gives. Its real use is to distill salty or dirty water: pour seawater or urine into the hole around the container, never into it.

## Seawater and urine: distill only

Seawater has more salt than your kidneys can remove, so drinking it pulls water out of your body. Urine has the same effect. Distillation makes both safe:

1. Put the seawater or urine in a pot. Stand a clean cup in the middle on stones, so the cup sits above the liquid.
2. Turn the pot lid upside down, so the handle points down over the cup.
3. Boil gently. Steam turns back into water on the lid, runs down to the handle and drips into the cup.
4. Put snow, cold water or a wet cloth on top of the lid. A cooler lid makes more water.
5. Taste the water in the cup. If it is salty, liquid splashed in. Boil more gently.

Distillation gives little water and uses a lot of fuel, but the water it makes is safe.
