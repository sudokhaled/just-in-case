> [!CAUTION]
> **Never store drinking water in a container that held fuel, chemicals, pesticides or any poison,** even after washing. Traces stay in the plastic.
>
> **Keep lids on and keep small children away from open buckets and tanks.** A toddler can drown in a bucket with only a few centimeters (inches) of water.
>
> **Never dip hands or cups into stored drinking water.** This is how clean water becomes dirty. Pour it, or use a tap.

## Quick steps

1. **Store at least 4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day,** for at least 2 weeks if you can, and never less than 3 days.
2. **Use food-grade containers** with tight lids.
3. **Clean and sanitize each container:** wash with soap, rinse, then shake with 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of bleach in 1 liter (1 quart) of water. Empty and air-dry.
4. **Fill** with tap water from a treated public supply. Treat any other water first.
5. **Label** each container "drinking water" with the date.
6. **Store** it cool and dark, away from fuel and chemicals.
7. **Replace** home-filled water every 6 months.

## How much to store

Half of the 4 liters (1 gallon) is for drinking. The rest is for food and basic washing.

| People | 3 days | 2 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 liters (3 gallons) | 56 liters (14 gallons) |
| 2 | 24 liters (6 gallons) | 112 liters (28 gallons) |
| 4 | 48 liters (12 gallons) | 224 liters (56 gallons) |
| 6 | 72 liters (18 gallons) | 336 liters (84 gallons) |

- **Store more** for children, pregnant and breastfeeding women, sick people, and in hot climates.
- For a longer emergency, aim for 15 liters (4 gallons) per person per day. That covers drinking, cooking and basic hygiene.
- **Pets:** dogs and cats need about 60 mL of water per kg of body weight per day (1 ounce per pound), more in heat. A 20 kg (45 lb) dog needs about 1.2 liters (5 cups) a day.
- **Weight:** 1 liter of water weighs 1 kg (2.2 lb). A full 20 liter (5 gallon) can weighs 20 kg (44 lb). A full 200 liter (55 gallon) drum weighs over 200 kg (440 lb). Put large drums in place before you fill them, and use a pump or siphon hose to empty them.
- **Toilets:** flushing a toilet with a bucket takes 4 to 6 liters (1 to 1.5 gallons) each time. Do not plan to flush with drinking water. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).

## Containers

| Good | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Sealed bottled water from a shop | Containers that held fuel, chemicals or poison |
| Food-grade plastic water cans and jerrycans (marked "food grade", "HDPE" or recycling number 2) | Milk and juice jugs or cartons: milk fat and sugar stay in them, and they crack within months |
| Clear plastic drink bottles (PET, recycling number 1) that held water or soda | Plastics not made for food, such as paint buckets |
| Glass bottles and jars (not for freezing) | Open buckets without lids |
| Stainless steel containers | Metal that rusts |
| Food-grade drums and tanks | Anything you cannot clean inside |

Containers with a narrow neck or a tap are best, because they make it hard to dip cups or hands inside.

## Clean and sanitize containers

1. Wash the container and cap with soap and water. Rinse well.
2. Mix **1 teaspoon (5 mL) of plain, unscented bleach (5-9%) into 1 liter (1 quart) of water.**
3. Pour it into the container. Close it and shake so the mix wets every inside surface, including the cap.
4. Wait at least **30 seconds.** Pour it out.
5. Let it air-dry, or rinse it with clean, safe water.

For a large drum, mix 4 liters (1 gallon) of water with 4 teaspoons (20 mL) of bleach. Pour it in, close it, and roll the drum so the mix touches every surface.

## Fill and treat

- **Tap water from a treated public supply:** fill directly. It needs no extra treatment.
- **Well water, rainwater or any other untreated water:** treat it before storing. Add 2 drops of 5-6% bleach per liter (8 drops per gallon), stir, and wait 30 minutes. For other bleach strengths and other methods, see [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).
- Do not touch the inside of the cap or the neck of the container.
- Close it tightly.

## Where to keep it

- **Cool and dark.** Sunlight lets algae grow in clear containers. Heat weakens plastic and speeds the release of chemicals from it.
- **Away from fuel, paint, pesticides and cleaning products.** Their fumes can pass through plastic.
- **In more than one place,** so one damaged room does not take all your water. Keep a few bottles in your car, and replace them more often, because cars get hot.
- **Off the floor of flood-prone rooms** if you can.
- **Freezing:** plastic bottles can be frozen if you leave 5 cm (2 in) of space at the top. Glass breaks. Frozen bottles also help a freezer stay cold in a power cut.

## Using stored water

- Pour from the container, or use its tap. If you must dip, use one clean ladle with a long handle, kept only for this.
- Keep the lid on between uses.
- Keep drinking water apart from washing water. Use different colored containers if you can.
- If the water looks cloudy, smells bad, or the container has been opened often, treat it again: 2 drops of bleach per liter and wait 30 minutes, or boil it.
- Stored water often tastes flat. Pour it back and forth between 2 clean containers to improve the taste.

## Rotation

- **Replace home-filled water every 6 months.** Use the old water for washing, plants or toilets.
- **Shop-bought bottled water:** keep it until the date on the label.
- Write the fill date on every container. Pick 2 dates each year to check and refill, for example when the seasons change.
- Bleach weakens with age. Replace your bleach every year.

## Before a storm or other warning

- Fill every clean container you have.
- Fill bathtubs and sinks. Use this water for washing and flushing, not drinking, unless the tub was cleaned first or lined with a new plastic sheet.
- Freeze bottles of water.

If your stored water runs out, see [Finding Water](../water/find-water.md) and [Collecting Rainwater](../water/rainwater.md). For what else to store with it, see [Emergency Kit Checklists](../reference/emergency-kits.md).
