> [!CAUTION]
> **Always disinfect rainwater before drinking it.** Rain is fairly clean, but roofs and tanks add bird and animal droppings, dust and dead insects. Boil it, chlorinate it or use sunlight.
>
> **Do not drink water from roofs with lead paint or lead sheeting, tar, treated wood or moss-killing chemicals,** or from any roof covered in ash after a fire or radioactive fallout.
>
> **Cover every container.** Open water breeds mosquitoes that spread dengue and other diseases in about 1 week.

## Quick steps

1. **Choose a clean surface:** a metal roof, a glazed tile roof, or a clean tarp or plastic sheet.
2. **Clean it:** sweep off leaves and droppings. Clear the gutters.
3. **Let the first rain run to waste.** It carries most of the dirt.
4. **Lead the water into a clean container** through a cloth or fine mesh.
5. **Cover the container tightly,** and screen every opening.
6. **Disinfect before drinking.** See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

## How much can you collect?

**1 mm of rain on 1 square meter gives 1 liter.** (1 inch of rain on 1 square foot gives about 0.6 gallons.)

To estimate what you will really get:

**liters = rain in mm x area in square meters x 0.8**

- Measure the area as seen from above: the length times the width of the ground the roof covers. Do not measure the sloping roof surface.
- The 0.8 allows for water lost to splashing, evaporation and the first flush.
- To measure rain, leave any straight-sided can or bucket in the open. The depth of water in it is the rainfall.

| Surface | Size | 10 mm (0.4 in) of rain gives about |
|---|---|---|
| Tarp | 3 m x 3 m (10 x 10 ft) | 72 liters (19 gallons) |
| Small roof | 5 m x 8 m (16 x 26 ft) | 320 liters (85 gallons) |
| House roof | 10 m x 10 m (33 x 33 ft) | 800 liters (210 gallons) |

One person needs at least 3 liters a day to drink, and 15 liters a day for all basic needs. A small roof can cover a family for days after one good rain. To water a garden with it, see [Watering Crops With Little Water](../farming/watering-crops.md).

## Roofs and surfaces

**Good:** metal sheet, glazed tiles, slate, clean plastic sheeting.

**Avoid for drinking water:**

- **Lead:** old paint, lead sheeting around chimneys and edges (common on older houses), lead solder. Lead harms children's brains, and there is no safe level.
- **Treated wood shingles** and roofs treated with moss killers. They can release copper, arsenic and other poisons.
- **Tar, bitumen and asphalt shingles.** They can add chemicals and a bad taste. Use this water for washing.
- **Asbestos cement sheets** (old gray corrugated sheets). Swallowed fibers are thought to be much less harmful than breathed fibers, but use another roof if you can. **Never** cut, drill, sand, scrub or pressure-wash asbestos: the dust is very dangerous to breathe.
- **Thatch and grass roofs:** low yield and brown water. Use it for washing, or filter and treat it well.
- **Roofs under bird roosts or covered in droppings,** and roofs near chimneys, busy roads or crop spraying.
- **Any roof after wildfire ash, volcanic ash or radioactive fallout.** Do not collect until heavy rain has washed it or officials say it is safe. See [Nuclear and Radiation Emergencies](../disasters/radiation.md).

Cut back tree branches over the roof. They drop leaves and bring birds and animals.

## Tarp and other quick catchments

1. Tie a tarp or plastic sheet between trees or posts, at least 1 m (3 ft) off the ground.
2. Tie the corners at different heights, so the water runs to one low corner.
3. Put a clean container under the low corner.
4. Rinse the sheet with the first rain, then start collecting.

Also useful: an upside-down umbrella, a raincoat, and clean pots and buckets set out in the open, away from trees. Wash a new tarp with soap and water first, or the water may taste of plastic.

## First flush: throw away the dirtiest water

The first water off a roof carries most of the dust and droppings. Throw away at least the first 0.5 to 1 liter for every square meter of roof. For a 40 square meter (430 square foot) roof, that is 20 to 40 liters (5 to 10 gallons). Throw away more if the roof is very dirty or it has not rained for weeks.

**Simplest method:** move the container away, or unhook the downpipe, for the first part of the rain. Then put it back.

**First-flush pipe:** a capped pipe below the downpipe fills first. When it is full, the cleaner water flows on to the tank. A pipe 10 cm (4 in) wide holds about 8 liters (2 gallons) for each meter (3 ft) of length.

```
  roof gutter
  ==========+
            |  downpipe
            |
            +============> to tank (screened)
            |
           | |  first-flush pipe:
           | |  fills first, then the
           | |  cleaner water flows
           | |  on to the tank
           | |
           |_|  cap with a small hole:
                drips empty slowly, or
                open it after each rain
```

Empty the first-flush pipe after every rain, or it will not work next time.

## Storage

- Use **opaque, food-grade** containers with tight lids. Light lets algae grow.
- Screen every opening, including the inlet and the overflow, with fine insect mesh.
- Put the tap about 10 cm (4 in) above the bottom, so the sludge stays behind.
- Put tanks on a firm, level base. 1,000 liters of water weighs 1,000 kg (2,200 lb).
- Lead the overflow away from walls and foundations.
- Clean the tank and remove sludge about once a year.
- Lock or weigh down lids. Children can drown in tanks.

For more on containers and cleaning them, see [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

## Stop mosquitoes

The mosquitoes that spread dengue, Zika, chikungunya and yellow fever lay their eggs in clean stored water. The young become flying adults in about 1 week.

- Cover containers tightly, or screen them with fine mesh.
- **Once a week,** empty and scrub the inside walls of any open container. The eggs stick to the walls just above the water and can survive dry for up to 8 months.
- Look for small wriggling larvae in the water. If you see them, pour the water through a cloth, then disinfect it.
- Keep gutters sloped and clean, so they do not hold standing water.

## Disinfect before drinking

1. Let the water settle. Pour off the clear top part. Filter it if it is cloudy (see [Building a Sand and Charcoal Filter](../water/sand-filter.md)).
2. Disinfect it as you take it out of the tank: boil for 1 minute, or add 2 drops of 5-6% bleach per liter and wait 30 minutes, or use sunlight (SODIS) for 6 hours.
3. Store treated water in a clean, covered container.

Rainwater tastes flat because it has few minerals. That is not harmful.
