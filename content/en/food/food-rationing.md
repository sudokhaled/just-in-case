> [!CAUTION]
> **Never ration water to save food.** You can live for weeks without food, but only days without water. Digesting food uses water. If water is short, eat less, not drink less.
>
> **After many days with little or no food, restart eating slowly.** A sudden large meal after more than 5 days of starvation can cause heart failure and death (refeeding syndrome).
>
> **Cut adult portions before you cut food for young children, pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers.** Hunger harms them fastest, and the damage to children can last for life.

## Quick steps

1. **Count what you have:** food, water and people. Work out how many days it will last: divide the food by the number of people, then by the days until you expect more.
2. **Start rationing early** if you do not know when more food will come. A small cut now is better than nothing later.
3. **Eat food that spoils first:** fresh food, then the fridge, then the freezer, then cans and dry food.
4. **Keep drinking.** Each person needs about 3 liters (3 quarts) of water a day, more in heat or with hard work.
5. **Share by measure:** use the same cup or scale for every portion, in front of everyone.
6. **Save energy:** rest, stay warm and do hard work in the cool hours. Less activity means less food needed.
7. **Restart food slowly** for anyone who has eaten almost nothing for more than 5 days.

## How much food people need

Aid agencies plan for **2,100 kcal (calories) per person per day.** This is an average for a mixed group of adults and children. Food labels often show energy in kilojoules (kJ): 1 kcal is about 4.2 kJ.

| Person | Approximate need per day |
|---|---|
| Child 2 to 3 years | 1,000-1,400 kcal |
| Child 4 to 8 years | 1,200-2,000 kcal |
| Child 9 to 13 years | 1,400-2,600 kcal |
| Teenager 14 to 18 years | 1,800-3,200 kcal |
| Adult woman | 1,600-2,400 kcal |
| Adult man | 2,000-3,000 kcal |

The lower number is for people who are not active. The higher number is for active people.

Some people need more:

- **Heavy physical work:** 3,000 kcal or more.
- **Cold:** about 100 kcal more per day for every 5°C (9°F) the average temperature is below 20°C (68°F).
- **Pregnancy:** about 300 kcal more per day in the second half of pregnancy. See [Pregnancy Care Without a Clinic](../medical/pregnancy-care.md).
- **Breastfeeding:** about 500 kcal more per day.
- **Sick or injured people,** while they recover.

### Energy in common foods

| Food (dry, uncooked) | kcal per 100 g (3.5 oz) |
|---|---|
| Rice, flour, pasta, oats, maize meal | 350-380 |
| Dried beans, lentils, peas | 330-350 |
| Sugar | 400 |
| Cooking oil, ghee, lard | 880-900 |
| Peanut butter, nuts | 550-650 |

1 cup (250 mL) of uncooked rice weighs about 200 g and gives about 700 kcal.

## Plan the ration

1. Add up the energy you have. Multiply each food's weight by its kcal per 100 g, or read the packages.
2. Multiply the number of people by 2,100. This is your daily need.
3. Divide the total energy by the daily need. This is the number of days at full rations.
4. Compare this with how long you may have to wait. Add a safety margin.
5. Decide the portion size, for example three-quarter rations.

Example: 20 kg (44 lb) of rice gives about 72,000 kcal. 5 liters (5 quarts) of oil gives about 40,000 kcal. Together that is 112,000 kcal. A family of 4 needs about 8,400 kcal a day. The food lasts about 13 days at full rations, or about 26 days at half rations.

## Water comes first

- Digestion and getting rid of waste both use water. Protein (meat, fish, beans) and salty food use the most.
- If water is short: eat less, and choose starchy or sweet foods (rice, bread, fruit, sugar). Avoid salty, dry and high-protein food.
- Food with a lot of water in it (fruit, vegetables, soup) counts toward your water.
- For storing water, see [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

## What happens without enough food

- **First days:** hunger, tiredness, headache, bad mood.
- **Then:** the body burns fat and muscle. Weakness, dizziness when standing up, feeling cold, slow thinking and poor decisions.
- **Later:** muscle wasting, slow wound healing, more infections, swelling of the feet and legs, and children stop growing.

In a famous experiment in the 1940s, healthy men ate about 1,570 kcal a day for 24 weeks. They lost about a quarter of their body weight and became weak, cold, depressed and obsessed with food. Most healthy adults can manage on reduced food for some weeks, but children, older people and sick people are harmed much sooner.

## What to eat first

1. **Fresh food at room temperature** that spoils quickly: bread, soft fruit, vegetables, leftovers.
2. **Fridge food:** safe for about **4 hours** without power if the door stays closed.
3. **Freezer food:** a full freezer stays frozen about **48 hours**, a half-full one about **24 hours**, if the door stays closed. Cook thawed food while it is still cold.
4. **Long-lasting food last:** cans, rice, beans, pasta, flour, dried food.

When unsure whether food is still safe, see [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md). For building a store, see [Storing Emergency Food](../food/food-storage.md).

## Share fairly

Fights over food are common and dangerous. Clear rules agreed early prevent them.

- Agree on the rules in advance, with everyone present.
- Measure every portion with the same cup, spoon or scale.
- Serve at set times, in the open, and keep a written record.
- The person who divides the food takes the last portion.
- Keep stores locked or guarded. Let 1 or 2 trusted people handle them, and check them together.
- Agree openly who gets extra, if there is any: pregnant and breastfeeding women, young children, sick people and people doing heavy work.
- Make sure children, older people and disabled people actually eat their share. They may be pushed aside or give it away.
- Help weak and sick people to eat.

For running a group fairly, see [Organizing a Community](../community/community-groups.md).

### Protect the most vulnerable

- **Children under 5,** especially under 2, need the most food for their size. Hunger harms brain growth for life. To grow your own calories and vitamins, see [Calorie Crops That Feed a Family for a Year](../farming/staple-crops.md) and [Fast Crops: Food in 3 Days to 3 Months](../farming/fast-crops.md).
- **Babies:** keep breastfeeding. It protects babies even when the mother is hungry. Feed the mother. See [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).
- **Pregnant and breastfeeding women,** older people and the sick.

## Vitamins on a limited diet

Living on only rice, flour or pasta for weeks causes vitamin diseases.

- Eat some vegetables, fruit or green leaves every day if you can.
- Without vitamin C, scurvy starts after about 1 to 3 months: bleeding gums, easy bruising, wounds that do not heal.
- If you have multivitamin tablets, take the dose printed on the package.
- Wild plants can help, but only if you are sure what they are. See [Foraging Wild Plants Safely](../food/foraging.md).

## Lean meat alone is not enough

If people eat only very lean meat (rabbit, lean fish, lean game) without fat or starchy food, they become sick within about a week: nausea, diarrhea, weakness and hunger that does not stop. This is called "rabbit starvation". The body cannot safely get more than about a third of its energy from protein.

- Eat the fat, bone marrow, liver and other organs of animals.
- Add any fat or starchy food: oil, nuts, roots, grains.

## Restarting food after starvation (refeeding syndrome)

**Who is at risk:** anyone who has eaten little or nothing for **more than 5 days.** The risk is highest after 10 days or more, in very thin people, and in people who drank a lot of alcohol.

1. Give small meals, spread through the day.
2. For the first 2 days, give **no more than half** of normal food.
3. If they ate almost nothing for 10 days or more, or are very thin, start with even less: about **a quarter** of normal food.
4. Increase slowly, over 4 to 7 days, to normal amounts.
5. Give mixed food. Do not start with large amounts of sugar or sweet drinks.
6. Give a multivitamin if you have one, at the dose on the package.
7. Watch for danger signs: swelling of the legs, shortness of breath, fast or irregular heartbeat, confusion, weakness or seizures. If you see them, reduce the food and get medical help.

> [!WARNING]
> **A severely malnourished child** (very thin, or with swelling of both feet) needs special medical feeding. Get the child to a health worker or feeding center as fast as possible. Until then, keep the child warm, give small feeds often, and keep breastfeeding. Do not force large meals. See [Malnutrition in Children and Adults](../medical/malnutrition.md).
