> [!CAUTION]
> **Botulism kills, and you cannot see, smell or taste it.** It grows in sealed, airless, low-acid food: vegetables, beans, meat, fish, soups, and garlic or herbs in oil.
>
> **Low-acid foods (vegetables, beans, meat, poultry, fish, soups) must be canned in a pressure canner.** A boiling water bath, an oven, or filling hot jars without processing does not kill botulism spores in these foods. No pressure canner: dry, salt, ferment or cellar them instead.
>
> **Never keep garlic, herbs, chilies or vegetables covered in oil at room temperature.** Never taste food from a jar or can that is bulging, leaking, spurting or smells wrong.

## Quick steps

1. **Choose a method that suits the food.** Without a pressure canner: dry or ferment vegetables, dry or salt meat and fish, cellar roots, make jam from fruit.
2. **Start with fresh, undamaged food,** clean hands and clean tools.
3. **Follow tested amounts exactly:** salt by weight, vinegar strength, times and temperatures.
4. **Store the finished food cool, dry, dark and sealed** from insects and mice.
5. **Check stores every week.** Throw out anything moldy, slimy, bulging or bad-smelling, without tasting it.

## Drying

Germs and mold need water. Drying takes it away.

1. Cut food into thin, even slices, about 6 mm (1/4 in) thick.
2. Spread it in one layer on racks, screens or clean cloth so air reaches both sides.
3. Cover it with thin cloth or netting to keep insects off.
4. Bring it indoors at night. Dew makes it damp again.

**Sun drying** needs at least 30°C (86°F), humidity below about 60%, and several sunny, breezy days. In damp climates, dry food over a low fire, or in an oven on its lowest setting with the door slightly open. Blanch vegetables (boil or steam them for a few minutes) first.

| Food | Dry enough when |
|---|---|
| Fruit | Leathery and bendable, with no wetness when torn and squeezed |
| Vegetables | Hard and brittle: they snap or rattle |
| Jerky | Bends and cracks, but does not snap |
| Grain, beans | Very hard: you cannot dent them with a fingernail |

Keep dried fruit in a closed jar for 7 to 10 days, shaking it daily. If drops of water appear, dry it more. To kill insect eggs, freeze sun-dried food at -18°C (0°F) for 48 hours, or heat it at 70°C (160°F) for 30 minutes.

### Jerky (dried meat)

1. Use lean meat. Cut off all fat: fat goes rancid.
2. Slice it 6 mm (1/4 in) thick.
3. **Heat the meat first:** boil the strips in salty water or marinade for 5 minutes. Drying alone at low heat does not kill dangerous germs.
4. Dry until it bends and cracks. Store it sealed. Home-dried jerky keeps for weeks at room temperature, not months.

Bear, wild pig and some other wild meat can carry worms (trichinella) that drying and smoking do not kill. Cook them to 71°C (160°F) first.

## Salting

Salt pulls water out of food and stops most germs. It needs a lot of salt.

1. Split fish flat, or cut meat no thicker than about 2.5 cm (1 in).
2. Rub salt into every surface and pack the pieces in layers with salt between them. A common traditional amount is about 1 part salt to 3 parts fish by weight.
3. Leave in a cool place until firm, pouring off the liquid. Then dry in sun and wind until hard.
4. Before cooking, soak in fresh water for several hours, changing the water a few times.

Do not make cured sausages or whole hams without curing salt (sodium nitrite) and a tested recipe. Without them, these meats can carry botulism.

## Smoking

- **Hot smoking** cooks the food. The inside of fish must reach at least 63°C (145°F) for 30 minutes, and meat 71°C (160°F). Hot-smoked food still spoils within days unless you also dry it until hard.
- **Smoke-drying** over a low fire for days, until the food is hard and dry, is the traditional method in many hot countries.
- **Cold smoking** (below about 30°C or 85°F) does not cook food or kill germs. Do not rely on it. Never seal smoked fish in airtight bags or jars at room temperature.
- Smoke food outdoors only, with dry hardwood, never painted or treated wood. Smoke contains carbon monoxide: see [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).

To get fish and meat, see [Catching Fish](../food/fishing.md) and [Trapping and Preparing Small Game](../food/trapping.md).

## Fermenting vegetables (sauerkraut)

Good bacteria turn the sugar in vegetables into acid, which preserves them.

1. Shred cabbage finely and weigh it.
2. Add **20 to 25 g of salt for every 1 kg of cabbage** (2 to 2.5% salt), about 3 level tablespoons for 2.3 kg (5 lb). Weigh the salt if you can: spoon amounts vary between salt types.
3. Mix and squeeze with clean hands until juice comes out.
4. Pack it tightly into a clean glass jar, crock or food-grade bucket.
5. Hold the cabbage under the juice with a plate and a weight, such as a sealed plastic bag of salty water.
6. If the juice does not cover the cabbage after 24 hours, add salt water: 25 g of salt per liter (1.5 tablespoons per quart) of boiled, cooled water.
7. Cover loosely with a cloth. Remove any white film from the top 2 or 3 times a week.
8. Wait about 3 to 4 weeks at 21 to 24°C (70 to 75°F), or 5 to 6 weeks at 15 to 18°C (60 to 65°F). Below 15°C (60°F) it may not ferment. Above 24°C (75°F) it may go soft.

It is ready when it tastes sour and bubbling stops. The same method works for other shredded vegetables, such as turnips and carrots. **Throw it out** if it is slimy, pink or dark brown, smells rotten instead of sour, or has fuzzy or colored mold.

Do not ferment meat or fish without a tested traditional method. Fermented fish sealed in plastic has caused many botulism deaths.

## Pickling with vinegar

- Use vinegar with **at least 5% acidity** (check the label), not homemade vinegar of unknown strength.
- Use a tested recipe. Never use less vinegar or more vegetables than it says.
- Unprocessed pickles are not shelf-stable: keep them cool and eat them soon. For long storage, process the jars in a boiling water bath.
- **Never use copper, iron, aluminum or galvanized (zinc) containers.** Acid dissolves metal into the food.

## Canning (bottling) in jars

| Food | Method |
|---|---|
| Most fruits, jams, jellies | Boiling water bath |
| Tomatoes | Water bath only with added acid: 2 tablespoons of bottled lemon juice, or half a teaspoon of citric acid, per 1 liter (1 quart) jar |
| Pickles from tested recipes | Boiling water bath |
| Vegetables, beans, corn, potatoes, mushrooms, meat, poultry, fish, soups, stews | **Pressure canner only** |

Boiling water reaches only 100°C (212°F). Botulism spores survive this. A pressure canner reaches 116°C (240°F), which kills them.

1. Use a tested recipe with the processing time for your food, jar size and altitude. Above 300 m (1,000 ft), increase the time or pressure as the tables say.
2. Use proper canning jars and new flat lids. Never "can" in an oven, dishwasher or microwave, or by turning hot jars upside down.
3. After 12 to 24 hours, press each lid. It should curve down and not move. Eat unsealed jars soon.
4. Store jars cool and dark, and use them within about 1 year.

If you are not sure a jar of low-acid food was processed correctly, and it shows no signs of spoiling, boil the food for 10 minutes before tasting it. Add 1 minute for every 300 m (1,000 ft) above 300 m.

Do not open a suspect jar. Seal it in a bag and throw it where children and animals cannot reach it. If it leaked, wear gloves and clean the area with 1 part bleach to 5 parts water, left for 30 minutes. Signs of botulism are in [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md).

## Root cellars and clamps

| Food | Best temperature | Air |
|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | 4 to 7°C (40 to 45°F) | Damp, completely dark |
| Carrots, beets, turnips, cabbage | 0 to 4°C (32 to 40°F) | Very damp |
| Apples, pears | 0 to 4°C (32 to 40°F) | Damp, away from vegetables |
| Onions, garlic | 0 to 4°C (32 to 40°F) | Dry |
| Pumpkins, winter squash | 10 to 13°C (50 to 55°F) | Dry |

- Store only perfect, undamaged produce. Brush off soil, but do not wash it. Cure potatoes, sweet potatoes, squash and onions first: see [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md).
- Check every 1 to 2 weeks and remove anything rotting. Do not let it freeze.
- Keep apples and pears away from potatoes and carrots. Their gas makes potatoes sprout and carrots bitter.
- Cut green parts and sprouts off potatoes. Throw away very green potatoes: the green part is poisonous.

No cellar: use an unheated room that stays above freezing, a barrel buried in the ground, or a clamp on ground that drains well:

## Grain and dry beans

Dry them in the sun until very hard, then store them airtight. Without air, insects and their eggs die. See [Storing Emergency Food](../food/food-storage.md).
