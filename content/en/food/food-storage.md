> [!CAUTION]
> **Use oxygen absorbers and airtight sealing only for very dry foods,** such as white rice, dried beans, oats, pasta and grain. Moist foods (soft dried fruit, home-dried meat, anything that is not dry and hard) sealed without air can grow botulism, a deadly poison.
>
> **Never store food in containers that held fuel, chemicals or poisons,** or in plastic that is not made for food.
>
> **Throw away bulging, leaking or deeply dented cans without tasting them.** See [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md).

## Quick steps

1. **Store at least 2 weeks of food for every person,** and water to go with it.
2. **Store food you already eat** that keeps without a fridge and needs little water and fuel to cook.
3. **Write the date** on everything.
4. **Keep it cool, dry, dark and sealed,** off the floor and away from fuel and chemicals.
5. **Eat the oldest food first** and replace it with new food.
6. **Keep 2 manual can openers.**
7. **Build up to 1 month or more** : per adult per month, about 12 kg (26 lb) of grain, 1.8 kg (4 lb) of dried beans, 0.8 liters (27 fl oz) of oil, 0.5 kg (1 lb) of sugar, 150 g (5 oz) of iodized salt and 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) of protein food.

## What to store

Choose food that:

- you and your family already eat. Sudden changes of diet cause stomach problems, and children may refuse strange food.
- gives a lot of energy for its weight.
- keeps for a long time without a fridge.
- needs little water and fuel to cook, or none.

Good choices:

- **Ready to eat, for the first days:** canned meat, fish, beans, vegetables and fruit, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, nuts, cereal bars.
- **Staples:** white rice, pasta, rolled oats, flour, whole grain, dried beans, lentils, split peas.
- **Fats:** cooking oil, ghee.
- **Sugar, honey and salt** (iodized salt if possible), plus spices you like.
- **Powdered milk.**
- **Multivitamin tablets,** to make up for a limited diet.

## How long food keeps

"Best before" dates are about quality. "Use by" dates are about safety. Canned food in perfect cans is usually safe after its "best before" date, but the taste and vitamins slowly decline.

| Food | Cool cupboard, sealed | Sealed without oxygen, below 24°C (75°F) |
|---|---|---|
| White rice | Several years | Up to 30 years |
| Dried beans, lentils | 1 to 2 years for best quality | Up to 30 years, but old beans cook slowly |
| Rolled oats | About 1 to 2 years | Up to 30 years |
| White pasta | About 2 years | Up to 30 years |
| Whole wheat grain | Many years | 30 years or more |
| Brown rice, whole wheat flour, nuts | Months: their oil goes rancid | Somewhat longer |
| Sugar, salt, honey | Keep almost forever if dry | Same |
| Canned tomatoes and fruit (high acid) | 12 to 18 months for best quality | Not needed |
| Canned meat, fish, vegetables (low acid) | 2 to 5 years for best quality | Not needed |

The 30-year figures come from long-term studies of food sealed in cans or foil bags with the oxygen removed and kept at room temperature or cooler. Food stored in warm places keeps for much less time.

Honey can turn solid. Warm the jar in hot water to soften it. **Never give honey to a baby under 1 year.**

## The 5 enemies of stored food

1. **Heat.** Store food in the coolest place you have. As a rough rule, every rise of about 5°C (10°F) can halve storage life. Avoid attics, garages, and places near stoves and water heaters.
2. **Moisture.** It causes mold and rot. Store only dry food in sealed containers, off the floor and away from outside walls.
3. **Light.** It destroys vitamins and makes fats go rancid. Use a dark place or containers that block light.
4. **Oxygen.** It makes fats rancid and lets insects live. Fill containers full, or use oxygen absorbers.
5. **Pests.** Insects, mice and rats. Use hard, sealed containers. Clean up spills. Check stores every month.

Store food on shelves or boards, a few centimeters (an inch or more) off the floor, so air can move and you can see pests.

> [!WARNING]
> **Throw away grain, beans, nuts or flour that is moldy, clumped with mold, or smells musty.** Some molds make poisons (aflatoxins) that cooking does not destroy. A few insects in grain are not dangerous: sift them out. See [Harvesting, Drying and Storing Your Crops](../farming/harvest-storage.md).

## Containers

| Container | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Food-grade plastic bucket with a sealed lid | Bulk rice, beans, grain | Look for "food grade", or the recycling number 2 (HDPE). Rats can chew through plastic. |
| Foil (mylar) bag with oxygen absorbers, inside a bucket | Long-term storage | Seal the bag with a hot clothes iron. Blocks light and air. |
| Glass jar with a tight lid | Small amounts | Pest-proof. Keep in the dark. Can break. |
| Metal can or tin with a tight lid | Anything dry | Pest-proof. Can rust in damp places. |
| Clean, dry plastic drink bottle (PET) | Rice, beans, sugar, salt | Only for dry food. Dry it well first. |
| Paper, cardboard, thin plastic bags | Short term only | Insects, mice and damp get in easily. |

### Using oxygen absorbers

- Follow the packet. A common guide is at least 300 cc of absorber for every 4 liters (1 gallon) of container, about 2,000 cc for a 20-liter (5-gallon) container.
- Work quickly. Absorbers start working as soon as you open their packet. Seal unused ones in a small, airtight jar.
- Do not use them with sugar (it turns into a hard block) or with salt (it does not need them).
- A sealed foil bag should pull in tight within a day or two. If it does not, it may leak.

### Killing insect eggs

Grain and flour often contain tiny insect eggs. If you have a working freezer, freeze dry grain at -18°C (0°F) for 3 days before storing it. Sealing grain without oxygen also kills insects.

## Rotation: first in, first out

- Write the date you bought or packed it on every item.
- Put new food at the back. Take food from the front.
- Cook from your store every week and replace what you use.
- Check the whole store every 6 months. Use up anything close to its date.

For more ways to prepare without wasting money, see [Preparing Wisely: Things and Skills That Pay Off Every Day](../reference/everyday-prep.md).

## How much to store

- **Minimum:** 2 weeks for every person.
- **Better:** 1 to 3 months, if you have the money and space.
- **Water:** store water as well. See [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

This basic ration for 1 adult for 1 month gives about 2,100 kcal a day. It is based on the food rations used by aid agencies.

| Food | Per adult per month | Per day |
|---|---|---|
| Grain: rice, flour, maize meal, oats, pasta | 12 kg (26 lb) | 400 g (14 oz) |
| Dried beans, lentils or peas | 1.8 kg (4 lb) | 60 g (2 oz) |
| Cooking oil | 0.8 liters (27 fl oz) | 25 g (1 oz) |
| Sugar or honey | 0.5 kg (1 lb) | 15 g (half an ounce) |
| Iodized salt | 150 g (5 oz) | 5 g (about 1 teaspoon) |
| Protein food: powdered milk, peanut butter, canned fish or meat | 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) | 50 g (2 oz) |

This is a minimum diet, and it is low in vitamins. Add canned or dried vegetables and fruit, and multivitamins. Young children need less. People doing heavy work, and people living in the cold, need more. To make food last, see [Food Priorities and Rationing](../food/food-rationing.md).

## Special needs

- **Babies:** if a baby is not breastfed, store infant formula, cups, and safe water and fuel to prepare it. See [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).
- **Medical diets:** diabetes, kidney disease, celiac disease (gluten-free), food allergies.
- **Older people:** soft foods for people with few teeth or dentures.
- **Religious and cultural diets.**
- **Pets and working animals.**

## Can openers and cooking

- Keep 2 manual can openers, or choose cans with pull-rings.
- No opener: rub the top rim of the can back and forth on a rough flat stone or concrete until the rim wears through. Then squeeze the sides to lift the lid. Wipe off any grit before eating.
- **Plan fuel for cooking.** Dried beans need 1 to 3 hours of cooking, even after soaking for 8 to 12 hours. A pressure cooker cuts this a lot. Very old beans may never soften.
- Red lentils, split peas, white rice, bulgur, quick oats and noodles need much less fuel. Couscous and quick oats only need soaking in hot water.
- Plan enough water to cook with, not just to drink. Pasta needs a lot of water.

For cooking with little fuel, see [Cooking Without Power](../fire/cook-without-power.md).
