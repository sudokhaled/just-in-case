> [!CAUTION]
> **Never eat mussels, clams, oysters or scallops from water you do not know,** especially in warm months, after a "red tide" or near sewage. Their poison is not destroyed by cooking. It can stop your breathing within hours.
>
> **Never eat pufferfish, porcupinefish or boxfish.** In tropical seas, never eat barracuda or moray eel, or the liver, guts or eggs of any reef fish. Cooking does not destroy these poisons.
>
> **Cook all fish thoroughly** and eat it within hours. Raw freshwater fish spreads tapeworms and liver flukes.

## Quick steps

1. **Fish at dawn and dusk,** near cover: fallen trees, rocks, weed beds and deep shaded pools.
2. **Set many hooks.** A set line with 5 to 10 baited hooks works while you rest.
3. **Bait with what lives there:** worms, insects, or guts from your first fish.
4. **Check lines and traps** every few hours and at first light.
5. **Kill, gut and cool the fish at once.** Unless you can keep it cold, cook it within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C (90°F).
6. **Cook until the flesh is white or opaque all through and flakes easily.**

## Where the fish are

- **Time:** fish feed most at dawn and dusk. Catfish and eels feed at night.
- **Temperature:** in hot weather, fish go to deep, shaded, cool water. In cold weather, try the shallows warmed by afternoon sun.
- **Structure:** fish hide near fallen trees, rocks, undercut banks, weed beds and bridge supports.
- **Moving water:** fish face upstream and wait for food in the calm water behind rocks, in deep pools below rapids, and where small streams enter bigger water. See [Living by a River or Lake](../land/river-living.md).
- **Sea:** rocky points, tide pools, and the hours around high tide.
- **Stay hidden:** keep low and quiet, with your shadow off the water.

## Hooks

If you have no real hooks:

- **Safety pin:** open it, bend it into a J, and sharpen the point on a stone.
- **Wire, nail, paper clip or can tab:** bend into a J shape and file a sharp point.
- **Thorn:** cut a twig with a strong thorn (hawthorn, blackthorn, citrus). Tie the line to the twig so the thorn points back up the line.
- **Gorge hook:** a sliver of hard wood, bone or wire 2.5 to 4 cm (1 to 1.5 in) long, sharp at both ends. Cut a groove around the middle and tie the line there. Push it lengthwise into the bait. When the fish swallows it, wait a few seconds, then pull: the gorge turns sideways and sticks in the throat.

## Lines and bait

- **Line:** fishing line, dental floss, strong thread, the inner strands of parachute cord, or twisted plant fiber.
- **Weight:** a small stone or nut tied above the hook.
- **Float:** dry wood, cork or foam tied higher up holds the bait off the bottom and shows a bite.
- **Bait:** earthworms (dig in damp soil), grubs from rotten logs, grasshoppers, small fish, bits of meat, fish eyes and guts. Bright cloth or foil makes a lure for fish that hunt.

## Lines that fish for you

### Handline

1. Wind 10 to 20 m (30 to 60 ft) of line around a short stick. Tie on a hook, with a weight 30 cm (1 ft) above it.
2. Throw it out, keep the line just tight, and pull firmly when you feel a tug.

### Set line (trotline)

1. Stretch a strong main line across a stream or along a bank. Tie the ends to trees or stakes, or weight one end with a rock.
2. Tie short side lines with baited hooks every 1 m (3 ft). Check it every few hours and at dawn.

## Fish traps

### Bottle trap

1. Cut the top third off a 2-liter plastic bottle. Turn it around and push it, neck first, into the bottle. Fix it with string or wire.
2. Make small holes in the sides so water flows through.
3. Put bait inside, weight it with stones, tie it to the bank, and leave it in slow water overnight.

### Stone weir

- **In a stream:** build 2 low stone walls in a V, with a narrow gap at the point leading into a small closed stone pen. Fish swim in and rarely find the way out.
- **On a tidal beach:** at low tide, build a low curved stone wall on a gently sloping beach, both ends touching the shore. Fish swim over it at high tide and are trapped as the tide falls. See [Living by the Sea](../land/coast-living.md).

## Spearing

Spearing burns a lot of energy. It works best in shallow, clear water.

1. Split one end of a straight 2 m (6 ft) pole into 2 to 4 prongs, 15 cm (6 in) deep. Wedge them apart with a small stick and sharpen each point.
2. Put the tip in the water before you strike, and move it slowly toward the fish.
3. Aim **below** the fish: water makes fish look higher and closer than they are.

Never use poison or explosives to catch fish. They are illegal in most countries, kill everything downstream, and can poison you.

## Cleaning fish

1. Kill the fish at once with a hard blow to the top of the head, behind the eyes.
2. Scrape off scales from tail to head with the back of a knife.
3. Cut the belly from the vent (the hole near the tail) to the gills. Pull out the guts and the gills.
4. Scrape out the dark blood line along the backbone with your thumb.
5. Rinse in safe water. Keep guts for bait; bury the rest far from your shelter.

**Do not eat fish that is spoiled:** sunken cloudy eyes, gray or slimy gills, soft flesh that stays dented when pressed, or a sour or ammonia smell.

## Cooking and parasites

- **Cook until the center reaches 63°C (145°F).** Without a thermometer: the flesh is opaque all the way through and flakes apart easily.
- **Never eat raw or lightly cooked freshwater fish.** It can carry tapeworms and liver flukes.
- **Freezing kills parasites** only at -20°C (-4°F) or colder for 7 days, colder than most home freezers.
- **Keep fish cold.** Tuna, mackerel and similar fish left warm form a poison (histamine) that causes flushing, headache and vomiting. Cooking does not remove it.
- To dry or smoke fish for later, see [Preserving Food Without Power](../food/food-preservation.md). To cook with little fuel, see [Cooking Without Power](../fire/cook-without-power.md).

## Fish and shellfish that poison

### Shellfish (mussels, clams, oysters, scallops)

These filter algae from the water and store their poisons. **Paralytic shellfish poisoning** starts 30 minutes to 2 hours after eating: tingling and numbness of the lips, tongue and face, then the arms, then weakness and trouble breathing. There is no antidote. A person who stops breathing may survive with rescue breathing until the poison wears off: see [CPR](../medical/cpr.md).

- Risk is highest in warm months, but toxic blooms can happen in any month. The old "months with an R" rule is not reliable.
- The water often looks normal. Dead fish or birds on the beach are a warning.
- Shellfish near sewage outlets, harbors or floodwater also carry cholera, hepatitis A and norovirus.
- Cook only live shellfish: shells closed, or closing when tapped. Boil for **3 to 5 minutes after the shells open.** Throw away any that stay shut.

### Fish

- **Pufferfish, porcupinefish and boxfish** contain a nerve poison that can kill within hours. Do not eat any fish that puffs up or has a beak-like mouth.
- **Ciguatera** comes from large predator fish on tropical and subtropical reefs: barracuda, moray eel, large groupers, snappers and jacks. Symptoms are vomiting and diarrhea, then tingling and a feeling that cold things are hot. Eat small reef fish rather than large ones, and never eat the head, liver, guts or eggs.

## Safety and the law

- Cold water kills fast. Do not wade above your knees in fast water. Do not fish alone on ice, and do not walk on clear ice thinner than **10 cm (4 in)**. See [Water and Ice Rescue](../navigation/water-rescue.md).
- Fish from water near mines or factories can carry mercury and other chemicals. Eat smaller fish and remove the skin, fat and organs.
- In normal times, fishing usually needs a license, and set lines, traps, weirs and spears are often illegal. In a true emergency, survival comes first.
