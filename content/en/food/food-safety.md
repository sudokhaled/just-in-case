> [!CAUTION]
> **When in doubt, throw it out.** Food that makes you sick often looks, smells and tastes normal. **Never taste food to test whether it is safe.**
>
> **Never eat from a can or jar that is bulging, leaking, spurting or deeply dented, or that smells bad when opened.** It may contain botulism toxin. A tiny taste can paralyze and kill.
>
> **Throw away food that touched floodwater,** unless it was in an undamaged, sealed metal can or sealed foil pouch.

## Quick steps

1. **Keep fridge and freezer doors closed** as much as possible.
2. **Fridge food stays safe for about 4 hours** after the power goes off. After that, throw out meat, fish, milk, eggs, soft cheese and cooked leftovers that have been above 4°C (40°F) for more than 2 hours.
3. **A full freezer stays frozen about 48 hours,** a half-full one about 24 hours. Food that still has ice crystals can be cooked or refrozen.
4. **Eat in this order:** fridge food first, then freezer food, then cans and dry food.
5. **Cook food until it is steaming hot all the way through,** and eat it within 2 hours.
6. **Cook only what you will eat at one meal.** Without a fridge, leftovers are dangerous.
7. **Wash your hands** with soap and safe water before handling food. See [Handwashing and Personal Hygiene](../hygiene/handwashing.md).

## The danger zone

Germs grow fastest between **4°C and 60°C (40°F and 140°F).** In this range, their numbers can double in as little as 20 minutes.

- Do not leave perishable food in this range for more than **2 hours** in total.
- If the air is above 32°C (90°F), the limit is **1 hour**.
- Keep cold food at 4°C (40°F) or colder. Keep hot food above 60°C (140°F).
- Freezing stops germs from growing but does not kill them.
- Cooking kills most germs, but **some germs make poisons that cooking does not destroy.** Reheating spoiled food does not make it safe.

## After the power goes off

Put a thermometer in the fridge and freezer if you have one. It is the only reliable way to know.

| Food | Keep or throw out? |
|---|---|
| Meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, yogurt, soft cheese, cooked leftovers, cooked rice and pasta, cut fruit: above 4°C (40°F) for more than 2 hours | Throw out |
| Hard cheese (such as cheddar or parmesan) | Keep |
| Butter and margarine | Keep |
| Whole raw fruit and vegetables | Keep |
| Bread, and cakes without cream | Keep |
| Peanut butter, jam, ketchup, mustard, pickles, vinegar dressings | Keep |
| Opened mayonnaise and similar sauces | Throw out if above 10°C (50°F) for more than 8 hours |
| Frozen food that still has ice crystals, or is at 4°C (40°F) or colder | Cook it or refreeze it |
| Thawed food that has been above 4°C (40°F) for more than 2 hours | Throw out |

### Making the cold last longer

- Keep frozen food packed closely together. A full freezer stays cold longer.
- Move fridge food into a cooler box with ice, frozen bottles of water, or clean snow.
- In freezing weather, fill containers with water and leave them outside to freeze. Use the ice in your fridge or cooler. Food left outside in snow can warm in the sun or be reached by animals.
- Dry ice keeps a freezer cold. About 23 kg (50 lb) keeps a full 500-liter (18-cubic-foot) freezer frozen for about 2 days. Handle it with thick gloves. Never keep it in a closed room or car: the gas it gives off can suffocate you.

## Keeping food cool without electricity

These methods keep food cooler than the air, but not as cold as a fridge. Use them for fruit and vegetables and to slow spoilage for hours or days. They do not keep meat, fish or milk safe for long.

### Clay pot cooler (zeer pot)

1. Put a smaller unglazed clay pot inside a larger one.
2. Fill the gap between them with sand. Pour water into the sand until it is wet all through.
3. Put food in the inner pot. Cover it with a wet cloth.
4. Keep the cooler in shade where air moves.
5. Add water to the sand 1 or 2 times a day.

```
        wet cloth on top
  .-------------------------.
  |  .-------------------.  |
  |  |                   |  |
  |  |  food in the      |  |  wet sand
  |  |  inner clay pot   |  |  between
  |  |                   |  |  the pots
  |  '-------------------'  |
  '-------------------------'
     outer clay pot, in shade
```

It works best in hot, dry, breezy weather. In humid weather it cools very little.

### Other ways

- **Cold water:** put food in sealed containers in a cold stream or spring. Tie them so they cannot float away. Do not let the stream water touch the food.
- **Underground:** a cellar, a basement, or a covered hole dug in shade stays cooler than the air.
- **Buy, harvest or cook small amounts** that you will eat the same day.

## Cooking safely

| Food | Cook until the inside reaches at least |
|---|---|
| Poultry (chicken, duck, turkey), and all reheated leftovers | 74°C (165°F) |
| Minced (ground) meat, sausages, wild game, bear, wild pig | 71°C (160°F) |
| Whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb or goat, then rest 3 minutes | 63°C (145°F) |
| Fish | 63°C (145°F) |
| Eggs | Yolk and white are firm |

No thermometer:

- Cook until the food is steaming hot all the way through. Meat should have no pink inside and clear juices. Fish should be white or opaque and flake easily. Color is not a perfect test, so cook longer if you are unsure.
- Bring soups, stews and sauces to a rolling boil.
- Reheat leftovers until they boil or steam all the way through.
- Keep raw meat away from food that is ready to eat. Wash knives, boards and hands after touching raw meat.

> [!WARNING]
> **Cooked rice is risky without a fridge.** Germ spores survive cooking. Left warm, they make a poison that reheating does not destroy. Eat rice soon after cooking it, and cook only what you will eat.

## Cans, jars and packets

Throw these away **without opening or tasting them**:

- Cans with bulging or swollen ends, or a lid that is pushed up.
- Cans that leak, are sticky on the outside, or are rusted through.
- Deep dents, especially on a seam. A deep dent is one you can lay your finger in, or one with a sharp point. A small dent away from the seams is usually fine.
- Cans or jars that spurt liquid or foam when opened, or smell bad.
- Home-canned jars with a broken seal or a bulging lid.

Put a bad can or jar in a sealed plastic bag and throw it where children and animals cannot reach it. Wash your hands afterwards.

**Signs of botulism** usually start 12 to 36 hours after eating, but can start from a few hours to 10 days later: drooping eyelids, blurred or double vision, slurred speech, trouble swallowing, dry mouth, and weakness that spreads down the body. Breathing can stop. Get medical help at once.

## Food after a flood

Throw out:

- Any food that touched floodwater, unless it was in an undamaged, sealed metal can or sealed foil pouch.
- Food in plastic, paper, cardboard or cloth, and jars with screw tops, snap lids or crimped caps.
- Home-canned food.
- Fridge and freezer food that touched floodwater.
- Wooden cutting boards, plastic utensils, baby bottle teats and pacifiers (dummies) that touched floodwater.

To save undamaged metal cans and sealed pouches:

1. Remove the labels.
2. Scrub the outside with soap and safe water.
3. Disinfect them: boil in water for 2 minutes, or soak for 15 minutes in 15 mL (1 tablespoon) of plain unscented bleach mixed into 4 liters (1 gallon) of safe water.
4. Write the contents and date on each can with a marker.

See also [Floods](../disasters/flood.md).

## Food poisoning

**Signs:** nausea, vomiting, stomach cramps and diarrhea, sometimes with fever. They can start 1 hour to several days after eating.

The main danger is dehydration. Start oral rehydration solution early. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).

Get medical help if possible for:

- Blood in the stool, or high fever.
- Vomiting so much that the person cannot keep fluids down.
- Signs of dehydration: very little urine, dizziness, a dry mouth.
- Babies, older people, pregnant women and people with weak immune systems.
- Any weakness, double vision or trouble swallowing (possible botulism).

For ways to keep food for months without a fridge, see [Preserving Food Without Power](../food/food-preservation.md).
