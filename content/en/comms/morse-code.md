> [!CAUTION]
> **SOS is one continuous signal: `...---...`** (3 short, 3 long, 3 short). Do not pause between the letters. Pause after it, then repeat.
>
> **If you are trapped, tap. Do not shout all the time.** Shouting uses strength and makes you breathe dust. Tap on a pipe or wall, and shout only when you hear rescuers close by.
>
> **Never send SOS or MAYDAY unless life is in danger.** False alarms are illegal in many countries and take rescuers away from people who need them.

## Quick steps

1. **SOS** is 3 short, 3 long, 3 short signals: `...---...`
2. A **short** signal lasts as long as you count "one". A **long** signal lasts "one, two, three".
3. Send it with whatever you have: a flashlight turned on and off, a whistle, a car horn, or a stone tapped on a pipe.
4. After each SOS, pause for about the length of 7 short signals, then send it again.
5. Keep sending at regular times, for example for 1 minute, then rest for 1 minute, to save strength and batteries.
6. When someone answers, stop and listen or watch for their reply.

## Timing

Morse code is built from one basic time unit.

| Part | Length |
|---|---|
| Dot (short signal, "dit") | 1 unit |
| Dash (long signal, "dah") | 3 units |
| Gap between dots and dashes inside one letter | 1 unit |
| Gap between letters | 3 units |
| Gap between words | 7 units |

For a light or sound signal to someone who is far away or untrained, go slowly: a unit of about 1 second works. Radio operators send much faster.

## Sending by light

- **Flashlight:** switch it on and off, or cover and uncover the lens with your hand. Covering is easier and saves the switch. Point the light straight at the person or aircraft and hold it steady. Car headlights or a phone light also work.
- **Mirror:** in daylight a mirror flash can be seen many kilometers away. Sending Morse with a mirror is hard. A series of flashes aimed at the target is usually enough to get attention. For how to aim a mirror, see [Signaling for Rescue](../navigation/signal-rescue.md).
- **Reading light signals:** write down the dots and dashes as they come. Split them into letters at the longer gaps afterwards.

## Sending by sound

- **Whistle:** use short and long blasts. A whistle carries much farther than your voice and uses much less energy.
- **Alpine distress signal** (used in Europe and many mountain areas): 6 blasts or flashes, evenly spread over 1 minute, then 1 minute of silence, then repeat. The reply is 3 signals in 1 minute, then silence.
- **Signals in threes** (3 blasts, 3 flashes, 3 fires) are widely understood as a call for help, especially in North America.
- A car horn, banging on a pot, or a drum also work.

## Tapping when trapped

1. Tap with something hard (a stone, a piece of metal, a shoe heel) on a pipe, beam or wall. Metal pipes and concrete carry sound a long way.
2. Tap in groups of 3, or tap SOS: 3 quick taps, 3 slow taps, 3 quick taps.
3. Rescue teams often stop all machines and call for silence so they can listen. **When it goes quiet, or you hear voices, tap.**
4. Rest between rounds to save your strength.
5. Cover your mouth and nose with cloth against dust. See [Earthquakes](../disasters/earthquake.md).

## How to learn Morse code quickly

1. **Learn the sound, not the picture.** Say "di" for a dot and "dah" for a dash. A is "di-dah". B is "dah-di-di-dit".
2. **Learn SOS first.** Then learn the letters in opposite pairs, where dots and dashes swap: E and T, I and M, S and O, A and N, U and D, R and K, W and G, B and J, F and Q, L and Y, P and X. For example, A is `.-` and N is `-.`.
3. Learn the last 4 letters on their own: C, H, V and Z.
4. **Numbers follow a pattern.** 1 to 5 start with that many dots, then dashes. 6 to 9 start with dashes (6 has 1 dash, 7 has 2), then dots. 0 is 5 dashes.
5. Practice for 10 to 15 minutes every day. Tap words on a table. Spell signs and names in your head.
6. Practice receiving with someone else. At first, send each letter at full speed but leave long gaps between letters. Shorten the gaps as you improve.

To build a practice buzzer, a key or a wired telegraph between houses, see [Sending and Receiving Morse With What You Have](../comms/morse-devices.md).

## The phonetic alphabet

Use these words to spell names, places and codes on a radio or bad phone line. They are the same in aviation, at sea and in the military (the NATO or ICAO alphabet).

| Letter | Word | Letter | Word |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Alfa | N | November |
| B | Bravo | O | Oscar |
| C | Charlie | P | Papa |
| D | Delta | Q | Quebec |
| E | Echo | R | Romeo |
| F | Foxtrot | S | Sierra |
| G | Golf | T | Tango |
| H | Hotel | U | Uniform |
| I | India | V | Victor |
| J | Juliett | W | Whiskey |
| K | Kilo | X | X-ray |
| L | Lima | Y | Yankee |
| M | Mike | Z | Zulu |

Example: "Smith" is spelled "Sierra, Mike, India, Tango, Hotel".

## Saying numbers on the radio

| Digit | Say | Digit | Say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | ZE-RO | 5 | FIFE |
| 1 | WUN | 6 | SIX |
| 2 | TOO | 7 | SEV-EN |
| 3 | TREE | 8 | AIT |
| 4 | FOW-ER | 9 | NIN-ER |

- Say numbers one digit at a time. 156.8 is "WUN FIFE SIX DECIMAL AIT".
- Say "NINER" for 9 so it is not confused with 5.

## Procedure words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| OVER | I have finished talking. Your turn to reply. |
| OUT | I have finished. No reply is needed. |
| ROGER | I have received your message. It does not mean "yes". |
| WILCO | I will do what you asked. |
| SAY AGAIN | Repeat your message, or part of it ("SAY AGAIN ALL AFTER bridge"). |
| I SPELL | I will now spell the next word with the phonetic alphabet. |
| WAIT | Pause for a few seconds. "WAIT OUT" means a longer pause: I will call you back. |
| AFFIRMATIVE / NEGATIVE | Yes / No. |
| CORRECTION | I made a mistake. The correct version follows. |
| MAYDAY | Danger to life. Immediate help needed. |
| PAN-PAN | Urgent problem, but not yet a danger to life. |

- Never say "over and out". OVER asks for a reply. OUT ends the call. Use one or the other.
- Say "SAY AGAIN", not "repeat". In military radio, "repeat" can be an order to fire again.
