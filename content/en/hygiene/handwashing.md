> [!CAUTION]
> **Unwashed hands carry the germs that cause deadly diarrhea, cholera and pneumonia.** Always wash with soap after the toilet and after cleaning a child, and before touching food or feeding a child.
>
> **Do not wash in a shared bowl** that everyone dips their hands into. It spreads germs. Use poured or running water.
>
> **Hand sanitizer is poisonous to drink and burns easily.** Keep it away from children and flames. Never use sanitizer that contains methanol (wood alcohol).

## Quick steps

1. **Wet** your hands with clean, poured or running water.
2. **Add soap,** soapy water or ash.
3. **Rub every surface for at least 20 seconds:** palms, backs, between fingers, thumbs, fingertips, nails and wrists.
4. **Rinse** under poured or running water.
5. **Dry** in the air or on a clean cloth, not on your clothes.

## When to wash

These are the moments that matter most:

- **After using the toilet.**
- **After cleaning a child's bottom** or changing a diaper (nappy).
- **Before preparing food.**
- **Before eating.**
- **Before feeding a child.**
- **After caring for a sick person,** or touching their waste, laundry or wounds.

Also wash after touching animals, raw meat or rubbish, after coughing or blowing your nose, before and after treating a wound, and before touching stored drinking water.

## How to wash well

1. Remove rings if you can. Keep nails short.
2. Rub palm to palm.
3. Rub the back of each hand with the other palm.
4. Lock your fingers together and rub between them.
5. Rub each thumb inside the other hand.
6. Rub your fingertips and nails in the other palm.
7. Rub your wrists.
8. Count slowly to 20 while you do this. Then rinse.

**Any soap works.** Bar soap is fine. Keep it dry on a rack, or hang it on a string. **Soapy water works too:** dissolve washing powder or soap scraps in a bottle of water. It is cheap and nobody steals it.

## No soap?

- **Wood ash:** use clean, cold ash from a wood fire, rubbed with water. It is probably better than water alone, but evidence is limited. Use soap when you can.
- **Sand or soil:** use only if there is nothing else, and rinse well. Soil can contain germs and worm eggs.
- **Water alone:** rubbing hands hard under running water removes some germs. It is better than nothing.
- **Hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol:** use enough to wet both hands, and rub until dry, about 20 seconds. It works poorly on dirty or greasy hands, and does not kill all diarrhea germs. After the toilet and during diarrhea outbreaks, soap and water are better.

## Build a tippy-tap

A tippy-tap is a hands-free tap made from a jug, sticks and string. It uses very little water, and your hands touch only the soap.

```
 post                               post
  |============ crossbar =============|
  |       |                    |      |
  |    handle on string     soap on   |
  |       |                 string    |
  |    +-------+ neck                 |
  |    |  JUG  |o-+  <- small hole    |
  |    |       |  |                   |
  |    +-------+  |                   |
  |               | string from neck  |
  |               | down to pedal     |
  |     ==========+=====  foot pedal  |
 _|___________________________________|_
           :: gravel and stones ::
```

![Tippy-tap: 1 two forked sticks, about 60 cm (2 ft) apart. 2 straight stick laid across the forks, about 1 m (3 ft) high. 3 jug hanging by its handle so it can swing. 4 one small hole near the top, on the side that tips down. 5 string from the jug's neck down to the pedal. 6 pedal stick on the ground, one end tied loosely to a post: press it with your foot and the jug tips and pours. 7 soap hanging on a string. 8 stones or gravel under the tap so the water soaks away.](../../images/tippy-tap.svg)

You need a plastic jug of 2-5 liters (2-5 quarts) with a handle, 2 forked sticks, 2 straight sticks, string, a nail, soap and some stones.

1. **Set the posts:** push or dig the 2 forked sticks into the ground about 60 cm (2 ft) apart. Lay one straight stick across the forks, about 1 m (3 ft) high.
2. **Make the tap:** heat the nail and push it through the jug near the top, on the side that will tip down. Make 1 small hole.
3. **Hang the jug:** tie its handle to the crossbar so it can swing freely.
4. **Make the pedal:** tie a string from the jug's neck down to the second straight stick lying on the ground. Tie one end of that stick loosely to a post so it stays in place.
5. **Hang soap** on a string from the crossbar.
6. **Put stones or gravel** under the tap so the water soaks away. This stops mud and puddles where mosquitoes breed.
7. **Fill the jug.** Press the pedal with your foot: the jug tips and water pours. If the water stops, loosen the cap a little to let air in.

Put a tippy-tap beside every toilet and every place where food is prepared.

## Saving water

- Wet your hands, then stop the water while you soap and rub.
- Use a thin stream, a tippy-tap or a container with a tap. Or let one person pour for another.
- Catch the used water in a basin. Use it to flush a toilet or wash floors.

For storing water, see [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

## Bathing with little water

A full sponge bath needs about **2 liters (2 quarts)** of water.

1. Wash your face first, with the cleanest water.
2. Then neck, arms, armpits, chest, back, legs and feet.
3. Wash the groin and bottom last.
4. Rinse the cloth often. Change the water if it gets dirty.

If you cannot bathe, wash **hands, face, armpits, groin, bottom and feet** every day. Dry skin folds well.

Bathing water need not be drinking quality, but **do not use floodwater or water with sewage in it.** Use clean water for babies, for the face and for wounds. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

## Teeth

- **Brush twice a day for 2 minutes.** Plaque on teeth causes decay and gum disease. A brush with plain water still removes most of it. Fluoride toothpaste adds protection.
- **No toothbrush:** chew the end of a fresh twig until it frays like a brush. Chewing sticks such as miswak (from the arak tree) and neem are used this way across Africa, the Middle East and Asia. Or wrap a clean cloth around a finger and rub teeth and gums.
- **Sore gums:** rinse with 1/2 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
- Use safe water for brushing. Do not share toothbrushes.
- Eat sweet foods with meals, not between them.

## Skin

- Keep skin clean and dry. Change out of wet clothes. Damp skin folds and feet get fungal infections.
- Wash small cuts with soap and clean water and cover them. See [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md).
- Do not share towels, razors or combs.
- **Scabies** causes strong itching, worse at night, with tiny bumps or lines between the fingers, on the wrists, waist and genitals. Everyone in the household needs treatment from a pharmacy or health worker **at the same time.** Wash bedding and clothes in hot water and dry them in the sun, or seal them in a bag for at least 3 days.
- **Impetigo** shows as sores with yellow-brown crusts, often on a child's face. Wash gently with soap and water, keep the sores covered, and see a health worker.

## Lice

### Head lice

Head lice cause an itchy scalp. Their eggs (nits) stick to hairs close to the scalp. They do not spread disease.

1. Wet the hair and add conditioner or cooking oil.
2. Comb small sections from the roots to the ends with a fine-toothed lice comb. Wipe the comb after each stroke.
3. **Repeat on days 1, 5, 9 and 13.**

Lice treatments from a pharmacy also work. Follow the package.

### Body lice

Body lice live in the seams of clothing, not on the skin. They spread where people cannot wash or change clothes. **They can spread typhus and other dangerous fevers.**

1. Bathe and change into clean clothes.
2. Wash clothes and bedding in hot water of at least 55°C (130°F), and dry them in the sun.
3. If you cannot, iron the seams, or seal the clothes in a bag for 2 weeks.

**Fever with a rash in a place with body lice needs a health worker urgently.**

## Laundry with little water

Wash these first: underwear, socks, baby clothes, diapers, menstrual cloths and a sick person's laundry. For a sick person's laundry, see [Caring for the Sick and Stopping Spread](../medical/infectious-disease.md). See [Menstrual Hygiene](../hygiene/menstrual-hygiene.md).

1. **Soak** clothes in a bucket of soapy water for an hour or more.
2. **Scrub or swish:** rub with your hands, push them up and down with a clean stick or plunger, or shake them in a bucket with a lid.
3. **Wring, rinse once** in clean water, and wring hard.
4. **Reuse** the rinse water to soak the next load, then to flush a toilet.
5. **Dry completely,** in direct sun if possible. Damp clothes cause skin infections.

Wash at least 30 m (100 ft) from wells and streams. Air bedding in the sun every day. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).
