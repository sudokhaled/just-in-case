> [!CAUTION]
> **Human feces spread cholera, typhoid, dysentery, hepatitis and worms.** After disasters, these diseases can kill more people than the disaster itself. Everyone, including every child, must use a toilet, and every toilet needs soap and water beside it.
>
> **Keep toilets at least 30 m (100 ft) from any well, spring, stream or other water source, and downhill from it.**
>
> **Do not flush if sewer pipes may be broken,** for example after an earthquake or flood. Sewage can back up into homes and drinking water.

## Quick steps

1. **If the toilet cannot flush:** line the bowl with a strong plastic bag. Cover each use with a handful of sawdust, ash, dry soil or cat litter.
2. **Or set up 2 buckets:** one for urine, one for feces. Cover feces with dry material after each use. Keep the lids on.
3. **Outdoors, for 1 or a few people:** dig a cat hole **15-20 cm (6-8 in)** deep, at least 30 m (100 ft) from water. Fill it in after use.
4. **For a group:** dig a trench latrine at once. Build pit latrines for longer stays.
5. **Put a handwashing station with soap** next to every toilet.
6. **Cover all waste** so flies cannot reach it.
7. **Make toilets safe:** light at night, a lock on the inside, and separate toilets for women and men.

## How feces make people sick

Germs from feces reach the mouth by many routes:

```
            +--> FINGERS --+
            |              |
            +--> FLIES ----+
  FECES ----+              +--> FOOD --> MOUTH
            +--> FIELDS ---+
            |              |
            +--> FLUIDS ---+
                (water)
```

A toilet stops germs at the start. Handwashing, safe water and covered food block the other routes. You need all of them. See [Handwashing and Personal Hygiene](../hygiene/handwashing.md).

## Where to put toilets

- At least **30 m (100 ft)** from any water source, and **downhill** from it.
- Not where water collects or floods.
- Close enough to use safely at night: **no more than 50 m (55 yd)** from shelters.
- Aim for **1 toilet per 20 people.** In the first days of a crisis, 1 per 50 people is the emergency minimum.
- Women need more toilets than men, because they need longer and take children with them.

## Option 1: a bag in the toilet bowl

Use this at home when the water is off or the sewer may be broken.

1. Turn off the water to the toilet. Do not flush.
2. Lift the seat. Put 2 strong plastic bags, one inside the other, over the bowl. Lower the seat to hold them.
3. After each use, add a cup of sawdust, cat litter, wood ash, dry soil or shredded paper.
4. Tie the bag when it is half full. Put it in a bucket with a lid, outside, away from people, animals and water.
5. Pee in a separate bucket if you can. The bags stay lighter and smell less.

## Option 2: the two-bucket toilet

You need 2 buckets with lids, plastic bags, dry cover material and a seat. A real toilet seat, or a board with a hole, makes it comfortable.

- **Urine bucket: pee only.** Urine from healthy people carries few germs. Empty it into a latrine, or pour it onto soil away from homes, gardens and water. Never pour it into drains or streams. During a cholera or typhoid outbreak, empty it only into a latrine. To use urine and stored faeces safely as fertilizer, see [Soil, Compost and Natural Fertilizer](../farming/soil-compost.md).
- **Feces bucket:** line it with a bag. After each use, cover the feces with a cup of dry material: sawdust, wood ash, dry soil or crushed dry leaves. Keep the lid closed.
- **Keep it dry.** Mixed urine and feces become wet, heavy and smelly, and attract flies. Dry, covered feces smell much less.
- **Empty it** into a latrine or collection point. If there is none, bury it at least 30 m (100 ft) from water and cover it with at least 30 cm (12 in) of soil.
- **Clean the buckets** with soap and water, then bleach solution. See [Caring for the Sick and Stopping Spread](../medical/infectious-disease.md).

## Option 3: cat holes

For 1 or a few people for a short time:

1. Walk at least 30 m (100 ft) from water, paths and camp. 60 m (200 ft) is better.
2. Dig a hole **15-20 cm (6-8 in) deep.**
3. Use it, then fill it with the soil you dug out.
4. Use a new spot every time.

Cat holes do not work for groups. The ground soon becomes covered with waste. Dig a trench.

## Option 4: trench latrine (days to weeks)

The US Army "straddle trench" is quick to dig:

1. Dig a trench **30 cm (1 ft) wide, 75 cm (2.5 ft) deep and 1.2 m (4 ft) long.** People squat with one foot on each side.
2. Dig about **2 trenches for every 100 people.**
3. Pile the soil beside the trench, with a shovel or tin. **Each user covers their waste with soil.**
4. Put up a screen for privacy, and a handwashing station.
5. Dig a small ditch around it so rain runs away.
6. When the waste is **30 cm (1 ft) from the top,** fill it with soil, mound it, mark it, and dig a new trench.

Trenches are for the first days or weeks. Build pit latrines if you will stay longer.

## Option 5: pit latrine (weeks to years)

```
                 lid
     ~~~~~~~~~~[=====]~~~~~~~~~~  slab, raised and mounded
     ground   |       |   ground
              |       |
              |  PIT  |      about 2-3 m (6-10 ft) deep
              |       |
              |_______|
                  ^
                  |  at least 1.5 m (5 ft) of dry soil
                  v
     - - - - - - - - - - - - - -  highest groundwater
```

1. **Dig a pit** about 1 m (3 ft) across and 2-3 m (6-10 ft) deep.
2. **Keep the bottom at least 1.5 m (5 ft) above the highest groundwater** (the water level in the wet season). If water seeps in while you dig, stop. Use a raised latrine or buckets instead.
3. **Line the walls** with stones, bricks, logs or old drums if the soil is loose or sandy, so it cannot collapse.
4. **Cover it with a strong slab** of wood, logs or concrete, with a drop hole small enough that a child cannot fall through.
5. **Raise the rim and mound soil around it** so rain runs away from the pit.
6. **Keep a lid on the hole.** This keeps flies in and smell down.
7. **Build walls, a roof and a door** for privacy and shelter.
8. **Close it** when the waste is **50 cm (20 in) from the top.** Fill it with soil, mound it, mark it, and dig a new pit.

A vent pipe with a fly screen on top, on the sunny side of the latrine, reduces flies and smell.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never climb into a pit latrine, septic tank or sewer.** Gas inside can kill in minutes, and rescuers often die too.

## Flies

Flies breed in uncovered feces and rotting rubbish, then walk on food. Cover all waste. Keep lids on toilet holes. Add ash or soil after each use. Bury or burn rubbish. Keep food covered.

## Children's feces

**Children's feces are as dangerous as adults',** and often carry more germs.

- Put all children's feces in a toilet or latrine, including from diapers (nappies). Rinse cloth diapers into the toilet. Bag disposable ones.
- Give small children a potty. Empty it into the latrine and rinse it.
- Wash the child's bottom and your hands with soap afterwards.
- Clean up any feces where children play.
- Make latrines safe for children: a small hole or a child seat, and an adult with them at night.

## Menstrual waste

- Put a covered bin inside women's toilets for used pads. Empty it often. Burn or bury the contents.
- Give women a private place, with water and soap, to wash and dry reusable cloths. Cloths must dry completely, in the sun if possible. See [Menstrual Hygiene](../hygiene/menstrual-hygiene.md).

## Cleaning toilets

- Clean seats, slabs, handles and doors **every day** with soap and water, then 0.1% bleach solution.
- Agree on who cleans, and give them gloves.
- Fix broken doors, locks and lights quickly. People stop using toilets that are dirty or unsafe.

## Safety for women, children and disabled people

- **Light** the path and the inside of the toilet at night. See [Light Without Electricity](../power/emergency-lighting.md).
- **Locks on the inside,** solid walls and a roof.
- **Separate, clearly marked** toilets for women and men, not side by side.
- Place toilets in busy, visible areas near shelters, not hidden away.
- Accompany children.
- For people who cannot squat or walk far, add a handrail and a seat over the hole, or use a bucket commode inside the shelter.

## Floods

Floods fill latrines and spread sewage everywhere. Do not use flooded latrines. Use bucket toilets until the water goes down. Treat all water. See [Floods](../disasters/flood.md).

If anyone gets diarrhea, start oral rehydration at once. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).
