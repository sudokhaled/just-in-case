> [!CAUTION]
> **Never use a charcoal grill, barbecue, camp stove, gas oven, patio heater or generator inside a home, garage, basement or tent.** They make carbon monoxide, an invisible gas with no smell that kills whole families in their sleep. Run generators outside, at least 6 m (20 ft) from doors, windows and vents.
>
> **Headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness or sleepiness in several people (or pets) at once means carbon monoxide.** Get everyone into fresh air immediately. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
>
> **Candles cause many deadly house fires during power cuts.** Never leave a flame alone, and keep it away from curtains, bedding and children.

## Quick steps

1. **Choose 1 small room** for everyone to live and sleep in. Close the doors to all other rooms.
2. **Block cold air.** Hang blankets over windows and doorways. Push rolled towels against gaps under doors.
3. **Make a small warm space inside the room:** a tent, or blankets over a table.
4. **Dress in layers** indoors, with a hat and warm socks. Sleep close together under shared blankets.
5. **Heat only with safe heat:** a working fireplace or stove with a chimney, or a heater made for indoor use, with a window opened slightly and a battery carbon monoxide alarm.
6. **Protect your water.** Let taps drip slowly on freezing nights. Keep drinking water in the warm room.
7. **Check on neighbors,** especially older people, babies and sick people.

## Choose one room

- Pick a small room with few windows and few outside walls. A window that gets sun in the day is a bonus.
- If you have a working fireplace or wood stove, use that room.
- Bring in mattresses, blankets, sleeping bags, and pets. Close off the rest of the home.
- Every resting person gives off about 100 watts of heat, like an old-style light bulb. Many people in a small room keep it warmer.

## Keep the heat in

### Windows

- Hang thick blankets, quilts or sleeping bags over windows, especially at night.
- Cover the glass with plastic sheet, bubble wrap or cardboard. Bubble wrap sticks to glass if you spray the glass with a little water first.
- In the day, open the coverings on windows that get direct sun. Close them as soon as the sun leaves.
- Fill gaps around window frames with tape, cloth or paper.

### Doors and floors

- Roll a towel or clothes against the gap under each door.
- Hang a blanket over the doorway you use. Go in and out quickly.
- Cover cold floors with rugs, carpets, cardboard or blankets, especially where people sit and sleep.

### A room inside the room

- Put up a tent inside the room and sleep in it. Your body heat warms the small space.
- No tent: put blankets over a table, bunk bed or chairs, and sleep under it on mattresses.
- Put plenty of insulation under you. The floor pulls heat away.
- **Never put a heater, candle or flame inside a tent or blanket shelter.**

## Heat safely

| Heat source | Safe indoors? | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Wood stove or fireplace with a working chimney | Yes | Open the damper or flue first. Burn only dry wood. Use a fire screen. |
| Kerosene (paraffin) heater made for indoor use | Yes, with care | Use only the correct fuel, never gasoline (petrol). Refuel outside when cold. Some places ban them indoors. |
| Propane or gas heater labeled for indoor use | Yes, with care | Many small gas heaters are for outdoor use only. Read the label. |
| Generator (gasoline, diesel or gas) | **Never** | Outside only, at least 6 m (20 ft) from doors and windows |
| Charcoal or wood grill, barbecue, camp stove | **Never** | Outside only |
| Gas oven or gas cooker | **Never for heating** | Use it only for cooking, with a window open |
| Candles | Very little heat | Use for light only, with great care |

For any heater that burns fuel:

1. Use only heaters made for indoor use. Read the label.
2. Open a window slightly, about 2 to 3 cm (1 in), while it burns.
3. Fit a battery carbon monoxide alarm in the room, if you have one.
4. Keep anything that can burn at least **1 m (3 ft)** away: curtains, bedding, clothes, furniture, paper.
5. Turn portable heaters off before you sleep or leave the room.
6. Keep children and pets away from it.

A chimney that has not been cleaned for a long time can catch fire. If you are not sure about yours, burn small fires and watch for smoke coming into the room.

> [!WARNING]
> **Carbon monoxide has no smell.** A heater can make it even when it seems to burn well. If anyone feels unwell, turn off the heater and get everyone outside into fresh air.

## Keep your body warm

- Wear layers indoors: warm underwear, wool, a hat, a scarf, thick socks. See [Staying Warm in the Cold](../shelter/stay-warm.md).
- Eat regular meals and have warm drinks. Your body burns food to make heat.
- Move around for a few minutes every hour, but do not sweat.
- Use hot water bottles in beds. Wrap them in cloth to prevent burns.
- Dress babies in layers and keep them close to an adult. Do not cover a sleeping baby's face or head, and do not use pillows or heavy quilts for babies.
- Older people, babies and sick people are at higher risk when indoor temperature falls below 18°C (64°F). Check on them often.

If you cannot keep at least 1 room warm, or anyone shows signs of hypothermia (strong shivering, confusion, clumsiness, sleepiness), move to somewhere with heat: a neighbor, a warming center or a shelter. See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](../medical/hypothermia-frostbite.md).

## Stop pipes from freezing

Water gets bigger when it freezes and can burst pipes. When the ice melts, the burst pipe floods the home.

- On freezing nights, let cold taps drip slowly. Moving water freezes less easily.
- Open cupboard doors under sinks so room air reaches the pipes.
- Wrap exposed pipes with towels, foam, newspaper or rags.
- Find the main water valve (stopcock) now. If a pipe bursts, close it at once.
- To thaw a frozen pipe, wrap it in towels soaked in hot water. **Never use an open flame** on pipes.

If the home will stay below freezing for days, drain the pipes:

1. Fill containers with drinking water first.
2. Close the main water valve.
3. Turn off the fuel or power to the water heater.
4. Open every tap, starting on the top floor, and let them run until empty.
5. Flush every toilet to empty the tanks.

## Keep drinking water from freezing

- Store drinking water in the warm room.
- Fill containers only about 90% full. Ice takes more space and can split them.
- Wrap containers in blankets, or put them in a cooler or a box of clothes.
- Store bottles upside down. Water freezes first at the top, so the opening stays clear longer.
- Keep 1 bottle in your bed at night.

For more on storing water, see [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md). For safe light, see [Light Without Electricity](../power/emergency-lighting.md). For other problems in a power cut, see [Long Power Outages](../disasters/power-outage.md).

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"A gas oven with the door open is a safe heater."** No. It can make carbon monoxide and is a fire risk.
- **"Using a grill indoors for a few minutes is fine."** No. Charcoal makes deadly amounts of carbon monoxide quickly.
- **"Flowerpot candle heaters warm a room."** They make very little heat and are a fire risk.
- **"Opening a window wastes all the heat."** A small gap gives the air a heater needs and lets fumes out. It is necessary.
- **"If I cannot smell anything, the air is safe."** Carbon monoxide has no smell.
