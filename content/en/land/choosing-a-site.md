> [!CAUTION]
> **Never build on low ground beside a river, in a dry riverbed or gully, or below old flood marks.** Floods return, often at night. A site that looks safe in the dry season can be under water in the rainy season.
>
> **Never build at the foot of a cliff, a steep or cracked slope, or in a strip of missing trees on a mountainside.** Rockfall, landslides and avalanches bury houses without warning.
>
> **Never put your toilet, animals or washing place uphill or upstream of your drinking water.** Dirty water kills more slowly than a flood, but it kills more people.

## Quick steps

1. **Find water first.** You need a source that flows all year, within about 500 m (550 yards). Check it in the dry season, or ask people who know.
2. **Find the highest flood mark** and build well above it: at least 60 cm (2 ft) above the highest flood anyone remembers. Higher is better.
3. **Look up and uphill.** Reject sites below cliffs, loose rock, cracked or steep slopes, avalanche paths, and dead or leaning trees.
4. **Choose gently sloping ground** that drains after rain. Never build where water pools.
5. **Check sun and wind** for your climate: warmth in cold places, shade and breeze in hot places.
6. **Check what is within walking distance:** firewood, building materials and soil for a garden.
7. **Talk to the people nearby.** Ask who owns and uses the land. Get permission before you build.
8. **Score the site** Reject any site that fails a deal-breaker, however good the rest is: no water all year within 500 m (550 yd), below flood marks or in a dry channel, signs of landslide or rockfall, inside storm surge or tsunami reach, or land that others claim and defend.

## The order of priorities

Decide in this order. A site that fails an early step is not saved by a later one.

1. **Water:** enough, all year, close and clean.
2. **Safety:** floods, slides, rockfall, avalanches, sea surges and trees.
3. **Drainage:** ground that sheds rain.
4. **Climate:** sun, shade and wind.
5. **Resources:** firewood, building materials, garden soil.
6. **People:** neighbors, roads and the right to use the land.

## Water

- **Distance:** aid workers set **500 m (550 yards)** as the longest distance from a home to water. Closer is much better. A 20 liter (5 gallon) container of water weighs 20 kg (44 lb).
- **Amount:** each person needs at least **15 liters (4 gallons) a day** for drinking, cooking and washing. A family of 5 carries 75 liters a day or more. Gardens and animals need far more.
- **All year:** a stream that is full in the wet season can be dry for months. Visit in the driest season, or ask older people. See [Finding Water](../water/find-water.md).
- **Upstream:** collect drinking water above where people and animals wash, drink or defecate.
- **Toilets:** you will need space for latrines at least **30 m (100 ft)** from the water and downhill from it. Pit latrines need ground where the water table is more than about 3 m (10 ft) deep. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).
- **Close, but not on the bank:** live a short walk up the slope from the water, not beside it. Banks flood, erode and breed mosquitoes.

Treat all drinking water, even from a good source.

## Natural dangers: look up, look uphill, look for marks

Walk the whole site and the slopes above it. Then ask the oldest people nearby: "Where did the water reach in the worst flood? Has this hill ever slid?" Their memory is better than any mark.

| Danger | Signs to look for | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| River flood | Lines of mud, grass and sticks caught in bushes and trees; mud stains on rocks and walls; flat land beside a river | Build at least 60 cm (2 ft) above the highest known flood, more if you can |
| Flash flood | Dry channels, gullies, gravel fans at the mouth of a valley, boulders in a dry bed | Never build in or beside a dry channel, even a small one |
| Landslide | Tilted trees, cracks in the ground, bulges at the foot of a slope, new springs or wet patches on a slope, bare scars | Stay off steep slopes and away from their foot |
| Rockfall | Scattered boulders below a cliff, fresh pale scars on rock, broken trees | Stay well beyond the farthest fallen rocks |
| Avalanche | Long open strips through forest, broken or missing trees, slopes of 30-45° above | Never build in or below these strips, even on flat ground far below |
| Storm surge and tsunami | Low, flat coast; debris lines far inland; local stories | Sleep high and inland: aim for 30 m (100 ft) above sea level or 3 km (2 miles) inland |
| Falling trees | Dead, leaning, rotten or split trees and hanging branches | Keep the home farther from any large tree than the tree is tall, or fell it first |
| Wildfire | Dry grass, brush or pine forest; burned stumps | Clear dry plants around the home; avoid the top of steep, brushy slopes, because fire runs uphill fast |

In earthquake country, also avoid loose filled ground, very wet sandy ground, and the foot of steep slopes. Shaking turns them into slides. See [Floods](../disasters/flood.md) and [Tsunamis](../disasters/tsunami.md).

## Slope, drainage and ground

- **Best slope:** gently falling ground, about **2-4%**: a drop of 2-4 cm in every meter (1 in 50 to 1 in 25). Rain runs off, but soil does not wash away.
- **Too steep:** more than about **10%** (1 in 10) erodes and needs heavy digging or terraces for every building and path.
- **Pools and marsh:** visit after heavy rain. Standing water, reeds, sedges or thick moss mean the ground stays wet. Wet ground rots walls, floods latrines and breeds mosquitoes.
- **Cold air sinks.** On clear, still nights, valley bottoms and hollows are much colder and get frost first. In cold climates, build part way up the slope.
- **Soil under the house:** firm, well-drained soil is best. Bare rock is hard to dig for latrines and foundations. Very loose sand makes pits cave in. Soil that cracks deeply in the dry season swells in the wet season and breaks walls.

## Sun and wind

At midday the sun is on the side toward the equator: **south in the northern hemisphere, north in the southern hemisphere.** Near the equator, the sun is almost overhead, so shade matters more than direction.

| Climate | Look for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cold or cool | A slope facing the midday sun, with a hill or forest blocking the cold wind | Winter sun warms walls and gardens; less firewood |
| Hot and dry | Shade from trees or a hill in the afternoon; shelter from dust storms | Keeps the house cool; dust causes lung disease |
| Hot and wet | Slightly raised ground open to the breeze; away from still water | Moving air cools people and dries walls; fewer mosquitoes |
| Mountains | The sunny side, part way up, away from avalanche and rockfall paths | Valley floors are cold and flood; ridges are windy |

Watch which way smoke drifts and which way trees lean. Put latrines, animal pens, cooking fires and rubbish **downwind** of the home.

## Firewood, materials and soil

- **Firewood:** a home that cooks and heats with wood burns a large pile every week. Within 1 or 2 years, the dead wood near camp is gone. Choose a place with enough woodland to cut, regrow and replant. In treeless places, plan for dung, crop waste or a very efficient stove.
- **Building materials:** look for clay soil, stone, straight poles, bamboo, or grass and reeds for a roof. Carrying them far takes months of extra work. See [Building a Lasting Home From Natural Materials](../land/build-home.md).
- **Garden soil:** dark, crumbly soil with strong plants growing in it is a good sign. White salty crusts, pure sand, hard clay that stays waterlogged, and land near mines or factories are bad signs.

## People, roads and the right to use land

- **Roads bring both help and danger.** Near a road you get news, trade, medical help and rescue. You also meet thieves, armed groups, disease and many hungry people. Many families choose a place a short walk from a road, but out of sight of it.
- **Neighbors matter more than hiding.** A family alone cannot defend itself, nurse its sick, or do the heavy work of building and harvest. Join or build a community. See [Organizing a Community](../community/community-groups.md).
- **Almost all land belongs to someone:** a family, a village, a clan, indigenous people or the state. Ask the local leader or elders who owns it and who uses it.
- **Agree on what you may do:** build, cut trees, collect grass and mud, take water, graze animals, hunt or plant. Agree in front of witnesses. Write it down if you can.
- **Avoid other people's fields, grazing land, water rights, graves and sacred places.** Settling on land that others claim leads to violence. Owners often return when a crisis ends.
- Many countries ban building or cutting trees in national parks and protected forests. In a true emergency survival comes first, but expect conflict later.

## How it differs by place

- **Forest:** wood and building poles are easy. Falling trees and fire are the main dangers. Build in a clearing, away from big trees. See [Living in a Forest](../land/forest-living.md).
- **Tropical rainforest:** heavy daily rain, insects and poor soil under the trees. Choose raised, well-drained ground and build raised floors. See [Living in a Tropical Rainforest](../land/jungle-living.md).
- **Desert:** water decides everything. Settle near a reliable well or spring, but never in a dry wash, where flash floods strike. See [Living in the Desert](../land/desert-living.md).
- **Coast:** fish, salt and trade, but storms, storm surge, tsunamis and salty wells. Sleep on high ground. See [Living by the Sea](../land/coast-living.md).
- **River or lake:** water, fish, fertile soil and easy travel, but floods and mosquitoes. Build on a natural step or terrace above the flood plain. See [Living by a River or Lake](../land/river-living.md).

## Site checklist

Score each line 0, 1 or 2. Lines marked **(D)** are deal-breakers: a 0 on any of them means **reject the site**, whatever the total.

| Question | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| (D) Water all year within 500 m? | No | Seasonal, or far | Yes, close |
| Water upstream of people, animals and toilets? | Below them | Unsure | Above them |
| (D) Above floods and out of dry channels? | No | Just above the highest mark | Well above |
| (D) Free of slide, rockfall and avalanche signs? | Signs present | Steep ground near | None |
| (D) Above storm surge and tsunami reach (coast only)? | No | Unsure | Yes |
| Dangerous trees within reach of the home? | Many, cannot fell | Some, can fell | None |
| Drains after rain? | Water pools | Flat | Gentle slope |
| Sun, shade and wind suit the climate? | Poor | Fair | Good |
| Firewood within 1 hour's walk? | No | Little | Plenty |
| Building materials nearby? | No | Some | Plenty |
| Soil for a garden? | Poor | Fair | Good |
| Neighbors you can work with? | Hostile | None near | Friendly, near |
| (D) Right to use the land? | Others claim it and object | Unclear | Agreed |

**Total out of 26** (inland sites: score the coast line as 2):

- **20 or more:** a good site.
- **14-19:** workable, with more labor.
- **Below 14:** keep looking if you can.

Score 2 or 3 sites the same way. Walk each one in rain and in dry weather if time allows.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing in the dry season without asking.** Flat land by the river looks perfect until the first flood.
- **"The big flood was a once-in-a-lifetime event."** It will come back.
- **Choosing a ridge for the view.** Ridges are windy, cold, hit by lightning and far from water.
- **Building first and asking permission later.** Land disputes are hard to undo once a house stands.
