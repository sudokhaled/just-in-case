> [!CAUTION]
> **Any fever in a rainforest may be malaria, which can kill within a day.** Sleep under a tucked-in mosquito net every night. Anyone with a fever needs a malaria test and treatment within 24 hours.
>
> **Rivers can rise fast and high after rain far upstream,** even under a blue sky. Never sleep on sandbars, low river banks or in dry stream channels.
>
> **Never eat raw cassava (manioc) or unknown fruit, and never drink milky or bitter plant sap.** Raw cassava contains cyanide.

## Quick steps

1. **Get off the ground.** Sleep in a hammock or on a raised bed, under a roof, inside a mosquito net.
2. **Catch rain** with a tarp or big leaves. Treat all stream and river water.
3. **Camp on high ground,** above flood marks, away from dead trees.
4. **Dry your feet every night.** Keep one set of dry clothes for sleeping only.
5. **Clean and cover every cut and bite** the same day.
6. **Eat known food first:** banana, plantain, coconut, palm, fish. Process cassava.
7. **Keep a fire going** under a roof, with wood drying above it.
8. **Mark every trail** from camp.

## What the rainforest is like

Up to 3.5 m (140 in) of rain falls a year, and nothing dries. Under the canopy you can see only about 50 m (160 ft), and aircraft cannot see you. Rescue is unlikely: plan to live where you are, or to travel out slowly along rivers.

## Water

Most water here carries germs. **Treat all stream, river, pond and swamp water.** See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).

| Source | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rain | Tarp or plastic sheet with one corner low; banana or palm leaves as gutters | The cleanest source. 1 mm of rain on 1 m² (11 sq ft) gives 1 liter |
| Green bamboo | Bend a green stem, tie the top down and cut off the tip. Water drips out overnight | Treat it |
| Banana or plantain stump | Cut the plant down, leaving a 30 cm (1 ft) stump. Scoop a bowl in the top; water seeps in | Throw away the first 3 fillings (bitter). Works up to 4 days |
| Palms | Drink green coconuts. Or cut the tip off a palm's flower stalk and catch the sweet sap | Up to about 1 liter a day. Milk of ripe brown coconuts is a laxative |
| Muddy streams | Dig a hole in sandy soil about 1 m (3 ft) from the bank. Clearer water seeps in | Always treat |

**Cover all stored water** and empty unused pots, cans and shells every week. Dengue mosquitoes breed in small pools of standing water.

### Water vines

1. Choose a vine with rough bark, about 5 cm (2 in) thick.
2. Cut a deep notch as high as you can reach. Then cut the vine through near the ground.
3. Let it drip into a container or your mouth. Do not touch the vine with your lips: some irritate skin.
4. Drink it only if it is **clear and watery, with no bitter or strange taste.**

**Never drink vine liquid that is milky, sticky, colored or bitter.** It may be poisonous. Learn which vines local people use.

## Staying dry and off the ground

The ground is wet and holds ants, scorpions, snakes and leeches. Get off it from the first night.

- **Hammock:** tie it between 2 strong live trees, with a mosquito net over it and a tarp or thatch roof above.
- **Raised bed:** use 4 trees in a rectangle, or drive 4 strong posts into the ground (bamboo is ideal). Tie 2 strong poles across them, lay more poles on top, and cover them with leaves. Build it higher than any flood mark.
- **Roof:** tie a ridge pole between 2 trees and lean poles on both sides into a steep A-frame. Cover it with a tarp, or with palm fronds laid in rows from the bottom up, each overlapping the one below like roof tiles.
- **Before you build,** look up for dead branches and coconuts, and around for ant and wasp nests.

## Food

Old gardens and village sites often still have banana, cassava and papaya. Leave food on the plant until you need it: it spoils fast. **Never eat a fruit you cannot name,** however ripe it looks. See [Foraging Wild Plants Safely](../food/foraging.md).

### Banana and plantain

Pick only from plants carrying hanging bunches of fruit, so you do not confuse them with other large-leaved plants. Eat ripe bananas raw. **Cook plantains and green bananas.** Each stem fruits once, then new shoots grow from its base. To plant them, see [Fruit Trees and Perennial Food Plants](../farming/perennial-food.md).

### Cassava (manioc, yuca)

**Identify:** a shrub 1 to 3 m (3 to 10 ft) tall, with hand-shaped leaves of 5 to 9 narrow fingers and long roots with brown skin and white flesh. **Look-alikes:** the castor oil plant and physic nut also have hand-shaped leaves, and their seeds can kill. Never eat the seeds of any of these plants.

All cassava contains cyanide. You cannot tell the safer "sweet" kinds from bitter kinds by looking, so **process all cassava:**

1. **Peel** it thickly. The peel holds most of the poison.
2. **Cut or grate** the root.
3. **Soak** it for **3 to 5 days,** changing the water every day or two.
4. **Boil** it in fresh water and throw that water away. Or sun-dry it and grind it into flour.
5. **For flour,** mix it with water into a paste, spread it no thicker than a fingernail, and leave it **5 hours in shade or 2 hours in sun** before cooking.

If it still tastes bitter, it is still poisonous. Badly processed cassava eaten daily can cause **konzo,** a sudden, permanent paralysis of the legs, especially in droughts and when people eat little protein. Eat it with fish, meat or insects.

### Sago palm

Sago palms grow in clumps in freshwater swamps of Southeast Asia, New Guinea and the Pacific islands. They flower once, at the top, then die. One trunk often holds more than 100 kg (220 lb) of starch.

1. Fell the palm before it flowers. Split the trunk.
2. Scrape or pound the soft inside into coarse sawdust.
3. Knead it in water over a cloth. The milky water drains into a container below.
4. Let the starch settle, pour off the water, and cook the starch as porridge or flat cakes.

> [!WARNING]
> **Cycads are deadly.** Some garden and wild plants called "sago palm" are cycads, not palms. They have stiff, very shiny, feather-shaped leaves on a short thick trunk, with a cone or a ball of seeds in the center. Never eat any part of a cycad.

### Palm hearts

The heart is the soft, pale growing tip inside the top of the trunk. Eat it raw or cooked. **Cutting it out kills a single-trunk palm,** such as a coconut palm that could feed you for decades. Palms growing in clumps regrow from other stems. Take hearts only from palms you can name.

### Fish and insects

Rivers are often the richest food source: see [Catching Fish](../food/fishing.md). Termites and beetle grubs live in rotting logs and palm trunks. **Cook all insects.** Avoid adults that sting or bite, hairy or brightly colored insects, caterpillars, insects with a strong smell, and flies, mosquitoes and ticks.

## Fire in the wet

- **Tinder:** dried coconut husk fiber, shavings from inside dry dead bamboo, tree resin, and the dry wood inside dead standing trees.
- Carry tinder and matches in sealed containers.
- Build the fire on a platform of logs or a pad of clay, with a rack above it to dry wood and clothes.
- **Keep the fire alive** under its own roof, high enough not to catch fire.
- **Closed bamboo sections explode in a fire.** Split them first.

See [Making and Keeping a Fire](../fire/make-fire.md).

## Clothing and foot care

Wear long sleeves and long trousers, tucked into socks or boots. Keep one dry set in a waterproof bag for sleeping only, and put the wet set back on each morning. Let sun reach skin rashes: fungus grows on damp skin.

**Feet that stay wet for days get immersion foot,** even in warm water. The soles turn white, wrinkled and swollen, then painful, cracked and infected, until you cannot walk.

1. Take off boots and socks at every long rest. Let your feet air-dry.
2. Every night, wash and dry your feet. Sleep barefoot or in dry socks.
3. Rotate 2 pairs of socks: wear one, dry the other.
4. Check your feet daily for cuts, blisters, white skin and swelling.
5. Never sleep in wet boots.

## Diseases: prevention first

| Disease | Spread by | Prevention | Warning signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Malaria | Mosquitoes, mostly from dusk to dawn | Treated bed net every night, long clothes, repellent | Fever, chills, headache, often 10 to 15 days after a bite. Test and treat within 24 hours |
| Dengue | Mosquitoes that bite in daytime | Cover skin, net when sleeping by day, empty standing water weekly | Fever and aches. After the fever drops: severe belly pain, repeated vomiting, fast breathing, bleeding gums. Use paracetamol, not ibuprofen or aspirin |
| Leptospirosis | Animal urine, mostly from rats, in water and mud | Waterproof cover on cuts, shoes, treat water, keep your head out of fresh water, seal food from rats | Fever, headache, muscle pain 2 to 30 days later. Needs antibiotics from a health worker |
| Leishmaniasis | Tiny sand flies, mostly from dusk to dawn | Fine-mesh or treated net (they pass through ordinary nets), cover skin | A skin sore that does not heal for weeks, or long fever and weight loss |
| Schistosomiasis (bilharzia) | Tiny worms from snails, through the skin in fresh water | Where it exists, do not wade, swim or bathe in fresh water. Boil bath water, or store it 1 to 2 days first | Itchy rash after water contact, fever weeks later, then blood in urine or stool |

**Any scratch or bite can become badly infected within days.** Wash it with soap and clean water, cover it and check it daily. Spreading redness or fever needs treatment. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).

## Dangerous animals

- **Snakes:** most bites are on feet, ankles and hands. Wear boots, watch where you step and reach, part plants with a stick, and use a light at night. See [Bites, Stings and Venom](../medical/bites-stings.md).
- **Leeches:** tuck trousers into boots and check your body often. Slide a fingernail under the narrow end to break its grip, then the wide end. The bite bleeds for a while: wash and cover it. Never drink untreated water: swallowed leeches cling inside the nose and throat.
- **Crocodiles and caimans** live in rivers, lakes, swamps and river mouths. Treat any longer than 1 m (3 ft) as dangerous. Never swim, wade, wash or clean fish where they live. Fetch water quickly, from a different place each time, and camp well back from the water.

## Getting lost under the canopy

- Mark every trail: cut bark or bend saplings to point the way, each mark visible from the last.
- To travel out, follow streams downhill to bigger rivers, where people live. Walk beside the water, not in it. Turn back at waterfalls and gorges.

See [What to Do When You Are Lost](../navigation/lost.md).

## Flash floods

Rain in the hills can send a wall of water down a stream within minutes.

- **Signs:** water rising fast, turning muddy or carrying leaves and branches, or a roar upstream.
- Mud lines and debris caught in branches show past floods. Camp well above them.
- If water rises, move uphill at once. Leave your gear.
- Moving water only 15 cm (6 in) deep can knock an adult down.
