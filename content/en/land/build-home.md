> [!CAUTION]
> **Heavy walls of mud brick, cob, rammed earth or stone kill people in earthquakes.** In earthquake country, build on 1 floor only, with thick low walls, a wooden ring beam on top of every wall, small openings and a light roof. A light timber or bamboo frame is safer still. See [Earthquakes](../disasters/earthquake.md).
>
> **Never sleep in a closed room with an open fire, brazier or charcoal burning.** A stove or fireplace is safe only with a working chimney that carries all the smoke out, some fresh air coming into the room, and ideally a battery carbon monoxide alarm. Carbon monoxide kills in sleep. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
>
> **Thatch burns in seconds.** Keep open fires and chimneys well away from it. Most thatch fires start where heat passes from a chimney into the thatch.

## Quick steps

1. **Choose the site first.** See [Choosing Where to Live: Water, Safety and Land](../land/choosing-a-site.md).
2. **Choose a method** that fits your climate, materials, and earthquake and flood risk.
3. **Give it "good boots":** a firm foundation and a stone or fired-brick base at least 30 cm (1 ft) above the ground.
4. **Build in the dry season.** Earth walls must dry before the rains.
5. **Give it "a good hat":** a roof with eaves about 60 cm (2 ft) past the walls.
6. **Dig drains** so rain flows away from every wall.
7. **Plaster the walls smooth** inside and out, and fill every crack.
8. **Make fire safe:** use a chimney, keep fire away from thatch, and always leave air openings.

## Be honest about the work

- One room of 4 x 5 m (13 x 16 ft), with walls 2.4 m (8 ft) high and 40 cm (16 in) thick, holds about **16 cubic meters (560 cu ft) of wall:** roughly **25-30 metric tons** (28-33 US tons) of wet earth to dig, mix, carry and lift. In adobe, that is about **1,500-2,000 bricks** of 16-18 kg (35-40 lb) each.
- Plan for months of work by several strong adults. Start with 1 small room.
- Live in a temporary shelter while you build. See [Building an Emergency Shelter](../shelter/emergency-shelter.md).

## Choose a method

| Method | Best where | Work | Lasts | Earthquake | Flood |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Log cabin | Cold forests with straight trees | High; needs axes | Decades if kept dry | Fairly good | Poor if logs stay wet |
| Pole frame with wattle and daub (bajareque, quincha) | Almost anywhere with poles and clay | Medium | 20-40 years on a stone base | Good: light, flexible | Frame often survives |
| Adobe (mud brick, banco) | Dry and semi-dry climates | High | Centuries if kept dry | **Deadly** unless reinforced | Melts in standing water |
| Cob | Dry to mild climates | Very high, slow | Centuries if kept dry | Poor unless reinforced | Melts |
| Rammed earth | Dry climates, gravelly soil | Very high; needs forms | Centuries if kept dry | Poor unless reinforced | Poor |
| Stone | Where loose stone is everywhere | Very high | Centuries | **Deadly**, worst in mud mortar | Good if well mortared |
| Bamboo | Wet tropics where it grows | Low to medium | 1-5 years untreated; decades treated | Very good | Fair if raised on posts |
| Earthbag | Dry areas, most soils | Medium to high; needs bags | Bags rot in sun unless plastered | Fair with barbed wire; little tested | Unknown |

## Earthquake rules for earth and stone walls

- **1 floor only.** Walls at least **40 cm (16 in) thick.**
- **Height no more than 6 times the thickness:** a 40 cm wall stands no higher than 2.4 m (8 ft).
- **Length no more than 10 times the thickness** between cross walls or buttresses: 4 m (13 ft) for a 40 cm wall.
- **Small openings:** together no more than one third of a wall's length. Each no wider than 1.2 m (4 ft), with at least 1.2 m of solid wall between openings and beside corners.
- **A ring beam** (bond beam, collar beam) on top of every wall, tied firmly at the corners: 2 timbers about 7.5 x 7.5 cm (3 x 3 in) joined by cross pieces every 40 cm (16 in), or poles or bamboo 10-12 cm (4-5 in) thick.
- **Buttresses** (thick pillars) at corners and along long walls.
- **Vertical canes or bamboo** inside the walls, tied to the foundation and ring beam, help hold cracked walls together.
- **A light roof.** Never a heavy earth roof or heavy clay tiles.

## Foundations, base and drainage

Earth walls fail at the bottom, where rain splashes and ground water rises.

1. **Remove the soft topsoil** under the walls.
2. **Dig a trench to firm soil,** at least 30 cm (1 ft) deep for a light house and deeper for heavy walls, about **twice as wide as the wall.**
3. **Fill it with stone** laid tightly in mud or [lime mortar](../land/natural-materials.md).
4. **Build a base wall (plinth)** of stone or fired brick at least **30 cm (1 ft) above the ground,** or 50 cm (20 in) where rain is heavy.
5. **Lay a damp-proof layer** on top: plastic sheet, tar or flat slates. It stops water rising into the earth wall.
6. **Make the roof overhang about 60 cm (2 ft).** A veranda protects walls even better.
7. **Slope the ground away** from the house. Dig a shallow ditch just outside the roof drip line and lead it downhill. Lay gravel or stones where the roof drips.

## Adobe mud bricks

### Test the soil

Use **subsoil** from below the dark topsoil. Good adobe soil has roughly **15-30% clay and at least 50% sand.** Too much clay cracks. Too little crumbles.

1. **Jar test:** fill a clear jar one third with soil, top it up with water and a pinch of salt, shake hard, and leave it 8 hours. Sand settles at the bottom, then silt, then clay. This test shows less clay than there really is.
2. **Drop test:** drop moist balls 4 cm (1.5 in) across from 1.5 m (5 ft) onto hard ground. Many small pieces: too little clay. A few pieces: often good. Flattens with few cracks: a lot of clay, so add sand.
3. **Roll (ribbon) test:** roll moist soil into a sausage 2 cm (0.75 in) thick and push it slowly over the edge of your palm. If it breaks off under 5 cm (2 in), there is too little clay. 5-15 cm (2-6 in) is good. Longer than 15 cm (6 in): add coarse sand.
4. **Shrinkage box:** fill a trough 4 x 4 cm (1.5 x 1.5 in) inside and 60 cm (2 ft) long with the wet mix, and dry it in shade for 7 days. Shrinking more than 6 cm (2.5 in), or more than 3 large cracks, means too much clay.
5. **Make 10 test bricks** before you make hundreds.

### Make and lay the bricks

1. **Soak the soil** in a pit for 12-24 hours, after removing roots and stones bigger than a thumbnail.
2. **Mix with your feet** into a stiff, even mud, with chopped dry straw or grass 5-10 cm (2-4 in) long. Straw stops cracking.
3. **Use a wooden mold** with no top or bottom. A common size is **10 x 25 x 36 cm (4 x 10 x 14 in).** Wet it and set it on ground sprinkled with sand.
4. **Throw the mud in hard,** press it into the corners, scrape the top flat, and lift the mold straight up.
5. **Dry flat for 3-4 days,** then turn each brick on edge. After about 10 more days, stack them loosely. Use them only after **3-4 weeks.**
6. **Protect drying bricks** from rain, and from hot sun and wind at first. Expect 1 brick in 10 to break.
7. **Lay them** in mortar of the same soil, sieved, without straw. Dip each brick in water for about 5 seconds first. Fill every joint, and overlap the joints.
8. **Set a strong wooden beam (lintel)** over every door and window, reaching well into the wall on both sides.

## Other walls

- **Cob:** mix the same soil with water and plenty of long straw into a stiff dough. Press balls about **20 cm (8 in)** across onto the wall, working each into the layer below. Build layers up to about **60 cm (2 ft)** high, letting each stiffen for a few days.
- **Wattle and daub:** posts carry the roof. Split sticks, canes or bamboo are woven between them, and clay mud with straw or dung is pressed into both sides. Stand posts on stones: posts buried in soil rot and feed termites.
- **Bamboo:** cut mature stems, not young green ones, in the dry season. Soak fresh poles in water, ideally running, for **4-12 weeks** to remove the starch insects eat. A borax and boric acid soak works better, if you have it. Keep bamboo off the ground and out of rain.

## Roofs

- **Grass thatch** needs a pitch of at least **45°**; palm leaves at least about 35°.
   - Tie bundles to thin poles (battens), starting at the bottom edge. Each row covers most of the row below, like feathers. Lay it about 15-20 cm (6-8 in) thick.
   - Expect repairs every 2-3 years. Well-laid thatch lasts 20-30 years.
- **Wooden shingles or bark sheets** need 35° or more. **Salvaged metal sheets** need at least 1 in 5 (about 11°).
- **Earth roofs** suit only very dry climates. They are deadly in earthquakes.
- **Tie every roof down** to the ring beam or wall top, or storms lift it off.
- Leave at least 2 m (6.5 ft), better twice the roof height, between thatched buildings, so fire cannot jump.

## Doors, windows and air

- In cold places, face the door away from the winter wind and put windows toward the midday sun.
- In hot, humid places, put openings on opposite walls for a cross breeze.
- Leave high vents or a gap under the roof for hot air and smoke. Cover openings with cloth or mesh against mosquitoes.

## Fire, smoke and stoves

- **Cook outside or in a separate kitchen hut** when you can. Daily smoke causes pneumonia in children and lung disease in adults.
- **Indoors, use a stove with a chimney:** an enclosed firebox of stone or mud, a hole the pot fits into, and a chimney of clay pipe, brick or metal leading outside. See [Cooking Without Power](../fire/cook-without-power.md).
- **Chimney height:** at least 1 m (3 ft) above where it leaves the roof, and 60 cm (2 ft) higher than any roof within 3 m (10 ft).
- **Through thatch:** use a thick masonry or clay chimney, never a thin metal pipe. Keep its top at least 1.2 m (4 ft), better 1.8 m (6 ft), above the thatch.
- **Clearances:** keep a stove at least 90 cm (3 ft) from wood, cloth, straw or thatch, unless stone, brick or thick mud stands between them. Keep metal pipe at least 45 cm (18 in) from anything that burns.
- **Clean the chimney often.** Soot catches fire.
- **Never close a room** while a fire, embers or charcoal still burn. See [Keeping a Home Warm Without Power](../shelter/heat-home-no-power.md).

## Insects, rats and Chagas disease

- In Latin America, **kissing bugs (triatomines)** hide in cracks in mud walls and thatch roofs. They bite at night and spread **Chagas disease,** which damages the heart years later. **Plaster walls smooth** and fill every crack, especially behind beds. Keep chickens, dogs and woodpiles out of the house and away from its walls.
- **Termites:** stand timber on stone and use hard or treated wood. A metal sheet between the base wall and wooden sills keeps them out.
- **Rats:** store food in sealed pots.

Place latrines at least **30 m (100 ft)** from any water source, downhill from it, and at least 6 m (20 ft) from the house. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).

## Common mistakes

- **Earth walls built straight on the ground.** They soak up water and collapse at the base.
- **Big windows, tall walls and heavy roofs** on earth or stone in earthquake country.
- **"A fire with a chimney is safe in a closed room."** Chimneys block and leak. Always leave air openings.
