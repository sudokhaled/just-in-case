> [!CAUTION]
> **Never build or sleep on low ground beside a river, or below old flood marks.** Rivers rise fast, often at night.
>
> **Never wade across fast water above your knees.** It knocks people over and drowns strong swimmers.
>
> **Treat all river and lake water.** Rivers carry germs from every village and animal upstream. In parts of Africa, the Middle East, Asia and South America, even wading or washing in still or slow fresh water can give you bilharzia.

## Quick steps

1. **Build above the highest flood marks,** a short walk from the water, never on the bank.
2. **Collect water upstream** of your camp, toilets, washing and animals. Settle, filter and treat it.
3. **Put toilets downstream,** at least 30 m (100 ft) from the water.
4. **Fish with traps and set lines** that work while you do other things.
5. **Cross only where the water is wide, shallow and slow,** below your knees, with pack straps undone.
6. **Stay out of the water** where there is bilharzia, and back from the edge where there are crocodiles or hippos.
7. **Sleep under a mosquito net.**
8. **Never drink or swim in water with green scum** or a paint-like film.

## What the river gives

### Water

- Collect from moving water, upstream of your camp and of any village, animals or fields you know of.
- Do not collect where people bathe or wash clothes, where animals drink, or below a town.
- **Cloudy water:** let it stand for 30 minutes or more, pour off the clear top, and filter it through cloth or sand. Then boil or chlorinate it. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).
- A hole dug in sand or gravel a few steps from the water's edge fills with clearer water. Still treat it.

### Fish

- Fish rest in deep pools, behind rocks, under banks and fallen trees, and where streams join.
- **Traps work while you sleep:** a V-shaped stone weir in a stream, a basket or bottle trap, a set line with many hooks. A net across slow water catches more, but can empty a small river. Leave gaps so some fish pass. See [Catching Fish](../food/fishing.md).
- Cook all freshwater fish fully. Raw freshwater fish spreads tapeworms and liver flukes.

### Farmland and irrigation

- Floods leave fresh silt, so floodplains have the richest soil. **Farm the floodplain, live above it.**
- **Flood-recession farming:** along rivers such as the Niger and the Senegal, farmers plant on the damp ground as the flood water goes down.
- **Lead water by gravity:** dig a small channel from a point upstream, sloping gently along the hillside to your fields. Return extra water to the river.
- **Lift water** with a shadoof: a long pole on a post, with a bucket on one end and a heavy stone on the other.
- Keep channels flowing and free of weeds. Still water breeds mosquitoes and the snails that carry bilharzia.
- See [Watering Crops With Little Water](../farming/watering-crops.md).

### Travel and power

- A raft or canoe carries heavy loads downstream easily. Going upstream is slow and hard. Never travel on a river in flood, or near rapids, weirs or low dams. Wear something that floats.
- A water wheel can grind grain or lift water, but it needs a strong, steady flow, careful building and repairs after every flood. It is a project for later, not the first weeks.

## Choosing a safe place

**Read the flood marks:**

- Grass, sticks and rubbish caught in bushes and tree branches.
- Mud stains and water lines on tree trunks, rocks and walls.
- Layers of sand or silt on the ground, and plants bent all in one direction.

The highest mark you find may not be the highest flood. **Ask local people where the worst flood reached.** Build at least 60 cm (2 ft) above the highest known mark. Higher is better. A natural step or terrace above the floodplain is ideal. See [Choosing Where to Live: Water, Safety and Land](../land/choosing-a-site.md).

| Flood type | Where | How fast |
|---|---|---|
| Slow flood | Large rivers in wide valleys | Rises over hours or days after long rain upstream |
| Flash flood | Small streams in hills, canyons, dry riverbeds | Rises in minutes, even with no rain where you are |

- **Watch the river every day.** Push a stick in at the water's edge each morning. Rising, muddy water carrying branches means move to high ground now.
- **Stay off undercut banks.** On the outside of bends, the river eats under the bank. The top looks solid, then collapses. Stay back from the edge.
- **Low gravel bars and islands** flood first. Never camp on them.
- **Live a short walk from the water.** Floods, mosquitoes, snakes and animals all come to the edge.

## Sanitation

- **Toilets at least 30 m (100 ft) from the water,** downhill and downstream of where you collect water. See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).
- **Wash bodies, clothes and dishes in a basin, not in the river.** Pour the dirty water onto the ground at least 30 m (100 ft) from the water.
- **Water animals downstream** of your collecting place, or carry water to them.

## Dangers

### Floods

Never walk or drive through moving flood water. 15 cm (6 in) of fast water can knock an adult down. See [Floods](../disasters/flood.md).

### Drowning and crossing rivers

Children drown quickly and silently. Keep young children within arm's reach of an adult near water.

**Do not cross** if the water moves faster than walking pace, is brown and muddy, carries branches or trees, or you hear a roar or rocks rolling. Wait. Small rivers often drop quickly after the rain stops.

If you must cross:

1. **Scout from high ground.** Choose a wide, shallow, slow stretch, or a place where the river splits into several channels. Make sure nothing downstream could trap you: rapids, falls, fallen trees.
2. **Undo your pack's hip belt and chest strap** so you can throw it off if you fall.
3. **Keep your shoes on.**
4. **Use a strong pole** on the upstream side as a third leg.
5. **Move one foot at a time,** feeling the bottom. Angle slightly downstream.
6. **Cross together** in a tight group, holding on to each other.
7. **If you fall,** drop the pack and float on your back, feet first downstream. Then swim at an angle to the bank.

Stay far from weirs and low dams: the water below them circles back and holds people under. Never swim across very cold water. See [Water and Ice Rescue](../navigation/water-rescue.md).

### Bilharzia (schistosomiasis)

Tiny larvae from freshwater snails pass through the skin of anyone who touches the water. More than 90% of people who need treatment live in Africa, but it also exists in parts of the Middle East, Asia, South America and the Caribbean. The worst places are still or slow water, lake shores, reeds, dams and irrigation canals.

- **Do not swim, wade, bathe or wash clothes** in fresh water in these areas.
- **For bathing,** use water that has stood in a container for at least 24 hours (2-3 days is safer), or heat it to 50°C (122°F) for 5 minutes.
- **Boil or chlorinate drinking water.**
- **Wear rubber boots** if you must work in water.
- **Never urinate or defecate in or near water.** Eggs in urine and stool infect the snails.
- Rubbing yourself dry after contact is not reliable protection.

Signs come weeks later: blood in the urine or stool, belly pain, diarrhea. A clinic can treat it cheaply with praziquantel.

### Leptospirosis

Urine of rats, cattle, pigs and dogs carries it into water and mud. It enters through cuts and through the eyes, nose and mouth. Risk is highest after floods, and for people who wade, fish or farm rice.

- Cover cuts with waterproof dressings. Wear boots in mud and water.
- Store food sealed so rats cannot reach it.
- Fever, headache, muscle pain (often the calves) and red eyes, 2 days to 4 weeks after contact, need antibiotics. Get care.

### Cholera and diarrhea

Rivers below villages carry cholera, typhoid and hepatitis A. For watery diarrhea, start oral rehydration solution at once. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).

### Malaria and mosquitoes

- Mosquitoes breed in still water: swamps, pools, puddles and containers. Camp on higher, breezy ground away from swamps.
- Sleep under an insecticide-treated net, tucked in. Cover your arms and legs from dusk.
- **Fever in a malaria area needs a test and treatment within 24 hours.** Severe malaria can kill within a day.

### Crocodiles, alligators and hippos

Crocodiles live in rivers, lakes, estuaries and mangroves across the tropics. Alligators and caimans live in the Americas and China. They wait unseen in murky water near the edge.

- **Stay at least 5 m (16 ft) back from the water's edge.** Do not swim or wade where they live.
- **Collect water quickly, do not crouch, and never use the same spot twice.** Crocodiles learn your habits.
- Clean fish, wash dishes and prepare food well away from the water. Throw scraps away from camp.
- Camp well away from the water (50 m or 160 ft is a common rule), and at least 2 m (7 ft) above the high-water mark.
- **Hippos** leave the water at night to graze. They attack people who come between them and the water. Keep away from riverbanks at dusk and night, and give hippos wide space in a boat.

### Leeches

Leeches live in slow fresh water and wet plants. Their bites bleed for hours and can get infected, but they are not dangerous.

1. Slide a fingernail under the narrow front end to break its grip, then the back end, and flick it away.
2. Do not pull hard, burn it or put salt on it. It may vomit into the wound.
3. Wash the bite, press on it, and cover it.

Check your body after wading. Tuck trousers into socks.

### Lakes: ice and toxic algae

- **Ice:** stay off clear ice thinner than 10 cm (4 in). Ice is thinner near inflows, outflows and reeds. See [Water and Ice Rescue](../navigation/water-rescue.md).
- **Toxic algae (cyanobacteria)** grow in warm, still water. Look for water like green paint or pea soup, scum, mats or foam, or a bad smell.
   - **Do not drink it, even boiled.** Boiling does not destroy the poison and can make it stronger.
   - Do not swim, and keep animals out. Dogs and cattle can die within hours.
   - Rinse skin with clean water after contact. Find another water source.
