> [!CAUTION]
> **Never walk, drive or swim through moving flood water.** **15 cm (6 in)** of fast water can knock an adult down. **30 cm (1 ft)** can float and carry away a small car. **60 cm (2 ft)** can sweep away most vehicles, including trucks and SUVs. Most flood deaths happen in vehicles. Turn around and find another way.
>
> **Flood water can carry electricity.** Stay out of water near fallen power lines, or in a building where the power may still be on.
>
> **Do not climb into a closed attic (loft) to escape rising water.** You can be trapped there and drown. Go to the roof only if you must.

## Quick steps

1. **Move to higher ground now** if you see water rising fast, hear a roar from upstream, or get a flood warning. Do not wait to be told twice.
2. **Leave early** if told to evacuate. Take water, medicines, documents and a phone.
3. **Never drive into a flooded road.** You cannot see how deep it is or whether the road is still there. Turn around.
4. **Trapped in a building:** go to the highest floor. Take water, a light, a phone and a whistle. Go onto the roof only if the water keeps rising.
5. **Car in deep or moving water:** seatbelt off, window open, children out, climb onto the roof.
6. **After the flood:** treat all flood water as sewage. Wash with soap and clean water, clean every wound, boil or treat drinking water, and throw away food that touched flood water.

## Before a flood

- Find out if you live in a flood zone: near a river, coast, lake, dam or in a low area or dry riverbed.
- Learn the local warning signals. Warning systems and terms differ by country. In the US, a flood **watch** means flooding is possible and a flood **warning** means it is happening or about to happen. Other countries use names such as "flood alert" and "severe flood warning", or sirens, flags or messengers.
- Plan a route to high ground that you can walk. Keep documents and medicines in waterproof bags. Store drinking water: taps and wells often become contaminated.
- If you have time before water arrives: move food and chemicals upstairs, turn off the electricity at the main switch (only if you are dry), and turn off gas and water. Tie down fuel tanks and gas cylinders: they float and leak.

## Flash floods

A flash flood can rise in minutes, sometimes when it is not raining where you are. Rain far upstream or a broken dam can send a wall of water down a valley.

- Dangerous places: dry riverbeds (wadis, arroyos), canyons, low roads, underpasses, basements, underground car parks and campsites next to streams. See [Living in the Desert](../land/desert-living.md) and [Living by a River or Lake](../land/river-living.md).
- Warning signs: water rising quickly, water turning muddy, branches and debris floating past, a loud roar from upstream.
- Act at once: climb to higher ground. In a valley or canyon, climb up the side. Do not try to outrun the water down the valley.
- Floods at night are especially deadly because you cannot see the depth or speed of the water.

## Why you must stay out of flood water

You cannot judge the depth or the speed. A road under water may already be washed away. Open manholes and drains can suck you under. The water carries logs, broken glass, sewage, fuel and snakes. "Turn around, don't drown" is the US National Weather Service rule: if a road is covered with water, do not drive or walk into it.

If you have no other choice, walk only in still or very slow water below your knees. Wear shoes. Feel ahead with a long stick for holes. Hold on to other people.

## Electricity

- Never touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water.
- Stay at least 10 m (33 ft) away from fallen power lines, and from any water they touch.
- Once water is inside the building, do not touch the main switch or the fuse box. Wait for an electrician or the power company.
- If someone is shocked in water, do not go in after them. Turn off the power if you can, or reach them with a dry wooden pole or rope.

## Trapped in a building

1. Go to the highest floor.
2. Take water, food, a phone, a flashlight, a whistle, warm dry clothes, medicines and a brightly colored cloth to signal.
3. **Do not go into a closed attic.** If the attic is the only high place, take an axe, saw or hammer so you can break through the roof if the water keeps rising.
4. Go onto the roof only if the water keeps rising. Sit away from the edge. Tie yourself to something strong if the current is strong.
5. Signal for help with the cloth, a light or a whistle. See [Signaling for Rescue](../navigation/signal-rescue.md).
6. Wait for rescue. Do not try to swim to safety unless the building is collapsing.

## Trapped in a car

If the car stalls in shallow water that is not moving, get out and walk to higher ground at once, before the water rises.

If the car is floating or sinking, you have about 1 minute while it floats. Do not spend that minute phoning for help. Act in this order:

1. **Seatbelt off.**
2. **Window open** right away, while electric windows may still work. If it will not open, break a side window near a corner with a hard pointed object (a window breaker, the metal posts of a headrest). Do not try the front windshield: it is made not to break.
3. **Children out first.** Unbuckle them, starting with the oldest, and push them out through the window.
4. **Get out** through the window and climb onto the roof.
5. Stay on the roof and call for help. Do not try to swim in fast water unless the car is sinking and safe land is very close.

Do not waste time on the doors. Once water is above the bottom of the door, the pressure makes it almost impossible to open.

## After the flood

### Flood water is sewage

- Flood water contains sewage, chemicals, fuel and dead animals. It causes diarrhea, skin infections and leptospirosis (a disease spread by rat urine).
- Wear boots and rubber gloves. After any contact, wash with soap and clean water. Wash clothes in hot water.
- Do not let children play in flood water or with toys that were in it.
- Clean every cut and scratch at once with soap and clean water, and cover it. Watch for redness, swelling, pus or fever. See [Cleaning and Caring for Wounds](../medical/wounds.md). Get a tetanus vaccine if the wound is dirty and your last tetanus shot was more than 5 years ago.
- Fever, headache, muscle pain (often in the calves) and red eyes from 2 days to 4 weeks after the flood can be leptospirosis. Get medical care: it is treated with antibiotics.
- For diarrhea, start oral rehydration at once. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md).

### Drinking water and wells

- Treat tap water and well water as unsafe until officials say it is safe. Boil it or disinfect it. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md).
- A flooded well must be pumped out, disinfected and tested before you drink from it. Until then, treat every drop.
- Boiling and bleach do not remove fuel or chemicals. If the water smells of fuel or chemicals, find another source.

### Food

- **Throw away any food that touched flood water**, including food in plastic bags, cardboard, jars, bottles with screw caps, and home-canned food.
- Throw away cans that are swollen, leaking, rusted or badly dented.
- Undamaged factory-made metal cans and sealed foil pouches can be saved. Remove the labels, wash with soap and water, rinse, then soak for 15 minutes in 15 mL (1 tablespoon) of unscented bleach per 4 liters (1 gallon) of clean water, or boil them for 2 minutes. Write the contents on the can with a marker.
- Wash and disinfect dishes, pots and baby bottles the same way before use.
- For food from a fridge without power, see [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md).

### Mold (mould)

- Dry the building within **24 to 48 hours** to prevent mold. Open doors and windows. Throw out soaked carpets, mattresses, soft furniture and wallboard that cannot be dried quickly.
- Wear an N95, FFP2 or P2 mask, gloves and eye protection when cleaning mold.
- Clean hard surfaces with soap and water. If you use bleach, use no more than 240 mL (1 cup) in 4 liters (1 gallon) of water. **Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners**: it makes poison gas.
- People with asthma, lung disease or a weak immune system should not clean mold.

### Snakes and animals

- Snakes, rats, scorpions, fire ants and, in some regions, crocodiles move into houses and debris piles during floods.
- Wear boots and gloves. Move debris with a stick. Look before you reach into any space. Do not touch dead animals with bare hands.

### Returning home

- Go back only when officials say it is safe.
- Do not enter if water is still around the building, or if the walls, roof or floor are cracked or sagging.
- If you smell gas, leave at once.
- Use a flashlight, not a candle. Do not turn on the electricity until an electrician has checked it.
- Run generators, pumps and pressure washers outside only, far from doors and windows. Many people die from carbon monoxide while cleaning up after floods. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
- Take photos of the damage before you clean, for insurance or aid.
