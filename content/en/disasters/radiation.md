> [!CAUTION]
> **Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned.** Fallout is most dangerous in the first hours. Being outside during that time can kill. Do not try to drive away unless officials tell you to.
>
> **Potassium iodide (KI) is not a general "radiation pill".** It only protects the thyroid gland from radioactive iodine. Take it only when public health officials tell you to.
>
> **Never swallow iodine tincture, povidone-iodine (wound antiseptic) or iodized salt instead of KI.** They do not protect you, and iodine tincture and povidone-iodine are poisonous when swallowed.

## Quick steps

1. **Get inside** the nearest solid building now. Brick or concrete is best.
2. **Go to the basement or the middle of the building,** away from the outside walls and the roof.
3. **Stay inside for at least 24 hours** unless officials tell you otherwise.
4. **If you were outside,** take off your outer clothes, put them in a bag, and wash or wipe your skin and hair.
5. **Close** windows, doors and vents. Turn off fans, air conditioning and heating that pull in outside air. Bring pets inside.
6. **Stay tuned** to a battery or crank radio for official instructions.
7. **Eat and drink** only food and water from sealed containers or from inside the building.

## Nuclear explosion: the first minutes

1. If you see a blinding flash, **do not look at it.** It can blind you.
2. Get behind something solid if one is right next to you, and **lie face down.** Cover your head and bare skin with your arms. Stay down until the blast wave has passed. It can arrive many seconds after the flash and break windows far away.
3. If you are in a vehicle, stop safely and duck down below the windows.
4. **Then get inside the best building you can reach within a few minutes.** Near the blast, fallout can start falling in about 15 minutes. Farther away it may take an hour or more. Fallout can look like dust, sand or ash, or you may not see it at all.
5. If a much better shelter (a basement or a large concrete building) is only a few minutes away, go there now. After that, stay where you are. Do not go out to look for family.

## Where to shelter

| Place | Protection from fallout |
|---|---|
| Outside, or in a car | Almost none |
| Ground floor of a wooden or light house | Low |
| Basement of a house | Good |
| Middle of a large brick or concrete building, away from the roof and outer walls | Good to very good |
| Underground: basement of a large building, underground car park, metro or subway tunnel | Best |

The more thick material (concrete, brick, soil) between you and the outside, the better. Fallout settles on roofs and on the ground, so stay away from both. After 24 hours, follow official instructions: you may be told to stay longer, or to leave by a set route.

### Why the first hours matter: the 7-10 rule

Radiation from fallout drops fast. For every 7 times more time after the explosion, the radiation level falls about 10 times.

| Time after the explosion | Radiation level, compared with 1 hour after |
|---|---|
| 1 hour | 100% |
| 7 hours | 10% |
| 49 hours (about 2 days) | 1% |
| 343 hours (about 2 weeks) | 0.1% |

This rule is for fallout from a nuclear explosion. After a reactor accident, radioactive material may keep being released for days, so follow official advice on when to leave.

## Removing contamination

If you were outside when fallout arrived:

1. As soon as you are inside, **take off your outer clothes and shoes.** This removes much of the radioactive material (up to 90%, according to the CDC).
2. Put them in a plastic bag, tie it, and put it far from people, such as in an outer room or outside.
3. **Shower with warm water and soap.** Wash your hair with shampoo or soap. **Do not use hair conditioner:** it makes radioactive material stick to hair. Do not scrub hard or scratch your skin.
4. If you cannot shower, wipe your skin and hair with a damp cloth or wet wipes, especially hands and face. Bag the used cloths.
5. Gently blow your nose. Wipe your eyelids, eyelashes and ears with a clean wet cloth.
6. Put on clean clothes that were kept indoors. Help children do the same. Wash pets with shampoo and water.

Never delay first aid for a serious injury in order to clean someone. A person who has taken off their outer clothes is safe to touch and help.

## Food and water

- **Safe:** food and drinks in sealed containers (cans, bottles, sealed packets and boxes) and food that was in the fridge, freezer or closed cupboards. Wipe the outside of containers with a damp cloth before opening them.
- **Not safe until officials say so:** food left uncovered outside, garden vegetables and fruit, rainwater collected after fallout, water from open wells or ponds, and milk from animals that grazed outside.
- For tap water, follow official advice. Water stored in closed containers indoors is safe. See [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

## Potassium iodide (KI)

KI fills the thyroid gland with safe iodine so it cannot take up radioactive iodine. That lowers the risk of thyroid cancer later, especially in children.

- It only helps when radioactive iodine is released, as after a reactor accident or a nuclear explosion. It does **not** protect any other part of the body. Shelter and safe food matter more.
- **Take it only when public health officials tell you to.** It works best shortly before or within a few hours of exposure.

| Who | Dose of KI (once in 24 hours) |
|---|---|
| Adults over 18 | 130 mg |
| Pregnant or breastfeeding women of any age | 130 mg |
| Teenagers 12-18 years who weigh 70 kg (154 lb) or more | 130 mg |
| Children and teenagers over 3 years through 18 years | 65 mg |
| Babies and children over 1 month through 3 years | 32 mg |
| Newborn babies (birth through 1 month) | 16 mg |

These are the US FDA doses, also printed on KI packages.

- **Take only 1 dose in 24 hours.** More does not protect you more. Take more doses only if officials say exposure is continuing.
- Pregnant women and newborn babies should not have repeated doses. A newborn who was given KI should have a thyroid check by a health worker.
- Adults over 40 gain little benefit. They should take KI only if officials say a very large exposure is expected.
- **Do not take KI if you are allergic to iodine.** People with thyroid disease or certain rare skin conditions should ask a health worker first if they can.
- For small children, crush the tablet and mix it into a little water, milk, juice or formula, as the package explains.
- Possible side effects: upset stomach, rash, swollen salivary glands. Rarely, a severe allergic reaction.

## Other radiation emergencies

- **Nuclear power plant accident:** follow official orders to shelter or evacuate. Planning zones around plants differ by country (about 16 km or 10 miles in the US). Keep a grab bag ready. See [Emergency Kit Checklists](../reference/emergency-kits.md).
- **"Dirty bomb"** (explosives with radioactive material): the explosion is the main danger, and the radiation usually affects a small area. Move away, get inside, remove your outer clothes and wash.
- **Lost radioactive sources:** never touch or pick up a metal object or container marked with the radiation symbol (a black three-bladed sign on yellow). Move away and tell the authorities.

## Radiation sickness

Radiation sickness comes from a large dose in a short time: close to the explosion, or outside in heavy fallout without shelter.

- **Early signs** (minutes to days): nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, fever, weakness. The sooner vomiting starts after exposure, and the worse it is, the higher the dose.
- The person may then feel better for days or weeks.
- **Later signs:** hair loss, bleeding gums, easy bruising, skin burns, infections and bloody diarrhea.
- Fear and stress also cause nausea and vomiting. Not everyone who vomits has radiation sickness.
- Radiation sickness is **not contagious.**
- **Care:** get medical help if any exists. Give fluids and oral rehydration solution (see [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md)). Keep the person clean and resting. Prevent infection with clean water, handwashing and wound care.
