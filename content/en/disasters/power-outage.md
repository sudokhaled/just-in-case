> [!CAUTION]
> **Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or charcoal indoors,** or in a garage, basement or shed, even with the doors open. Carbon monoxide has no smell and can kill in minutes. Keep generators at least **6 m (20 ft)** from doors, windows and vents.
>
> **Never plug a generator into a wall socket.** This "backfeeding" sends power into the grid and can electrocute neighbors and line workers.
>
> **Candles start many house fires during power cuts.** Use flashlights or battery lanterns. If you must use a candle, never leave it alone and keep it away from anything that can burn.

## Quick steps

1. **Check if it is only your home.** Look at neighbors' windows and street lights. Check your fuse box, circuit breakers or prepaid meter.
2. **Switch off or unplug** appliances and electronics. Leave **one light switched on** so you know when power returns.
3. **Keep fridge and freezer doors closed.**
4. **Fill clean containers with water now,** in case pumps stop.
5. **Save your phone battery.** Get news from a battery, crank or car radio.
6. **Check on neighbors,** especially older people and anyone who uses medical equipment.

## The first hour

- If only your home is dark, check your fuses or breakers, then report the fault to your electricity supplier if you can.
- If your area has planned outages (load shedding or rolling blackouts), learn the schedule and plan around it.
- Turn off large appliances (heaters, air conditioners, pumps, ovens, irons) and unplug computers, TVs and chargers. When power returns there can be a surge that damages them.
- Turn off electric stoves and ovens. A hot plate left on could start a fire when power returns while you are asleep or away.
- Find your flashlights and spare batteries.
- Write down the time the power went off. You will need it to judge food safety.

## Food

- A closed fridge keeps food cold for about **4 hours**. A full closed freezer keeps food frozen for about **48 hours** (about 24 hours if half full).
- Throw away perishable food (meat, fish, eggs, milk, cooked leftovers, soft cheese) that has been above **4°C (40°F) for more than 2 hours.**
- Frozen food that still has ice crystals, or is at 4°C (40°F) or colder, can be refrozen.
- In freezing weather, fill containers with water and put them outside to freeze. Use the ice in the fridge or a cool box.
- Eat food from the fridge first, then the freezer, then canned and dry food.
- For more detail, see [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md).

## Water

- Electric pumps stop: wells, pumps in tall buildings, and in time the town supply when its backup power runs out.
- When water pressure drops, germs can get into the pipes. Follow any "boil water" notice.
- Fill bottles and clean containers at once. Fill a bath or buckets for flushing and washing.
- To flush a toilet without running water, pour a bucket of water into the bowl.
- See [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

### Water from the water heater tank

A hot water tank often holds 100 to 200 liters (25 to 50 gallons) of clean water.

1. Turn off the electricity or gas to the heater.
2. Close the cold water supply valve to the tank.
3. Open a hot water tap somewhere in the house to let air in.
4. Let the water cool, then drain it from the tap at the bottom of the tank into clean containers. The first water may have sediment: let it settle.
5. **Before the heater is switched back on, refill the tank.** Open the supply valve and run a hot tap until water flows steadily. Heating an empty tank can damage it.

## Staying warm or cool

- **Cold:** live in one small room, close its doors, cover the windows with blankets at night, and wear layers and a hat. Sleep close together. Never use an oven or a gas stove to heat the room. See [Keeping a Home Warm Without Power](../shelter/heat-home-no-power.md).
- **Heat:** close curtains during the day and open windows at night. Stay on the lowest floor. Wet your skin and clothes, drink water, and check on older people often. If someone becomes confused or stops sweating in the heat, it is an emergency.

## Medical devices and medicines

- **Devices that need power** include oxygen concentrators, CPAP machines, home dialysis, suction machines, ventilators, medicine pumps and powered wheelchairs.
- Before an outage: get backup batteries, learn how long they last, and keep oxygen cylinders or manual alternatives if possible.
- Many electricity companies keep a priority list for people who rely on medical equipment. The name differs by country. Register in advance.
- **If a life-support device will run out of power, go early** to a hospital, clinic, shelter or any place with a generator. Do not wait until the battery is empty.

### Medicines that need a fridge

- **Insulin:** unopened insulin can usually be kept at room temperature, 15-30°C (59-86°F), for about **28 days**. Some types last longer or shorter. **Check the package leaflet.**
- Keep insulin out of the sun and away from heat. Never let it freeze or touch ice directly.
- Do not use insulin that has frozen, or that looks different from normal (clumps, crystals, or cloudy when it should be clear).
- If insulin got too hot and you have nothing else, it may still work, but less well. Use it and check blood sugar more often.
- A clay pot cooler, a cool bag, or a container wrapped in a wet cloth in the shade helps keep medicines cool.
- Other medicines that may need a fridge include some eye drops, liquid antibiotics, vaccines and injected medicines. Check the label and ask a pharmacist.

## Communication and phone battery

- A battery, crank or car radio gives news when phones and internet fail. See [Emergency Radio](../comms/emergency-radio.md).
- To save phone battery: turn on low power mode, dim the screen, close apps, and turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and location. Use airplane mode when you do not need signal.
- Send text messages instead of calling. They use less battery and get through more often when networks are busy.
- Mobile phone towers have backup power for a few hours to a few days, so service may stop.
- Charge phones in a car only with the car outside, never in a closed garage. Power banks and small solar chargers help. See [Batteries: Use, Charge and Stay Safe](../power/batteries.md).

To make any radio hear farther, or build one that needs no battery, see [Building and Improvising Radios](../comms/build-radio.md).

## Generator safety

- **Outdoors only,** at least 6 m (20 ft) from doors, windows and vents, with the exhaust pointing away from buildings.
- Put a battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm inside the home.
- Headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness or confusion, especially in several people at once, mean carbon monoxide poisoning. Get everyone into fresh air at once. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
- Plug appliances straight into the generator with heavy outdoor extension cords. To connect a generator to the house wiring, an electrician must install a transfer switch.
- Keep the generator dry, under an open cover. Never touch it with wet hands.
- Turn it off and let it cool before refueling. Fuel spilled on a hot engine catches fire. Store fuel away from living areas.

## Elevators and tall buildings

- Do not use elevators (lifts) during an outage or a storm.
- If you are stuck in an elevator, press the alarm button or call for help, and wait. Do not try to climb out or force the doors. Elevators are not airtight: you will not run out of air.
- In tall buildings the water pumps stop, so upper floors lose water. Stairwells may be dark: keep a flashlight with you.
- People who cannot use stairs may be stuck on upper floors. Neighbors should check on them and bring water and food.

## Security

- Lock doors and windows. Some people take advantage of dark streets.
- When traffic lights are out, drive slowly. In many countries the rule is to treat the crossing as a stop sign in every direction, but rules differ.
- Cash machines, card payments and fuel pumps may not work. Keep some cash and keep the car's fuel tank at least half full.

## When power returns

1. Wait a few minutes before switching things on. Power often flickers on and off at first.
2. Switch on large appliances one at a time, a few minutes apart.
3. Check the fridge and freezer. Throw out food that got too warm.
4. Refill the water heater tank before turning it on.
5. Replace used batteries, fuel and water, and write down what you were missing.
