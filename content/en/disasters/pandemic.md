> [!CAUTION]
> **Breathing-borne diseases spread most easily indoors, in crowded rooms with stale air.** Keep sick people apart, open windows, and wear the best-fitting mask you have around sick people.
>
> **Never drink, inject or spray bleach, disinfectant or alcohol on or into the body as a cure.** They poison and kill. Do not take animal medicines or miracle cures sold during outbreaks.
>
> **Get help early for danger signs:** trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, blue lips, or a person who cannot drink. See [Fever, Infection and Sepsis](../medical/infections-fever.md).

## Quick steps

1. **Find out how the disease spreads** (air, water, touch, body fluids or insects). Follow official health advice.
2. **Reduce contacts.** Stay home when you can. Meet outdoors. Keep at least 1 m (3 ft) away from others, more if you can.
3. **Wear a well-fitting respirator** (N95, FFP2, KN95 or P2), or the best mask you have, in crowded indoor places and around sick people.
4. **Open windows** to bring in fresh air.
5. **Wash hands** with soap and water for 20 seconds, often.
6. **If someone is sick,** keep them in one room with one caregiver.
7. **Get vaccinated** when a safe, approved vaccine is offered.

## Recognizing an outbreak

Warning signs:

- Many people in the same area fall sick with the same symptoms within a short time.
- Many households are sick at once.
- Unusual deaths, especially of young, healthy adults or children.
- Health workers fall sick.
- Many animals or birds die suddenly.
- Health authorities announce an outbreak.

An **outbreak** is more cases than expected in one place. An **epidemic** spreads over a large area. A **pandemic** spreads across many countries and continents. Alert levels and words differ by country.

Different diseases spread in different ways, so the right protection differs too:

| How it spreads | Examples | Main protection |
|---|---|---|
| Air and droplets from breathing, talking and coughing | Flu, COVID-19, measles, tuberculosis | Fewer contacts, masks, fresh air, distance |
| Dirty water and food | Cholera, typhoid | Safe water, toilets, handwashing |
| Body fluids: blood, vomit, diarrhea | Ebola | Gloves, no touching the sick or the dead without protection |
| Insects | Malaria, dengue | Bed nets, repellent, removing standing water |

## Reducing contacts

- Stay home when you are sick. Work from home if you can.
- See fewer people, in smaller groups, and the same few people each time.
- Meet outdoors. It is much safer than indoors.
- Send one person to shop for the household, less often.
- Greet people without touching.

## Masks

- **Best:** respirators (N95, FFP2, KN95, P2). They filter most particles, but only when they seal tightly to the face. Press the metal strip around your nose. A beard stops a good seal.
- **Next best:** surgical masks.
- **Least protection:** cloth masks. Several layers are better than one.
- Cover both nose and mouth. Do not touch the front. Change the mask when it is wet, dirty or damaged.
- In a shortage, a respirator can be used again if it still fits and is not wet, dirty or damaged. Between uses, let it dry in a paper bag.
- **The sick person should wear a mask** whenever anyone else is in the room.
- Do not put masks on children under 2 years old.

## Fresh air

- Open windows and doors, ideally on opposite sides of the room so air flows through. Even a small opening helps.
- A fan in a window, blowing out, pulls fresh air in through other openings.
- In cold weather, open windows wide for a few minutes several times a day.
- If you have power, an air cleaner with a HEPA filter helps in the sick room.

## Clean hands and surfaces

- Wash hands with soap and water for 20 seconds: after the toilet, before cooking and eating, and after coughing or caring for a sick person. See [Handwashing and Personal Hygiene](../hygiene/handwashing.md).
- Alcohol hand rub with at least 60% alcohol works when hands are not visibly dirty.
- Cough or sneeze into your elbow or a tissue. Throw the tissue away and wash your hands.
- Clean door handles, taps, phones and tables every day, more often if someone is sick.

## Plan for your household

- **Store supplies for 2 to 4 weeks:** water, food that keeps, refills of regular medicines, fever and pain medicines (paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen), oral rehydration salts, a thermometer, soap, bleach, masks, gloves, rubbish bags and tissues.
- **Choose one caregiver** for the sick, ideally someone healthy and not at high risk.
- **Prepare a sick room** with a window, and its own towel, cup, plate and, if possible, its own toilet.
- Decide who will care for children if the parents fall sick. Write down everyone's medicines, doctors and contacts.
- Agree with neighbors to check on each other by phone or through a window.
- For how to care for the sick and stop spread at home, see [Caring for the Sick and Stopping Spread](../medical/infectious-disease.md).

## Isolation and quarantine

- **Isolation** is for people who are **sick** or have tested positive. They stay away from others until they can no longer spread the disease.
- **Quarantine** is for people who were **exposed** but are not sick. They stay away from others because they may fall sick and spread the disease before they feel ill.
- How long each lasts depends on the disease. Follow health officials.
- At home: the isolated person stays in one room with the window open, eats there, and wears a mask when anyone comes in. Only the caregiver goes in.

## Vaccines

- Vaccines are one of the best ways to prevent severe illness and death in an outbreak.
- Accept approved vaccines when they are offered, especially for older people, health workers, people with long-term illness and, for vaccines recommended in pregnancy, pregnant women.
- Keep children's routine vaccines up to date. Measles and other diseases spread fast when vaccination stops during a crisis.

## Misinformation

False rumors spread faster than the disease and kill people too.

- Ask: Who said this? Is it a health authority? Is someone selling something?
- Be careful with miracle cures, secret "truths", urgent requests to "share with everyone", and messages with no named source.
- Trust the health ministry, the World Health Organization, local health workers and official radio.
- Do not forward messages you cannot check.

## Protecting vulnerable people

- People at higher risk include older people, people with heart or lung disease, diabetes or a weak immune system, pregnant women and babies. It depends on the disease.
- Shop and collect medicines for them. Leave things at the door. Talk by phone or through a window.
- Visitors should wear masks and meet them outdoors.
- Plan who will help if their caregiver falls sick. See [Elderly, Disabled and Chronically Ill People](../community/vulnerable-people.md).

## Keeping a routine

- Keep regular times for waking, meals, work or school, exercise and sleep.
- Limit news to once or twice a day, from trusted sources.
- Stay in touch with family and friends by phone, letters or outdoor visits at a distance.
- Fear, boredom and grief are normal. Talk about them. See [Psychological First Aid](../community/psychological-first-aid.md).
