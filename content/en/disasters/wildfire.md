> [!CAUTION]
> **Leave early.** Most people who die in wildfires left too late and were caught on the road, or stayed to defend a home that could not be defended. When the fire danger is extreme, or you are told to go, go.
>
> **In a house fire, get out and stay out.** Never go back inside for anything. You may have less than 2 minutes to escape.
>
> **Never throw water on burning oil or fat.** It explodes into a fireball. Cover the pan with a lid and turn off the heat.

## Quick steps

### Wildfire

1. Follow official warnings and fire danger ratings. Leave as soon as you are told to, or earlier.
2. Wear long cotton or wool clothes, strong shoes, and an N95 or P2 mask if you have one.
3. If you are trapped, shelter in a building, in a car, or on ground that has already burned. Get low, cover your skin with wool, and protect your face.

### House fire

1. Shout "Fire!", wake everyone and get out. Crawl low under the smoke.
2. Touch doors with the back of your hand before you open them. If a door is hot, use another way out.
3. Close doors behind you. Go to your meeting place. Call the fire service from outside.
4. If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop and roll.

## Wildfires

### Before: prepare your home and plan

- Learn your local fire danger ratings and warnings. They differ by country: for example, Australia rates days from "Moderate" to "Catastrophic", and the US issues "Red Flag Warnings". On the worst days, leaving early is the only safe choice.
- Most houses are set on fire by **embers** (burning pieces carried by the wind), often far ahead of the flames. Clear the area around your house:

| Distance from the house | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0-1.5 m (0-5 ft) | Nothing that burns: use gravel, stone or bare soil. No firewood, mulch, dry leaves or wooden furniture. |
| 1.5-9 m (5-30 ft) | Remove dead plants, dry grass and leaves. Keep tree branches at least 3 m (10 ft) from the roof and from other trees. |
| 9-30 m (30-100 ft) | Cut grass short, to 10 cm (4 in) or less. Space out bushes and trees. Remove low branches. |

- Clean leaves out of gutters and off the roof. Cover vents with metal mesh with holes no bigger than 3 mm (1/8 in).
- Plan at least 2 ways out of your area. Pack a grab bag (see [Emergency Kit Checklists](../reference/emergency-kits.md)). Keep the car fueled.

### When fire threatens

1. Leave early. If you see or smell fire nearby and feel threatened, go without waiting for an order.
2. If you have time: close all windows, doors and vents, move curtains and furniture away from windows, and turn off the gas.
3. Wear long sleeves and long trousers of cotton or wool, strong boots, gloves and a hat. **Synthetic clothes melt onto the skin.**
4. Drive with headlights on, windows closed and the air set to recirculate. Drive slowly in smoke.

### If you are trapped

**In a car** (a last resort, but much better than being in the open):

1. Park away from trees and bushes: in a clearing, on a road with no plants beside it, or on ground that has already burned. Do not block the road.
2. Turn on headlights and hazard lights. Close all windows and air vents.
3. Engine: if it still runs, many fire services advise keeping it running with the air on recirculate. Some say to turn it off. The next steps matter more.
4. **Get down below the windows**, on the floor. Heat from the flames passes through glass.
5. Cover yourself with a wool blanket, or with cotton or wool clothing. Not synthetic.
6. Stay inside until the flames have passed. It will be hot, smoky and frightening, but fuel tanks rarely explode. Then get out onto burned ground.

**At home** (if you cannot leave):

1. Go inside before the flames arrive. A solid house protects you far better than being outside.
2. Close windows, doors and vents. Fill sinks, baths and buckets with water. Put wet towels along the bottom of doors.
3. Stay in a room on the side away from the fire, away from outside walls, with 2 ways out.
4. Check the house often for embers and small fires, including the roof space. Put them out.
5. When the flames have passed, put out small fires around the house. Keep checking for hours: many houses catch fire long after the flames pass.

**On foot:**

- Go to a place with nothing left to burn: ground that has already burned (once the flames have died down), a wide road, a plowed field, a large rocky area, a beach, or shallow water.
- Do not try to escape uphill. Fire runs uphill fast. Avoid narrow valleys, which channel fire.
- Lie face down in a ditch or low area with no plants. Cover your body with wool or cotton clothing, or with soil. Breathe the air close to the ground through a cloth.

### Smoke

- Wildfire smoke harms the lungs and heart, most of all in children, older people, pregnant women and people with asthma or heart disease. See [Diabetes, Asthma, Heart Disease, Epilepsy Without Supplies](../medical/chronic-conditions.md).
- Stay indoors with windows and doors closed. If you have electricity, run an air cleaner with a HEPA filter in one room.
- **N95, P2, FFP2 and KN95 respirators** filter most smoke particles, but only if they fit tightly with no gaps. Beards stop them sealing.
- **Cloth masks, bandanas, wet cloths and loose surgical masks do not filter smoke well.** No mask protects you from carbon monoxide.
- Cough, wheezing, chest pain or trouble breathing need cleaner air and medical help. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).

## House fires

### Before: smoke alarms and an escape plan

- Fit a smoke alarm on every level of the home, inside every bedroom and outside each sleeping area. Test them every month. Replace alarms after 10 years.
- Make an escape plan with **2 ways out of every room** (usually a door and a window) and **1 meeting place** outside. Practice it twice a year, including at night. Teach children to get out and never to hide. See [Family Emergency Plan](../reference/family-plan.md).
- Keep keys for locked doors and window bars where everyone can find them in the dark.
- Sleep with bedroom doors closed. A closed door slows fire and smoke.

### Escaping

1. When the alarm sounds or you see smoke, shout "Fire!" and get out fast. Do not stop for belongings.
2. Smoke rises. Crawl on your hands and knees, where the air is cleaner and cooler.
3. Before opening a door, touch the door, the handle and the gap around it with the **back of your hand**. If it is hot, do not open it: use another way out. If it is cool, open it slowly and stay low. If smoke or heat rushes in, close it at once.
4. Close doors behind you as you go.
5. Go to the meeting place and count everyone. Call the fire service from outside.
6. **Never go back inside.** Tell firefighters who is missing and where they might be.

If you are trapped in a room:

1. Close the door. Push wet towels or clothes into the gaps and over vents.
2. Call the fire service and tell them exactly where you are.
3. If there is no smoke outside, open the window and wave a light-colored cloth or a flashlight.
4. Jump only as a last resort. Hang from the sill by your hands first, to shorten the fall.

### If clothes catch fire: stop, drop, roll

1. **Stop.** Running makes the flames bigger.
2. **Drop** to the ground.
3. Cover your face with your hands.
4. **Roll** over and over until the flames are out.
5. Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. See [Burns](../medical/burns.md).

For a small child, wrap them tightly in a blanket, coat or rug, or pour water on them.

### Kitchen fires

- **Burning oil or fat in a pan:** turn off the heat if you can. Slide a lid, a flat baking tray or a fire blanket over the pan. Leave it covered until it is completely cool. **Never use water.** Never carry the pan outside. Never throw flour on it: flour can burn.
- **Oven or microwave fire:** turn it off and keep the door closed.
- If the fire is spreading or you cannot cover it, get out and close the door.

### Fire extinguishers: PASS

Only fight a fire if **all** of these are true: it is small (like a fire in a wastebasket), everyone else is leaving, the fire service has been called, the room is not filling with smoke, and your way out is behind you. If in doubt, get out.

1. **Pull** the pin.
2. **Aim** the nozzle at the base of the fire, not at the flames.
3. **Squeeze** the handle.
4. **Sweep** from side to side across the base of the fire.

Small extinguishers empty in seconds. Labels for each type of fire differ by country. **Never use a water extinguisher on burning oil or electrical equipment.**
