> [!CAUTION]
> **Your life is worth more than your things.** If someone threatens you with a weapon, or a group demands your supplies, hand them over and get away. Things can be replaced.
>
> **Being alone in the dark makes you an easy target.** In camps and shelters, do not go alone at night to toilets, water points or isolated places.
>
> **Never move a generator indoors to stop it being stolen.** Its fumes kill in minutes. Chain it up outside instead.

## Quick steps

1. **Stay alert** in public: phone away, eyes up, know your way out.
2. **Do not show** or talk about your supplies, cash or fuel.
3. **Move in groups,** in daylight. Tell someone where you are going and when you will be back.
4. **If someone is angry,** keep 2 arm-lengths away, keep your hands open and visible, and speak low and slow.
5. **If threatened,** give up your property, then leave.
6. **If attacked,** shout, break free and run toward people and light.

## Stay aware

| Level | What it looks like | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Relaxed | Behind a locked door, with people you trust | Rest. |
| Alert | Normal state outside: calm, looking around | Notice people, exits and places to get help. |
| Something is wrong | Someone following you, a crowd forming, shouting, your path blocked | Decide where you will go. Move toward people, light and exits. |
| Danger | A threat, a weapon, an attack starting | Act at once: leave, hand over property, or fight only to escape. |

Trust the feeling that something is wrong. Do not wait for proof. Warning signs: someone asking what you have or when you are home; the same strangers passing again and again; someone trying to get you alone, or hiding their hands as they come near.

## Do not become a target

- Keep supplies out of sight. Carry them in and out when nobody is watching.
- Do not talk about what you have, except with people you trust.
- Dress like the people around you. Hide jewelry and expensive phones.
- Carry a little cash in an easy pocket and the rest hidden elsewhere. You can hand over the small amount.
- Do not leave supply packaging outside. Cooking smells, lights and engine noise also carry far when others have nothing.
- Change the routes and times of regular trips. Collect aid in groups and go straight home.

To trade and barter without showing your supplies, see [What Is Worth the Most When Everything Stops](../reference/most-valuable.md).

## Calm an angry person

How you stand:

- Keep **at least 2 arm-lengths** away: about 1.5-2 m (5-6 ft).
- Stand slightly sideways, not square on. Keep your hands open, visible and in front of your chest.
- Keep your face relaxed. Do not stare, point or touch.
- Never block their way out, and keep your own way out.

What you say:

- Speak slowly and quietly, in short sentences. Use their name if you know it. "I can see you are upset. Tell me what you need."
- Listen. Repeat back what they want: "You need water for your children. I understand."
- Agree where you can: "You are right, this is not fair."
- Do not argue, threaten, insult or laugh. Do not say "calm down"; it often makes people angrier.

If it is not working, leave: "Okay. Take it. I am leaving."

## When to give up property

- Always give it up if there is a weapon, more than one attacker, or the person is drunk or out of control.
- Throw the bag or cash to one side, away from you, and move the other way.
- Do not chase thieves. Do not go alone to get things back later.
- **The exception:** if someone tries to take you or a child somewhere else (into a vehicle, a building, away from other people), that is far more dangerous. Shout, resist and try to escape while others can still see you.

## Travel safely

- Go in a group of 3 or more if you can. Stay together, at the pace of the slowest person.
- Travel in daylight. Leave early enough to be back well before dark.
- Tell someone your route and when you will be back. Agree what they will do if you are late, and where to meet if you get separated.
- Avoid isolated shortcuts and crowds that are forming. See [Crowds, Crushes and Stampedes](../disasters/crowd-crush.md).
- Carry water, a whistle, a light, a little cash and copies of your documents.

For checkpoints and armed groups, see [Surviving Armed Conflict as a Civilian](../disasters/armed-conflict.md).

## Safety at night in camps and shelters

- **Light:** carry a flashlight (torch) or headlamp. Ask camp managers for lights at toilets and paths. See [Light Without Electricity](../power/emergency-lighting.md).
- **Buddy system:** go to the toilet at night with a trusted person. Children never go alone. Keep a potty or bucket inside for children at night.
- **Locks:** toilets and showers should be separate for women and men and lock from inside. If not, tell camp managers. A hook, bolt or strong cord loop can serve as a lock. See [Menstrual Hygiene](../hygiene/menstrual-hygiene.md).
- **Sleep** with your documents, cash and shoes close to your body.
- **Neighbors:** agree to come when someone shouts or blows a whistle. 3 whistle blasts means "help".

## Gender-based violence: risks and help

Rape, sexual exploitation and violence at home all increase after disasters and in camps. Women and girls are most at risk, but men, boys and people with disabilities are also targeted.

Common risks: collecting water or firewood, using toilets at night, crowded shelters with no privacy, and people demanding sex in return for food, aid, documents, transport or money.

**Aid is free.** Any aid worker, official, guard or leader who asks for sex or favors is breaking the rules. Report them to the agency or camp managers.

After sexual assault:

1. Get to a safe place and to someone you trust.
2. **Go to a health clinic as soon as possible.** Medicines can prevent HIV if started within **72 hours (3 days)**, and pregnancy if taken within **5 days (120 hours)**. Care for injuries and other infections helps at any time. See [Family Planning and Contraception](../medical/contraception.md).
3. If you might want to report to the police, try not to wash or change clothes before the examination. Get care either way.
4. Ask for a women's center or protection staff at UN agencies, the Red Cross or Red Crescent, or aid organizations.

If someone tells you they were assaulted: believe them, do not blame them, keep it private, and let them decide what to do next.

## Make your home harder to enter

- **Doors:** keep them locked, even when you are home. Fit a strong wooden bar across the inside, resting in two brackets, or use a wedge under the door.
- **Screws:** replace short screws in lock plates and hinges with long ones, at least 7.5 cm (3 in), that reach the wall frame.
- **Windows:** lock them, block ground-floor windows, and close curtains at night.
- **Light and noise:** an outside light at the door, bells on doors, tin cans on a string, gravel under windows.
- **Dogs:** a barking dog is one of the best warnings.
- **Neighbors:** agree to watch each other's homes.
- **Valuables:** hide them in several places, not one.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not block every way out. In a fire you must get out fast. Keep at least 2 exits that open quickly from inside, with keys next to locked doors.

## If you are attacked

- Your goal is to escape, not to win.
- Shout loudly or blow a whistle. Point at one person in a crowd: "You in the blue shirt, help me!" People act more when asked directly.
- Break free and run toward people and light.
- If you cannot get away, strike hard at the eyes, nose, throat or groin to make a moment to escape. Then run.
- A weapon you are not trained to use can be taken and used against you.

Afterwards, get first aid and talk to someone you trust. See [Psychological First Aid](../community/psychological-first-aid.md). For safety as a group, see [Organizing a Community](../community/community-groups.md).
