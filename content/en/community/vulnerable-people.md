> [!CAUTION]
> **Do not stop some medicines suddenly:** insulin, epilepsy medicines, steroid tablets taken for weeks, and some heart and mental health medicines. Stopping them can kill. See [Withdrawal and Running Out of Medicines](../medical/withdrawal.md).
>
> **A person with diabetes who becomes shaky, sweaty, confused or strange probably has low blood sugar.** If they can swallow, give sugar now. If they cannot swallow or are unconscious, put nothing in their mouth.
>
> **Never run a generator indoors** to power medical equipment. Keep it outside, at least 6 m (20 ft) from doors and windows. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).

## Quick steps

1. **Check** on older, disabled and ill neighbors daily, and twice a day in heat waves or severe cold.
2. **Write** each person's medicines, doses, conditions, allergies and devices on paper. Keep it with them.
3. **Find out** who depends on power (oxygen, dialysis, ventilators) and plan backup power or move them early.
4. **Evacuate early.** Bring mobility aids, glasses, hearing aids, dentures, medicines and chargers.
5. **Ask** the person how to help. Include them in decisions.

## Personal support network

- Choose **at least 3 people** (neighbors, friends, relatives) who will check on the person in an emergency.
- Give one a spare key. Show them where medicines are and how equipment works.
- Agree a signal for when phones fail, for example a sign in the window: "OK" or "HELP".
- Make sure the household is on the community's list of people needing help. See [Organizing a Community](../community/community-groups.md).

## Medicines and supplies

- The medicine list gives each medicine's generic name, strength, how often, and what it is for.
- Keep **at least 7 to 14 days** of extra medicine if you can. Store it cool, dry and dark, in its labeled box.
- If medicine runs out, take the box or list to any clinic, pharmacy or aid health post. Aid agencies often carry medicines for diabetes, high blood pressure, epilepsy and asthma.

See [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).

## Power-dependent medical devices

| Device | When the power fails |
|---|---|
| Oxygen concentrator | Keep oxygen cylinders as backup. Ask the supplier how many hours each lasts. Move the person to a place with power before they run low. |
| Dialysis | Contact the dialysis center early, or find another. Until dialysis, strictly limit fluids, salt and high-potassium foods (bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, dried fruit). Missing dialysis for several days can kill. |
| CPAP (sleep apnea) | Many run from a 12 V battery with the right adapter. Without it, sleep on the side, head raised, with no alcohol or sleeping pills. For most people this means poor sleep, not immediate danger. |
| Ventilator, suction machine | Life-sustaining. Keep backup batteries and a hand-powered pump or breathing bag, and learn to use them. Move to a hospital early. |
| Air mattress, electric wheelchair | Charge whenever possible. Without the mattress, turn a bed-bound person every 2 hours to prevent pressure sores. |
| Nebulizer | For most asthma attacks, an inhaler with a spacer works as well. A 500 mL plastic bottle can be made into a spacer. |

> [!WARNING]
> Oxygen makes fires burn fiercely. Keep oxygen at least 3 m (10 ft) from flames, stoves, candles and cigarettes.

## Mobility and evacuation

- Leave at the first warning. People with disabilities need more time.
- Plan who helps and how: a car, a cart, a wheelbarrow, or a chair carried by two people.
- Ask first: "How can I help you?" "What is the best way to move you?"
- Never separate a person from their wheelchair, walker, cane or guide dog.
- In shelters, give them a ground-floor place near toilets, with a chair to sit on.

## Hearing and vision loss

- **Hearing loss:** get attention with a wave or a light touch on the shoulder. Face them, with light on your face, and speak clearly. Do not shout. Write, draw or use gestures. Give them a buddy to alert them to sirens and warnings.
- **Vision loss:** say who you are, and say when you leave. Offer your arm: they hold it just above your elbow and walk half a step behind you. Describe what is ahead: "Two steps down". Do not move their things. Read notices aloud.

## Dementia and confusion

- New places, noise and darkness make confusion worse. Keep a familiar person and routine. Use short sentences. Do not argue or correct. Reassure.
- **Wandering is common.** Put an ID card in a pocket and inside the clothing: name, "has dementia", carer's contact. Keep a recent photo. Hang a bell on the door at night.
- If they go missing, search close by at once: bushes, ditches, drains, sheds, cars and near water. They may not answer when called.
- **Confusion that gets suddenly worse** over hours or days is often a medical problem: infection, dehydration, pain, constipation, low blood sugar or a medicine. Give fluids and find a health worker.

## Diabetes without normal supplies

### Keeping insulin

- Store unopened insulin in a fridge at 2-8°C (36-46°F). **Never freeze it**, and do not use insulin that has frozen.
- Without a fridge, most insulin can be kept at 15-30°C (59-86°F) for up to **28 days**. Write the date on it.
- Keep it in the shade, never in the sun or a hot car. A clay pot, or a container wrapped in a wet cloth, stays cooler.
- Do not use insulin that is clumpy, frosted or discolored, or clear insulin that has turned cloudy.
- Overheated insulin may work less well. If it is all you have, use it and check sugar more often.

People with type 1 diabetes must **never stop insulin completely**, even when not eating. Without it they develop a deadly condition: great thirst, passing lots of urine, vomiting, belly pain, deep fast breathing, drowsiness. Get medical help urgently. See [Diabetes, Asthma, Heart Disease, Epilepsy Without Supplies](../medical/chronic-conditions.md).

### Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)

It happens with insulin or some tablets (gliclazide, glibenclamide (glyburide), glimepiride), especially with missed meals, extra work or alcohol. When food is short, ask a health worker whether doses should change.

Signs: shaking, sweating, fast heartbeat, hunger, irritability, confusion, behavior that looks drunk, fits, unconsciousness.

If the person is awake and can swallow:

1. Give **15-20 g of fast sugar**, any one of:
   - 4 teaspoons of sugar stirred into water.
   - 150-200 mL (5-7 fl oz) of fruit juice or a non-diet soft drink.
   - 1 tablespoon of honey or syrup.
   - Glucose tablets as the packet says.
2. Wait **15 minutes**. Check again with a meter, or see if the signs have gone.
3. If sugar is still below 4 mmol/L (70 mg/dL), or signs remain, repeat step 1.
4. When better, give a snack with starch: bread, rice, biscuits.

If the person cannot swallow safely or is unconscious, put nothing in the mouth. Lay them on their side and get help. Give glucagon only if you have it and know how.

**No meter?** If someone on insulin or these tablets acts strangely, treat it as low sugar. A little sugar does little harm if you are wrong. Untreated low sugar can kill.

## Heat and cold

- Older people, people with heart or kidney disease, diabetes or dementia, and people taking water pills (diuretics) or some mental health medicines are at high risk in heat. They often do not feel thirsty. Offer drinks often. See [Heat Exhaustion and Heat Stroke](../medical/heat-illness.md).
- In cold, older people lose heat fast and may not feel cold. Keep one room warm. Give layers, a hat, warm drinks and food. Confusion and drowsiness can mean hypothermia. See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](../medical/hypothermia-frostbite.md).

## Dignity and choice

- Speak to the person, not only to their carer.
- Ask what they want and let them decide whenever possible. Even confused people can make many choices.
- Give privacy for washing and toilets. Help with incontinence quietly.
- Older and disabled people have skills and knowledge. Give them roles.
- Watch for neglect and abuse, including theft of their food, aid or money.
- **Service animals:** assistance dogs, such as guide dogs, go wherever the person goes, including shelters. Many countries require this by law. Bring the dog's food and papers. Do not pet or distract a working dog.
