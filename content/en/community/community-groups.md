> [!CAUTION]
> **Keep the list of vulnerable households and the location of shared stores private.** In the wrong hands it becomes a list of targets for thieves and abusers.
>
> **Never allow beatings or mob punishment,** even for theft. Separate the people involved and hand serious crimes to the authorities when you can.
>
> **A shared kitchen can make many people sick at once.** Cooks with diarrhea, vomiting, fever or infected cuts must not handle food.

## Quick steps

1. **Call a meeting** of at least one adult from every household, women included, at a fixed place and time.
2. **Deal with urgent problems first:** injured people, missing people, fires, gas leaks, unsafe buildings.
3. **List** people, skills, tools, supplies and needs.
4. **Mark** households with vulnerable people on a simple map.
5. **Choose** a lead for water, sanitation, health, food, watch and information.
6. **Write** the rules for sharing and post them where everyone can read them.
7. **Fix** the time of the next meeting.

## Why groups survive better

- Neighbors rescue most people trapped in rubble, in the first hours, long before professional teams arrive. See [Finding and Freeing Trapped People](../navigation/search-rescue.md).
- Shared work leaves time to rest. Tools and skills go further.
- People who live alone, especially older people, die more often in heat waves and disasters. A group checks on them.
- Belonging to a group protects mental health.

## The first meeting

Keep it short and practical. Write everything down.

| Ask about | Examples |
|---|---|
| Health skills | Nurse, doctor, midwife, pharmacist, first aider, vet |
| Building skills | Builder, carpenter, plumber, electrician |
| Food skills | Farmer, gardener, cook, butcher, fisher |
| Other skills | Mechanic, driver, radio operator, teacher, interpreter, faith leader, bookkeeper |
| Tools and supplies | Water containers, filters, fuel, stoves, big pots, generators, solar panels, radios, carts, tools, tarps, medicines |
| Needs | Medicines, baby food, power for medical devices, help to move, shelter |

Then draw a simple map. Number each home and mark who needs extra help (see [Elderly, Disabled and Chronically Ill People](../community/vulnerable-people.md)).

 | 4 B|  | 5  |  |6 DM|     M  needs medicine or power

## Roles and rotations

| Team | Main jobs |
|---|---|
| Water | Find sources, treat water, keep containers clean, protect the source. |
| Sanitation | Build and clean toilets, set up handwashing stations, manage rubbish. |
| Health | Run a first aid point, visit vulnerable homes every day, watch for diarrhea and fever. |
| Food | Count stores, ration, run the kitchen. |
| Watch | Lookouts in pairs, day and night, for fire and danger. They raise the alarm. They do not fight. |
| Information | Notice board, radio listening, messages, missing persons list, rumor checks. |
| Children | Supervised play and lessons, so parents can work. |

- Give every team a lead and a deputy, so the work goes on if one is sick.
- Rotate hard, dirty and night jobs so they are shared fairly. Keep night watch shifts short, for example 2 to 4 hours.
- Everyone who can work does some work. Older people and teenagers can take lighter roles.

See [Toilets and Sanitation](../hygiene/sanitation.md).

## Fair rationing and record keeping

1. **Count** all shared food, water, fuel and medicine.
2. **Record** every movement in one book: date, item, amount, from or to whom, and 2 signatures.
3. **Open stores only with 2 people present,** from different families.
4. **Agree the rules in public:** the same basic share per person, with extra for pregnant and breastfeeding women, young children, the sick and people doing heavy work.
5. **Distribute in daylight, in public, at fixed times,** from a household list. Tick names off. See [Crowds, Crushes and Stampedes](../disasters/crowd-crush.md).
6. **Every week, work out how many days the stores will last:** total amount divided by daily use.

Many aid agencies give household rations to a woman in each family, because the food then more often reaches the children.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 May | Rice | 50 kg | | Aid truck | A.K., M.S. |
| 4 May | Rice | | 12 kg | Kitchen | M.S., J.O. |

For how much food each person needs, see [Food Priorities and Rationing](../food/food-rationing.md).

## Making decisions and settling conflicts

- Choose a small committee of 5 to 7 people. Include women, older people, young people and every main group in the area.
- Agree how long they serve, for example 1 month, then choose again.
- Discuss openly. If you cannot agree, vote. Write every decision on the notice board.
- Rules apply to everyone, including leaders and their families.

When two people or families clash:

1. Separate them and let them calm down.
2. Ask a respected, neutral person to help.
3. Let each side speak without interruption.
4. Focus on the need behind the fight (water, space, fairness), not on who is bad.
5. Agree on a solution, write it down, and check again a few days later.

## Sharing information

- **Notice board** in a central place. Put the date and time on every notice. Sections: water and food times, health advice, missing and found people, rules, news.
- **Mark news** as "confirmed" (with its source, for example official radio) or "not confirmed".
- **Runners:** teenagers in pairs carry written messages. Spoken messages change as they pass.
- **Meetings** at a fixed time and place: daily at first, then less often. Keep them short.
- **One alarm signal** for danger, such as a bell or a whistle, that everyone knows.
- Tell people who cannot read or cannot come, in person and in their language.

For a neighborhood text network without internet, see [Text Messages Without Internet: Mesh Radios and Phones](../comms/mesh-networks.md).

## Stopping rumors and hoarding

- Before passing on news, ask: who saw it, when, and did official sources confirm it?
- The information team corrects false rumors quickly, at meetings and on the board.
- Rumors that blame a group ("they poisoned the well") can lead to violence. Stop them early.
- People hoard when they do not trust the system. Open records, fair rules and regular distributions reduce hoarding.
- Families may keep their own supplies. Shared stores are for everyone. Selling shared or aid supplies is not allowed.

## Protecting women, children and minorities

- Put women on the committee and in every team, especially water, food and safety.
- Make toilets and washing places separate for women and men, lockable from inside, lit at night and close to shelters.
- Collect water and firewood in groups.
- Nobody may ask for sex, money or favors in return for aid or a job. Remove anyone who does from their role and report them.
- Register children without their families. See [Keeping Children Safe and Calm](../community/children-safety.md).
- Minorities, migrants and newcomers get the same share and the same voice.

For safety at night and travel, see [Staying Safe from Other People](../community/personal-safety.md).

## Community kitchen

One large kitchen uses less fuel than many small fires, and it feeds people who cannot cook.

- Put a handwashing station with soap at the kitchen and at the toilets.
- Use treated water for cooking and drinking.
- Cook food through. Serve it hot. Eat cooked food within 2 hours, or keep it hot.
- Keep raw meat away from other food. Cover food from flies.
- Wash pots and spoons in hot, soapy water after every meal.

See [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md).

## Working with neighboring groups

- Meet their representatives regularly. Swap news, warnings and lists of missing people.
- Share skills: your nurse, their mechanic. Trade what you have extra.
- Agree on shared water sources: drinking water upstream, washing downstream, no toilets nearby.
- Make a joint plan for fire, flood and crime. Write down every agreement.

For what people value most in long crises and how to trade fairly, see [What Is Worth the Most When Everything Stops](../reference/most-valuable.md).
