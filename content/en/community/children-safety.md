> [!CAUTION]
> **Children get separated from families in crowds, evacuations and aid queues.** Hold their hands. Never put a child on a different vehicle from you, or pass them over a fence to strangers.
>
> **Small children can drown in a few centimeters (an inch or two) of water.** Cover every bucket, barrel and water container, and empty them after use. Never leave a young child alone near water.
>
> **Never store fuel, bleach or pesticide in drink bottles.** Children drink them. Kerosene (paraffin) poisoning kills many children.

## Quick steps

1. **Label** each child with their name and a family phone number, inside their clothes or on their arm in pen.
2. **Photograph** each child. Keep a paper copy.
3. **Teach** children their full name, your full names and where you come from.
4. **Agree** a meeting point in every new place.
5. **Teach:** if lost, stay where you are and ask a mother with children or a uniformed helper.
6. **Remove dangers:** cover water, lock away fuel and medicines.

## Preventing separation

- **ID on every child:** the child's name, parents' names, a phone number and an out-of-area contact (see [Family Emergency Plan](../reference/family-plan.md)). Put it in a sealed plastic bag pinned inside clothing. In a crisis, write it on the upper arm in pen and renew it daily.
- **Do not write the name on the outside** of clothes or bags. A stranger can use it to gain the child's trust.
- **In crowds,** carry babies in a sling and have older children hold your bag strap. Count heads before you move. See [Crowds, Crushes and Stampedes](../disasters/crowd-crush.md).
- **If a child must go to hospital,** send an adult too, or write down who took the child and where.

## If a child is lost

Teach your child:

1. Stay where you are, if it is safe. "We will come back for you."
2. Ask a mother with children, or someone in uniform who works there: police, Red Cross or Red Crescent, camp staff.
3. Say your full name, your parents' names and where you are from.
4. Do not leave with anyone you do not know, even if they say they will take you to your parents.

Parents:

1. Go back to where you last saw the child, and to your meeting point. Leave one adult there.
2. Tell the police, camp management, Red Cross or Red Crescent, or UNICEF staff at once. Show the photo.
3. Register the child as missing with the Red Cross or Red Crescent family tracing service. It works across borders.

## If you find a child alone

1. Stay with the child where you found them for a while, if it is safe. The family may be nearby.
2. Write down where and when you found the child, the names they give, and what they wore.
3. Take the child to the authorities, camp management, Red Cross or Red Crescent, or UNICEF to be registered. Keep brothers and sisters together.
4. **Do not** take the child to another town, and do not keep or adopt them yourself.
5. **Do not** hand the child to anyone claiming to be family until the authorities have checked. Traffickers pretend to be relatives.

> [!NOTE]
> Many children who seem to be orphans have a living parent or relative searching for them. Adoption is not appropriate during an emergency.

## Explaining what happened

| Age | What helps |
|---|---|
| 0-2 years | Holding, feeding, warmth, a calm voice, the same carer. |
| 3-6 years | Short, true words: "The storm broke our house. We are safe here. I will stay with you." Say it is not their fault. Answer the same questions again and again. |
| 7-12 years | More facts. Correct rumors. Explain what adults are doing to keep them safe. Give small jobs. |
| Teenagers | Honest, full information. Include them in plans and give real responsibilities. |

Keep children away from frightening adult talk and images when you can.

## Routines and play

- Keep the same times for waking, meals and sleep, and a bedtime habit: a song, story or prayer.
- Make time for play every day: drawing, songs, games. Play is how children recover.
- Give some lessons every day, even for 1 hour.

## Changed behavior is normal

After a disaster, children often cling, cry, wet the bed, suck their thumb, talk like a baby, have tantrums or nightmares, or fear the dark. Teenagers may withdraw, argue or take risks.

- Respond calmly. Do not punish, shame or hit. Hitting adds fear and slows recovery.
- Give extra closeness. Most of these behaviors fade over weeks as life becomes stable.

Get help if there is no improvement after about 1 month, or if the child stops eating, talking or playing, or talks about dying or hurting themselves. See [Psychological First Aid](../community/psychological-first-aid.md).

## Protecting children from abuse and exploitation

Abuse, trafficking, child labor, early marriage and recruitment by armed groups all increase in disasters and camps. Abusers are usually people the child knows or people in positions of trust, including people who give aid.

- Do not leave children with people you have just met.
- Send children to toilets, water points and aid queues in groups with an adult, especially at night.
- **Aid is free.** Nobody may ask for sex, money or favors in exchange for food, help or documents. Report anyone who does.
- Distrust offers of jobs, school, marriage or travel for a child, especially abroad.
- Warning signs: an adult giving gifts, wanting time alone with the child, or asking for secrets. Sudden fear of a person. Pain or bleeding in the genital area.
- Teach: "Your body belongs to you. If anyone touches you in a way you don't like, say no, get away, and tell me. You will not be in trouble."

If a child tells you about abuse: believe them, stay calm, and say it is not their fault. Keep them away from that person. Get medical care within 72 hours (3 days) if possible, to help prevent infections. Report to protection staff, a health worker or the authorities. Do not confront the abuser alone.

## Safe sleep for babies

- Lay babies **on their back** to sleep, every time.
- Use a **firm, flat surface** with no pillows, soft toys or loose heavy blankets: a cot, a basket, or a clean box with a firm pad that fits with no gaps, next to the carer.
- **Never** sleep with a baby on a sofa or armchair.
- Do not share a bed with a baby if any adult in it has drunk alcohol, taken drugs or sleepy medicines, smokes, or is exhausted, or if the baby was born early or small.
- Keep the head uncovered. Dress the baby in no more than 1 layer more than an adult needs.
- Keep babies away from smoke and fumes. Use a mosquito net where there is malaria.

For feeding, see [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).

## Dangers that hit children hardest

| Danger | What to do |
|---|---|
| Buckets, barrels, wells, pits, floodwater | Cover or empty containers. Cover pits and wells. No playing in floodwater. |
| Fuel, bleach, pesticides, medicines | Keep labeled and locked or high up. If a child swallows fuel, do not make them vomit. See [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md). |
| Cooking fires, candles, hot pots | Keep a 1 m (3 ft) child-free zone around fires and stoves. |
| Unexploded bombs, shells, mines | Do not touch, do not go near, go back the way you came, tell an adult. See [Surviving Armed Conflict as a Civilian](../disasters/armed-conflict.md). |
| Diarrhea and heat | Children dehydrate fast. Give oral rehydration solution early. Keep breastfeeding. See [Dehydration, Diarrhea and Oral Rehydration](../medical/dehydration.md). |
| Damaged buildings, glass, fallen power lines | Keep children out. Make them wear shoes. |
