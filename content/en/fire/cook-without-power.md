> [!CAUTION]
> **Never cook with charcoal, a barbecue grill or a camp stove indoors,** in a tent or in a garage. Carbon monoxide kills without warning. Cook outside, or under a roof with open sides. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke: The Silent Killers](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
>
> **Boil dried beans hard for at least 10 minutes before slow cooking.** Raw or undercooked red kidney beans are poisonous. A haybox or solar cooker alone may not get them hot enough.
>
> **Food in a haybox must stay hot.** If it is only warm when you open it, boil it again before eating.

## Quick steps

1. **Cook outside,** or with strong ventilation.
2. **Always use a lid.**
3. **Soak** dried beans for 8 to 12 hours and rice or whole grains for 30 minutes before cooking.
4. **Cut food into small pieces.**
5. **Bring it to a boil, then use the smallest flame** that keeps a gentle simmer.
6. **Finish cooking in a retained-heat box:** boil, then wrap the pot in insulation for 1 to 4 hours.
7. **Burn small dry sticks in a rocket stove** instead of logs on an open fire.

## Save fuel

- **Lid on.** An uncovered pot loses much of its heat as steam.
- **Simmer, do not boil hard.** Water cannot get hotter than about 100°C (212°F), however much fuel you burn.
- **Soak.** Soaking beans overnight, then draining and rinsing them, cuts their cooking time a lot.
- **Cut small.** Small pieces cook much faster.
- **Use only the water you need.** Cook rice and grains in just enough water to be absorbed.
- **Choose quick foods:** red lentils, oats, couscous, bulgur, thin noodles. Many canned foods are already cooked and safe to eat cold.
- **Shield the flame** from wind, and put the pot right in the tips of the flames.
- **Use dry, split wood.** Wet wood wastes heat drying itself out.
- **A pressure cooker** cooks beans and grains much faster, with much less fuel.

## Rocket stove

A rocket stove burns small sticks in an insulated L-shaped tube. The hot gases rise straight up a short chimney to the pot. It burns hotter and cleaner than an open fire, so it uses less wood and makes less smoke.

```
        +-------------------+
        |        POT        |
        +-------------------+
          ||     gap     ||     supports leave a
      +-----+           +----+  2-3 cm (1 in) gap
      |#####|     ^     |####|  under the pot
      |#####|  chimney  |####|
      |#####|     ^     |####|  # = insulation:
      |#####|     ^     |####|  wood ash, or clay
 +----+#####+     ^     |####|  mixed with sawdust
 | sticks ==>   FIRE    |####|
 +--------- shelf ------+####|
 |  air ==>             |####|
 +----------------------+----+
```

### Build one

- **From a large can:** use a big metal can or bucket as the outside. Inside, make an L-shaped tube about 10 cm (4 in) wide from a pipe elbow or smaller cans. Fill the space around it with wood ash. Put a piece of metal across the feed opening as the shelf.
- **From bricks or stones and mud:** build a square chimney with a feed opening at the bottom of one side. Pack mud mixed with ash or sawdust around it.
- **From hollow concrete blocks:** stack 4 or 5 so one lies on its side as the feed tube and the others form the chimney. It wastes more heat, and concrete can crack.

### Rules that make it work

- **Keep the chimney short:** about 2 to 3 times as tall as it is wide.
- **Keep the same width all the way through:** feed opening, chimney and the gap under the pot.
- **Insulate around the chimney** with light materials such as wood ash, pumice, or clay mixed with sawdust. Heavy sand and stone soak up the heat.
- **Burn only the tips of the sticks.** Lay them on the shelf so air flows underneath. Push them in as they burn.
- **Use it outdoors.** A rocket stove still makes carbon monoxide.

## Retained-heat cooking (haybox)

Food that has been brought to a boil keeps cooking if you wrap it in thick insulation. You use fuel only for the first few minutes.

1. **Make the box:** a box, basket, bucket or hole in the ground, with at least 10 cm (4 in) of insulation on every side, top and bottom. Use hay, straw, wool, blankets, crumpled paper, foam or a sleeping bag. Make a nest the size of your pot.
2. **Use a pot with a tight lid,** filled as full as you can. A full pot holds heat longer.
3. **Boil the food** for the time in the table below.
4. **Move the pot into the nest quickly** and cover it. Do not open it until the time is up.
5. **Check it is still steaming hot** before eating.

| Food | Boil first | Then leave in the box |
|---|---|---|
| White rice | 5 minutes | 1 to 1.5 hours |
| Lentils | 5 to 10 minutes | 1 to 2 hours |
| Soaked dried beans | 10 minutes or more, at a hard boil | 3 to 4 hours |
| Potatoes, root vegetables (cut small) | 5 minutes | 1 to 2 hours |
| Stew with meat (cut small) | 15 minutes | 3 to 4 hours |

These times are a guide. Thicker insulation and fuller pots cook faster.

> [!WARNING]
> Germs multiply fast in food between 5°C and 60°C (40°F and 140°F). If food is only warm when you open the box, or has been in the box more than 4 hours, boil it again before eating. Do not leave food in the box overnight to eat cold in the morning.

## Solar cookers

- They work only in **strong, direct sun,** mostly from mid-morning to mid-afternoon.
- **Box cooker:** an insulated box, black inside, with a glass or clear plastic lid and a shiny reflector.
- **Panel cooker:** cardboard covered with aluminum foil, folded around a dark pot. Put the pot in a heat-resistant clear cooking bag or under a glass bowl.
- Use thin, dark pots with lids. Turn the cooker to face the sun every 30 to 60 minutes.
- Cooking is slow: rice and stews often take 2 to 4 hours. Food may not reach a full boil, so boil dried beans hard on a fire first.
- **Dish-shaped (parabolic) cookers** focus the sun into a very hot spot that can burn skin, start fires and damage eyes. Wear dark glasses and never look into the dish.

## Camp stoves, candles and other options

- **Gas canister and propane camp stoves** are efficient but make carbon monoxide. Use them outdoors or with plenty of fresh air, never in a tent or small closed room.
- **Alcohol stoves** have an almost invisible flame in daylight. Never refill one while it is lit or still hot.
- **A home gas stove** often still works in a power cut. Light the burner with a match. Open a window. Never use it to heat the home. Do not use it if you smell gas, or after an earthquake may have damaged the pipes.
- **A fireplace** can be used to cook over coals, if the chimney is undamaged and clear.
- **Candles** give too little heat to cook a meal, and they start house fires. Keep them for light.
- **Gel fuel cans** (chafing fuel) are good for warming food, not cooking it.
- **Never heat an unopened can.** It can burst and scald you.

## Cooking safely

- Put stoves and fires on stable, non-flammable ground, away from walls and anything that can burn.
- Keep children away from fires and hot pots. Turn pot handles inward.
- Keep water or sand nearby. **Never throw water on burning oil.** Turn off the heat if you can and cover the pot with a lid.
- Wash your hands after handling dung or crop-waste fuel, and before touching food.

For keeping food safe without a fridge, see [Food Safety Without a Fridge](../food/food-safety.md).

## Cooking with poor fuel

- **Dried dung and crop waste** (straw, maize cobs, husks): dry them well and burn them in a stove. They make a lot of smoke, so cook outside.
- **Charcoal and coal:** a lot of carbon monoxide. Outside only.
- **Wet or green wood:** smoky, with little heat. Split it and dry it near the fire.
- **Never burn plastic, rubber, painted or treated wood, or rubbish** for cooking. The smoke is poisonous and can coat the food.

To boil drinking water with the least fuel, stop as soon as it reaches a rolling boil. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](../water/purify-water.md). To build and keep a fire, see [Making and Keeping a Fire](../fire/make-fire.md).
