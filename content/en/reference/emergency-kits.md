> [!CAUTION]
> **Water is the most important item and the heaviest.** Plan **4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day**. Three days of water for one person weighs 12 kg (26 lb), too much for most bags, so carry 2-3 liters plus a way to treat more.
>
> **A kit without your daily medicines is not complete.** Pack extra medicine and a written list of names and doses.
>
> **Never use camping stoves, barbecues or generators indoors.** Their fumes kill.

## Quick steps

1. **Start today** with the first 10 items: water for 3 days, bleach or purification tablets, food for 3 days, 7 days of medicines, a flashlight, a radio, first aid supplies with oral rehydration salts, document copies, cash, and a whistle with a foil blanket.
2. **Put copies** of documents and a contact card in a waterproof bag.
3. **Pack a grab-and-go bag** for each person and keep it near the door.
4. **Build up** the home supply to 2 weeks, a little at a time.
5. **Choose 2 dates,** 6 months apart, to check everything.

## The first 10 items

If money is short, get these first, in this order.

1. Water in clean, closed containers: 3 days at 4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day.
2. Plain, unscented bleach or water purification tablets.
3. Food for 3 days that needs no cooking.
4. At least 7 days of your daily medicines, and a written list.
5. A flashlight (torch) or headlamp, and spare batteries.
6. A battery or hand-crank radio.
7. Basic first aid supplies and oral rehydration salts.
8. Copies of documents and a contact card, in a waterproof bag.
9. Cash in small notes and coins.
10. A whistle and an emergency (foil) blanket.

## Grab-and-go bag (72 hours, one per person)

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Water | 2-3 liters (2-3 quarts), plus purification tablets or a small filter |
| Food | 3 days, no cooking: nuts, dried fruit, energy bars, crackers, cans with ring-pull lids |
| Medicines | 7 days of daily medicines, and a list with doses |
| First aid | Bandages, gauze, tape, gloves, pain relief, oral rehydration salts |
| Light | Flashlight or headlamp, spare batteries |
| Radio | Small battery or hand-crank radio |
| Phone | Charger cable and a charged power bank |
| Clothes | 1 change, a warm layer, rain jacket or poncho, hat, socks, sturdy shoes |
| Warmth | Emergency blanket or light blanket |
| Hygiene | Soap, toothbrush, sanitary pads, toilet paper, hand sanitizer, small towel |
| Tools | Knife or multi-tool, lighter and matches in a waterproof bag, duct tape, whistle |
| Masks | 2-3 dust masks (N95 or FFP2) |
| Papers | Document copies, contact card, family photos, local paper map, pen and notebook |
| This library | Printed copies of the most important pages |
| Cash | Small notes and coins |
| Personal | Spare glasses, hearing aid batteries, comfort items for children |

Test the bag: walk with it for 30 minutes. If it is too heavy, take things out.

## Home supply (2 weeks)

- **Water:** 4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day. For 2 weeks that is **56 liters (15 gallons) per person**, plus extra for pets. See [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).
- **Food:** 2 weeks of food your family already eats, mostly needing little or no cooking. A manual can opener. See [Storing Emergency Food](../food/food-storage.md).
- **Water treatment:** plain, unscented bleach. Replace it every year.
- **Cooking:** a camping stove and fuel, for outdoor use only.
- **Light and radio:** flashlights, headlamps, batteries, a radio.
- **Medical:** a first aid kit and at least 2 weeks of medicines. See [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).
- **Toilet:** 2 buckets with lids, strong rubbish bags, toilet paper, soap, bleach for cleaning.
- **Tools:** a wrench to turn off gas and water, a tarp, rope, duct tape, work gloves, a fire extinguisher.
- **Warmth:** blankets or sleeping bags for everyone.
- **Alarms:** a battery smoke alarm and a carbon monoxide alarm.
- **This library:** download and print it while you still have power and internet. Keep a paper copy and a copy on a USB stick or e-reader.
- **For morale:** books, cards, games, paper and pencils for children.

To choose supplies that also make daily life cheaper or easier, see [Preparing Wisely: Things and Skills That Pay Off Every Day](../reference/everyday-prep.md).

## Car kit

- Water (4 liters or 1 gallon) and snacks.
- Blanket, warm clothes, sturdy walking shoes.
- Flashlight, first aid kit, phone charger, paper map.
- Jumper cables, tow strap, spare tire and jack, warning triangle.
- In winter: ice scraper, small shovel, sand or cat litter for grip.
- Keep the fuel tank at least half full.

## Workplace kit

Be ready to stay at work for at least 24 hours, or to walk home.

- Comfortable walking shoes and socks.
- Water, snacks and at least 3 days of your medicines.
- Flashlight, phone charger, small first aid kit, dust mask, rain jacket.
- Cash and a paper map of the route home.

## Baby kit

- Diapers (nappies) for at least 3 days. A newborn can use 10 a day.
- Wipes, rash cream, plastic bags for dirty diapers.
- If not breastfeeding: ready-to-use liquid infant formula, which needs no water, and clean cups. Cups are easier to keep clean than bottles. See [Feeding Babies and Young Children in an Emergency](../food/feeding-babies.md).
- If breastfeeding: keep going. The mother needs extra water and food.
- Clothes, a hat, blankets and a baby sling or carrier.
- Oral rehydration salts, a thermometer, infant medicines as labeled.
- Copies of the birth certificate and vaccination card, and a photo of the baby with a parent.

## Pet kit

- Food and water for at least 3 days, in waterproof containers, with bowls.
- Leash, collar with ID tag, carrier or crate.
- Litter or poop bags, medicines and vaccination records.
- A recent photo of you with your pet, to prove it is yours.
- Many shelters do not accept pets, except service animals. Find pet-friendly places in advance.

## Documents and cash

- Make paper copies of: ID cards and passports, birth and marriage certificates, property or rental papers, insurance, bank details, medical records, prescriptions (including glasses), and children's vaccination cards.
- Write important phone numbers on paper. Phones die.
- Add a recent photo of each family member, to help searches if you are separated.
- Put everything in 2 zip-lock bags, one inside the other. Keep originals somewhere safe and carry copies.
- A USB stick copy is a backup, not a replacement for paper.
- **Cash:** card machines and cash machines stop when the power fails. Keep cash in small notes, because nobody may have change. Split it between several places.

## Check every 6 months

| Check | What to do |
|---|---|
| Stored water | Replace it, unless it is sealed bottled water within its date |
| Food | Eat and replace anything that expires in the next 6 months |
| Medicines | Replace expired ones. Update the list. |
| Batteries, power banks | Test, recharge, replace |
| Bleach | Replace if more than 1 year old |
| Clothes and shoes | Check children's sizes. Change for the coming season. |
| Baby items | Update for the baby's age and size |
| Documents, contact cards | Update phone numbers and addresses |
