> [!CAUTION]
> **Skills before gear.** A kit you do not know how to use will not save you. A first aid course, cooking from basic food and swimming are worth more than any expensive "survival" product.
>
> **Never go into debt or skip food, rent or medicines to buy supplies.** Debt is a crisis of its own. Build slowly from what you can spare.
>
> **Do not store what you cannot store safely.** Large amounts of fuel or gas bottles in the home can burn or explode. Food you never eat rots and wastes money.

## Quick steps

1. **Learn before you buy.** Take a first aid course, learn to cook from rice, beans and flour, and learn to swim. These cost little and help every week.
2. **Buy things you will use now.** Choose items that make daily life easier or cheaper: a headlamp, a power bank, a heavy pot, good shoes, a bicycle.
3. **Store the food you already eat.** Buy 1 or 2 extra items on each shopping trip. Eat the oldest first.
4. **Buy slowly, and buy good.** For tools you use often, buy one strong one instead of several cheap ones. Skip expensive "survival" products.
5. **Keep free habits:** phone and power bank charged, fuel tank at least half full, some cash in small notes, medicines refilled as soon as allowed.
6. **Know your neighbors.** Help each other now. People with friends nearby recover faster after disasters.
7. **Look after your body and mind.** Fitness, sleep, teeth, glasses and vaccinations are the first preparation.

## Why everyday preparation works

Gear left in a cupboard is forgotten or expires. Things you use every day stay working, and you know how to use them.

Official advice is modest. The European Union's 2025 preparedness strategy asks people to manage alone for at least 72 hours, and Sweden's leaflet for at least 1 week. Most of what they list is ordinary: water, food you eat, light, a radio, warm clothes, cash and medicines. For the checklists, see [Emergency Kit Checklists](../reference/emergency-kits.md).

The rule: **if disaster never comes, nothing you bought or learned is wasted.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Haybox food must stay steaming hot.** If it is only warm when you open it, boil it again. Boil dried beans hard for at least 10 minutes first: undercooked red kidney beans are poisonous.

## Skills that pay off every day

Skills weigh nothing, cannot be stolen, and do not expire.

| Skill | Every day | In a crisis | Learn it free or cheap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooking from basic food | Cheaper, healthier meals | Turn stored staples into meals with little fuel | Cook 1 new basic dish each week. Ask older relatives. |
| Growing food | Fresh food, lower bills | Food when supply stops | Start with greens in pots. See [Growing Food Fast](../farming/grow-food.md). |
| First aid | Correct help for everyday injuries | Keep people alive until help comes | Red Cross and Red Crescent courses, often cheap or free. See [First Aid Basics: Check, Call, Care](../medical/first-aid-basics.md). |
| Swimming | Fitness, safety near water | Survive floods and boat accidents | Public pools, community lessons. Teach children early. |
| Repair: clothes, bicycle, plumbing, electrical safety | Things last longer | Keep clothes, transport and shelter working | Repair cafes, library books, neighbors. Never work on live mains wiring unless trained. |
| Budgeting and saving | Less stress and debt | Money ready when prices jump | Write down every expense for 1 month. |
| Map and compass | Less need for a phone | Find your way when GPS fails | Practice on walks with a paper map. |
| A second language | Work, travel, friends | Talk to aid workers and authorities if you must flee | Libraries, language exchange with neighbors. |
| Physical fitness | Health and mood | Walk, carry water and work hard for days | Walking, cycling, stairs. Costs nothing. |
| Knowing your neighbors | A safer street | Shared tools, food, news and rescue | Say hello. Share extra food or tools. |

## How to start on any budget

### Almost no money

- **Start with skills.** They are free. Learn 1 skill from the table each season.
- **Collect free things:** clean food-grade bottles and jars with lids, cardboard and old blankets for a haybox, seeds from beans, squash and peppers you eat.
- **Buy 1 extra item of food you already eat** each week: rice, beans, lentils, oil, salt or sugar.
- **Save a very small amount of cash** each week, even a coin.
- **Ask for free services.** Some fire services give free smoke alarms, and some groups give free first aid courses.
- **Share with neighbors:** one ladder, one saw, one sewing machine for a whole street.

### Some money

- Add a headlamp, a power bank, water containers, a first aid kit and a radio.
- Build the food you already eat up to 2 weeks. See [Storing Emergency Food](../food/food-storage.md).
- Buy a heavy pot or pressure cooker, good shoes and warm layers. Second-hand is fine.
- Start an emergency savings fund. Aim first for 1 month of basic costs.

### Well off

- Cover the basics first. Money buys gear fast, but not skills, fitness or trust.
- Pay down debt. Many financial advisers suggest savings for 3 to 6 months of basic costs.
- Then add things that also cut bills: solar power, a rain tank, insulation, a bicycle, garden beds, fruit trees.
- Help your neighbors prepare. A ready street protects you too.

### Buy once, buy good

- **For tools you use often** (knife, pot, shoes, saw), a strong one can last decades. A cheap one often breaks the day you need it.
- **Good tools are simple:** solid steel, few plastic parts, can be sharpened or repaired.
- **Second-hand quality tools** are often better than new cheap ones.
- **For things you rarely use,** cheap is fine.
- **Buy slowly,** at the end of a season when prices fall, not just before a storm.

### Avoid gimmicks

- **"Survival" products** often cost more for less: buckets of special food you never eat, gadgets that do 10 jobs badly, "lasts 25 years" claims.
- **Fear selling:** "buy now before it is too late".
- **Anything you cannot repair, recharge or refill** where you live.
- **Weapons:** this library does not cover weapons.

## Free habits that make you ready

- **Fuel tank at least half full,** especially in storm, fire and winter seasons.
- **Phone and power bank charged** every night.
- **Some cash at home in small notes,** split between 2 or 3 places. Card machines and cash machines stop when the power fails.
- **Medicines refilled as soon as the pharmacy allows,** not on the last day. Aim to always have at least 1 week extra.
- **A little extra** of what you use daily: soap, salt, toilet paper, sanitary pads, diapers (nappies).
- **Rotate:** eat stored food oldest first, use rechargeable batteries every day, replace stored tap water every 6 months and bleach every year.
- **Spare freezer space filled** with bottles of water. A full freezer stays cold longer in a power cut.
- **Know your gas, water and electricity shut-offs.** See [Family Emergency Plan](../reference/family-plan.md).
- **Do not advertise what you store,** online or in person.

## Health is the first preparation

Your body is the one piece of equipment you cannot replace.

- **Fitness:** being able to walk 10 km (6 miles) and carry 10 liters (2.5 gallons) of water helps in almost every crisis. Build up slowly.
- **Teeth, eyes and feet:** fix dental problems now. Keep spare glasses. These become disasters when clinics close.
- **Vaccinations:** keep them up to date for the whole family, including tetanus.
- **Long-term illness:** keep diabetes, blood pressure, asthma and heart disease well controlled now. After Hurricane Maria in Puerto Rico (2017), about one third of the extra deaths were linked to delayed or interrupted medical care.
- **Mind:** sleep, routines and staying calm under small stresses make big stresses easier.
- **Connection:** in the 1995 Chicago heat wave, many of the dead were old people living alone. Friends and neighbors are part of your health.

Avoid the common traps: a kit never opened, food nobody eats, preparing alone. Test your gear on a planned "power-off" evening. For what becomes most precious in a long crisis, see [What Is Worth the Most When Everything Stops](../reference/most-valuable.md).
