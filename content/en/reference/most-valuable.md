> [!CAUTION]
> **Never show your whole supply.** People have been robbed, hurt and killed for food and fuel. Trade small amounts, in public, with someone you trust nearby.
>
> **Do not store large amounts of fuel or gas bottles in your home.** They can burn or explode. Keep small amounts in proper containers, outside living spaces.
>
> **Do not trade away your daily medicines, your tools or your seed.** They are how you get through the next months.

## Quick steps

1. **Water first:** containers with lids, and a way to make water safe, such as plain bleach, a filter, or fuel to boil.
2. **Then food and salt:** staple foods you already eat, cooking oil and salt.
3. **Then health:** daily medicines, oral rehydration salts, soap and plain bleach.
4. **Then warmth, fire and light:** blankets, warm clothes, good shoes, lighters, matches, safely stored fuel, batteries.
5. **Keep things that do not get used up:** steel tools, containers, rope, tarps and seeds. They keep their value for years.
6. **Invest in people.** Learn a skill others need, such as healing, growing food or repair. Build trust with neighbors: skills and friends outlast goods.
7. **Trade safely and fairly:** small amounts, in public, never your whole supply.

## What matters most, in order

What keeps people alive becomes scarce first. Water matters within days, warmth within hours in hard cold, food within weeks. Disease from dirty water kills the whole time. Money, luxury goods and electronics come last: you cannot drink, eat or burn them.

| Item | Why it matters | Keeps in storage | Value in documented crises |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safe water, containers, bleach, filters | Dirty water spreads cholera and diarrhea | Containers: decades. Bleach: about 1 year | Very high: Sarajevo, Puerto Rico, Lebanon |
| Salt | Health, and preserving meat, fish and vegetables | Almost forever if dry | Essential; rarely recorded as a trade good |
| Staple food: grains, beans, oil, sugar | Energy to work and stay warm | Rice and beans: years. Oil: about 1 year | Very high: Sarajevo, Venezuela |
| Fire: lighters, matches, ferro rods | Cooking, boiling water, warmth | Matches: years if dry. Ferro rods: decades | Essential; few recorded trade prices |
| Fuel: wood, gas, kerosene, petrol | Cooking, heat, transport, generators | Dry wood and bottled gas: years. Petrol: months | Very high: Sarajevo, Lebanon, Ukraine |
| Medicines: daily prescriptions, painkillers, oral rehydration salts | Long-term illness kills without them | Tablets: often years. Insulin and liquids: much less | Very high: Venezuela, Lebanon, Puerto Rico |
| Soap and bleach | Stop diarrhea and infection | Bar soap: years. Bleach: about 1 year | High: rationed in wartime Britain |
| Warm clothes, shoes, blankets | Cold kills; shoes are needed to walk and work | Years if dry and safe from moths and mice | High in cold winters, such as Ukraine's |
| Steel tools: knife, axe, saw, files, shovel, needles | Make and repair everything else | Decades if dry and oiled | High: selling them is a sign of deep crisis |
| Containers: buckets with lids, jerrycans, jars | Carry and store water and food | Decades | High: Sarajevo |
| Rope, cord and tarps | Repair roofs, build shelter, carry loads | Years in the dark; months in strong sun | High after storms: a standard aid item |
| Light and batteries | Safety, work and first aid at night | Alkaline: 5-10 years. Lithium AA: 10-20 years | High: Sarajevo, Ukraine |
| Seeds | Next year's food | 1 to 5 years, depending on the crop | High: Sarajevo balcony gardens |
| Information: radio, printed knowledge | Know where help and danger are | Paper: decades if dry | High: Puerto Rico, phones down for weeks |

### Notes on items and budgets

- **Medicines:** your own daily medicines are the hardest thing to replace. See [Medical Kit and Medicines](../medical/medical-kit.md).
- **Antibiotics: only with care.** The wrong antibiotic, dose or length of course can fail and breed resistant germs. Some cause dangerous allergic reactions. Get advice from a health worker whenever one can be reached.
- **Soap:** aid agencies plan about 250 g (9 oz) of bathing soap per person per month.
- **Little money:** containers, bleach, salt, soap, a lighter and seeds give the most protection per coin. Skills cost nothing.
- **Savings:** keep some cash, a 2-week supply and spare daily medicines. Do not turn savings into piles of goods you cannot store or protect.

## What history shows

### Sarajevo, 1992-1996

The city was cut off for almost 4 years, mostly without regular electricity, water or gas.

- **Cigarettes became money.** Cash was scarce, so most trade was barter.
- **Candles ran out within months.** People made new ones from leftover wax.
- **Fuel was so scarce** that people burned furniture, floors, books and shoes, and cut down the city's trees.
- **Water was carried** in canisters on carts and in baby carriages.
- **Black-market food** cost many times its pre-war price, often only in German marks.
- **People grew vegetables** on balconies, in old tins and boxes.
- **Knowledge mattered as much as goods.** Survivor accounts describe improvised stoves, home-made electricity and recipes without meat.

### Venezuela, from about 2014

- In 2016-2018, the pharmacists' federation estimated that **85% of medicines** were missing.
- Researchers documented rising hunger and the return of measles and diphtheria.
- Prices rose so fast that the local currency became almost worthless. Many people turned to US dollars and barter.

### Lebanon, from 2019

- The currency lost most of its value, and banks limited withdrawals.
- In 2021, fuel imports could no longer be paid for. Blackouts lasted **up to 23 hours a day**.
- Medicine shortages closed some pharmacies.
- Some sellers, such as solar installers, accepted only US dollar banknotes.

### Argentina, 2001-2002

- The government froze bank deposits, and unemployment rose above 20%.
- Barter clubs grew to an estimated 2.5 million people by 2002 (estimates vary). People traded home-made food, clothes and services such as repairs.
- The clubs' own paper money later lost value through over-printing and forgery. When trust broke, the clubs shrank.

### Puerto Rico after Hurricane Maria, 2017

- The average household went **84 days without electricity, 68 days without water and 41 days without mobile phone service.**
- About one third of the extra deaths were linked to interrupted medical care, most often missing medicines and no power for breathing machines.

### Ukraine, from 2022

- Grid attacks cut power to up to 10 million consumers at a time.
- People bought power banks and generators: in November 2022, about 8,500 generators a day entered the country.
- Thousands of public points offered heat, water and phone charging.

### Gaza, from 2023

- With banks closed, people paid traders **20% or more** of the amount to get cash.
- Torn and worn banknotes were often refused.

### The pattern

People who could treat illness, fix stoves and radios, grow food or sew had something to offer even with no goods. This pattern recurs in survivor stories, but it is mostly anecdotal: few studies measure it.

Aid agencies watch for a warning sign: families in deep crisis sell their tools and animals, or eat next year's seed. This makes recovery much harder.

## People are the most valuable "thing"

Goods get used up. Skills and trust grow when they are used.

**Who is worth the most in a group:**

- **Healers:** nurses, midwives, doctors, pharmacists, anyone trained in first aid.
- **Growers:** farmers, gardeners, people who keep animals or preserve food.
- **Repairers:** mechanics, electricians, plumbers, builders, tailors.
- **Teachers:** they pass on every other skill.
- **Organizers:** fair, calm people trusted to share out food and settle arguments.
- **Older people:** many remember how to cook on fire, preserve food and mend.

Research after disasters in several countries found that neighborhoods with strong ties recovered faster than richer ones with weak ties.

**What you can do now:**

1. Learn 1 skill well enough to teach it.
2. Offer your skills, not only your goods.
3. Be known as fair and honest.
4. Join or start a group of neighbors. See [Organizing a Community](../community/community-groups.md).

## Trading and bartering safely and fairly

1. **Trade in public,** in daylight, at a known market or meeting place. Bring a friend.
2. **Bring only what you plan to trade.** Keep the rest hidden at home. Never say how much you have.
3. **Trade small amounts often,** not large amounts at once.
4. **Agree the exchange first,** then check the goods: medicines sealed and dated, fuel without water, food dry and free of mold.
5. **Be fair.** Do not take advantage of desperate people. People remember who cheated them.
6. **Trade skills and work when you can.** They cannot be stolen from your home.
7. **Walk away** if something feels wrong. If threatened, give up the goods. Your life is worth more. See [Staying Safe from Other People](../community/personal-safety.md).

Selling prescription medicines is illegal in many places, and fakes are common in crises.

### What loses value

- **Paper money when prices rise fast,** as in Venezuela and Lebanon. Spend it on useful things before it loses more.
- **Money in the bank** when banks close or limit withdrawals, as in Argentina and Lebanon.
- **Large or worn notes:** nobody has change, and damaged notes may be refused.
- **Gold and jewelry** may hold value, but are hard to trade for small things and easy to steal.
- **Luxury goods:** you cannot eat, burn or build with them.
- **Electronics without power.** A phone is precious only where networks and charging still work.
- **Tobacco and alcohol** were traded in many crises. This library does not recommend storing them: they harm health.
- **Weapons:** this library does not cover weapons.

## What not to hoard

- **Fresh food:** bread, fruit, milk and meat rot within days without a fridge.
- **Food you do not eat.** It will be wasted.
- **Large amounts of petrol (gasoline), kerosene or gas bottles.** They can burn or explode, and petrol goes stale within months. Store small amounts in proper closed containers, outside living spaces, away from heat and flames. Many places have legal limits.
- **More medicine than you can use before it expires.** Leave the rest for others.
- **Anything you cannot keep dry, cool and safe from insects and rats.**
- **So much that you become a target.**

See also [Emergency Kit Checklists](../reference/emergency-kits.md) and [Preparing Wisely: Things and Skills That Pay Off Every Day](../reference/everyday-prep.md).

## Sharing as a survival strategy

- **One family cannot do everything:** guard, cook, nurse, farm and repair at the same time.
- **Help returns.** People who share early are helped later.
- **Shared cooking saves fuel:** one big pot uses less wood per person than many small ones.
- **Share tools and skills:** one saw, one sewing machine, one roof mender for a whole street.
- **Share with care.** Give through a group or community kitchen, so nobody learns exactly what you have.
