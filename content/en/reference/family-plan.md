> [!CAUTION]
> **Phones and networks fail in disasters.** Write every important number on paper. Every family member carries a contact card, children included.
>
> **If you smell gas, get everyone out first.** Do not use light switches, phones or flames inside. Turn the gas off only if you can do it safely from outside. Never turn the main gas back on yourself.
>
> **Never touch the electrical panel while standing in water or on a wet floor.**

## Quick steps

1. **Choose an out-of-area contact** that everyone will call or text.
2. **Choose 3 meeting places:** just outside your home, outside your neighborhood, and outside your town.
3. **Make contact cards** for everyone. Children carry theirs every day.
4. **Give every job a main person and a backup:** collecting children, helping older or disabled relatives, pets, bags, utilities.
5. **Learn to shut off** gas, water and electricity.
6. **Agree a family code word.**
7. **Practice** the plan twice a year.

## Contact cards

- **Out-of-area contact:** a relative or friend who lives in another city or region. After a local disaster, long-distance calls often get through when local ones do not. Everyone reports to this one person, who passes news on.
- **Texts get through when calls fail.** A short text needs very little network capacity and keeps trying to send. Write: "Safe at school. Going to aunt's house. 3 pm."
- Make calls only for emergencies, so the network is not overloaded.
- **Each card holds:** the holder's name, the out-of-area contact, 2 local contacts, the meeting places, school and work numbers, and medical needs (allergies, conditions, medicines).
- Everyone memorizes at least one number. Practice with children until they can say it.

If phones fail, a pair of LoRa mesh radios lets family members text each other. See [Text Messages Without Internet: Mesh Radios and Phones](../comms/mesh-networks.md).

## Meeting places

| Place | What it is for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Just outside your home | A sudden danger such as a house fire | A tree, a gate, a neighbor's door |
| Outside your neighborhood | When you cannot get home | A school, library, place of worship or community center |
| Outside your town | When the whole area is evacuated | A relative's or friend's home |

Also choose the safest spot inside your home for storms and earthquakes: an inner room with no windows, or under a strong table.

## Evacuation routes and destinations

- Know **at least 2 routes** out of your area, in different directions. Mark them on a paper map.
- Plan for having no car: walking routes, public transport, neighbors with vehicles.
- Go first to friends or family outside the danger area. Use a public shelter as a backup.
- When warned, leave early. Roads fill quickly.
- In risky seasons, keep the fuel tank at least half full.
- Learn your local warning signals: sirens, bells, radio announcements. See [Emergency Radio](../comms/emergency-radio.md).

## Jobs in an emergency

Decide now who does what. Every job needs a backup person in case the main person is hurt, away or cannot get there.

- Collecting children from school or childcare.
- Helping older, ill or disabled relatives and neighbors. See [Elderly, Disabled and Chronically Ill People](../community/vulnerable-people.md).
- Taking pets. Many shelters do not accept pets, so plan where they can go.
- Grabbing the grab-and-go bags. See [Emergency Kit Checklists](../reference/emergency-kits.md).
- Turning off gas, water and power.
- Calling the out-of-area contact.

## School, childcare and work

Ask your children's school or childcare:

- Will they keep children inside or move them? Where to?
- How will they contact parents?
- Who is allowed to collect your child? Add at least one backup adult to their list.

Ask your workplace about its evacuation plan, and whether you must stay during an emergency. Health, utility and emergency workers often must, so they need a plan for their children. Know how to walk home from work.

## Shutting off utilities

| Utility | When to shut it off | How |
|---|---|---|
| Gas | You smell gas, hear hissing, or are told to | Leave first. At the meter, turn the valve a quarter turn so the handle crosses the pipe. For a gas bottle, turn the valve on top clockwise. |
| Water | Pipes burst, or you are told the water supply is contaminated | Turn the main valve (stopcock) clockwise. It is usually where the pipe enters the home. |
| Electricity | Sparks, burning smell, flooding or damage | Switch off each circuit first, then the main switch. Stay dry. |

- On most lever valves, a handle **across** the pipe means closed and **along** the pipe means open.
- After gas has been turned off at the meter, **only a trained person should turn it back on.**
- Keep a wrench next to the gas and water valves. Label the valves and the main switch.
- Show every adult and teenager where they are.

Closing the water main keeps dirty water out of your pipes and saves the clean water still in your pipes and water heater. See [Storing Water](../water/store-water.md).

## Family code word

- Choose a word or phrase only your family knows.
- Teach children: go only with an adult who knows the code word, even if the person says "your mother sent me".
- Use it to check calls or messages that ask for money or help, even when the voice sounds like a family member.
- Do not write it on contact cards. Change it if someone outside the family learns it.

## Practice

Twice a year:

- Walk to each meeting place, and drive or walk one evacuation route.
- Practice getting out of the home quickly, as in a fire. See [Wildfires and House Fires](../disasters/wildfire.md).
- Have children say their full names, your names and the out-of-area contact's number.
- Send a test text to the out-of-area contact.
- Find and touch each shut-off valve and switch.
- Check the grab-and-go bags.
