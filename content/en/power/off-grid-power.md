> [!CAUTION]
> **Never run a generator or a car engine indoors, in a garage, under a carport or near open windows, even with doors open.** Carbon monoxide has no smell and kills within minutes. Keep generators at least **6 m (20 ft)** from doors, windows and vents. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
>
> **Never plug a generator into a wall socket or connect it to house wiring** without a proper transfer switch fitted by an electrician. This "backfeeding" sends power out into the grid. It can kill line workers repairing the lines and neighbors, and start fires.

## Quick steps

1. **List what you really need:** phone, radio, a few LED lights, and any medical device.
2. **Charge small devices from power banks** or a 12 V car-socket USB adapter.
3. **Use the car battery carefully.** Charging phones with the engine off is fine. Do not drain it so far that the car will not start.
4. **Add a small solar panel** for anything longer than a few days.
5. **Run a generator only outdoors,** far from the house, for big loads such as a fridge.
6. **Switch everything off** when not in use. Use direct USB or 12 V power instead of an inverter when you can.

## Power in plain words

- **Volts (V):** the "push" of electricity. USB is 5 V. A car battery is 12 V. Home sockets are 120 V or 230 V, depending on the country.
- **Amps (A):** how much electricity flows.
- **Watts (W):** how fast a device uses energy. Watts = volts times amps. A 12 V device using 2 A uses 24 W.
- **Watt-hours (Wh):** how much energy is used or stored. A 10 W light on for 5 hours uses 50 Wh.
- **Battery capacity:** amp-hours (Ah) times volts gives watt-hours. A 12 V, 50 Ah battery holds 600 Wh on paper. Power banks list milliamp-hours (mAh) at about 3.7 V: a 10,000 mAh bank holds about 37 Wh, and delivers about two-thirds of that to a phone.

## Power budget

| Device | Power used | Typical energy per day |
|---|---|---|
| Phone, one full charge | - | 10-20 Wh |
| LED headlamp or flashlight | 1-3 W | 3-10 Wh |
| LED bulb | 5-10 W | 20-40 Wh (4 hours) |
| Portable radio | 1-5 W | 3-15 Wh |
| Wi-Fi router | 5-15 W | 50-150 Wh (10 hours) |
| Laptop | 30-60 W | 60-180 Wh (2-3 hours) |
| CPAP breathing machine, no heater | 30-60 W | 250-500 Wh (8 hours) |
| Fridge | 100-200 W running | 1,000-2,000 Wh |
| Kettle, heater, hair dryer | 1,000-2,000 W | Far too much |

Small sources (power banks, car sockets, small solar, pedaling) handle the top 5 rows. A fridge or anything that heats needs a generator or a large battery and solar system. Ask the maker or supplier how long a medical device runs on battery, and plan for it. See [Elderly, Disabled and Chronically Ill People](../community/vulnerable-people.md).

## Where to get power

### Power banks

The simplest store of power. A 10,000 mAh bank charges most phones about 2 times. Keep them charged. Recharge them from a car, solar panel or generator whenever you can.

### Car battery and 12 V adapter

1. Use a **12 V socket USB adapter** (the cigarette lighter socket). It wastes far less power than an inverter.
2. With the engine off, charging phones and radios is safe for hours. A typical car battery holds 500-900 Wh, but only a small part can be used and still start the engine.
3. Every few days, or after heavy use, start the engine **outdoors** and run it for 30 minutes, or drive.
4. If you have a voltmeter: a resting battery at **12.4 V** or more is still healthy. Stop using it at about 12.2 V (half charged).

An idling car burns about **1 to 2 liters (a quarter to half a gallon) of fuel per hour**, so do not idle just to charge a phone. Some cars switch off the socket when the key is out.

> [!WARNING]
> Never run an engine in a garage, even with the door open. In snow, clear the exhaust pipe before running the engine: a blocked exhaust fills the car with carbon monoxide.

An electric car can charge devices for days from its socket with no fumes. A plug-in hybrid may start its engine by itself: treat it like a fuel car.

### Small solar panels

- **Real output:** multiply the panel's watts by the hours of strong sun, then by about 0.7. A 20 W panel in 4 hours of good sun gives about **55 Wh**: 2 to 3 phone charges. Cloud cuts output to a tenth or a quarter.
- **Small USB panels** can charge a phone directly, but the output jumps with every cloud. Charging a power bank works better.
- **Bigger systems** go panel, then charge controller, then battery, then devices. Never connect a panel straight to a battery without a charge controller (except tiny "trickle" panels made for car batteries).
- **Aim it** toward the equator (south in the northern hemisphere, north in the southern), tilted at about your latitude. Keep it free of any shade: shading one corner can cut output by half or more.
- **Fit a fuse** in the wire close to the battery. A short circuit without a fuse can start a fire.

### Generators

- A small 2,000 W generator burns roughly **0.5 to 1.5 liters of fuel per hour**, depending on load. Run it a few hours a day to charge batteries and cool a fridge, not all day.
- Place it outside, at least **6 m (20 ft)** from the house, with the exhaust pointing away. Put a battery carbon monoxide alarm inside.
- Keep it dry under an open cover. Never touch it with wet hands.
- Connect devices with heavy outdoor extension cords rated for the load. Do not run cords under rugs.
- **Refuel only when the engine is off and cool.** Fuel on a hot engine can explode into flame. Store fuel outside the living area in proper containers.

### Hand-crank and bicycle generators

Be realistic. A fit adult pedaling hard makes about **50 to 100 W** for an hour, and less after losses. A hand crank gives only 5 to 20 W. One hour of pedaling gives a few phone charges, and it burns 250 to 400 kcal of food. When food is short, use it only for small, important loads such as a radio or a phone.

## Inverters

An inverter turns 12 V battery power into 120 V or 230 V household power.

- It wastes 10 to 20% of the power. Many also use a few watts, up to 20 W, even with nothing plugged in. Switch it off when not in use.
- Small inverters (up to about 150 W) plug into a car socket. Bigger ones must connect straight to the battery with thick cables and a fuse.
- **Pure sine wave** inverters are best. Some medical devices, motors and chargers do not work properly on cheaper "modified sine wave" inverters.
- An inverter's output is as deadly as a wall socket. Treat it the same way.

## Safety

- **No backfeeding, ever.** Power fed into house wiring can be stepped up to thousands of volts by the transformer outside. It kills line workers and can destroy your generator when grid power returns.
- Keep electricity away from water. Never use devices standing in floodwater.
- Cables that get warm are overloaded. Use thicker cables or a smaller load.
- Stay at least **10 m (33 ft)** from fallen power lines. Assume they are live.
- Store and charge batteries safely: see [Batteries: Use, Charge and Stay Safe](../power/batteries.md).
