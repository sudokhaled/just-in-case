> [!CAUTION]
> **Candles and lamps cause many deadly house fires during blackouts.** Use battery lights first. Never leave a flame burning while you sleep or leave the room. Keep flames at least **30 cm (1 ft)** from anything that can burn.
>
> **Never burn kerosene (paraffin) lamps, fuel lanterns or heaters in a closed room.** They make carbon monoxide, which has no smell and kills. See [Carbon Monoxide and Smoke](../fire/carbon-monoxide.md).
>
> **Never put petrol (gasoline) in a lamp.** It can explode. **If you smell gas, use no flames or switches.** Leave and use only a flashlight or light stick.

## Quick steps

1. **Use LED flashlights and headlamps first.** A headlamp leaves both hands free.
2. **Keep a light by every bed** and in the same place every night.
3. **Charge solar garden lights in the sun by day** and bring them inside at night.
4. **Use the lowest brightness** that works. It makes batteries last many times longer.
5. **If you must use a flame,** put it in a jar or deep bowl on a stable surface that does not burn. One awake adult watches it.
6. **Put out every flame** before anyone sleeps.

## Best choices, in order

| Light | Fire risk | Fumes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| LED headlamp or flashlight | None | None | Best choice. Weeks of use on low |
| Solar garden light | None | None | Free night light |
| Light stick | None | None | One use, 4-12 hours |
| Vegetable oil lamp | Low | Little | Oil itself is hard to light |
| Candle | Medium | Little | Needs constant watching |
| Kerosene lamp | Medium to high | Carbon monoxide | Needs fresh air |

## LED flashlights and headlamps

- **Low mode often lasts 10 times longer than high mode.** Most tasks need only low.
- **Use rechargeable NiMH batteries** if you can charge them. Lithium AA batteries work best in freezing cold.
- **Red light mode** keeps your night vision. Your eyes take 20 to 30 minutes to adjust fully to the dark.
- **Make a lantern:** strap a headlamp to a jug or bottle of water, light facing into the water. The water spreads the light around the room.
- **Store spare batteries** outside the device, so they do not leak inside it. See [Batteries: Use, Charge and Stay Safe](../power/batteries.md).

## Solar garden lights

Cheap garden stake lights make good free night lights.

1. Pull the light off its stake.
2. Put it in full sun during the day, 6 to 8 hours if possible.
3. At night, bring it inside and stand it in a jar or cup. It gives a dim light for 4 to 8 hours.
4. Use them in hallways, toilets and stairs, and as night lights for children.

Their batteries are usually rechargeable AA or AAA NiMH cells. You can swap them into other devices.

## Light sticks (glow sticks)

- Safe indoors: no heat, no flame, no fumes, no sparks. Good near gas leaks, for children, and in tents.
- Bend to start. They are brightest in the first hour and fade over 4 to 12 hours. Cold makes them dimmer but last longer.
- They keep for about 2 to 4 years unopened.
- Do not cut them open. The liquid irritates skin and eyes.
- **Tie one to a 1 m (3 ft) cord and swing it in a circle** to make a large ring of light that rescuers can see.

## Candles

1. **Use a stable holder** that cannot tip, or put the candle in a glass jar or a tall metal can with sand or soil in the bottom.
2. **Place it away** from curtains, paper, bedding, children and pets, on a surface that does not burn.
3. **Trim the wick** to about 6 mm (1/4 in) before lighting. A long wick smokes and flares.
4. **Do not move a lit candle,** and do not carry one as a flashlight.
5. **Put it out** when 5 cm (2 in) remains, or 1 cm (1/2 in) in a container.
6. **Put it out with a lid or snuffer,** not water. Water makes hot wax spit.

Keep a bucket of water or sand nearby. If you have a smoke alarm, check that its battery works.

A household candle burns for about 5 to 8 hours. A tea light burns about 4 hours.

## Oil lamp from vegetable oil

Vegetable oils (olive, sunflower, rapeseed or canola, palm, and others) are much safer than kerosene. They do not catch fire from a spark or a dropped match; they burn only from a wick. But a tipped lamp can still set fire to oil-soaked cloth or bedding.

1. **Choose a heavy glass jar or ceramic bowl** with a wide base, so it cannot tip.
2. **Pour in 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) of oil.**
3. **Make a wick** from 100% cotton: cotton string, a strip of an old cotton T-shirt about 1 cm (1/2 in) wide twisted tight, or a cotton shoelace. Synthetic fabric melts and smells.
4. **Make a wick holder:** bend stiff wire (a paper clip or a piece of a coat hanger) into a flat spiral that sits on the bottom, with one end bent upright. Wrap the wick around the upright end.
5. **Set the height:** the wick tip should stand about 5 to 10 mm (1/4 to 1/2 in) above the oil.
6. **Soak the wick** in oil fully before lighting, then light the tip.
7. If it smokes, trim the wick or lower it.

A candle-sized flame uses about 1 tablespoon (15 mL) of oil every 2 hours. Animal fat, butter and ghee also work.

## Kerosene (paraffin) lamps

- **Use only the fuel the lamp is made for:** kerosene or lamp oil. Never petrol, gasoline, camping fuel or alcohol.
- **Fill it outdoors, when cool and unlit.** Fill no more than three-quarters full and wipe up spills.
- **Keep a window open** a little in every room with a lamp burning. Never use one in a tent or a small closed room, and never sleep with one burning.
- **Fit a battery carbon monoxide alarm** if you have one. Headache, dizziness and nausea in several people at once mean carbon monoxide: get everyone into fresh air.
- **The glass chimney gets very hot.** Keep children away.
- **Store fuel outside the living space,** in a labeled container. Never store fuel in drink bottles: children drink it, and swallowing kerosene can cause deadly lung damage. See [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md).

Pressure lanterns and gas camping lanterns make even more carbon monoxide. Use them outdoors.

## Making light go further

- **Bounce light** off a white wall or ceiling. Point a headlamp at the ceiling to light a whole room.
- **Use mirrors.** A mirror behind a light sends back light that would be wasted, nearly doubling the light in front of it.
- **Use foil.** Aluminum foil behind a candle reflects light forward. Keep it at least 10 cm (4 in) from the flame.
- **Gather in one room** in the evening. One light for the whole family saves fuel and batteries.
- **Light-colored sheets and walls** make any room brighter.

## Darkness safety

- **Clear paths** of toys, tools and debris before dark. Falls in the dark cause many injuries.
- **Mark stairs and doorways** with white tape, reflective tape or a light stick.
- **Carry a light** around your neck or on your wrist at night.
- **After an earthquake, storm or flood, use no flames** until you are sure there is no gas leak.
- **In conflict areas,** cover windows. Light can be seen from far away.
