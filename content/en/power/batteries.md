> [!CAUTION]
> **A swollen, hot, hissing or smoking lithium battery can burst into fire.** Stop charging it. Do not press, puncture or crush it. If you can do it safely, move it outdoors onto bare ground, sand or concrete, away from anything that burns.
>
> **Charging lead-acid batteries (car, deep-cycle) gives off hydrogen, which explodes.** Charge only with good airflow, with no flames, cigarettes or sparks nearby.
>
> **Never short a battery** by touching both terminals with metal. A car battery can melt a wrench and burn you. Never try to recharge non-rechargeable batteries.

## Battery types

| Type | Volts | Rechargeable | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkaline (AA, AAA, C, D, 9 V) | 1.5 per cell | No | Flashlights, radios | Keeps 5-10 years; leaks when old |
| Lithium AA, AAA | 1.5 | No | Cold weather, long storage | Keeps 10-20 years; works in hard frost |
| Coin cell (button) | 1.5-3 | No | Watches, small devices | Deadly if swallowed |
| NiMH (AA, AAA) | 1.2 | Yes | Everyday use | Hundreds of charges; "low self-discharge" types hold charge for a year |
| Lithium-ion (phones, laptops, power banks) | 3.6-3.7 per cell | Yes | High energy, light weight | Fire risk if damaged |
| LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) | 3.2 per cell, 12.8 per pack | Yes | Solar storage | Safer lithium type; thousands of charges |
| Lead-acid, car (starting) | 12 | Yes | Starting engines | Not for deep draining |
| Lead-acid, deep-cycle (leisure, AGM, gel) | 12 | Yes | Solar, boats, camping | Lasts longer if kept above half charge |

> [!WARNING]
> **A swallowed coin or button battery can burn through a child's throat in 2 hours.** Get medical help immediately, even if the child seems fine. Do not make the child vomit. US poison centers advise giving a child over 1 year old **10 mL (2 teaspoons) of honey every 10 minutes, up to 6 times**, on the way to help. Keep these batteries locked away. See [Poisoning](../medical/poisoning.md).

## Capacity: how much a battery holds

- **mAh or Ah** (milliamp-hours, amp-hours) tells you capacity at that battery's own voltage.
- **Wh** (watt-hours) lets you compare any batteries: Ah times volts = Wh.

| Battery | Rough energy |
|---|---|
| AA NiMH, 2,000 mAh at 1.2 V | 2.4 Wh |
| AA alkaline | 2-3 Wh (much less in high-power devices) |
| Phone battery | 12-20 Wh |
| Power bank, 10,000 mAh at 3.7 V | 37 Wh |
| Car battery, 60 Ah at 12 V | 720 Wh (only part usable) |

## Storing batteries

- **Keep them cool and dry,** ideally 10-25°C (50-77°F). Heat drains and damages all batteries. Never leave them in a hot car or in sun.
- **Lithium-ion: store at about 40-60% charge** (about half). Check every 3 to 6 months and top up to half. Do not store full or empty. A lithium battery left completely flat for months may be damaged and unsafe to charge.
- **NiMH:** store charged. Recharge every 6 to 12 months.
- **Alkaline:** keep in the original packet. Take them out of devices you will not use for months. If one leaks, wear gloves, wipe the white crust away with vinegar or lemon juice on a cloth, and keep it off skin and eyes.
- **Lead-acid: store fully charged** and recharge every 1 to 3 months. A flat lead-acid battery is damaged quickly and can freeze and crack in hard frost.
- **Cover terminals.** Tape the ends of 9 V and lithium batteries. Never carry loose batteries in a pocket with coins or keys.

## Series and parallel

```
SERIES: volts add, capacity stays the same

  (+)[ 6 V ](-)----(+)[ 6 V ](-)
   |                          |
  out +        12 V        out -

PARALLEL: capacity adds, volts stay the same

  (+)[ 12 V, 50 Ah ](-)
   |                |
  (+)[ 12 V, 50 Ah ](-)
   |                |
  out +   12 V,   out -
         100 Ah
```

- **Series:** join the + of one battery to the - of the next. Volts add up. This is how cells sit in a flashlight.
- **Parallel:** join + to + and - to -. Volts stay the same; capacity adds up.
- **Only combine identical batteries:** same type, size, age and charge level. Mixing old and new, or different types, makes one overheat or leak. Never mix lithium and lead-acid.

## Charging lead-acid batteries safely

1. **Charge outdoors or in a well-ventilated space.** Never in a closed room where people sleep.
2. **Wear eye protection** and take off metal rings and watches.
3. **Connect the charger to the battery first, then switch it on.** Switch off before disconnecting. Sparks happen at the connection.
4. **Use the right setting** for the battery type: flooded, AGM, gel or lithium.
5. **Stop if the battery gets hot, swells, or smells of rotten eggs.** It is overcharging.
6. For flooded batteries with caps, top up with distilled water after charging, just to cover the plates. Never add acid.

Battery acid burns. If it gets on skin or in eyes, rinse with lots of clean water for at least **15 minutes**.

**State of charge by voltage** (12 V lead-acid battery, resting for a few hours with nothing connected):

| Voltage | Charge |
|---|---|
| 12.7 V | Full |
| 12.4 V | About 75% |
| 12.2 V | About 50% |
| 12.0 V | About 25% |
| 11.9 V or less | Flat |

**Jump-starting a car:** connect red to the + of the flat battery, red to the + of the good battery, black to the - of the good battery, then black to bare metal on the stalled car's engine, away from its battery. Remove in reverse order.

## Lithium battery safety

- **Charge on a hard surface that does not burn,** not on a bed, sofa or pillow. Do not cover devices while charging.
- **Use the charger made for the device,** or a good-quality one.
- **Never charge a lithium-ion battery below 0°C (32°F).** It can damage the battery inside and cause a fire later. Warm it to room temperature first. Using it in the cold is fine; it just runs down faster.
- **Keep it out of heat.** Above about 60°C (140°F), a hot car dashboard for example, the fire risk rises.
- **Stop using any battery** that is swollen, dented, leaking, or gets hot when charging.

### If a lithium battery catches fire

1. **Get people away.** The smoke is poisonous.
2. **Small device (phone, power bank):** if it is safe, put out the flames with any fire extinguisher, then **cool it with lots of water**, or drop it into a bucket of water or cover it with sand or soil. Water is fine for lithium-ion batteries.
3. **Large battery** (e-bike, scooter, power station, car): leave, close the doors behind you, and call the fire service if one exists. Do not try to fight it.
4. **Expect it to reignite.** Leave the battery in water or sand, outdoors, away from buildings, for at least 24 hours.

## Making a phone battery last

- **Use airplane mode** whenever you do not need the phone. Searching for a weak signal drains the battery fastest.
- **Turn the phone on at set times** to check messages, then off again.
- **Lower the screen brightness** and set a short screen timeout.
- **Use dark mode** if your phone has an OLED screen. It saves power on OLED, not on older LCD screens.
- **Turn off** Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, location and background app refresh when not needed. Use battery-saver mode.
- **Send text messages** instead of calling. They use less power and get through weak signals.
- **Keep the phone warm** in an inside pocket in cold weather. Cold makes the battery seem empty.

## Disposal

- **Never burn batteries.** They can explode.
- **Do not put lithium or lead-acid batteries in household trash.** They cause fires in trash trucks and leak poisons.
- Tape lithium battery terminals and keep used batteries in a metal box away from anything that burns until they can be recycled.
- Lead-acid batteries contain lead and acid. Return them to a seller or recycler. Never bury or dump them near water.
